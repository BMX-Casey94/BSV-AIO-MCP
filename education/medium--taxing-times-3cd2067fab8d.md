# Taxing Times… — core principles

- **The protocol is set in stone.** One of the key aspects of Bitcoin is that the protocol does not change.
- **Nodes do not vote to change the protocol.** The white paper’s “vote” concerns only honest nodes voting on the ordering of transactions. There is no mechanism for users or the community to change the protocol, and none was ever designed or intended.
- **Enforcement is not legislation.** Enforcement is a police or regulatory function and does not involve the creation of rules. Miners enforce; they do not legislate.
- **Bitcoin is not a parliament.** As a system for democracy it is horrible: those who invest the most get to vote more. The higher investor has more to lose, and law enables a large investor to lose large amounts of money if they act dishonestly.
- **Hard forks are not part of Bitcoin.** The concept of hard forks or even soft forks was added later. A hard fork is a protocol change resulting in a permanent diversion from the legacy distributed ledger.
- **SPV and direct exchange are part of the original design.** A system that does not implement SPV, and that drops the payment protocol enabling peer-to-peer exchange, is not peer-to-peer in any reasonable manner.
- **Script can host voting on top.** The scripting engine can host voting systems built on Bitcoin, where the ordering of votes could be important — that is application, not protocol change.

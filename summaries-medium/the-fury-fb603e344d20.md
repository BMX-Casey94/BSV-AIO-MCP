# The Fury
**Date:** 2018-12-10 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-fury-fb603e344d20
**Subtitle:** I have spent a lot of my life being angry. The drive and passion in me that drives the creative spark has many sides. It is not just…

## Core thesis
A confessional memoir of the anger driving Craig's work: childhood poverty, late-stage cancer in his early 20s, Dave Kleiman's death in 2013, and the Liberty Reserve seizure. Written three weeks after the BCH/BSV split, it declares the "anarchist and criminal element" purged from SV and pivots to building Bitcoin as honest, lawful money.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Risk and security are economic quantities" — a long-stated position (linking a 2010 TaoSecurity exchange) that has "coloured my reactions".
- Background: raised by a working single mother — "To have had a humble start, I would have needed more"; treated for late-stage cancer in his early 20s, which derailed his first degree at a sandstone university; too proud to ask for help.
- Dave (Kleiman): they worked together for years; Dave "edited a lot of my papers, and helped with some of my businesses". They fought in early April 2013 — "That fight was the last time I ever spoke to Dave"; Dave died later that month, and Craig "grieved for years refusing to face it".
- Gaming-industry history: he helped set up Lasseter's Online in the 90s; after the US restricted payment operations in 2005, he and Dave ran companies in Panama and Costa Rica servicing licensed gaming, using Liberty Reserve on the reasoning that NAB and Westpac had ties to it. The US seized Liberty Reserve in March 2013 while he was restructuring to repatriate funds — which "would have resulted in taxes payable to the Australian and US government".
- Hotwire PE took off in 2013 and was hit by the 2014 Bitcoin crash; rather than liquidate, "we paid the debts. 100 cents in the dollar."
- Bitcoin's purpose: "not a system to allow drugs, but one to allow people to take control of their own lives… based on honesty, of work and effort"; it lets law-breakers "be tracked and brought to face justice", enables "fair taxation", and protects people in countries lacking Western institutions.
- Post-split declaration: "we have the anarchist and criminal element out of SV, and they will not be back… The road is clear. Now, we build."
- Footnote claim: mobile-banking 2FA systems derive in part from systems he helped create (archived gse-compliance blog).

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Ethos-by-confession: personal suffering and a gambling/forensics-industry backstory are marshalled to frame Bitcoin as a moral project of honesty, work and law rather than anarchist escape. The mode is emotional self-disclosure in service of a post-hash-war positioning statement — biography as legitimacy argument.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the cypherpunk framing of Bitcoin as a tool to route around the state: Bitcoin is "a system of truth" where criminals are traced and taxed.
- Contradicts the anti-tax, anti-bank ethos of the era: he notes willingness to pay Australian and US tax and praises "fair taxation".
- Implicitly recasts the BCH (ABC) camp after the November 2018 split as the purged "anarchist and criminal element", framing BSV as the lawful continuation.

## Notable quotes
- "My beginning was not humble; To have had a humble start, I would have needed more."
- "Facing death multiple times in youth changes a person. Some are broken, others have to prove something."
- "That fight was the last time I ever spoke to Dave."

## Connections
Key primary source for the Dave Kleiman relationship (later central to the Kleiman v. Wright litigation) and for his Liberty Reserve/gaming-industry history.

# The Asset That Pays Rent to Exist — core principles

- **Proof-of-work security is a flow, not a stock.** Once produced, gold and equities persist without further expenditure. A PoW ledger must be continuously re-secured with real resources; hashes function solely as a cost signal, and the only service sold is settlement.
- **Miner revenue pins the annual burn to price within a subsidy epoch.** The identity R = s(t)·B·P(t)·(1+f) makes carrying cost linear in the asset's own price. Security per dollar of market value follows the halving schedule whatever the price does, because in ρ = S/(P·N) the price cancels.
- **Efficiency cannot cheapen a cost signal.** Cheaper energy enlarges the fleet rather than cutting the burn: security depends solely on cost levels, and the difficulty adjustment reinvests every saving. You cannot make a cost signal cheaper; you can only make it larger for the same money.
- **Fees price delay, not value secured.** A large transfer and a small transfer pay identically for queue position. Fee revenue depends on capacity, congestion and waiting costs, not the exchange rate — so a shortage of mining resources does not bid missing security into existence.
- **Elastic capacity is the prescribed design.** The canonical fee-market model brands fixed block parameters undesirable and prescribes μ = λ/(Kρ*): capacity that expands with demand. Steady-state security equals the number of transactions multiplied by what each pays — high volume at low fees, not a ceremonial scarce slot.
- **Issuance is a transfer, not output.** Counting newly issued coins as "value created" flatters the energy denominator. The correct denominator is settlement service sold; otherwise the energy debate is a category error.
- **Siting benefits are orthogonal to the carrying-cost problem.** Mining can absorb dispatch-down energy at a wind farm, but that is a local siting argument, not a scale answer to a price-indexed security bill.

# The Toll Booth Economy — core principles

- **Intermediaries must earn their keep.** They are justified only when they reduce the costs of exchange more than they increase dependency, delay, rent and control. Once the route is compulsory, friction becomes revenue.
- **The deeper cost is lost information.** Markets are Hayekian discovery procedures: knowledge is dispersed, local and tacit, and prices compress it. Compulsory intermediation corrupts the signal — inserting charges, delaying settlement, suppressing trades and converting price discovery into platform discovery.
- **High fees erase micropayments from the record.** When payment costs are too high, small trades disappear and the market appears to say there is no demand. The intermediary prevented the question from being asked; the lost knowledge is counterfactual and unrecoverable.
- **Fee wedges destroy exchange that would otherwise exist.** A buyer willing to pay £10 and a seller willing to accept £9 cannot trade if intermediaries insert £2 of cost — a deadweight loss on the graph, a business that never existed in the world.
- **Direct settlement unbundles banking.** Banks capture settlement and wrap it with custody, credit and compliance. De-risking then excludes lawful but inconvenient customers. Separating payment from custody threatens that bundle.
- **Identity as a permissions registry is not a market.** Collapsing contextual trust into centralised credentialing economically disables anyone wrongly excluded from the identity–payment–platform stack.
- **Data extraction is hidden taxation.** The intermediary that observes all participants becomes an intelligence layer, enabling personalised, opaque pricing. Privacy in payment is a defence of market knowledge, not a cloak.

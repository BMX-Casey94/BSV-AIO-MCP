## Problem addressed
The specification says unconfirmed transactions accumulate in a per-node mempool and that Bitcoin-style ~10-minute blocks and ~3 transactions per second cannot support high-volume use (it cites payment-card scale). Replicating a large, fast-changing mempool at every node also hits storage limits. It seeks faster, consistent, attack-resistant storage and retrieval of mempool records.

## Core mechanism
A distributed mempool (DMP) of specialised mempool nodes implements a DHT. Validator nodes check incoming transactions and send store(Ti, idi) or reject(idi) messages (idi = H(Ti)); a mempool node stores Ti only after a quorum (N/2+1) of store messages. Each mempool node is identified by a signed (public key, IP) record. Routing tables are built in two phases: random walks collect key-value samples into Tab_RND; then α iterations fill Tab_ID (a random identity from the previous long-distance table), Tab_DIST (random-walk pointers uniformly over the key space) and Tab_CLOSE (lexicographically nearest keys). Look-up walks long-distance pointers until a node whose Tab_CLOSE holds the key answers; consistency requires M/2+1 matching replicas. Keys are not placed by a deterministic hash, which the specification says frustrates clustering and Sybil attacks.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method at a first mempool node of a DMP DHT: on a request to update routing information, first initialise random walks to fill Tab_RND, then for α iterations build Tab_ID from a random identity in the previous Tab_DIST (first entry a random Tab_RND key), Tab_DIST by walking to another node and collecting its address and i-th Tab_ID, and Tab_CLOSE from Tab_RND records lexicographically closest to the identity at that iteration.

## BSV relevance
The specification is about scaling a Bitcoin-style mempool and UTXO set via a DHT overlay. A consistent distributed mempool is directly relevant to high-throughput BSV node design.

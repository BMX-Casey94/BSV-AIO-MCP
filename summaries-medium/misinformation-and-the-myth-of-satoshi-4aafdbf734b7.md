# Misinformation and the myth of Satoshi
**Date:** 2019-04-25 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/misinformation-and-the-myth-of-satoshi-4aafdbf734b7
**Subtitle:** Bitcoin is a story of myths; it was never designed for such an end, but it's what they who have no idea of science or testing hypotheses…

## Core thesis
Craig attacks Sergio Lerner's "Patoshi" single-miner theory as an untested supposition that lacks explanatory power, and offers his own autobiographical counter-account: in 2009 he ran many machines in many locations on his own public C-class networks (e.g. 203.57.21.0/24) to make the young network resilient, later donating the machines to charities. The "myth of Satoshi" persists, he argues, because the cypherpunk narrative community refuses to do basic hypothesis testing or check simple facts.

## Key arguments and claims
- Lerner's mythical-miner analysis is "a supposition based on a false hypothesis that has never been tested" — it has no explanatory power because it never explains *how* the pattern occurred.
- No one has tested how early Bitcoin clients behaved on the actual platforms of the era: "How does Windows XP SP2 vs SP3 operate? How does Vista operate? … Does domain versus workgroup make a difference? How about Windows time services?"
- If you analyse early versions properly, "the IP addresses and locations of the machines don't match" a single miner — because he ran machines in many locations.
- In 2009 "Bitcoin had no value. There was no market", so he "set up lots of machines" purely to make the network resilient against attack.
- He claims he owned multiple public C-class address ranges in Australia and the US, not ISP-supplied — "One example of addressing ranges I used includes 203.57.21.0/24" — and has been associated with domains and addresses "since the 90s".
- Simple checkable facts are ignored: "Vistomail utilised credit card payments in 2008", which undercuts the mythologised history.
- The machines were later donated: some "ended up running churches… became email servers… became domain controllers for Burnside and the Uniting Church"; he links an archived Burnside newsletter and says he donated "several thousand computers", put 20 people through university, and fed 100+ people at annual Christmas parties.
- Motive claim: the myth-makers "want a story told by cypherpunks of a myth that cannot exist and a lie of Utopia" — evidence "doesn't suit their false narrative".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Popperian framing: a valid hypothesis must have explanatory power and survive testing, and he lists concrete unasked technical questions (OS service packs, network classes, time services) as the missing experimental work. Against the "myth" he pits autobiographical but checkable evidence — IP ranges, domains, charity records — casting himself as the empiricist and his critics as narrative-driven. The rhetorical mode is corrective and scornful: "Enjoy your myth. Reality is always more interesting."

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the widely accepted Patoshi-pattern attribution (Lerner's extranonce analysis), which mainstream researchers treat as solid evidence of a single dominant early miner — Craig calls it untested hype.
- Contradicts the cypherpunk mythos of Satoshi as an anonymous ideological purist; he recasts early mining as a mundane multi-machine, multi-site systems-administration task done by an identifiable businessman.
- Contradicts the "USENET penny share pink sheet dumps" world of altchains and sidechains, which he lumps in as part of the same myth-seeking culture.

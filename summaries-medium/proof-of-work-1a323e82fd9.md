# Proof of Work
**Date:** 2019-02-16 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/proof-of-work-1a323e82fd9
**Subtitle:** In 2016, I could have come forward and jumped through many hoops in a vain attempt to have people "like and accept me." Vain, as it is not…

## Core thesis
Wright explains why he refused to perform a public Satoshi key-signing in 2016 and still will not: the "crypto" community does not want the real Satoshi, it wants "a constructed myth" of anonymous money, whereas the system he created "is not anonymous and it cannot be made to be anonymous" — it is an immutable, court-admissible evidence trail. Signing would prove nothing cryptographically, would only escalate demands (he names Ira Kleiman's expectations), and would fuel a quick pump-and-dump. His own "proof of work" is instead decades of research output — 1,168 white papers and a planned thousands of patents — which secures his place in history whether or not anyone believes him.

## Key arguments and claims
- Public signing is a empty ritual: "the so-called 'sign publicly, and everyone will know' mantra doesn't actually prove anything"; a key "can be shared, stolen, copied, or assigned", and "there is actually no such thing as non-repudiation" — in court a key must be attested to, so "the key is not identity".
- The community rejects the real Bitcoin: they seek an anonymous system of money, but "Bitcoin provides pseudonymous transactions, and removes anonymity altogether"; the novelty is "the knife edge of pseudonymous transactions" — privacy that stays within the law.
- Anonymous cash is self-defeating: citing Froomkin's 1997 paper "The Unintended Consequences of eCash", regulators will never legalise anonymous cash; anonymity invites total profiling — "If you want privacy, the last thing you want is anonymous money" — and lets courts tie anonymous speech to money laundering, restricting free speech.
- Chaum's eCash failed because of anonymity, and "eCash was regularly traced and recovered" despite having no audit trail; Bitcoin's immutable trail stops Enron/Madoff-style double books and "will radically alter the face of modern accounting".
- All ICOs are frauds: "There has not been one single ICO that is not a scam"; utility tokens are "a paid invoice for goods that are never delivered", old USENET penny-share scams "in new bottles".
- Only one blockchain survives: "It is a protocol war. Like the Internet before it"; economics lead to "a strong single system or many extremely weak and fragmenting systems that end in death and destruction".
- BTC and ETH already have "less than 20 nodes" (a node being a validating entity that may fill a data centre); long-term he expects "upwards of a thousand" miners, including governments and banks protecting their interests.
- His personal signalling problem: miners prove commitment through capital investment; his equivalent is labour — "1,168 white papers" yielding 2–3 patents each, ~70 peer-reviewed publications expected in the current year, and lifetime targets of "3,000 patents and 5,000 or more peer-reviewed publications".
- Bitcoin's nLockTime is a time lock "taken from Asimov" allowing provably timed, pseudonymous release of information — part of the "evidential trail that now insures my place in history".
- Trailer for coming essays: "Lightning is a dead-end, decentralisation is but a tool and taken too far is a drug that will kill its host."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
An autobiographical apologia that reframes "proof of work" from cryptographic signing to lifetime labour output, turning the community's proof-demand back on itself. He mixes mechanism claims (pseudonymity, nLockTime, node economics) with cited scholarship (Froomkin 1997, Lamport 1979 Merkle trees, Finney's reusable PoW), Roman-history allusions (Marcus Aurelius, Octavius, Machiavelli's Prince) on succession and power, and a populist contrast between blind peer review ("judged on the quality of my work") and "twitter trolls". The rhetorical mode is grievance-as-credential: every attack is reclassified as evidence that the attackers need him.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **"Sign with Satoshi's keys to prove identity"** — the 2015–16 unveiling-era orthodoxy (Wired/Gizmodo expectations) is dismissed as a "quick fix" sold by people who "know perfectly well" it proves nothing.
- **Cypherpunk anonymity as the goal of cryptocurrency** — inverted: anonymity is the defect that killed eCash; Bitcoin was built to remove it.
- **"Not your keys, not your coins" / non-confiscation** — denied; tracing and recovery are always possible under law.
- **Multi-chain ecosystem / "blockchain not Bitcoin"** — protocol-war logic permits exactly one global ledger.
- **Decentralisation as intrinsic virtue** — "taken too far is a drug that will kill its host".
- **Code is law / cryptographic non-repudiation** — "There is a problem in the IT in the cryptographic community in thinking that code creates law… It doesn't and it can't."

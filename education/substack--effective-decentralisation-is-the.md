# Effective Decentralisation Is the Minimum, Not the Average — core principles

- **Decentralisation is a vector, not a scalar.** At least four components — admission, consensus operation, governance, and mutability — and the economically meaningful summary is the minimum: \(D_{\mathrm{eff}} = \min(D_{\mathrm{admission}}, D_{\mathrm{consensus}}, D_{\mathrm{governance}}, D_{\mathrm{mutability}})\).
- **A system cannot be more decentralised than its most concentrated control layer.** Each layer is an independent attack surface. Strength at consensus does not protect against a governance attack; improving a non-binding layer changes nothing.
- **Governance and mutability are distinct.** Concentrated governance over a low-mutability rule set is constrained governance.
- **Standard metrics measure one layer and ignore the rest.** Hash-rate distribution and the Nakamoto coefficient measure consensus, and only partially — they ignore shared clients, infrastructure, sponsors and jurisdiction. Validator and stake counts ignore client diversity, delegated stake and common control.
- **Node count measures observation, not control.** A node that only watches the chain exercises no authority; conflating node count with decentralisation is a durable category error.
- **Client diversity is a governance sub-layer.** A system dominated by one client is governed by that client's maintainers regardless of miner or validator dispersion.
- **Missing metrics that actually bind.** Sponsor and funding concentration; adoption-critical infrastructure (exchanges, pools, staking services that could block activation); custody concentration; cloud/hosting concentration; legal-jurisdiction concentration; social-coordination concentration.
- **Cross-layer correlations make the true minimum lower.** Sponsors fund implementations; professional operators collapse into a few services; adoption-critical actors are also the heavyweight governance voices. Unadjusted standard metrics are upper bounds, not point estimates.
- **Complete reporting names the binding constraint.** Layer-by-layer dispersion, correlation adjustments, the minimum coalition needed to harm participants at each layer, and an acknowledgement of what is not measured.

# The Cult of Scarcity — core principles

- **Wealth is productive capacity, not price.** Price is an afterimage; treating it as substance is the first small treason against reality — you can't eat the chart, but you can certainly starve while staring at it.
- **A throughput ceiling determines institutional shape.** At five transactions per second a system must shrink to a boutique settlement toy, migrate into custodianship, or ration by price; power consolidates where capacity is, and capacity made scarce by design manufactures gatekeepers.
- **A non-productive asset must recruit converts.** With no dividend and no surplus, earlier holders' gains are sourced entirely from later buyers — greater-fool logic in polite dress; evangelism is structural, not optional.
- **Money is a high-volume instrument of trade.** Redefining money as a "store of value" is the foundational error; digital cash is a function, not a faith, and trust accrues the way a road earns traffic.
- **Fee auctions are rationing by price.** A congested mempool is failure, not a healthy market signal; a fee market prices the vulnerable out of medicine and bread before it prices out speculation.
- **Hoarding freezes capital; building creates wealth.** Stores that never re-enter circulation are tombs, not bridges; when hoarding is rewarded as genius, venture capital thins and builders are mocked as naïve.
- **Custodial re-intermediation is the downstream of bottlenecks.** Where the rail cannot carry life, commerce is pushed into tolls, and tolls that become the system become lords — surveillance custodians arrive with terms of service, not jackboots.
- **The builder's criteria for real digital cash.** On-chain base-layer scaling, fees falling as adoption rises, miners competing to include more transactions rather than fewer, and capital rewarded for building — progress by allowing reality to choose.

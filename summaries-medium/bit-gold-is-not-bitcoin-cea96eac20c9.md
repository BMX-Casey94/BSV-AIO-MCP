# Bit Gold Is Not BitCoin
**Date:** 2019-04-08 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bit-gold-is-not-bitcoin-cea96eac20c9
**Subtitle:** Bit gold has never had anything to do with Bitcoin in any way or form, and in fact, the systems have no relationship to each other.

## Core thesis
Nick Szabo's bit gold shares nothing with Bitcoin but a similar name: its architecture — every user minting their own proof-of-work money, registered and timestamped, with no blockchain and no supply limit — is "in many ways the opposite" of Bitcoin. Craig uses the comparison to attack Szabo's monetary history, deny Szabo any claim to Satoshi's identity, and fold BTC into the same frame: the 2017 "airdrop" made BTC "a completely new system" that is no more Bitcoin than bit gold was.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Bit gold has never had anything to do with Bitcoin in any way or form... There is a similarity in the name. That's it. The technology behind bit gold is utterly different."
- Szabo's origins-of-money story is "a good low-level, by which I mean high-school, analysis": precious metals are not "unfortunate[ly] scarc[e] due to their costliness of creation", and trusted third parties stamped and assayed coin "not... so that it can be trusted, but rather so that they could control the system".
- Even stamped coins were weighed in practice: "A gold coin can be clipped... rubbing against a bag creates gold dust. Wear through mere use reduces the amount of gold in a coin. So, where gold was used, it would still be weighed."
- Bit gold's mechanics invert Bitcoin's: it starts "with a challenge string. There is no challenge string in Bitcoin"; each user "creates their own proof-of-work" and sends it "to be registered and timestamped", whereas in Bitcoin "individuals don't run nodes... Miners validate transfers between individuals".
- "There is no blockchain or timechain and nothing related to it in bit gold", and "there are no limits to the amount of money that can be printed in bit gold... a system that seeks to allow all users to individually create their own money" — which is "what the BTC or SegWit-coin coders want to turn a blockchain into".
- Lineage: Szabo's design "was far closer to Wei Dai's b-money than to Bitcoin", and bit gold is "an attempt to make Adam Back's hashcash work"; proof-of-work itself dates to the 90s, and "Hal Finney's replaceable proof-of-work system was one that functioned and was coded well".
- "Nick Szabo is not a cryptographer. Neither is Adam Back."
- Motive attack: Szabo "is a part of why they needed to try and destroy me. He was clearly pliable if not complicit" in a fraud "to create a system that is not Bitcoin" — namely "Bitcoin Core or rather SegWit coin BTC", whose 2017 airdrop "created a completely new system that fraudulently drew people into believing it's Bitcoin".
- On Satoshi claims: Szabo "has basically written a false concept and misled people into believing that he could be Satoshi. It is me", and having done so "to obtain financial gain... He knows enough of law to know that doing so alone is fraud."
- "Bit gold is a failed dead end that has no relationship to Bitcoin whatsoever", and Bitcoin "is nothing at all like the cypherpunk anarchist system that Bitcoin is designed to destroy".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues by architectural contrast — mechanism by mechanism (challenge string, per-user PoW, registration, no chain, no cap) — to sever the intellectual lineage the mainstream draws from Szabo to Satoshi. Monetary history is used as a supporting brief (coinage, clipping, seigniorage-as-control) to discredit Szabo's scholarship, and the whole is wrapped in an ad hominem frame: bad motives and legal fraud explain both bit gold's reputation and BTC's continued use of the Bitcoin name.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Demolishes the canonical prehistory of Bitcoin (b-money → bit gold → hashcash → Bitcoin) that mainstream sources, and Szabo himself, trade on; Craig reassigns genuine credit only to Finney's RPOW as working code.
- Rejects the "digital gold" monetary narrative at its root: scarcity-by-costliness (Szabo's "unforgeable costliness") is labelled a high-school story, undercutting the stock-to-flow/store-of-value ideology built on it.
- Equates BTC with bit gold's unlimited-minting flaw: "BTC has no relationship to Bitcoin anymore", the SegWit "airdrop" of 2017 being a new system falsely wearing the name — the core BSV-era polemic.
- Inverts the cypherpunk ancestry claim: Bitcoin is not the fulfilment of cypherpunk anarchism but "is designed to destroy" it.

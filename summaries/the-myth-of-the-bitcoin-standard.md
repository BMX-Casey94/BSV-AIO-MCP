# The Myth of the Bitcoin Standard: Debt, Delusion, and the Enduring Economics of State Spending
**Date:** 2025-11-05 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-myth-of-the-bitcoin-standard
**Subtitle:** Why Governments Will Always Borrow Against the Future—Gold, Fiat, or Bitcoin Be Damned

## Core thesis
A Bitcoin Standard would neither prevent deficit spending nor enforce fiscal discipline, because the problem of government overspending lies in the nature of governance, not in the nature of money. States do not naïvely “print” currency; they borrow against the future, and they will do so whether the reserve is gold, fiat or Bitcoin — replicating the Gold Standard’s structural failures and merely gilding debt with cryptographic rhetoric before the inevitable default.

## Key arguments and claims
- The “standard” is a moral prosthetic: gold and Bitcoin fetishism both spring from the hope that scarcity can substitute for discipline, letting politicians promise rectitude without reform.
- Modern money creation runs through debt, not presses: governments issue bonds bought by institutions and central banks, which place them on balance sheets in exchange for reserves that multiply into credit — “temporal arbitrage” spending today what tomorrow must earn.
- Inflation is reframed as a moral rather than monetary failure: borrowing that funds roads, energy networks and productive infrastructure seeds the returns that repay it; borrowing for consumption, patronage and subsidies cannibalises the economy that must redeem it.
- Keynes is defended against his disciples: deficit spending was justified only as temporary investment with a multiplier exceeding unity, and he warned that “the boom, not the slump, is the right time for austerity” — a reciprocity modern states inverted into permanent deficit.
- The American record 1921–1971 is Exhibit A: every dollar was notionally gold-redeemable while wars, public works and social programmes were debt-financed; Nixon’s 1971 suspension of convertibility was “an admission of insolvency”, not innovation.
- The gold standard’s collapse was the fulfilment of its logic, not a deviation: Britain suspended gold payments in 1931, France followed, and the US four decades later — each “temporary” abandonment made permanent.
- Under a Bitcoin Standard the pattern persists digitally: governments would issue Bitcoin-denominated bonds, intermediaries would layer synthetic Bitcoin derivatives and off-chain IOUs, and redemption would again be “temporarily suspended” when liabilities outran reserves.
- Scarcity without elasticity is paralysis, not discipline: a deflationary unit drives capital into hoarding, withers investment, and “would turn every recession into a depression” by forbidding the flexibility that absorbs shocks.
- The decisive distinction is productive capital versus political capital: bridges, grids, ports and research repay debt through growth, while subsidies, handouts and vanity projects are “economic cannibalism disguised as compassion” — a bipartisan insolvency in which the left cultivates dependence and the right imitates it as “stimulus”.
- Default is a continuum, not an event: every inflation is a partial default, every unredeemed bond a quiet betrayal, spread thinly across generations as the slow theft of purchasing power.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The essay works through institutional and moral-historical analysis rather than formal modelling: a close reading of US fiscal experience from 1921 to 1971, a rehabilitation of Keynes’s actual doctrine against its bureaucratic corruption, and an analogy mapping gold-standard mechanics onto a hypothetical Bitcoin Standard. Its central move is to relocate causation from monetary mechanism to political character — “the problem has never been the medium. It has always been the man.”

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies the core “Bitcoin fixes this” claim of sound-money maximalism: fixed supply cannot restrain a state that will borrow, tax or seize regardless of the reserve asset.
- Denies that scarcity equals stability: an inelastic monetary base strangles capital formation and forbids crisis response, producing “a nightmare of inelastic despair”.
- Denies that code is immune to politics: a state need not hack the blockchain when it can regulate gateways, tax, surveil and expropriate in Bitcoin as easily as in fiat.
- Reframes the inflation debate: the enemy is not money printing but unproductive borrowing — so austere “hard money” and Keynesian profligacy are “extremes of the same disease”, the belief that perfection can replace prudence.

## Notable quotes
- “When the ledger conflicted with the sword, the sword always won.”
- “Nations collapse not because they print too much, but because they promise too much—and lack the courage to stop.”
- “Gold failed because it assumed men could be governed by metal. Bitcoin will fail because it assumes men can be governed by code.”
- “The world does not collapse when debt grows; it collapses when debt ceases to build.”

## Connections

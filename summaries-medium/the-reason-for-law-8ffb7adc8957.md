# The Reason for Law
**Date:** 2019-04-08 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-reason-for-law-8ffb7adc8957

## Core thesis
Evidence law exists because "simply claiming a document is real does not make it real". Craig performs a forensic deconstruction of Exhibit 11 from the Kleiman litigation — a trust document he says is an altered compilation of at least four documents — to argue that the hearsay rule, not internet accusation, must judge authenticity, and that WikiLeaks-style leaked-document culture is a campaign to discredit him because Bitcoin's immutable record threatens the liars' business model.

## Key arguments and claims
- The rules of admissibility versus hearsay exist for "very clear reasons": a party with no connection to a piece of evidence who asserts it is real "needs to support their assertion".
- Forensic anomalies in Exhibit 11 (a Scribd-hosted "Denariuz" trust document): 10 pages consisting of a 9-page document plus an exhibit cover sheet; only seven pages numbered, starting at page 2; the trust name on the cover sheet ("Denariuz Seychelles Trust") differs from the contract body ("Denariuz Seychelles International Trust") — "similar, but they refer to completely different trusts".
- Internal cross-references fail: "Page 2... lists Appendix A, but there is no Appendix A. Appendix 1 is given on page 7 of 7... The fonts on the same page are different"; there is "no witness page — to be executed as a deed, it needs to be witnessed".
- The document is "an altered compilation of four other documents (at least). Some parts are real, and others are not", assembled from material leaked by people who "claimed they had hacked into my servers" — possibly ex-Hotwire staff who "had stolen data and broken into servers and drives they had not been allowed to access".
- Hearsay point against the Kleiman estate: "Mr. Kleiman und nobody associated with him were ever a part of or a party to any agreements or companies", so he "has no way to know which documents may or may not be substantially correct or which have been altered".
- His defence of his own records is delegation: "I don't do my own accounts, I don't do my own filing", and he pays for "bookkeeping, accounting, internal audits, external audits, and legal tax advice"; there is "a big distinction between signing an authorisation and creating something".
- He announces libel actions: "In the coming weeks, we shall be starting a number of actions for libel... When you make a false allegation claiming that someone has engaged in fraud... you better check the probity of the source"; documents "obtained through hacked servers" plus claims "based on hearsay... leave yourself liable to prosecution for malicious statements".
- WikiLeaks and Anonymous attack him because he threatens their model: they "seek a world where records are altered, changed, and copied and where they can set the truth... Welcome to Wikileaks, and welcome to 1984"; Bitcoin and Metanet provide "immutable truth that cannot be altered, a system that provides a single record that stops corruption, a system that makes fraud expensive".
- Closing threat: "Enjoy bankruptcy, coming through a libel court near you…"

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He applies the lawyer's method rather than the cryptographer's: page numbering, font mismatches, naming inconsistencies, missing execution formalities and provenance (hacked servers, disgruntled ex-staff) are used to attack authenticity the way counsel would on a voir dire. The argument then generalises from the document to an epistemology — leaked files from anonymous sources are presumptively worthless — and finally to a motive-based theory of why he is targeted: immutability is existential for those who trade in alterable records.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the "just verify it yourself" / leaked-document culture celebrated by crypto media and WikiLeaks sympathisers: provenance and admissibility, not viral possession of a file, establish truth.
- Against code-is-law epistemology (continued from "Two steps forward"): courts, libel law and evidence rules — not signatures or crowd consensus — are the arbiters of fact.
- Against the crypto-anarchist valorisation of anonymity and leaks: he casts anonymous document dumps as Orwellian ("welcome to 1984") and positions Bitcoin's immutable ledger as the tool that ends them, inverting the usual association of Bitcoin with WikiLeaks-style radical transparency.

# Settlement Speed Is the Wrong Margin
**Date:** 2026-06-14 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/settlement-speed-is-the-wrong-margin
**Subtitle:** Faster finality — CBDCs, instant rails, mBridge — can remove at most a few percent of the working-capital cost in large-value trade. The binding constraint is the receivables cycle, and the firm-level

## Core thesis
The cross-border payments reform agenda (CBDCs, multi-CBDC platforms such as mBridge, instant rails, tokenised deposits) assumes settlement latency is a meaningful cost in large-value trade. Firm-level measurement shows it is not: working capital is locked in the receivables cycle of 45–110 days, not in the 1–5 days of rail latency, so faster finality can remove at most low single digits of the carry. The binding constraint is receivables finance, and the instruments that matter act on the financing spread, documentary friction and counterparty risk — not on speed.

## Key arguments and claims
- In trade-credit sales the seller ships, books a receivable, and "has, in effect, lent the buyer the value of the shipment"; the financing cost of that loan across the receivables cycle is the real price of moving goods on credit. Days sales outstanding (receivables/sales × 365) measures the duration.
- The decisive ratio: λ = L/T, the share of working-capital carry a faster rail can reach. With T = 90 days and L = 2, λ ≈ 2%; driving latency to zero leaves the other ~98% untouched.
- λ is rate-invariant: the financing spread enters numerator and denominator and cancels, so no interest-rate assumption can inflate the importance of settlement speed — "the single most useful thing in the analysis".
- Data: Compustat Global for eight China–BRICS-relevant economies (China, India, Indonesia, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, UAE, South Africa, Russia) — 12,241 firms, 73,533 firm-years, FY2019–2025 — plus 13,031 Dealscan syndicated loan facilities collapsed to one all-in-drawn spread per facility.
- Median DSO runs from ~45 days (Russia) to ~108 (China); spreads from ~145bp (Saudi Arabia) to 441bp (China); implied annual carry of ~29–130bp per unit of trade value.
- Against 2–5 day latency, median λ is ~2.6% (two-day) to ~6.5% (five-day); the panel maximum is 11% in Russia — the economy most favourable to the speed story.
- Distribution analysis: below 20 days the firm-year share ranges from 2.6% (Brazil) to 17.2% (Indonesia); below 10 days — where finality would genuinely dominate — at most 9.1% in any economy. "The distribution does not rescue the speed thesis. It buries it."
- The "voluntary cycle" objection: rails do not shorten negotiated terms by one minute; and even measured against only the involuntary frictional residual (collection lag, documentary reconciliation), the latency share remains mid-single digits.
- Sector-composition robustness: recomputed four ways (manufacturing only; ex-construction/real estate; ex-financials; industry-adjusted against global industry medians), every economy's median stays between ~45 and ~113 days. The cross-economy ranking shifts and is treated as descriptive colour; the order of magnitude is the claim.
- Dollar illustration (explicitly order-of-magnitude, not a welfare estimate): China's 2024 goods trade with seven partners implies ~$3.5–4 billion annual receivables carry, of which a perfect, instant, free rail could remove ~$110 million.
- Policy ordering by margin: (1) financeability of the trade claim — invoice verification, receivables registries, enforceable assignment, credit insurance — because the spread is paid on every day of the cycle; (2) documentary and legal transferability (UNCITRAL Model Law on Electronic Transferable Records); (3) counterparty-risk compression; (4) rail speed, last but not useless (resilience, sanctions exposure, inclusion are separate merits).
- Where speed is right: when counterparty, credit term and value collapse — machine-to-machine settlement for compute, bandwidth or data in fractions of a cent — T falls toward L and λ rises toward one; "finality is the holding period". The error is importing a machine-regime architecture into trade-credit analysis.
- Stated limits: descriptive measurement, not causal identification (the 2022 Russian rail exclusion bundles sanctions and counterparty exit); listed firms only, with SME corroboration from World Bank Enterprise Surveys treated as consistent, not probative; an accounting identity, not a behavioural model.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Accounting-identity decomposition (λ = L/T) disciplined by firm-level empirical measurement: build the ratio, show it is rate-invariant, then measure T across 73,533 firm-years and stress the result against distribution, voluntariness, sector composition and screening objections. The normative method is "measure the margin before you choose the instrument" — rank interventions by the cost component each actually reaches.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post is an empirical critique of the cross-border payments speed agenda (CBDCs, mBridge, instant rails), though its machine-economy regime (λ → 1 for instant, sub-cent settlement) implicitly grounds the case for high-throughput micropayment rails.

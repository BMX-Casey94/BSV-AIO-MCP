## Core thesis

Craig explains the intellectual source of the "Nakamoto" pseudonym: Tominaga Nakamoto (1715–46), the Kaitokudō-trained philosopher who held that language and text are unreliable as objective sources and that history is unstable, endlessly rewritten by the ambitious. The "solution" to his dilemma is Bitcoin itself: an append-only record in which every change is recorded defeats the myth-making that corrupts texts — including, Craig says, the myths now projected onto the Bitcoin white paper.

## Key arguments and claims
- Tominaga Nakamoto: trained at the Osaka merchant academy (Kaitokudō); wrote on metaphysics, cosmology and history, holding history to be "the proper subject of study from which to gain insight into the present".
- Against his contemporaries, he denied that "language and text could serve as objective sources of data" for universal statements: meaning is fluid and shifts with cultural context.
- History is relative and unstable: it is rewritten "time and time again by those seeking to constitute what they saw was pure origin based on their own philosophy"; the only constant in human nature is "self-promotion and bragging", "contradictions and deceiving ways".
- Application to Bitcoin: "The making of myths can be seen in the Bitcoin white paper" — people claim miners could make rules and deliver a system without government, "even though the white paper makes it clear that miners enforce the rules — the difficulty here being that an enforcer is not a creator."
- The technological fix: "a system without the ability to change without a record of the change always leads to the truth", and "a predicate can always be formed in a way that does not allow rhetorical dishonesty" — the immutable ledger answers the instability of language.
- The pseudonym is thus programmatic: naming Bitcoin's author after a sceptic of textual stability encodes the design goal of an evidentiary record immune to semantic drift.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Intellectual genealogy as self-exegesis: he reads his own pseudonym as a philosophical programme, then maps Tominaga's scepticism about texts onto the dispute over the white paper's meaning. The method is hermeneutic — original text versus later mythologising overlays — with the blockchain cast as the technological solution to the drift of language and history.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against miner-sovereignty and "code is law" readings: miners enforce rules, they do not create them — "an enforcer is not a creator".
- Against the stateless-money reading of the white paper: the idea of "a system without government" is a later myth projected onto the text, not the text itself.
- Implicitly against the community's claim to interpret Satoshi: correct reading is anchored to the immutable original, which he alone authored.

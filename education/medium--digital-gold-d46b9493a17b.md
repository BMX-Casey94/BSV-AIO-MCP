# Digital Gold — core principles

- **Bitcoin is peer-to-peer electronic cash, not digital gold.** The white paper’s title defines the system. A store-of-value meme of hoarded, reused addresses is not that design.
- **A new key pair per transaction is the privacy firewall.** Using a fresh key each time keeps transactions from being linked to a common owner — that is the key to making Bitcoin private.
- **Coin-splitting makes tracing exponentially hard.** Split coins into many pieces with fresh keys, and have counterparties do the same; the graph problem moves towards an NP-hard analysis.
- **Key reuse removes privacy.** A few large addresses used as accounts make Bitcoin simple to trace without warrants and enable public tracing, not only law-enforcement tracing.
- **Security is economic, not cryptographic.** Bitcoin is not secured by hash algorithms. Hash functions do not secure systems; security is based on economics.
- **Hoarded coins are a seizure surface.** Gold has been seized throughout history. Once you have a few easily traced keys reused over and over, it becomes easy to capture those coins.
- **Miners can be required to blacklist or reassign outputs.** Courts can require miners to blacklist particular coins and, where keys are withheld, force a change of output and seize bitcoin.
- **Privacy techniques need capacity.** Coin-splitting creates bigger transactions. A constrained chain makes fees exceed value for all but the largest transfers, and so removes the ability to add privacy.

# On Decentralisation — core principles

- **Decentralisation means no unilateral change.** A network is decentralised only if it is not controlled and not reasonably likely to be controlled, or unilaterally changed, by any single person or group (the Peirce safe-harbour test).
- **The protocol must be set in stone.** To function within regulation, the protocol must be stable. Protocol fixity is the condition that satisfies the test.
- **Protocol mutability is the centralisation vector.** Developers who can change the base protocol can withhold information, impose selective lockout of competing groups, and stifle innovation because commercial software-development times are not instantaneous.
- **Only a fixed protocol is Bitcoin.** Every system whose developers demonstrably alter the core protocol fails the decentralisation test and, by that definition, is not Bitcoin.
- **Developer groups can be partnerships at common law.** Income (including donations), support websites, meetings, and hierarchical source-code access are partnership indicia; the failure to make a direct profit does not preclude a partnership.
- **Partners are jointly liable.** Unlike corporate shareholders with limited liability, common-law partners are jointly and equally liable for the actions of other partners.
- **A token need not pay a dividend to be a security.** Absence of dividends does not settle the classification; many Fortune 500 companies pay none.

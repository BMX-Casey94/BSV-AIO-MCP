# On Scammers, a redux.
**Date:** 2019-11-04 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/on-scammers-a-redux-b194e5185453
**Subtitle:** It is interesting to see what happens when scumbags don't get their way.

## Core thesis
A broadside against Ira Kleiman amid the Kleiman v. Wright litigation: Craig accuses Ira of perjuring himself and misleading the court by attributing to Craig a quoted statement that was actually from Ira's own email. He recasts the lawsuit's origin as a 2015 extortion attempt — Ira allegedly tried to force liquidation of Craig's companies to extract money — and asserts flatly that Satoshi was Craig alone, with no partnership and no group.

## Key arguments and claims
- Ira Kleiman "is perjuring himself and misleading the court": a court filing quotes one of Craig's blog posts but attributes to Craig words that were "from an email made by Ira Kleiman", signed "as being true and without error to the court".
- Such conduct "in the UK... is covered by the fraud act (2006). It is a criminal fraud" — and "It isn't the first time."
- The motive is money: "He is doing this to get money." In 2015 Ira "sought to force a liquidation so that he could get access to money in the company", exploiting the fact that "private companies don't need to sell or buy back shares"; Craig also alleges Ira covered up inheriting Dave's shares to avoid state and federal taxes.
- After the alleged extortion failed, "material from the company that had been shared started leaking... anonymously sent to journalists", followed within months by "a complete doxing and character assassination" and accusations to the Australian government of money-laundering designed to damage his companies.
- On Satoshi authorship: "Satoshi was me, and a year into already having written papers, asking one of around 10 people to review my paper and some of my code does not a partnership make." Ira "knows there is no group. There is no partnership."
- Dave and Ira Kleiman "were estranged — they did not talk and in fact they didn't like each other"; Dave "never once talked about him. Not in over a decade", which is why Craig knew nothing of Ira beyond a name.
- Craig admits "some of my Australian companies were compromised" by insiders — "When there are insiders, it's practically impossible to stop an attack" — and floats forensic evidence of "computer hacking and altered emails", leaving open "whether we can tie Mr Ira Kleiman directly" to it, while noting Ira "has certainly benefited from them".
- He frames future Bitcoin-based contracts and ledgers as the antidote: "we can start having contracts and exchanges based on bitcoin and the ledger that will show all the alterations."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Pure adversarial litigation rhetoric: categorical accusations ("categorically and unequivocally"), legal framing (perjury, Fraud Act 2006, extortion), and a reconstructed timeline of motive (2015 liquidation pressure → leaks → doxing → lawsuit). He inverts the plaintiff's narrative — the accuser becomes the criminal — and pre-commits to future revelations ("In the coming months you're going to watch as we start showing just how many lies"). Anticipating defamation risk, he pleads truth: "This could be defamatory in nature other than the fact it is true."

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the community's "Satoshi was a group / Dave Kleiman co-created Bitcoin" folklore head-on: "there is no group. There is no partnership."
- Contradicts the era's dominant media narrative (fed by the Kleiman suit) of a disputed Satoshi fortune partnership, recasting it as extortion by a litigant who inherited shares he allegedly hid from tax authorities.
- Otherwise not a protocol argument — this is a litigation/reputation post, not an engagement with BTC technical orthodoxy.

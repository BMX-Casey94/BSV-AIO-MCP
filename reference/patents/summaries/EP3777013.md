## Problem addressed
The specification says Bitcoin-exchange “2-of-3” multisignature wallets leak privacy (rare script type, visible which keys signed), cost more in fees, and rest on P2SH collision bounds. Existing threshold-optimal ECDSA schemes are slow and rest on newer assumptions, while faster schemes exclude combinations such as 2-of-3. Payment-channel “failure mode” and the need for Segregated Witness are also cited. It seeks a dealer-free threshold ECDSA wallet that can emulate 2-of-3 behaviour with fast, largely non-interactive signing.

## Core mechanism
Four parties — client, exchange, trusted third party (TTP) and escrow — each hold an elliptic-curve key pair and a dealer-free share xn of a group private key x that never exists in one place. Secure channels are set up between the pairs. The TTP has a low-latency link to the exchange and will emit a partial signature on an authenticated exchange request (optionally restricted to certain destinations). Combining a threshold of partial signatures yields a full ECDSA signature on a transaction hash, so a “3-of-4” share scheme behaves as a 2-of-3 authorisation (client+exchange, or exchange+TTP; escrow is for fault resolution). Ephemeral-key and masking shares can be pre-distributed so that applying a share to a message is fast. If a party is unresponsive, access can move to a third private key for a limited time; the exchange’s share can itself be re-shared.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method in which second participants receive a first blockchain transaction signed with a first participant’s private key, verify that signature, each apply their share of a second private key to produce a share of a signed second transaction, and combine a threshold of those shares to obtain the signed second transaction. Independent claim 7 is the first-participant counterpart (send the signed first transaction, receive shares, combine). Independent claim 12 distributes shares of a private key, an ephemeral key, and a third value so that applying those third shares to a message yields threshold shares of a signature. Independent claim 15 is a system that carries out the methods.

## BSV relevance
The specification targets Bitcoin-exchange wallets and payment-channel signing with ECDSA threshold shares instead of on-chain 2-of-3 multisig. That is directly relevant to BSV custodial and high-frequency signing designs that want a single-signature on-chain footprint.

# The postal acceptance rule in Bitcoin — core principles

- **Contract formation by non-instantaneous communication.** Where acceptance travels by a non-instantaneous medium via a third party, the postal acceptance rule (Adams v Lindsell, 1818; Household Fire Insurance Co v Grant, 1879) completes the contract at delivery into the system, not at reading.
- **Bitcoin satisfies both postal-rule conditions.** Bitcoin is non-instantaneous, and the mining network that verifies transactions acts as the third party, so Bitcoin exchange mirrors the postal acceptance rule already settled for e-mail.
- **Acceptance occurs at delivery to a mining node.** The legally operative moment is the delivery and transfer of a transaction to a mining node — not block inclusion, and not the recipient opening a wallet.
- **Block inclusion is not acceptance.** Treating block confirmation as acceptance would be analogous to a recipient not accepting a letter until the letter box was opened; a broadcast (unconfirmed) transaction therefore carries legal weight before confirmation.
- **Statutory footing.** Under the UK's Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002, reg. 11(2)(a) (an order is deemed received when the addressee can access it) maps to delivery to a mining node; in the US the Uniform Electronic Transactions Act 1999 settles e-mail acceptance; the Sale of Goods (United Nations Convention) Act 1994 offers the alternative that acceptance bites when consent "reaches the offeror" — arrival at the offeror's Bitcoin address and acceptance into the blockchain.
- **Instantaneous media are the exception.** Courts declined to extend the postal rule to telex (Entores v Miles Far East Corp, 1955; Brinkibon v Stahag Stahl, 1983), and the web fails the analogy because checksums and constant client-server verification make receipt near-instantaneous; Bitcoin differs because the white paper states messages are "broadcast on a best effort basis" — analogous to e-mail, not the web.
- **In-person exception.** Handing over a transaction face to face excludes the postal rule entirely.

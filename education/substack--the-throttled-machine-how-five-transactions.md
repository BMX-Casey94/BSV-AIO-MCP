# The Throttled Machine — core principles

- **Five transactions per second is a restriction, not a revelation.** The white paper defined electronic cash; serving one billion people making one modest transaction daily would require more than 10,000 per second. Five is a denial of purpose.
- **A fee auction on a fixed pipe is a tariff on freedom.** Small-value payments vanish first; a £5 payment with a £10 cost is mockery, not inclusion. Congestion is pathology — a hospital overcrowded with the sick is not evidence of health.
- **Priced-out users surrender keys.** Exchanges and processors batch millions into single on-chain footprints and become banks in all but name — fractional, opaque and indispensable.
- **Privacy dies by economics, not cryptography.** Aggregation into custodial pools concentrates users into shared addresses; a starved ledger makes each transaction glow like a flare. Privacy that must be purchased is not a birthright.
- **Scarcity of usage is not scarcity of supply.** Bitcoin’s scarcity was meant to be in the coin, not in the right to transact. Velocity is the measure of economic vitality; a token whose price rises because it does not circulate is a store of inertia.
- **Scale is security’s guarantor, not its enemy.** The more a network is used, the harder it is to control. Constraint concentrates power in those who decide what the code cannot do.
- **Layered dependence on a choked foundation institutionalises the problem.** Channels must open and close through the same bottleneck; liquidity is locked capital; users become slaves to uptime against fraud.
- **Restoration is abundance.** Billions of transactions per second make exchange too abundant to monopolise; a right that exists only in theory is no right at all. Money that cannot move is not wealth — it is a sculpture.

# Subsidised Growth — core principles

- **The block subsidy is temporary bootstrap pay.** Early users were paid to run the network when coins had no market value and no commercial player would competitively operate a distributed database.
- **Launch software combined mining and wallet.** The original Bitcoin-Qt client was designed to both mine and act as a wallet — alpha software, far from perfect.
- **IP-to-IP payments are part of cash.** Peer aspects of cash such as IP-to-IP transactions are essential to making Bitcoin a cash system; removing them degrades that design.
- **Early coins were easy to lose.** Because 2009 bitcoin had practically no value beyond a promise of the future, many people failed to protect what they were mining.
- **Users bore real costs for a worthless token.** Downloading the chain, hard-drive and network capacity, CPU heat and electricity were paid by early users with no promise beyond the dream that Bitcoin would one day be valuable.
- **Subsidy is scaffolding, not the long-run security model.** Once the system can stand alone, fee revenue from transaction volume replaces the paid-bootstrap mechanism.

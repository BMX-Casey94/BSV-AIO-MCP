# Decentralised or just inefficient? — core principles

- **Bitcoin is an economic incentive system.** Security comes from parties investing in infrastructure that earns an expected return, not from a lottery in which a billion hobbyists each win once in millennia.
- **Egalitarian, non-parallelisable mining destroys the incentive.** If one machine earns as much as a million, expected reward collapses to a pure lottery and nobody builds the facilities that actually secure the network.
- **Unpaid validation is a tragedy of the commons.** With no expected return, few will leave a node running, consuming electricity, to validate other people’s transactions.
- **Altruism cannot secure the network.** Even a thin layer of always-on altruists wins too few blocks to cover tens of thousands of pounds a month in cost; the system then needs to be saved by government.
- **Incentive collapse fragments the graph.** Without a reason to converge on one chain, orphan histories multiply and never rejoin; the ultra-small-world network degrades into an insecure mesh.
- **Only a stable protocol disseminates power.** Once nobody can change the rules, developers lose the power that an evolving protocol would hand them over every user’s life.
- **Serialised proof-of-work was considered and rejected.** One-way functions that cannot be parallelised already existed; they were not used for Bitcoin because they would have removed the industrial incentive that keeps the network secure.

# Bitcoin is for Business — core principles

- **Cash requires a commodity ledger.** For Bitcoin to become cash it must be a commodity ledger, not a speculative gambling asset; to be money, Bitcoin must carry commercial use.
- **Scarcity alone does not create value.** If scarcity alone made something valuable, many unwanted things would be collected and exchanged — an obsolete Telex is extremely rare yet worthless. Value does not come from scarcity; rather, scarcity allows things that are in demand to be traded at the margin.
- **Bitcoin was never about removing banks and government.** It is sound money, a stable supply, and an efficient and auditable ledger — everything anonymity-and-anarchy seekers oppose is at the heart of Bitcoin.
- **The HODL story is a zero-sum fallacy.** For many, Bitcoin is a short-term zero-sum game built on the false idea that money and value come from simple scarcity.
- **On-chain capacity is the scaling path.** Live-network tests hit 1,500 transactions per second and 64 MB blocks; the roadmap is 512 MB in six months, 2.0 GB in twelve months, then uncapped blocks, and beyond that 1 TB blocks with no limits.
- **Throughput is measured against real payment rails.** 512 MB is about 2,000 TPS sustained; 2.0 GB blocks allow 8,000 TPS settled — Visa-level volumes as they occur in the real world, not laboratory claims. PayPal processed 194 TPS against the test's 1,500 TPS.
- **Legally registered securities and bank-issued digital fiat belong on-chain.** A platform such as Tokenized can issue legally registered securities with bank-issued digital fiat on Bitcoin, enabling debit-card and other payment traffic on-chain for less than Visa or MasterCard.

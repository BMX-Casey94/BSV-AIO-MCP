# From simplicity comes … — core principles

- **Simple rules iterate to unbounded complexity.** Chaos is what happens when the rules keep changing; Conway's Game of Life shows that fixed simple rules yield infinite combinations of outcomes.
- **Bitcoin is a simple protocol miners cannot update.** A miner who refuses a transaction and rejects blocks containing it risks orphaning anything they win, and even then merely delays the transaction.
- **Replayability is the litmus test.** A transaction can be replayed a week, a month, a year or a decade later, whenever the user decides.
- **Miners set rules; they do not set protocol.** If the protocol changes, you have left Bitcoin. Protocol changes are not forks but new competing protocols, possibly with an airdrop.
- **The block cap is a miner-set rule.** If just over 50% of miners want to raise it, it will rise; miners who reject the increase may go broke.
- **Bitcoin was always destined for data centres.** It was never designed so that everyone could run a node; hobby miners who cannot compete are forced out.
- **The white paper allows any necessary rules.** A majority of miners may reject transactions by size, fees or anything else within the protocol, at the possible cost of lost revenue if they choose wrongly.
- **Rule changes need no user-software update.** SPV clients and applications stay the same; only miner policy moves.
- **Script can encode almost anything that can be thought of.** The included scripting language is the extension surface, not protocol mutation.
- **Typical payments are one party paying another.** Signature-aggregation schemes save negligible space on ordinary one-to-one transactions.
- **Value requires lawful exchangeability.** Bitcoin must be easily exchanged and legal; the fiat on/off-ramp is the simplest point to attack, and cutting it removes usefulness and value.
- **Bitcoin was never designed to be anonymous.** Traceability is a strength; removing the ability for users to trace transactions easily is not a scaling improvement.

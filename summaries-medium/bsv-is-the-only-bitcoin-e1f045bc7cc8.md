# BSV is the only Bitcoin.
**Date:** 2018-12-11 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bsv-is-the-only-bitcoin-e1f045bc7cc8
**Subtitle:** You do not get to change a system and say it is the same.

## Core thesis
The post's body is a single aphoristic statement, published 26 days after the BCH/BSV split: "BSV is the only Bitcoin. You do not get to change a system and say it is the same." There is no developed argument in the post itself — it is a slogan asserting that altering a system's protocol forfeits its claim to the name "Bitcoin".

## Key arguments and claims
- The entire substantive claim: changing a system's rules means it is no longer the same system; therefore BTC (and BCH), having changed the protocol, are not Bitcoin, while BSV — which restores and locks the original protocol — is.
- Implicit criterion: a system's identity is defined by protocol continuity, not by market ticker, hash-rate majority, exchange listings or brand recognition.
- Honest scope note: the body is two sentences long; there is no supporting evidence, mechanism or elaboration to summarise beyond the slogan itself.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Assertion by definition rather than evidence-based reasoning — a one-line essentialist argument (system identity equals rule-set identity) functioning as a movement rallying cry at the height of the post-hash-war "Satoshi's Vision" campaign.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "BTC is Bitcoin because the market and exchanges say so" convention, and the "Bitcoin is whatever the consensus of developers and users runs" view: for Craig, the 2009 protocol definition is fixed and normative.
- Equally contradicts BCH's "Bitcoin Cash is Bitcoin as peer-to-peer electronic cash" claim — under his criterion, any rule change forfeits the name, which is why the slogan targets both chains.

## Notable quotes
- "BSV is the only Bitcoin."
- "You do not get to change a system and say it is the same."

## Connections
Published in the immediate aftermath of the 15 November 2018 BCH/BSV split and hash war. The immutability premise underpins his later "protocol is set in stone" essays and his legal arguments that developers owe duties to preserve the original protocol; it is the thesis statement that the surrounding December 2018 essays ("Instant transactions", "Bitcoin's privacy model") elaborate technically.

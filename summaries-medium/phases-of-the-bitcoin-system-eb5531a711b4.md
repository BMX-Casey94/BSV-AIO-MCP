# Phases of the Bitcoin system
**Date:** 2018-10-20 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/phases-of-the-bitcoin-system-eb5531a711b4
**Subtitle:** The Bitcoin system may be broken down into the following “phases”:

## Core thesis
A descriptive taxonomy that divides the Bitcoin system into three "phases" — mining, holding, and transferring — built from authoritative sources (the US GAO's May 2013 report to the Senate Finance Committee, the bitcoin.it wiki, a JBFLP article). The analytical payload is concentrated in two claims: bitcoins are "owned" by whoever controls the wallet's key pair, and miners validate the ledger "but are not payment intermediaries".

## Key arguments and claims
- Mining (per the GAO, May 2013): miners run software to "solve complex equations" that verify transactions by grouping them into blocks and mathematically proving no double-spend; the network then accepts the block and awards new bitcoins (25 BTC at the time of the GAO quote).
- The network collectively maintains the log: "every new transaction broadcast across the Bitcoin network", with participating machines agreeing updates to the official log — and this computationally intensive agreement "is in fact the process used to mine Bitcoin".
- A footnote corrects the subsidy to "12.5 new Bitcoin... at the time of this paper", noting the algorithmic halving "approximately every 4 years" — a tell that 2014-era source material is being updated for 2018.
- Holding: a Bitcoin wallet incorporates a public/private key pair; coins are recorded on the shared ledger as under a wallet's control, and "the wallet and key pair are the only way of accessing the Bitcoin".
- Ownership follows control: bitcoins are "'owned' and 'held' by the user who has control of the relevant Bitcoin wallet".
- Transferring is a four-step process: recipient provides an address (an alphanumeric string derived from the wallet's public key); the sender builds a transaction message; the sender's private key signs it and the public key is announced for verification; the network verifies and moves the coins.
- Only the first two steps require human action: "the latter two steps are automatically executed by the Bitcoin software and the Bitcoin network".
- The key legal characterisation: "Bitcoin are transferred directly from the sender to the recipient. Bitcoin 'miners' validate and update the transaction in the public ledger, but are not payment intermediaries."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Taxonomic legal writing: establish agreed, authoritatively sourced definitions first (GAO report, Bollen's 2013 JBFLP article, the bitcoin.it wiki) so that later legal arguments can treat the system's mechanics as settled fact. The mode is expository and citation-based rather than argumentative — a foundation-laying extract, with the normative work done quietly by the choice of characterisations (ownership-as-control, miners-as-non-intermediaries).

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the regulatory reading that miners process payments: by insisting miners "are not payment intermediaries", Craig pre-empts money-transmitter classification attaching to mining — a live 2018 regulatory threat.
- Ownership-by-key-control is stated as legal fact ("owned and held by the user who has control"), which both affirms "not your keys, not your coins" and sets up his broader property-law programme in which control, not anonymity, grounds rights.
- The GAO-centred description makes miners the verifiers of the system, with no role for non-mining "full nodes" as validators — quietly against the BTC full-node-validation narrative.
- No direct polemic: the piece is descriptive, and its contradictions are structural (which definitions get locked in) rather than rhetorical.

## Notable quotes

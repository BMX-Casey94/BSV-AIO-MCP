# Concentration Is Not Centralisation
**Date:** 2025-12-04 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/concentration-is-not-centralisation
**Subtitle:** Why Bitcoin’s Mining Structure Is Competitive While BTC’s Governance Is a Chokepoint

## Core thesis
"Few miners equals centralisation" is a category error. Industrial concentration — five to twenty serious miners — is the normal oligopolistic equilibrium of a capital-intensive Proof-of-Work security industry and coexists with fierce competition; centralisation is the capture of rule-setting power, which is precisely what BTC exhibits through its development cartel and what Bitcoin's architecture was designed to prevent.

## Key arguments and claims
- Definitions with teeth: concentration is an economic descriptor of market structure (oligopoly, concentration ratios, the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index); centralisation is a governance descriptor — "sovereignty lodged in one hand… a protocol that can be rewritten by decree."
- High-fixed-cost analogies: airlines, container shipping, semiconductor fabs costing billions, cloud hyperscalers — concentrated "because scale economies are indifferent to slogans," not because a dictator arrived.
- Mining economics drive consolidation: ASIC fleets depreciate fast, power is the dominant cost, variance reduction matters because PoW rewards are probabilistic (large operators smooth revenue and lower their cost of capital), and engineering competence is a moat; the inefficient miner "is liquidated."
- Contestability keeps oligopoly honest: supernormal profits attract entry, losses force shutdowns; the five-to-twenty range is "the predictable resting place of a capital-intensive security industry that has grown past adolescence."
- The hobbyist mining tail is cultural, not productive — "like pointing at amateur pilots in a flying club and declaring commercial aviation decentralised."
- "Node" is defined by whitepaper Section 5: entities that gather transactions, assemble blocks, prove work and broadcast; spectator clients are "listeners," and "verification without production is observation."
- Sybil resistance is the point of Proof-of-Work: identities are cheap, work is expensive; "One CPU, one vote was always a shorthand for one unit of work, one unit of risk. The vote is the block."
- Governance is competition among block-creators under stable rules; "set in stone" is an anti-power principle — no priesthood, no mob sovereignty — and the precondition for the long-term investment, scaling and tokenisation that require an unshiftable foundation.
- BTC as real centralisation: the dominant client is treated as the constitution; maintainers decide which transactions, opcodes and capacity limits are "acceptable," enforcing via social leverage, gatekeeping and exchange delisting; miners become "the labour force carrying out the preferences of a rule-setting priesthood"; full-node "user sovereignty" is "a bedtime story told to people who want to feel important while being irrelevant."
- The consequence: throttled on-chain capacity forces commerce into custodial layers — "bottlenecks create gatekeepers the way drought creates water barons" — so BTC becomes "a settlement token for intermediaries, not cash for everyone."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Industrial-organisation economics (cost curves, contestability, concentration metrics) joined to conceptual analysis (the category error between market structure and political authority) and whitepaper exegesis (Section 5), with mechanism design — Sybil resistance — grounding why influence must be paid for in work rather than claimed by identity.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The maximalist chant "few big miners = centralised" is inverted: mining concentration is a market outcome; BTC's governance is the capture.
- "User sovereignty" through hobbyist full nodes is rejected as Sybil romanticism that reintroduces the very weakness Proof-of-Work exists to extinguish.
- Small-block ideology is reframed as rule-setting capture by an unelected group, not decentralisation.
- Protocol mutability by maintainers vs a protocol "set in stone" as the firewall against human power.
- Observer "validation" is not governance: only block production under competitive expenditure counts.

# Monetary transfer and transmission rules — core principles

- **Existing AML law already captures conversion.** "The exchange of bitcoin for other currencies clearly comes under existing AML regulations" — no new statute is needed to bring bitcoin-to-fiat exchange inside anti-money-laundering regimes.
- **Intermediaries trigger KYC.** "Where an exchange has been used as a part of this process, KYC requirements will still apply" — routing through an exchange adds customer-identification obligations rather than laundering them away.
- **Continuity of monetary law.** Requirements "will differ" across jurisdictions, "but, the laws of money and exchange remain valid" — bitcoin is treated under the same framework as any currency.
- **Regulatory anchor.** "Exchange" is defined via 31 CFR § 1010.100(ff) (the FinCEN money-services-business definition), under the Bank Secrecy Act MSB rule — "Definitions and Other Regulations Relating to Money Services Businesses, 76 FR 43585 (July 21, 2011)" — the basis on which US crypto exchanges were already treated as money transmitters.

# Scenario 1: Public Registry of an Asset
**Date:** 2018-10-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/scenario-1-public-registry-of-an-asset-f89787870e54
**Subtitle:** In this scenario, Bob decides to publish his ownership of an asset (e.g. his home) onto the Blockchain. Nothing else is done at this stage…

## Core thesis
First in a five-part series modelling real-world contracts as on-chain state machines. The simplest case: Bob publishes ownership of an asset (his home) onto the blockchain as a permanent public registry entry — "Nothing else is done at this stage" — creating a building block that later transactions (such as his "Non-debt Lending" construction) can reference. The body is a short setup paragraph whose substantive content is a state-machine diagram (an image not captured in the plain-text body).

## Key arguments and claims
- An asset can be registered on-chain by simply publishing ownership of it; no further structure is required at the registration stage.
- Once registered, the asset "may then get used in subsequent transactions", explicitly cross-referencing a companion piece on "Non-debt Lending".
- This registry arrangement has no termination date — it is an open-ended record, not a time-bound contract.
- The arrangement is formalised as "a simple state machine model", shown in a diagram (image only).

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Formal, taxonomic modelling: legal and commercial arrangements are treated as finite state machines that can be implemented on the blockchain, built up from the simplest case (bare registration) toward leases, rolling contracts, and conditional contracts in later instalments. The style is engineering exposition rather than polemic — Alice/Bob/Eve actors, explicit state variables such as the absence of a termination date.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- No direct engagement with BTC orthodoxy — but the series implicitly counters the 2018 mainstream view that Bitcoin could not support tokenisation or smart-contract-style constructs and that such use-cases required Ethereum or other platforms. Craig's premise is that asset registry and contract state machines belong on Bitcoin itself.

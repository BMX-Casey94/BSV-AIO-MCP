# Phases of the Bitcoin system — core principles

- **Three phases.** The Bitcoin system decomposes into mining, holding and transferring — a taxonomy sourced from the US GAO's May 2013 report, the bitcoin.it wiki and Bollen's 2013 JBFLP article.
- **Mining.** Miners run software to "solve complex equations" that verify transactions by grouping them into blocks and mathematically proving no double-spend; the network accepts the block and awards new bitcoins (25 BTC per the GAO quote; 12.5 BTC by 2018), with the subsidy halving "approximately every 4 years".
- **The network maintains the log collectively.** "Every new transaction broadcast across the Bitcoin network" is agreed into the official log by participating machines — and this computationally intensive agreement "is in fact the process used to mine Bitcoin".
- **Holding.** A wallet incorporates a public/private key pair; coins are recorded on the shared ledger as under a wallet's control, and "the wallet and key pair are the only way of accessing the Bitcoin".
- **Ownership follows control.** Bitcoins are "'owned' and 'held' by the user who has control of the relevant Bitcoin wallet" — control of keys grounds possession.
- **Transferring is four steps.** The recipient provides an address (derived from the wallet's public key); the sender builds the transaction message; the sender's private key signs it and the public key is announced for verification; the network verifies and moves the coins. "Only the first two steps involve action by the sender and recipient" — the rest is automatic.
- **Miners are not payment intermediaries.** "Bitcoin are transferred directly from the sender to the recipient. Bitcoin 'miners' validate and update the transaction in the public ledger, but are not payment intermediaries" — mining is verification, not money transmission.

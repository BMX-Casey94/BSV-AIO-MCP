# On scammers
**Date:** 2019-06-13 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/on-scammers-f5fca5801bb2
**Subtitle:** Bitcoin mining can be conducted to addresses that you do not own. It can be conducted in sending to addresses that other people hold and…

## Core thesis
A full-length defence brief against the Kleiman litigation narrative. Craig argues that Ira Kleiman is a "greedy conman" engaged in tax fraud and extortion who tried to force Craig's companies into liquidation to extract money he was never entitled to; that the widely reported "$54 million tax rebate" was never a rebate but a deduction/offset; and that his assets sit in companies and trusts structured since 2009–2011 precisely to resist such raids.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Bitcoin mining can be conducted to addresses that you do not own… to public keys with the private keys locked away" — control of early-mined coins does not prove ownership, undercutting the inference that early coins were shared Kleiman–Wright property.
- "Ira Kleiman was never close to his brother… the reason he is not in the will is that Dave didn't even like him."
- Ira "has presented documents to the court that were not public", originating from a hack of one of Craig's companies "with insider help".
- Ira refused the shares and directorship promised to Dave because accepting them would trigger tax and "an entire hour or two a month of work"; he wanted money "under the table" and, when refused, "worked with the tax office in Australia to falsify information in an attempt to force me into liquidation".
- The $54m figure: under the Australian R&D Tax Incentive, grouped companies get no rebate if group revenue exceeds $20 million; his reported revenue was $200 million. "You cannot get a rebate against $54 million; what you can get is a deduction" — a transfer-pricing and international-payment issue, not free government money.
- Worked example at the 30% Australian company rate: selling $100m of bitcoin means $30m tax and $70m kept; with a $54m offset, tax falls to $9m and the company keeps $91m — and "the government would still have a $9 million increase in revenue".
- The ATO dispute's real cost: operations moved abroad, "providing employment within the UK and Canada instead of Australia" — his ~50 UK staff "would likely have been in Australia".
- Jamie Wilson, Hotwire's CFO (June–Nov 2013), "fabricated documents", and "made a deal with people in New York in 2013 to sell both my intellectual property and my bitcoin"; his FASV/Panopticrypt assignment documents are "easily invalidated" because he misunderstood corporate requirements. "You wonder why he was fired."
- Asset protection: in 2011, as "the Australian Taxation Office was attempting to bankrupt me before my hearing", he moved everything into trusts; Wright International Investments Ltd dates from 2009; "company assets are not personal assets", and a minority shareholder in an unlisted company cannot force liquidation.
- Coin-Exch's value came from transferred IP including core banking software: "We demoed a working version of a commercial bank based on Bitcoin to the Australian Taxation Office in 2014" — the ATO's comment was that "all that money paid and it had an ugly front-end".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Forensic-accounting rebuttal: he takes the opposing camp's headline number ($54m) and dismantles it with statutory thresholds ($20m group-revenue cap) and a worked tax example, then reframes the whole affair through corporate law (trusts, minority shareholding, capital injections diluting equity). Documentary evidence (a quoted 2014 email from Ira) is mixed with motive attribution — greed — to recast litigation as extortion.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the media/Kleiman narrative that his ATO dispute was a rebate fraud: he reframes it as legitimate transfer pricing funded by his own capital injections.
- Against "early coins = Satoshi's coins = shared estate property": mining-to-others'-keys breaks the ownership inference the lawsuit and mainstream coverage rely on.
- Against the "Craig owes Dave's estate half the bitcoin" framing: the dispute concerned shares in unlisted companies, and minority shareholders cannot liquidate a company for cash.
- Notes Ira "didn't like that I wasn't going to admit that I was Satoshi Nakamoto and run around publicly" — his refusal to monetise the Satoshi claim on Ira's timeline is presented as a cause of the suit.

# Spam Away…
**Date:** 2019-07-25 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/spam-away-eb25b01a2514
**Subtitle:** Today, I am going to start with the narrative against sending many small transactions.

## Core thesis
An excerpt of a longer essay that continues on craigwright.net, opening a series in which Craig takes apart "false narratives" about Bitcoin. The first target is the doctrine that sending many small transactions is spam: he calls it a "pernicious and subtle lie" built on misquoting Satoshi (himself) out of context. Behind the myth-making, he claims, is motive: his critics "never wanted the version of Bitcoin I created," which is not an anti-government system.

## Key arguments and claims
- Motive claim: people attack him and "teach false mythologies about Bitcoin" because "they never wanted the version of Bitcoin I created."
- "Bitcoin isn't anti-government, and no derivative blockchain ever will be" — and believers in the anarchist version "are going to find this to be the case the hard way and lose a lot of money in the process."
- He has warned them: "if they choose to ignore what I say, there is little I can do."
- The anti-small-transaction narrative "is a very pernicious and subtle lie that stems from misquoting things I said and putting them out of context."
- The misquotation circulates through Satoshi quote lists — he links freedomnode.com's "21 Wise and Funny Bitcoin Quotes by Satoshi Nakamoto" as the vector.
- His verdict on the standard quote: "No, it is out of context, it is not what is being said."
- Series announcement: "I'm going to start taking apart some of the false narratives."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Debunking mode: identify a circulating maxim, trace it to a misquotation of Satoshi, and promise source-level correction. Authority rests on authorship — as the claimed author he alone can say what the quote meant — combined with a warning structure (ignore the correction and the market will punish you). The excerpt is a promissory note: the detailed dismantling of the "spam" doctrine is deferred to the full craigwright.net post.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the BTC "spam" doctrine used to justify small blocks, fee filters, and dust limits — the framing of low-value transactions as an attack, which underpinned the block-size cap defence and the retreat from on-chain cash and micropayments.
- Contradicts the anti-state reading of Bitcoin: he asserts no blockchain derivative can be anti-government, and predicts capital losses for those who bet otherwise.
- Era context: written eight months after the BCH/BSV split; BSV's positioning was large blocks and high volumes of small transactions, so the "spam" framing was its direct ideological enemy.

# “Lightning” Network and the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA)
**Date:** 2019-02-01 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/lightning-network-and-the-financial-industry-regulatory-authority-finra-bbbf28ccddac

## Core thesis
Crypto exchanges are money handlers fully subject to existing US securities regulation, and "crypto" is no excuse: SEC/FINRA record-keeping rules already cover their communications, US "long arm" jurisdiction reaches nearly every exchange on earth, and only an immutable archiving system — which Bitcoin's blockchain exemplifies — can meet compliance. Anonymous coins and Lightning-style arrangements place exchanges in outright criminal breach.

## Key arguments and claims
- Exchanges store "highly sensitive data concerning the exchange of various assets" — account numbers, cardholder data, bank statements, "secrets that could be used to commit insider trading" — making them regulated money handlers: "Basically, 'crypto' is not an excuse."
- Blockchain claims are irrelevant to exchange obligations: "blockchain is a settlement function, and next, it is not even remotely related to the clearing function of an individual exchange."
- Specific FINRA rules cited: 10–06 (firms must retain records of all social-media communications, including those of "crypto exchanges" — "a deleted tweet must be recorded as deleted"); 11–32 (tweets and text messages are written material requiring preservation); 11–39 (business communication must be retained, retrieved, and supervised "even when such communication is conducted from a personal device").
- Jurisdictional maximalism via US "Long Arm" provisions: the rules capture any exchange that uses a .com domain, accepts USD, holds or trades against USD or a USD equivalent ("such means USDT"), or "has any network communications that pass a US server at any point in time" — i.e. virtually all of them, not just US exchanges.
- Compliance standard: exchanges "require an immutable archiving system capable of archiving all necessary ESI… And not just capable — one that is in fact capturing all of the information. The mere archiving of data is not sufficient; it must be archived in an immutable format" (linking FINRA's SEA Rule 17a-4 interpretations).
- Anonymous coins are criminal exposure: when Zcash is traded, "the exchange is in criminal breach when it does not link the withdraw to the individual explicitly"; the individual must retain all coin movements, and reporting coins lost then moving them "itself being a crime".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Regulatory-text exegesis applied as a trap: he quotes specific FINRA rule numbers, lists jurisdictional tripwires as a checklist, and then shows that the only technology satisfying the immutable-archiving requirement is the very blockchain the industry claims exempts it. The rhetorical mode is the compliance audit as indictment — the essay ends with "Enjoy the read…" and a link to the SEA Rule 17a-4 interpretations, daring exchanges to measure themselves.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "decentralised code isn't a regulated entity" orthodoxy: Craig asserts money-handler status attaches to function, not form.
- Contradicts the Lightning Network scaling narrative (the title's scare quotes around "Lightning"): routing payments through channel operators and exchanges creates exactly the record-keeping and money-transmission liabilities FINRA/SEC enforce — part of his standing argument that LN nodes are unlicensed money services businesses.
- Contradicts the "jurisdiction-free crypto" belief: holding or trading USDT alone is asserted to import US regulatory reach.
- Contradicts the privacy-coin legitimacy thesis: anonymity features are framed not as rights-protecting tools but as compliance failures that are "criminal" for exchanges and record-keeping obligations for individuals.

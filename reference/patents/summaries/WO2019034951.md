## Problem addressed
Control of a blockchain asset is the private key that signs a spend. A single stored key is a single point of failure. The specification wants a dealerless threshold vault: no node or third party can assemble the private key, yet a threshold of nodes can produce a standard ECDSA signature.

## Core mechanism
Each node independently chooses a polynomial of degree k−1 with its secret as the free term and privately sends evaluations to the others (joint random secret sharing). A node’s private-key share is the sum of received evaluations; the group public key is recovered by interpolating elliptic-curve point multiplications of those shares, optionally with Berlekamp–Welch error correction. Shares can be refreshed by adding a joint-zero secret share without changing the private key. For signing, nodes share double-masked ephemeral-key shares (ephemeral share times a multiplicative mask, plus an additive mask) and curve points of the multiplicative masks; interpolation yields the first signature component r. Each node then broadcasts a second-component share s_i from the message hash, key share, r and a second additive mask; interpolating the s_i yields s. The pair (r, s) is attached to a blockchain transaction.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
A method, performed by a first node, generates a threshold vault by exchanging polynomial evaluations and their elliptic-curve point multiplications, storing a private-key share and interpolating the public key, then receiving threshold ephemeral-key shares that are additively and multiplicatively masked, and modifying the key share with a dealerless joint-zero secret share. A computing-device claim covers a node that runs that method.

## BSV relevance
Dealerless threshold ECDSA that emits a conventional (r, s) pair is usable for BSV multi-party wallets and vaults without a custom Script opcode or a dealer who ever holds the full key.

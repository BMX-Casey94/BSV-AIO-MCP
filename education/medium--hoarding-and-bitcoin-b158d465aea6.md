# Hoarding and Bitcoin — core principles

- **Money is a commodity whose distinguishing use is exchange.** There is no mystical "circulation" — only transfers of commodities subject to supply and demand like any other.
- **Holding a cash balance is rational precaution, not pathology.** In a risk-free world there would be no need to hold cash; balances exist because the future is uncertain. There is no such thing as hoarding — only differences in individual risk appetite.
- **Saving is delayed gratification that cheapens goods for everyone else.** If one individual saves, all goods become less expensive for all other individuals; the saver hedges risk and avoids reliance on government or charitable handouts. Holding bitcoins is no different from an investment in land.
- **Exhorting spending "to aid the economy" consumes capital.** It is a variation of the broken-window fallacy. Printing money does not create wealth: changing the amount of money only changes the distribution of goods. People care about purchasing power, not nominal balances.
- **Value accrues from on-chain use and velocity, not from idle balances.** Bitcoin's value growth is a sigmoid S-curve, not exponential. Never-spending is uninformed speculation; teaching people never to spend contradicts the white paper's first line.
- **Use is on-chain by nature.** Gateway protocols (Lightning, Plasma, WormHole) do not add the use that creates value. The prescription is spend-and-replace: move savings into Bitcoin, use it as cash, and convert speculation into genuine saving.
- **Bitcoin is P2P electronic cash.** A tokenised cash system whose design centre is on-chain spending, not a store-of-value end-state.

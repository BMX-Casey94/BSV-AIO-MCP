# The wheel of time is not on an axis
**Date:** 2019-04-27 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-wheel-of-time-is-not-on-an-axis-ccefa8963f6
**Subtitle:** The failure in understanding change lies in believing that the wheel of time is on a stationary axle. We move forward as the wheel does.

## Core thesis
Framed by a dialectical claim that every revolution (anonymous coins, "democratising finance") provokes a reaction that reforms the world, Craig argues that Bitcoin transactions are legally ordinary ledger entries already covered by existing AML/KYC law — and that ICOs and "decentralised finance" are merely recycled penny-share and web-IPO scams. Bitcoin's real purpose, he says, is to help corporations keep honest records, pay tax, and fight crime; it was never aimed at banks.

## Key arguments and claims
- Historical model: "No reformation can occur without a revolution that is strong enough to create a reaction." The Internet is a commons, and "as with all commons, the tragedy is that it leads to exploitation" by criminals and trolls — now comes "the reactionary response to a revolt".
- Legal characterisation: "In its simplest form, a Bitcoin transaction involves the creation of an entry in a ledger" reassigning value from one key pair to another; "AML law refers to funds, and Bitcoin is already covered."
- Harder cases he flags: multisig/multi-party addresses, conditional smart contracts, embedding data and "tokenisation of real property and chattel goods", theft and fraudulent procurement, and cross-jurisdictional assignment.
- Exchange of bitcoin for other currencies "clearly comes under existing AML regulations", and where an exchange is used, KYC applies — citing FinCEN guidance FIN-2008-G008, 31 CFR § 1010.100(ff), and Bank Secrecy Act regulations (76 FR 43585).
- "Bitcoin doesn't democratise finance. ICOs don't. The existing system does." Qualified-investor rules exist "to protect people without access to information from those who prey on the weak"; ICOs are "simply a means to bypass informational controls" — revivals of "penny share scams, pink sheet scams, and web-IPO scams".
- Dematerialisation already moved shares and derivatives from paper to databases decades ago; people could trade off regulated exchanges for decades but "lose money" when they do. In the 90s "webIPOs.com and hundreds of other distributed scams did the same thing".
- "Bitcoin helps corporations": maintaining records, paying tax, compliance, shareholder registries. "Bitcoin had been developed and designed before Occupy… and was never intended to attack banks or corporations."
- Crime-fighting utility: "Bitcoin makes corruption easier to fight. Bitcoin makes tracing illicit drug sales simpler. Bitcoin leaves an evidentiary trail that can be used in fighting human trafficking." Timeline: "It is a 20-year (at least) process from now."
- Footnoted doctrinal detail: offer-and-acceptance problems in Bitcoin transactions (does transmission constitute acceptance, or inclusion at 6 blocks?), wallet location affecting contract formation (Hyde v Wrench 1840; Apple Corps v Apple Computer [2004]; Art. 1335 Italian Civil Code; Restatement 2d of Contracts §56).

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Two registers run in parallel: a Hegelian/dialectical philosophy of history (revolution → reaction → reformation, the moving wheel) and black-letter legal-doctrinal analysis with numbered footnotes citing FinCEN guidance, the Code of Federal Regulations, the Bank Secrecy Act, and contract-law casebooks (Treitel). The method is assimilation: Bitcoin is slotted into existing categories (funds, money transmission, offer and acceptance) rather than treated as legally novel, and historical analogy (web-IPO scams of the 90s) does the debunking work against ICOs.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the ICO/DeFi "democratising finance" orthodoxy of 2017–19: he calls it an information-asymmetry scam that existing investor-protection law already handles.
- Contradicts the crypto-anarchist reading of Bitcoin as anti-bank and anti-corporate: Bitcoin "helps corporations" and "was never intended to attack banks".
- Contradicts the "regulation can't touch crypto" stance: AML/KYC already apply to Bitcoin as written; the law needs no new paradigm.
- Contradicts anonymous-coin advocacy by casting anonymity projects as the exploitative "revolution" that history will react against.

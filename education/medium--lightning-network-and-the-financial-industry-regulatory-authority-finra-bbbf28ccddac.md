# “Lightning” Network and the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) — core principles

- **Function determines regulation, not form.** Businesses storing or transmitting value are money handlers under existing securities law; dealing in 'crypto' is no excuse and changes nothing about the obligation.
- **Settlement and clearing are distinct functions.** A blockchain is a settlement layer; it neither performs nor excuses an exchange's clearing-side record-keeping duties.
- **FINRA record-keeping rules already cover digital-asset businesses.** Rule 10–06 requires retention of all social-media communications (a deleted tweet must be recorded as deleted); 11–32 treats tweets and text messages as written material; 11–39 requires business communication to be retained, retrieved and supervised even when conducted from a personal device.
- **US long-arm jurisdiction is nearly universal.** The net captures any business that uses a .com domain, accepts USD, holds or trades against USD or a USD equivalent (USDT counts), or whose network communications merely pass a US server at any point.
- **Compliance demands provably immutable archiving.** SEA Rule 17a-4 interpretations require capturing all necessary ESI in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format; mere archiving is insufficient. An append-only public blockchain is the model technology for meeting this standard.
- **Anonymity features create criminal exposure for intermediaries.** Trading an anonymous coin without explicitly linking withdrawals to individuals puts the operator in criminal breach; individuals must retain full records of coin movements, and reporting coins lost and then moving them is itself a crime.
- **Builder guidance: design for the audit trail.** Products that route or intermediate payments inherit money-transmission and record-keeping liabilities; building on an immutable-ledger architecture is the compliance-aligned choice, while architectures that let records be deleted accumulate regulatory risk.

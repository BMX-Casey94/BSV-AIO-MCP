# The Abolition of the Dealer — core principles

- **Fairness is a structure, not a character.** Mental poker showed that secrecy and proof are not enemies. The trusted third party is not regulated, audited or constrained; he is proven unnecessary, in full generality, for any game one can specify.
- **The interesting question is how to make trustworthiness unnecessary.** To trust is to take out a loan against another's character. Commitments, semantic security, verifiable shuffles, zero-knowledge proofs and secret sharing migrate trust from character to structure.
- **Any game of incomplete information has a protocol.** Given an honest majority, the outcome is exactly what a perfectly trusted referee would produce. Mental poker was the first move; the completeness theorems were the checkmate.
- **The ledger is the public table.** Nakamoto's append-only, ordered stage is where commitments are posted and outcomes anchored. Private machinery on public ground yields a grammar for the affairs of strangers without appointing anyone to be trusted with the outcome. The card is private; the table is public; and neither requires a dealer.
- **The same gesture abolishes the dealer in markets.** Sealed-bid auctions that never reveal losing bids; universally verifiable elections; randomness no one owns; confidential audit that proves thresholds without surrendering records; and threshold keys that a quorum signs without the key ever existing whole in one place.

# Proof of State, or, the new Fed — core principles

- **Proof-of-work is competitive capitalism, not voting.** The only thing that secures proof-of-work is competition: Bitcoin uses the blockchain not as a mere database but as a distributed competitive system, an engine of capitalism.
- **Immutability is economic, not structural.** Blockchains can be reversed and unwound; Bitcoin's immutability comes only from miners' competitive incentive to preserve the rule-set, because the system's value depends on the rules not changing.
- **A majority miner profits by maintaining the rules.** Even a miner with more than 51% short-term control finds more profit in maintaining the system than in breaking it.
- **Proof-of-stake recreates the central bank.** PoS is not new: a small group of stakeholders — plutocrats with little direct skin in the game — sets policy for the "good" of all while ensuring those in power remain in power, exactly as the Fed's Board of Governors does.
- **Permissioned chains are inferior to databases.** A permissioned blockchain or PoS chain is a far slower, less secure version of an Oracle database, which already performs atomic encrypted transactions across systems at a thousand times the speed of a system such as Ripple (XRP); adding a blockchain is a sham hiding a badly distributed database.
- **Proof-of-work removes the need for fiat-issuing central banks.** It creates a trust-free global money, P2P electronic cash; central banks could persist but would be constrained as national banks are under the Euro, losing reserves if they try to cheat.
- **Fair representation needs identity.** No monetary system allows one-vote-one-person without sharing full identity; stake-weighted power lets those with little skin in the game punish others and blows the economy into cycles and bubbles.

# MSBs and Account-Based Systems
**Date:** 2019-06-04 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/msbs-and-account-based-systems-e64dc056f92a

## Core thesis
A short Medium excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net. Craig argues that Monero and mixed coins (including Schnorr-based mixing) are engaged in money transmission and are therefore Money Services Businesses by nature: "Every node and every software wallet engaged in mixing" must satisfy US Bank Secrecy Act money-transmitter obligations — AML programmes, record-keeping, and SAR/CTR filings — and "avoiding the requirements of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) in the US is not an option".

## Key arguments and claims
- Classification premise: "Monero and mixed coins (such as those using Schnorr) are engaged in the activity of money transfers and money handling. Such activity, by nature, makes them an MSB."
- Subsystem liability: "Any subsystem is required to comply with the BSA obligations that apply to money transmitters."
- Liability extends to infrastructure, not just operators: "Every node and every software wallet engaged in mixing would need to fulfil the obligations that apply to a money transmitter."
- The specific duties enumerated: "complying with the anti-money laundering (AML) program, maintaining records, and issuing against the reporting requirements as defined in their jurisdiction".
- Named reporting instruments: "filing SARs (Suspicious Activity Reports) and CTRs (currency transaction reports)".
- Peer-to-peer is not exempt: "Peer-to-peer exchange is covered under the anti-money laundering (AML) rules."
- No opt-out: "Avoiding the requirements of the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) in the US is not an option."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
A regulatory syllogism: define the activity (money transfer/handling), map it onto the statutory category (MSB / money transmitter under the BSA), then enumerate the obligations that automatically follow (AML programme, records, SARs, CTRs). His characteristic move is to push liability down the stack — from coin operators to every node and wallet participating in mixing — treating legal category as determined by function, not by the system's self-description.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the privacy-coin orthodoxy that protocol-level anonymity (ring signatures, Schnorr-based mixing) places activity outside money-transmission law — Craig says the activity itself fixes the legal category.
- Contradicts the widespread 2019 reading of FinCEN guidance that nodes and non-custodial wallets are not money transmitters — he explicitly includes "every node and every software wallet engaged in mixing".
- Contradicts the "code is speech" / permissionless-innovation framing: compliance is mandatory and "not an option", and P2P exchange is squarely within AML rules.

# Property Law in the Age of Bitcoin — core principles

- **Existing property law already governs Bitcoin.** Servers are chattels, data is intellectual property, and the common-law bundle — control, benefit, transfer, exclusion — maps onto coins and network protocols.
- **Transactions are property.** Courts have upheld property rights associated with Bitcoin; law reaches the code.
- **P2P is the exchange, not the node mesh.** Peer-to-peer is one user exchanging a transaction with another — like cash — not the topology of validating nodes.
- **Miners are contracted to forward transactions.** Declining to include a transaction is within a miner’s rights; there is no obligation to accept every contract.
- **Deliberately damaging a transaction is conversion.** A miner who intentionally seeks to damage a transaction is liable in tort against property, whatever the protocol permits.
- **Assisting a double-spend is larceny.** Helping a user complete a double-spend is a fraud-based gain and a criminal offence.
- **Rights of control and exclusion apply to infrastructure.** An owner may forbid probing once notice is given; backbone access operates under easement-like duties, so blocking it (as in a DDoS) is a trespass.
- **Law sits on top of the protocol.** “Code is law” is inverted: the protocol does not extinguish conversion, larceny or contractual duties.

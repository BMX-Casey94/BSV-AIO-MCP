## Problem addressed
Bitcoin itself is described as resilient, but exchanges and wallets that hold keys have been attacked. A user may want a custodian to help sign transactions without that custodian learning addresses or balances, and without any single party being able to produce a valid spend on its own.

## Core mechanism
First participants hold second data (a message, its hash, or a blockchain transaction) that second participants must not see. They send first data derived from that second data. The second participant (or a threshold of second participants using JRSS/JZSS) returns a digital signature of the first data only. The first participants then process that signature into shares of a signature of the original second data; a threshold of those shares unblinds to a standard ECDSA signature. Because the on-chain signature is valid only after unblinding, colluding second participants cannot spend even if they later learn one another’s identities. The blinding party can act as escrow or backup without knowing how much bitcoin the address holds.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
The independent method sends first data (based on second data that the second participant cannot access) to a second participant, receives a signature of that first data, and has a plurality of first participants convert it into shares of a signature of the second data, reconstructable only at a threshold. Independent claims also cover a computer system and a non-transitory medium that perform the method.

## BSV relevance
The scheme produces a standard elliptic-curve signature intended to be fully compatible with Bitcoin transactions. It can be used on BSV so that a custodian or backup signer attests a blinded transaction without learning the address or amount.

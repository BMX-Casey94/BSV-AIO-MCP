## Problem addressed
The specification says Bitcoin-style INV/GETDATA diffusion is symmetric and therefore leaky: IP traffic analysis can link transactions to source addresses. It also says unconstrained mixing relays can exhaust a node’s link bandwidth. It seeks a relay scheme that obfuscates whether a node is a source or a relay, cuts redundant forwards, and sets relay fan-out, delay and hop count from measured bandwidth.

## Core mechanism
The Diffusion Mixer Protocol has two stages. In random differential relay a node waits a period T, collects generated and received transactions, and sends different transactions to an arbitrarily chosen subset of entry nodes (optionally several transactions on one link). Local RDR tables record sources, destinations and timestamps. Heuristics then assign relays: source mixing (locally generated transactions go to non-overlapping peer sets), relay mixing (received transactions go to peer sets whose intersection is bounded), destination mixing, time-of-arrival delay, source-control thresholds that drop abusive peers, and load balancing. Standard diffusion afterwards floods each transaction to all entry nodes for reliability. The claimed method measures available bandwidth on neighbour links and uses it to set, per packet, how many neighbours receive it, how long to delay after the scheduled relay time, and how many hops it may travel. Some neighbours get a modified packet that forbids further relay.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method that collects first data packets over a time period, measures bandwidth to neighbours, builds a mapping that sets per-packet fan-out, delay and hop count from that bandwidth, identifies which assigned neighbours may relay onward, sends the packet to that subset, and sends modified copies that prohibit further relay to the rest. Independent claims 10 and 11 cover a system and a computer-readable medium that perform the method.

## BSV relevance
The protocol is described for Bitcoin-family transaction relay. Bandwidth-aware, source-obfuscating propagation is relevant to BSV peer networks that want to reduce IP-level linking of transaction origins.

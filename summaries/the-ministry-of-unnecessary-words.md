# The Ministry of Unnecessary Words
**Date:** 2025-12-09T00:21:48.481Z | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-ministry-of-unnecessary-words
**Subtitle:** A Far-Future Ledger Fable Where the Currency Carnival Confuses “Concentration” with “Control”

## Core thesis
A far-future satire: the city of Ledgerfall runs on the Cashline, a planetary payment rail operated by competitive block-creators under fixed rules. The Currency Carnival — a transparent BTC allegory — arrives selling fear of "centralisation" by deliberately conflating consolidation (fewer, larger operators chosen by competition under stable rules) with centralisation (unilateral control of the rules). Jory Vale, the Bureau of Definitions' Auditor of Actual Meaning, fights the semantic capture through a public demonstration and a meaning referendum. The essay's claim: when a movement cannot win on performance, it invades the dictionary.

## Key arguments and claims
- The Bureau of Definitions practises "semantic hygiene", not censorship: "You could say almost anything. You just couldn't mean it unless the Bureau had pre-approved the meaning." An outage is a "resilience recalibration window"; labels, not events, are the battleground.
- The Carnival's business model: "First you sell fear. If the fear takes, you sell a solution to the fear." Its panic machine exhales soundbites — "ONE MINER TO RULE THEM ALL", "FREEDOM IS DYING" — engineered "to bypass the part of the brain that asks awkward follow-ups".
- Jory's core distinction: "Consolidation is about numbers. Centralisation is about control." Miners "compete within" fixed rules; "If any one of them could rewrite the protocol whenever they liked, that would be centralisation." His star-port analogy: Ledgerfall has three star-ports because three handle the traffic, yet star-port managers cannot "rewrite gravity".
- Institutional capture is shown mechanically: SEMANTIC ALIGNMENT UPDATE 7.3 redefines consolidation as "soft centralisation"; the donor list behind the Cashline Education Wing is the Carnival's foundation, a mailbox "research group", and a venture fund. "Quiet capture. The kind that doesn't need a coup because it already has the admin password."
- At the Forum of Continuous Outrage, polls offer only "YES / YES / YES BUT ANGRIER". Jory: "The people haven't spoken... They've been prompted." His eight-word phrase: "Fewer miners is not your problem. Rule-changers are."
- Demonstration Day pits Ledger A (consolidated operators, fixed rules) against Ledger B (tens of thousands of nodes plus governance committees). Ledger B suffers emergency parameter adjustments, then forks — "like a drunk historian arguing with his own diary" — ending "awaiting committee resolution", while Ledger A runs with stable confirmations, continuous finality and zero rule changes. The Carnival leader objects: "It used math... You can't reduce freedom to numbers." Jory: "I reduced your slogan to reality. And reality doesn't care about your vibes."
- The Word-Forge backroom reveals the method on a whiteboard: SCARY WORDS (centralisation, capture, control) aimed at TARGETS ("Consolidated mining, Rule-stability advocates, Protocol immutability, Anything that works without us") via REPLACEMENTS ("soft centralisation", "creeping capture"). The leader states it plainly: "Facts don't travel. Words do."
- The protocol-as-constitution argument: tram rails do not change gauge when someone gets a new hat; "Tools change... Foundations don't." The Carnival treats rules "like a playlist" and sells the resulting noise as progress.
- Resolution: a Meaning Referendum held on the Cashline itself approves restoring plain meanings, "RESTORE PLAIN MEANINGS — APPROVED (72.4%)"; the Education Wing is restructured down to a desk reading "PLEASE USE WORDS AS PREVIOUSLY DEFINED." The narrator's coda: "nonsense doesn't die; nonsense migrates" — and what the Carnival truly fears is "becoming irrelevant in a world where the ledger works without them".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Satirical allegory mapping one-to-one onto the BTC governance debate, using Socratic distinction-drawing (concentration versus control of rule-change) as the analytical blade. The live demonstration functions as falsification — performance data against slogans — while the Education Wing subplot applies institutional-capture analysis: follow the funding behind the definitions.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Node-count as virtue: Ledger B's vast headcount fails precisely because committees keep moving the rules; quantity is not security.
- Governance theatre: "Every extra committee that can change rules is centralisation" — the inverse of the mainstream "decentralised governance" claim.
- Miner FUD: miners enforce rules and are paid for following them; they cannot rewrite the protocol without destroying their own revenue and users' trust.

# Forking and Passing Off… — core principles

- **The protocol rules are set in stone.** Software may be forked under an open licence; changing the protocol while reusing the underlying ledger database is passing off the original system, not a legitimate fork.
- **The MIT Licence covers code, not the ledger.** A novel written on open-source tools still belongs to its author. Bitcoin is a distributed registry, and the distributed ledger is a database.
- **The token set was fixed at creation.** Approximately 21 million bitcoin, each an arbitrary verbal representation of 100 million individual and indivisible tokens. The block subsidy is payment from that initial issuance, not ongoing minting.
- **Nodes are paid contractual agents.** The original client’s generate-coins offer and the white paper’s six-step process form a unilateral contract. Nodes check double-spends — the digital equivalent of cheque fraud — and have no individual property rights in the registry.
- **Peer-to-peer is a hybrid.** Direct user-to-user exchange plus a separate commercial node network maintaining the registry under contract. “Completely decentralised” is cherry-picked out of context.
- **Blockchains are all traceable.** Bitcoin is not a system that allows you to avoid government.

## Problem addressed
The specification says existing peer-to-peer lending pools each require registration on a bespoke exchange and sit on conventional banking rails, which it describes as restrictive and complex. It seeks a blockchain-based channel in which parties can publish a prospective contract, bind to it or offer alternative terms, and transfer repayments, without a third party overseeing the channel.

## Core mechanism
An initiator publishes a first blockchain transaction whose redeem script holds the initiator’s public key and metadata: a hash of an off-chain invitation (stored in a DHT) plus a pointer to its location, locking an amount of digital currency. A responder stores a response in the same repository and publishes a further transaction with metadata hashing that response. Software monitors the chain, matches corresponding metadata, and, when terms align and pledged funds meet the threshold (the incomplete transaction can be completed with SIGHASH_ANYONECANPAY inputs), generates a smart contract recording the parties, any guarantor or facilitator, the asset and a repayment schedule. A P2SH address is created for each scheduled payment; draw-down and repayments are ordinary blockchain transactions, optionally with tokenised collateral or fiat.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method that publishes an invitation document via a first blockchain transaction pointing at a DHT, spends to the redeem address, stores a responder’s response in the same resource, publishes a further transaction with a hash of that response, monitors the chain for matching metadata, generates a smart contract (including a repayment schedule with a P2SH address per payment), and publishes the contract to a repository.

## BSV relevance
The embodiment uses Bitcoin-style P2SH, SIGHASH_ANYONECANPAY pledge assembly, DHT-stored documents hashed into transaction metadata, and scheduled repayment outputs. Those are the same patterns used on BSV for offer/accept flows and tokenised credit.

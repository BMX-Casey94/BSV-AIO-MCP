# The Quiet Violence of Sunday: Notes on Protocol Capture, Manufactured Ignorance, and the Cult of BTC-Core
**Date:** 2025-11-16 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-quiet-violence-of-sunday-notes
**Subtitle:** A sardonic meditation on engineered amnesia, performative decentralisation narratives, and the cultural decay masquerading as technical progress

## Core thesis
BTC-Core is a captured protocol: a small, coordinated developer cartel has rewritten the meanings of "node", "consensus", "immutability" and "scaling" to entrench its own authority, while inventing the "full node" mythology to give economically irrelevant spectators the illusion of power. Against this, miners — the only actors who bear cost and risk — hold genuine property and legal rights, grounded in promissory estoppel, to defend the original fixed protocol.

## Key arguments and claims
- The "full node" is "a political confection cooked up by those who found themselves without a role in the actual system". In the original design a node was an economic actor in the competitive business of creating blocks — "produce, or fall behind". The spectator node offers "participation without work, control without consequence".
- The cartel claim is argued through case law: *United Dominions Trust Ltd v Kirkwood* (partnership arising from acting in concert with collective influence), *Stekel v Ellice* (implicit partnership through shared enterprise and outward behaviour), and cartel jurisprudence from *Norris v United States* to EU horizontal-coordination decisions — "the refusal to acknowledge the cartel's existence is the cartel's first line of defence". Protocol changes emerge from a small aligned cluster via back channels, developer meetings and curated lists.
- Protocol capture is self-serving: mutability keeps developers indispensable. "The moment the rules are fixed, the authority of those who tinker with them evaporates. Stability would be the death of this priesthood. So instability must be canonised."
- Immutability has become theatre: slogans and memes substituted for engineering proofs, doctrine recited on Sunday while rules are revised during the week.
- Semantic collapse is enumerated term by term: "Layer 2" now covers networks that never reconnect to the base chain; "scaling" has been redefined from throughput into "a posture, a narrative of restraint"; "digital gold" is "a linguistic costume stitched together to hide the absence of function".
- Digital cash's actual purpose is restated: micropayments, seamless economic flow, rapid settlement, lawful privacy and traceability. "Privacy—real privacy—is traceable. Anonymous systems are not private; they are deceptive."
- Miners' rights are grounded legally: the ledger is common-law property; the repeated public representation that "the protocol is set in stone" gives rise to promissory estoppel — promise, reliance, detriment — once miners invest capital on that assurance. "Observation is not contribution. Validation is not governance."
- Manufactured amnesia keeps the narrative alive: history is "a palimpsest" — the full node was once irrelevant, now sovereign; small blocks once temporary, now a moral imperative; digital cash once the purpose, now "an embarrassing relic of a naïve past".
- Reality is the final arbiter: "a system that does always outlasts a system that merely declares."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The framework is institutional economics fused with legal doctrine — partnership law, cartel jurisprudence and promissory estoppel are applied directly to developer governance — combined with semantic analysis of technical vocabulary and an incentive account of who benefits from mutability. The Sunday motif supplies the sardonic framing: the day of performative rest is when the machinery of redefinition becomes visible.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against "anyone can run a node, therefore the system is decentralised": spectator nodes produce nothing, risk nothing, and cannot stop or alter a single transaction; only block creators enforce rules through economic action.
- Against Core's "community consensus": what is called emergent consensus is governance by a coordinated minority that culturally exiles dissenters — cartel behaviour recognisable in law.
- Against "the protocol must continually evolve": the original design intended a fixed foundation for others to build on; endless revision serves the revisers.
- Against "digital gold" and the "settlement layer" pivot: these are euphemisms adopted after the failure to function as digital cash.
- Against anonymity maximalism: digital cash was built for lawful, traceable privacy, not "anonymity without responsibility".

# The Dawn of the Nano-Economy — core principles

- **Sub-cent finality creates markets, it does not merely cheapen old ones.** Wherever the cost of metering, recording and settling falls below the value of the act, whole classes of labour, commerce and automation become viable. Such systems do not tolerate batching, reversibility or float; they require finality at their core.
- **Two preconditions govern every nano-economy.** Settlement must be cheaper than the act it meters, and increased resolution of action must itself create value through precision, coordination or speed. Verified action plus irreversible payment equals a concluded transaction.
- **Machines can trade with machines.** Idle edge devices auction compute, drones trade charge, mesh networks route packets for a fraction of a cent — participation is not virtue, it is profitable. This is the Economy of Things.
- **Information and content unbundle to the sentence and the second.** Sensors sell readings, knowledge pays per paragraph, streaming pays per second; a new metric — intent density — replaces advertising proxies. Individuals become sovereign data brokers with cryptographically provable, revocable consent.
- **Fraud is removed by making it uneconomical.** Nano-insurance, microwork with staked microbonds, and event-based access (parking by the second, identity pseudonymous, presence provable) replace claims processes and enforcement with proven events.
- **AI becomes a cooperative protocol, not a platform.** Verified corrections earn royalties; future users of a corrected answer trigger split payments; GPU compute clears second-by-second across decentralised providers.
- **We do not need to imagine this world — only to allow it to be priced.** Claims are not submitted; they are proven. Permanent, irrevocable micropayments at a hundredth of a cent and below rewrite the economics of everything that legacy rails made impossible.

# The Gospel According to Grok
**Date:** 2026-05-06 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-gospel-according-to-grok
**Subtitle:** How to Turn a Bitcoin Key into a Birth Certificate by Repeating Yourself Loudly Enough

## Core thesis
The claim that certain early Bitcoin keys are provably "Satoshi's keys" — because they are early, because the genesis block exists, because the story is convenient — is circular reasoning dressed in cryptographic vocabulary. The blockchain records transactions, not identity; a signature proves control of a key and nothing more; and attributing a key to a person requires evidence from outside the chain. No such external attestation has been produced.

## Key arguments and claims
- The blockchain records transactions, scripts, outputs, block headers, hashes and proof-of-work. It contains no evidential bridge from key to person: "the chain is the attestation" is a slogan that hides the missing step, not an argument.
- A signature proves only that whoever controlled the relevant private key produced a valid signature over a particular message. It does not prove identity, authorship of the white paper, originality of the key, continuity of possession, or lawful ownership.
- Attribution must precede a signature's evidential value: if Alice hands Bob a public key directly, later signatures confirm control within that relationship. Identity came first; the key was bound to it. Finding a key in a ledger years later and declaring it Alice's because convenient inverts the logic.
- The white paper's own privacy model — keeping public keys anonymous and using a new key pair for each transaction — is designed to prevent persistent attribution. The keys-as-identity claim contradicts the very architecture it invokes.
- The genesis block is not mined in the Section-5 sense: it has no previous block, extends no chain, and is accepted because it is hard-coded into the software. Treating it as identity evidence confuses initialisation with mining, and construction of data with attribution of personhood.
- The genesis coinbase creates no normal spendable output; there is no signed transaction and no chain of signatures linking it to later coinbases — "no magic thread running through the ledger tying all early activity to one named or pseudonymous actor".
- The circular argument in five steps: assume one actor performed all relevant acts; assume early mining is attributable to that actor; assume the keys are therefore his; assume control proves identity; conclude that control proves identity because the keys were assumed his. "The conclusion is not reached. It is planted."
- Early participants destroy the one-actor presumption: Hal Finney received the first transaction; Ray Dillinger and others had early involvement with code and discussions.
- Key control is not legal ownership: a thief's signature verifies; an employee holding a corporate key verifies; an escrow agent verifies. The protocol checks script conditions, not provenance — a deliberately narrow mechanism.
- Reductio: if ledger activity attributed identity, every user's pseudonymity collapses — every key an identity hook, every unspent output a confession. The opposing camp preaches privacy and key non-reuse for everyone while demanding permanent identity markers for Satoshi: "Privacy for everyone else; identity for Satoshi."
- The argument fails four ways: technically (genesis treated as mined), cryptographically (control treated as identity), evidentially (no attestation), logically (circularity). The remedy is a demand: produce the link — a statement, an email, a signed message from an already-attributed key, a direct key handoff, or an independent witness.

## How Craig reasons
Evidence-law discipline applied to cryptographic claims, combined with formal fallacy analysis (circularity, red herring, straw man) and reductio ad absurdum, delivered as extended satire against "Grok" and Reddit-grade legal theory. The operative standard is attestation: identity is established by a body of evidence in which a signature is at most one component, never the source of the attribution itself.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the mainstream "sign with Satoshi's keys" demand as capable of proving anything about identity: a signature from an unattributed key proves only control of that key.
- Rejects "because they are early", "because the blockchain shows it", "because everyone knows" and "because experts say so" as evidence — folklore and hearsay, not forensics.
- Exposes the inconsistency of a culture that champions pseudonymity and key non-reuse while treating early keys as permanent identity markers whenever a narrative requires it.

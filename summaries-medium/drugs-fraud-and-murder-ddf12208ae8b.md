# Drugs, Fraud and Murder
**Date:** 2018-11-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/drugs-fraud-and-murder-ddf12208ae8b
**Subtitle:** Bitcoin is money that is designed for Main St. It was not designed to be the Wall St casino some want, and it certainly was never designed…

## Core thesis
Written days before the BCH/BSV split, this essay attacks Bitcoin ABC/Bitcoin.com's OP_CHECKDATASIG (DSV) proposal, arguing that "permission-less" exchange functionality turns Bitcoin into an illegal bucket shop (an unlicensed betting operation under US law). Craig's conclusion: Bitcoin was designed as honest P2P cash operating within law, and any miner or pool that validates DSV transactions is criminally liable as a facilitator — a liability he claims to be actively packaging for prosecutors in the US, UK and China.

## Key arguments and claims
- Bitcoin "is pseudonymous by design. This allows for privacy and excludes anonymity"; privacy is required for a working legal system, and "the entire concept of 'blockchains' is a system built on law" (contracts need provable consideration and records recoverable across time).
- The push to add "permission-less" exchanges via OP_CHECKDATASIG means, in his words: "Silk road version 2.0", "Bucket shops", "Assassination markets", and "Money laundering, for things like people smuggling and sex slavery" — motivated by "Ethereum (ETH) envy".
- A bucket shop is defined through US legal authority: *Gatewood v. North Carolina*, 203 U.S. 531 (1906) (registration of bets on price movements with no transfer of the underlying), *U.S. v. Sanders*, 696 F. Supp. 327 (N.D. Ill. 1988), Senator Pope's 1936 Congressional Record definition, and 17 C.F.R. § 30.02(d), which expressly forbids "bucketing" in commodity transactions.
- CFTC spokeswoman Erica Elliott Richardson is quoted: releasing code onto a blockchain "does not absolve any entity or individual from complying with pertinent laws" — Craig stresses "The key word above is facilitating."
- Existing Bitcoin script keeps miners safe because "The miner is in effect a common carrier and thus not liable"; but a purpose-designed OP_CODE created to facilitate crime makes miners and especially pools "individually responsible for any and all blocks".
- He claims evidentiary intent: "I have the transcripts, logs, and more that determine beyond doubt that the purpose was to enable betting on-chain without a licence", and says he is "working on a prosecutors handbook".
- Recovery of proceeds is possible under the UK Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 and Chinese criminal/anti-unfair-competition law; he notes China's procuratorate makes charging decisions and that "Confiscation of mining equipment is the easy part".
- DSV-based contracts are "not cash, so it is not fungible"; a licensed-gaming PKI version could exist but would have "no need nor benefit" for being in Bitcoin.
- Personal authority claim: "I used to teach law to police and Feds in Australia… I have already put the charge sheets together as a template for the US, UK, and Chinese authorities."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Legalistic-doctrinal argumentation: he defines the target behaviour through a century of case law and regulation, then maps technical facts (a new opcode) onto the legal category of criminal facilitation. The key distinctions are cash vs. betting instrument, common carrier vs. knowing facilitator, and pseudonymity vs. anonymity. The rhetorical mode is prosecutorial threat mixed with mockery ("stiff bikkies, little girl, cry in a corner"), resting on the meta-principle "Law is law, code is just evidence in law."

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts "permissionlessness" as Bitcoin's core value: "Bitcoin is not, was not, and shall never be 'permission-less'. In fact, nothing you ever create will be."
- Contradicts "code is law" / protocol-governance orthodoxy: code is merely evidence within law, and protocol changes can themselves be criminal acts.
- Contradicts miner-neutrality absolutism: miners/pools are liable once an opcode is purpose-built for crime — the opposite of the "miners just process valid transactions" defence.
- Contradicts the smart-contract expansionism of the era (Ethereum-style expressiveness, later BCH DSV): adding such opcodes is "an attack on what Bitcoin is".

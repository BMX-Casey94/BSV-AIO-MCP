## Problem addressed
The specification wants a cryptographic protocol in which one party can derive a valid private key when a second private key is revealed, and applies that to instant off-line (zero-confirmation) blockchain payments. It says a fixed-r pay-to-public-key-hash one-time signature alone does not give a retailer enough protection if the customer double-spends before confirmation.

## Core mechanism
A prover generates two secp256k1 key pairs and a compensation key ck = sk2 · sk1⁻¹ mod n, plus a non-interactive zero-knowledge proof (Fiat–Shamir transform of a sigma protocol) that ck really links the two public keys, without revealing the private keys. The verifier checks the proof tuple (ck, R, x, z). In the payment scheme the customer first funds a fixed-r P2PK spend output and a time-locked deposit output. At the point of sale the customer signs a purchase from the fixed-r output and gives the retailer the signed transaction, both public keys, ck, and the proof. If a competing spend of the same output appears, the two signatures reveal sk1; the retailer computes sk2 = ck · sk1 and, after the lock time, takes the deposit. If there is no double-spend, the customer reclaims the deposit.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is the prover’s method of building the two key pairs, ck, and the specified sigma/Fiat–Shamir proof and sending them to a verifier. Independent claim 2 is the verifier’s method of checking that proof and, if sk1 is later disclosed, computing sk2 and using it to access assets at PK2.

## BSV relevance
The scheme is specified for Bitcoin scripts (FR-P2PK and OP_CHECKLOCKTIMEVERIFY) and secp256k1. It is relevant to BSV point-of-sale flows that accept unconfirmed payments and need a cryptographic penalty if the same output is signed twice.

# Hoarding and Bitcoin
**Date:** 2018-09-24 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/hoarding-and-bitcoin-b158d465aea6
**Subtitle:** One of the fundamental arguments posed against Bitcoin is that it is deflationary in nature. The misinformed argument made by many…

## Core thesis
Craig attacks the deflationary-hoarding critique of Bitcoin on two flanks. Against "prominent government economists" he argues the concept of hoarding is itself incoherent: holding money is rational saving and risk management, and saving benefits everyone else by cheapening goods. Against the BTC "HODL" culture he then turns the same logic inward: never-spending is "uninformed speculation", because Bitcoin's value derives from on-chain use as cash — "spend and replace" — not from idle balances. The essay concludes that Bitcoin is "P2P electronic cash... with the standard ticker symbol BCH".

## Key arguments and claims
- Money is "a commodity subject to supply and demand like any other"; what distinguishes it is "its use as an exchange medium" — there is no mystical "circulation", only transfers of commodities.
- Holding cash is rational precaution: "In a perfect risk-free world there would be no need to hold any cash balance" — balances exist because the future is uncertain, not because holders are irrational.
- Exhorting spending "to aid the economy" is "a variation of the 'broken window fallacy'. We are consuming capital and not creating it."
- The land analogy: holding bitcoins "is no different to an investment in land. Yet we do not run around calling land holders hoarders" — saving is delayed gratification that signals producers to deliver better goods.
- "The economy is nothing but people": spending choices are votes that "decide the structure of society"; the hoarding accusation is "an argument of paternalism", party A telling party B their choices are wrong — "not informed persuasion but rather deception".
- Printing money does not create wealth: "The amount of money may change but all this does is change the distribution of goods." People care about purchasing power, not nominal balances.
- Saving lowers prices for everyone else: "If one individual saves them money, then all goods become less expensive for all other individuals" — the saver hedges risk and avoids reliance on "government or charitable handouts".
- Protectionism (the Australian car industry example) misallocates capital to "unwanted services" and "is in fact diminishing the economy".
- The HODL fallacy: "There is a fallacy in Bitcoin, that of the HODL. This is not mere saving but uninformed speculation." Bitcoin's value growth "is a Sigmoid S-Curve, it is not exponential", and value comes from use and velocity, not idleness.
- "The error in the BTC-HODL mentality is that they cannot have use. In Bitcoin, use is by nature on-chain. It is not a gateway protocol (such as Lightning, Plasma, or WormHole) but on-chain use that adds to value."
- The prescription is "spend and replace": move savings into Bitcoin, use it anywhere, and thereby convert speculation into genuine saving; teaching people never to spend "is a scam" because it contradicts the white paper's first line.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is Austrian-flavoured monetary reasoning built on analogies and reductios: money-as-commodity, saving-as-land-investment, the broken-window fallacy, and a McDonald's-versus-good-restaurant illustration of subjective time preference. He proceeds by dissolving the opponent's category (there is "no such thing as hoarding", only "differences in the risk appetite of individuals"), then re-applying the dissolved category against his crypto rivals — the same move that clears savers condemns HODLers. Authority is anchored in the white paper's opening sentence rather than in cited economists.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies that a fixed-supply, appreciating asset can be a coherent end-state: "digital gold" is dismissed as "use... as a digital gold" in scare quotes, against BTC's store-of-value thesis.
- Asserts value accrues from on-chain use and velocity, not from holding — directly inverting HODL culture and the "number go up" investment case.
- Names "Lightning, Plasma, or WormHole" as "gateway protocols" that do not add value, rejecting BTC's layer-2 scaling narrative in favour of on-chain cash.
- Sides with the "government economists'" target only to outflank it: he accepts that never-spend behaviour is defective but relocates the defect in BTC culture rather than in deflation itself.
- Closes by claiming the BCH ticker for "Bitcoin... a tokenised P2P electronic cash", asserting the big-block chain's title to the Bitcoin name in the run-up to the November 2018 BCH/BSV split.

## Notable quotes

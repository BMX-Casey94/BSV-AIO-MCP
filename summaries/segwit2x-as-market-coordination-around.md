# SegWit2x as Market Coordination Around Incentives
**Date:** 2026-05-08 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/segwit2x-as-market-coordination-around
**Subtitle:** AKA - rule by bucketshop

## Core thesis
SegWit2x's collapse in November 2017 was not a victory of "nodes" over miners but a focal-point coordination failure among economically exposed actors. Miners follow expected revenue, not ideology; once the 2x chain's claim on the BTC ticker, exchange liquidity and custodial support weakened, miner support collapsed. The episode demonstrates institutional bargaining through commercial chokepoints, not consensus enforcement by passive verifiers.

## Key arguments and claims
- Empirical sequence: the New York Agreement proposed SegWit activation followed by a 2 MB block-size increase; BitMEX reported 56 signatories and roughly 94% of recent blocks signalling NYA support at one stage; the fork was cancelled in November 2017 after sponsors stated it lacked sufficient consensus (The Register). If nodes held a simple technical veto, the episode would have been mechanically trivial — it was not.
- The correct model is a multi-agent coordination game involving miners and pools, exchanges, custodians, payment processors, wallet providers, developers, large holders, media channels, and ordinary users as dispersed price takers.
- Miner objective function: E[Ri] = P(chain accepted) × (subsidy + fees) × market price − costs. Revenue denominated in an economically rejected asset is impaired, so expected commercial acceptance disciplines miners directly.
- A non-mining node's local rejection is "private non-participation", not network enforcement, unless tied to settlement, liquidity, exchange recognition or competing proof-of-work. The question is always whether rejection changes miners' and counterparties' expected payoffs.
- Exchanges are not neutral "economic nodes" but "opaque bucket shops": centralised order books, internal ledgers, synthetic balances, rehypothecation risk, selective listings. Their listing, crediting and ticker decisions are commercial policy — capable of pressuring miners through price and liquidity, but not consensus enforcement.
- Schelling focal-point logic: the BTC ticker, liquidity, exchange continuity and brand identity were the salient equilibria. SegWit2x failed once the market could no longer coordinate around the proposition that the 2x chain would be treated as BTC; "lack of consensus" is "the polite industry phrase for 'the coalition could no longer guarantee the payoff structure'".
- The UASF claim that "users and nodes control Bitcoin" is retrospective theatre: power flowed through chokepoints — exchanges, custodians, software maintainers, mining pools and media narratives — not through millions of private nodes independently enforcing technical law.
- Proposed econometric framework: event studies and structural models of expectation revision, with miner signalling as dependent variable, BTC/B2X futures or IOU spreads, exchange ticker announcements, hash-allocation changes, abnormal returns, volatility spikes and order-book depth as observables. H₀: support unaffected by commercial coordination signals; H₁: support declined as expected liquidity, ticker recognition and exchange acceptance weakened.
- Taxonomy error at the centre of the debate: "A bank deciding not to clear a cheque is not the same as a pen enforcing contract law." The causal agent is the organisation controlling liquidity, custody, settlement, customer access or hashpower — not the software instance.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Coordination game theory (Schelling focal points) combined with an expected-value model of miner behaviour and an econometric event-study framing. The episode is treated as a testable problem of institutional bargaining under uncertainty and asymmetric information, with named empirical sources (BitMEX, The Register) and explicit hypotheses, rather than as moral narrative.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies the "nodes enforce rules" catechism: local refusal without economic weight is economically irrelevant.
- Denies the UASF mythology of sovereign users defeating miners in 2017 — "democratic romance" replacing institutional analysis.
- Reframes exchange and custodian influence as off-chain market discipline, against both Core's node-populism and the vaguer "economic majority" rhetoric.
- Concludes BTC governance is a market-institutional system shaped by miners, exchanges, custodians, developers, media and price-exit by users — "not a libertarian nursery rhyme about sovereign laptops".

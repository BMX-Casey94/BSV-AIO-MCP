# Satoshi and the Sophists
**Date:** 2019-05-23 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/satoshi-and-the-sophists-9c940d4eb22e
**Subtitle:** U.S. Supreme Court Justice Louise Brandeis wrote:

## Core thesis
A short Medium excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net. Framed by a Brandeis quote on publicity as disinfectant, Craig argues Bitcoin was built as a transparency engine — "the most efficient system" for stamping out dishonesty and fraud — and explicitly not as an underground drug currency, a purpose he says E-gold and Liberty Reserve already served better than any blockchain can. His central mechanism claim: any system run by an individual or group has "a head that can be targeted", so privacy systems with visible heads (he names Monero) can be decapitated by enforcement until offenders give up.

## Key arguments and claims
- Opens with Justice Brandeis (the essay writes "Louise Brandeis", sic — Louis D. Brandeis), from *Other People's Money*, ch. V: "Publicity is justly commended as a remedy for social and industrial diseases. Sunlight is said to be the best of disinfectants; electric light the most efficient policeman." Bitcoin is cast as that electric light.
- "Bitcoin is the most efficient system, and will help us to stamp out dishonesty and fraud" — transparency, not anonymity, is the design goal.
- "I did not create Bitcoin to make another underground drug currency." E-gold and Liberty Euro (offered on Liberty Reserve) "both existed and worked well for such a misguided purpose; they do so better than any blockchain ever can."
- Targetability doctrine: "any system run by an individual or a group has a head that can be targeted" — organisational structure, not code, determines legal survivability.
- Hydra metaphor aimed at privacy coins: "If Hydra has heads that are visible, like those of Monero, they can be cut off and the individuals punished."
- Deterrence dynamics: "In time, the pain gets to be enough that those seeking to breach the law and bring destruction move to different pastures" — enforcement displaces crime rather than needing to make it technically impossible.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues from legal-historical authority (Brandeis) and reframes Bitcoin through it: sunlight, not secrecy, is the invention. The core distinction is between headless transparent systems and headed criminal ones; his enforcement model is displacement-through-pain rather than cryptographic immunity. Rhetorically it is a first-person Satoshi testimony ("I did not create Bitcoin to…") fused with a prosecutor's theory of crime.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the censorship-resistance / crypto-anarchist orthodoxy that Bitcoin is a tool against the state: Craig positions it as pro-transparency and pro-law, a system that helps "stamp out" fraud.
- Contradicts the privacy-coin thesis that decentralisation defeats enforcement — he claims visible heads (Monero's developers and operators) simply get cut off.
- Contradicts the Silk Road-era "Bitcoin as drug money" narrative: he says centralised e-money did that job better and that this was never Bitcoin's purpose.

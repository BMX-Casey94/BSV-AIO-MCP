# You Cannot Hoard Your Way to Money
**Date:** 2026-06-09 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/you-cannot-hoard-your-way-to-money
**Subtitle:** The unit of account follows whatever people actually spend. 

## Core thesis
Lawrence White's 1984 result — the unit of account adheres to the general medium of exchange — decides the gold/fiat/Bitcoin contest: monetary status is won only at the payments layer. A capped coin engineered to be held manufactures the purchasing-power volatility that deters transactional use, so the "digital gold" design forecloses the very monetisation it promises. The honest escape, which White concedes but does not endorse, is a payments-first electronic cash — the design intent the author identifies with Bitcoin SV.

## Key arguments and claims
- White (1984, "Competitive Payments Systems and the Unit of Account", AER) refuted the "new monetary economics" (Black, Fama, Hall, Greenfield–Yeager): an abstract numeraire divorced from any traded good "can have no operational significance for market participants". The unit of account emerges wedded to the medium of exchange by a Mengerian process.
- Historical evidence: during the Bank Restriction of 1797–1819 the pound sterling followed circulating Bank of England notes, not its abstract gold definition — the unit followed the medium, not the metal.
- White (2023, Better Money, chs 5–6): opening a wallet "is not the same as accepting or using Bitcoin as a medium of exchange"; rising asset value "is neither the same nor a pathway to monetization".
- Vertical supply: with quantity fixed by code, every demand shift lands entirely on price — White's phrase is "purchasing-power volatility is baked into Bitcoin’s design". BTC volatility ran 3.5–9× that of major fiat, gold or the S&P 500; 30-day volatility averaged ~4.5% (2018–21) versus ~1.2% for gold and 0.5–1.0% for fiat; the price fell from $67,566 (Nov 2021) to $15,709 (Nov 2022).
- The two demands conflict: speculative demand is fickle, transactional demand stable, and attracting appreciation-seekers "conflicts with attracting everyday medium-of-exchange users". Koning's point: the payment niche is kept small by the coin's popularity as a bet. Luther and White (2014) named the disease — "an inelastic supply in the face of volatile demand".
- Usage data: Visa averaged ~564 million retail transactions/day (year to mid-2021); Bitcoin ~250,000 confirmed transactions/day (Dec 2021); miner fees $1.20–$2.70 in early 2022 with over 98% of miner revenue from subsidy; US tax treats each spend as a taxable disposal; El Salvador's legal-tender experiment with $30 pre-loaded wallets was rejected by the public.
- Honest concessions: White's escape clause (volatility would fall if transactional demand supplanted speculative demand); gold's mean-reverting supply (induced output — Klondike, the 1887 cyanide process; the California/Australia rushes produced only ~6.4% annual stock growth 1849–59 and under 1.5% inflation); White ranks gold above Bitcoin; a capped coin implies ~2.1% annual deflation and a possible zero-lower-bound collision; the fiduciary-layer rejoinder (redeemable claims on a capped base) is a genuine open question.
- Rouanet and Hazlett (2023, Public Choice): Cantillon effects as public choice — winners from money creation are concentrated and identifiable (primary dealers; BlackRock's informational rent; Di Maggio et al.'s estimated $600 billion in extra refinancing from MBS purchases), losers diffuse and unidentifiable, so neutral operations "will not emerge". The euro case inverts the rule: identifiable losers (Germany, the north) organised and extracted treaty constraints; Sinn's statistic — 63% of 2017 ECB Council votes held by countries with negative net foreign-investment positions; Lagarde's 2020 "not there to close spreads" remark spiked Italian yields within hours.
- Fiat's entrenchment is political, not efficient: redemption was suspended to finance wars (a "rug pull"); 34 nations financed over a tenth of spending by money creation 1971–90; US CPI hit 9% by mid-2022; Eurozone inflation exceeded 10% in 2022 despite the ECB's price-stability charter — "the last great hope for constitutionally constrained fiat money".
- Lester (1938) supplies the fallacy of composition: individually rational hoarding, generalised, prevents the coin becoming a medium of exchange — "a Gresham-shaped trap in reverse". The author equally rejects Lester's discretionary-state politics via public choice.
- Regression theorem resolved: Patinkin's multiple equilibria (zero always among them), Szabo's critique, and Luther's coordination view show value can be bootstrapped on anticipated payment use — the Mengerian loop Nakamoto described.
- Section 6 (explicitly the author's own conclusion, departing from White): electronic cash optimised for use — fees driven toward zero so micropayments (Nakamoto's stated benchmark) are feasible, throughput scaled to real payment volume, and a spend-not-hoard culture; identified concretely with "Bitcoin SV's insistence on unbounded on-chain scaling and sub-cent fees".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Close, fully sourced synthesis of five named works (Lester 1938; Luther & White 2014; Rouanet & Hazlett 2023; White 1984; White 2023), with explicit labelling of where he departs from White's pro-gold verdict. The machinery is supply-and-demand analysis of monetary demand composition, public-choice theory of discretionary money, and the fallacy of composition applied to "store of value".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- "Digital gold" is a category error: it ranks monies as a portfolio manager ranks assets, not by the offices of money.
- Store-of-value-first is self-defeating: hoarding starves the circulation that drags the unit of account behind it.
- Metric discipline: "Adoption is wallets opened; monetisation is bills paid" — price and market cap measure the speculative demand that is the obstacle.
- The "digital gold" turn is "a later overlay on a design whose stated goal was to be cash".

## Notable quotes
- "Whoever wins the payments layer wins the pricing layer for free — and whoever loses payments cannot win pricing at any price."
- "purchasing-power volatility is baked into Bitcoin’s design" (quoting White)
- "You cannot brand your way to money, you cannot litigate your way to money, and above all you cannot hoard your way to money."
- "You can only be spent into it — by enough people, cheaply enough, often enough, that the prices of the world quietly start being quoted in your units."

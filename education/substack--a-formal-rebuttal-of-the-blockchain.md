# A Formal Rebuttal of the Blockchain Trilemma — core principles

- **Decentralisation is access under rules, not node count.** Anyone may participate under the protocol; that does not make every participant equal in effect or function. Equating decentralisation with headcount and geographic dispersion is a tautology, not a security parameter.
- **Security is economic finality, not a Byzantine monolith.** Bitcoin’s probabilistic finality is economic irreversibility, not absolute agreement among independently validating nodes. Consensus is an objective computational race, not message-passing among trusted parties.
- **Redundant validation has zero marginal utility after a block is mined and accepted.** Scalability is achieved precisely by not having everyone validate everything. Enforcement is governed by the fixed protocol and by miners who follow it.
- **Scalability is bounded by economic propagation and block size, not by consensus.** Under a fixed protocol, all three properties — access to participation, economic finality, and the ability to process a large number of transactions — can be maintained simultaneously.
- **Permissionlessness is rule-bound participation.** Anyone can join under the rules; it is not a claim that every participant is equal in influence. Economic incentives align through proof-of-work.
- **A proof without a falsifiable theorem is not a proof.** Symbolic notation that encodes assumptions as axioms is a semantic shell game. The claimed trade-off does not inherently exist once the terms are defined in their correct domains.

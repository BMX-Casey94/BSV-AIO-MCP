# Fully Peer-to-Peer
**Date:** 2019-06-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/fully-peer-to-peer-18817df99beb
**Subtitle:** In 2011, I sent my last email as Satoshi to Gavin…

## Core thesis
A short excerpt presenting what Craig says was his final email as Satoshi Nakamoto, sent to Gavin Andresen in 2011. Its implied argument: Satoshi's exit was partly a protest against the "mysterious shadowy figure" framing, which he believed fed the press's "pirate currency angle", and publishing the email itself serves as evidence in his Satoshi authorship campaign. The body continues on craigwright.net.

## Key arguments and claims
- The email asks Gavin to stop "talking about me as a mysterious shadowy figure", because "the press just turns that into a pirate currency angle" — the mystique marketing damaged Bitcoin's legitimacy in mainstream media.
- He urges Gavin to "make it about the open source project and give more credit to your dev contributors", arguing that credit "helps motivate them" — Bitcoin should be presented as a collaborative software project, not a cult of personality.
- The act of publication is itself the claim: he presents private correspondence that, on his account, only Satoshi could have sent, offering it as self-authenticating proof of authorship.
- The framing implies Satoshi's departure was a reasoned objection to messaging strategy, not a mysterious disappearance.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Rhetorical mode is primary-source revelation: a single quoted artefact (the email) offered as evidence that authenticates itself through possession. He reframes a foundational mystery of Bitcoin lore as a mundane, documented disagreement over public relations, letting the reader draw the authorship inference.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "Satoshi is unknowable and gone" orthodoxy: Craig presents Satoshi's exit as a traceable, human dispute over messaging — with himself as the author.
- Cuts against the reverential "Satoshi's vision" trope: here Satoshi is a project lead worried about press framing and contributor morale, not a prophet.
- Challenges the "pirate currency"/anti-establishment branding early Bitcoiners embraced — Craig's Satoshi saw that angle as a liability to be managed.

## Notable quotes
- "In 2011, I sent my last email as Satoshi to Gavin…"
- "I wish you wouldn't keep talking about me as a mysterious shadowy figure, the press just turns that into a pirate currency angle."
- "Maybe instead make it about the open source project and give more credit to your dev contributors; it helps motivate them."

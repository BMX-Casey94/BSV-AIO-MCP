# The Warehouse and the Mind — core principles

- **Property answers conflict, and conflict arises only over rivalrous things.** The conflictability criterion is sound. It is right that conflict requires rivalrousness, and wrong that rivalrousness cannot be engineered.
- **Engineered digital scarcity produces genuinely rivalrous, ownable things.** English law has tested the prediction: *AA v Persons Unknown* held a cryptoasset to be property; *Tulip Trading* confirmed it is rivalrous — holding by one person necessarily prevents another from holding that very thing; the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 enacts the third category.
- **The token is the asset; the private key is only information.** Pure information is not property. A private key comprises only information and is not the asset. Rivalrousness separates the token from the pattern.
- **Property describes a relationship between a person and a thing, not the thing itself.** If information were “just arrangements of features of material substrates already owned”, contracts, bank balances, shares, debts and trust interests would all dissolve.
- **Ownership tracks control, not authorship.** The token result does not establish copyright or patent over non-rivalrous patterns, and the law says nothing about who created any system.
- **Property is built, not found.** It is an institutional technology of title, transfer, priority, notice, registration and remedy, designed to secure produced values — the warehouse and the architect who made it worth protecting.

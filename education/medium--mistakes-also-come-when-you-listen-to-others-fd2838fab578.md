# Mistakes Also Come when You Listen to Others… — core principles

- **Bitcoin implements traceable pseudonymity.** Proof-of-work associates nodes (miners) and their facilities — it identifies operators rather than anonymising them.
- **A node is a miner.** The white paper’s node is what is now commonly called a miner. A separate layer sits above those supernode peers, running an SPV connection. User wallets were never nodes.
- **Hash power is only one of six node duties.** Miners do not secure the network using hash power alone. Only step 3 of the white paper’s six network steps involves solving the hash puzzle.
- **The ledger is not promised unchangeable.** The white paper says an alteration quickly becomes computationally impractical for an attacker if honest nodes control a majority of CPU power — not that the blockchain cannot be changed.
- **Honest nodes can reallocate under court order.** When honest nodes agree, they can write a change and reallocate proceeds of crime to another address when freezing or seizing bitcoin.
- **Mining aggregates into data-centre companies.** Even an ASIC-proof proof-of-work would lead to large corporate entities competing to validate transactions into blocks.
- **A 51% attack is a crime.** Consensus attacks violate statutes such as the US Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, with similar laws in most other countries.

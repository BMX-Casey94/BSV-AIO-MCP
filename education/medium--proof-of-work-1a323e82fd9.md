# Proof of Work — core principles

- **A key is not identity.** Keys can be shared, stolen, copied or assigned; there is no such thing as cryptographic non-repudiation. In court a key must be attested to — the key itself does not prove who acted.
- **Bitcoin is pseudonymous and cannot be made anonymous.** The novelty is the knife-edge of lawful privacy: transactions that stay private in ordinary use and leave an immutable, court-admissible evidence trail. Anonymous cash is a different and rejected design.
- **Anonymous money destroys privacy.** Froomkin's 1997 paper on the unintended consequences of eCash: regulators will not legalise anonymous cash; anonymity invites total profiling, and courts can tie anonymous speech to money-laundering. If you want privacy, the last thing you want is anonymous money.
- **eCash failed because of anonymity.** Chaum's system was regularly traced and recovered despite having no audit trail. Bitcoin's undeletable trail stops double-book frauds and changes the face of accounting.
- **It is a protocol war.** Economics produce either one strong global system or many weak, fragmenting systems that die. Only one blockchain survives, as with the Internet before it.
- **nLockTime is a timed, evidential release.** The original time-lock lets information be released at a proven future moment, pseudonymously, as part of the evidence trail — a wallet-held construct, not a new opcode.
- **Code does not create law.** Cryptographic constructions do not confer legal non-repudiation or confiscation-immunity; tracing and recovery remain possible under law.
- **Decentralisation is a tool, not a virtue.** Taken too far it kills its host. Nodes are validating entities that may fill a data centre; long-term the set is hundreds to a thousand commercial miners, including governments and banks protecting their interests.

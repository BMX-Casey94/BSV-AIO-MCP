# Bitcoin is not Anti State — it is Pro Honest government
**Date:** 2018-11-09 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-is-not-anti-state-it-is-pro-honest-government-1ab1ec0a9fba
**Subtitle:** Bitcoin is an open immutable ledger. It is not anonymous, it is private. There is a huge difference and it only works when it is not…

## Core thesis
Bitcoin is not an anarchist or anti-state tool; it is a technology for honest government because it is an open, immutable ledger that is private but never anonymous. Privacy, properly understood, requires traceability at a cost, and fungible money requires records and audit trails. Bitcoin therefore makes government, the press, and taxation more transparent, accountable, and fair — "Bitcoin is not anarchist; it is about freedom."

## Key arguments and claims
- The central distinction: "It is not anonymous, it is private." Privacy "requires traceability" — the ability to access and validate a transaction — and money only works when it is not anonymous.
- Fungibility depends on records, not cryptography: "To be fungible, money needs to be linked to records — even cash is; we have invoices and audit trails."
- Bitcoin permits investigation "but only at a cost", with a proper audit trail — and this applies symmetrically to users, government, and corporations, not just the "community".
- Free press: Bitcoin enables data to be written permanently (he cites Memo.cash), creating a free press — but "A free press is accountable" and therefore "CANNOT be anonymous". Anonymous trolling is "a means to censor others, to attack and drown them out".
- Reddit defamation critique: people claim a right to "make up false claims, to defame others"; defamation is "using force, it is an attack", and those who censor others with lies "must be held accountable".
- Tax: Bitcoin "allows government to tax more easily... it makes it fair" — a consumption tax (VAT/GST) applied equally at spend, while investment capital is left untouched. Merchants can "instantly remit tax to the government, with no forms", no intrusive audits, and no large enforcement bureaucracy.
- Inversion of the anonymity narrative: "anonymous systems only favour the rich, the powerful, and government". Bitcoin "brings transparency into the world", lets "honest governments compete fairly on open playing fields, and stops corruption and fraud".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues by conceptual inversion and distinction-drawing: splitting "private" from "anonymous", then redefining freedom as accountability rather than concealment. The framework is rule-of-law liberalism rather than cypherpunk — fungibility and liberty are treated as products of auditability and legal process. The rhetoric is aphoristic and assertive ("A free press is accountable"), moving from a definitional premise to sweeping political conclusions without empirical evidence.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the cypherpunk/crypto-anarchist reading of Bitcoin as anti-state money: Craig explicitly says Bitcoin is "not anarchist" and makes government taxation easier.
- Contradicts the privacy-enhancement orthodoxy (mixers, anonymity sets, privacy coins): he claims anonymity "only favour[s] the rich, the powerful, and government" and that money "only works when it is not anonymous".
- Contradicts the "censorship resistance means anyone can transact/speak without recourse" ethos: he frames untraceable speech as force and censorship, demanding traceable accountability — the opposite of the Reddit/r/btc free-speech-absolutist culture he names.
- Era context: posted six days before the BCH/BSV split (15 Nov 2018), this positions his camp as the law-and-order side against the "anonymous" wing of Bitcoin Cash.

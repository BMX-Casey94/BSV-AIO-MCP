# The Forked Illusion: How Both Sides Cannot Be Right About Bitcoin—and Why Both Are Exposed by Their Own Logic
**Date:** 2025-12-07T01:56:49.349Z | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-forked-illusion-how-both-sides
**Subtitle:** An Examination of the Contradictions in the Mythology of Satoshi, the Gospel of BTC, and the Intellectual Bankruptcy of Both Their Selective Beliefs

## Core thesis
BTC discourse is built on an "impossible duality": Satoshi is declared irrelevant whenever his architecture condemns BTC's deviations, yet resurrected as scripture whenever a fragment of his work — the 21 million cap, the halving schedule — flatters the mythology. Wright prosecutes a logical fork: if Satoshi was right, BTC is a betrayal of the design; if he was wrong, BTC's clinging to his monetary fragments is farcical. Either way the ideology collapses, and it survives only by refusing to acknowledge the contradiction.

## Key arguments and claims
- Section I frames the duality: "Satoshi must be dismissed to permit deviation, yet exalted to preserve myth." The whitepaper may be treated as "an irrelevant historical artefact", yet a single sentence about "21 million" is elevated to "a cosmic decree" — selective reverence as "the hallmark of a religion, not a protocol".
- Section II ("If Satoshi Was Right") enumerates seven design commitments that annihilate BTC's model: (1) Bitcoin as a scalable distributed timestamp server — the whitepaper's own definition, and "a timestamp server that cannot timestamp at scale is a broken machine"; (2) fees that diminish with volume rather than expand, making dollars-high congestion fees "a grotesque inversion of the intended economics"; (3) direct IP-to-IP exchange, replaced by a "crippled topology" of relay groups, gossip layers and gatekeeping infrastructure; (4) no artificial block limits — scaling left to hardware progression and miner competition, rendering small-block dogma "economic vandalism"; (5) tokenised fiat, tokenised gold and real-world asset representation, abandoned for a "barren 'digital gold' narrative"; (6) micropayments and global everyday access, annihilated by the "settlement layer" reframing; (7) verification through economic structure — competing block-producing nodes — replaced by passive archival "full nodes", "a semantic farce".
- Section III ("If Satoshi Was Wrong") presses the opposite horn: a movement that truly believed the designer fallible would re-evaluate everything, yet BTC keeps the halving cycle and emission curve "as though it were handed down from Sinai" while amputating the architecture. It retains the timestamping mechanism "because they cannot function without it, while simultaneously insisting it does not matter", and rejects scaling without building any superior alternative — freezing the system "in a diminished form" while pretending Satoshi never intended scale, "a claim contradicted by his own writings at every turn".
- Section IV names the two-part doctrine: "(1) Satoshi does not matter, and (2) Satoshi proves we are right." Invoking the designer's ghost to bless fragments while burying the design is "intellectual necromancy"; "BTC is a religion that denies it is religious."
- Section V dissolves the dichotomy itself: the protocol "was meant to function" as "global cryptographic timestamping infrastructure capable of anchoring truth, not a museum piece embalmed in scarcity rhetoric". The architecture stands on its own — "testable, observable, falsifiable" — requiring neither Satoshi's infallibility nor his irrelevance, only fidelity to a design of large blocks, low fees, competitive nodes and broad timestamping of "money but data, contracts, and knowledge".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
A dilemmatic reductio: he constructs a fork (Satoshi right / Satoshi wrong) and shows both horns impale BTC, then argues the dichotomy itself is a deliberate distraction sustained by those who "benefit from permanent confusion". The method rests on textual exegesis of the whitepaper and Satoshi's emails read as a coherent engineering specification, overlaid with a sociology-of-religion frame (dogma, liturgy, priesthood, selective canon) to explain how an incoherent position persists socially.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Small-block orthodoxy: the block size limit is "the mutilation of the system's core purpose" — a timestamp server "must accept input, not restrict it".
- Fee-market economics: BTC's high-fee model inverts the design; the intended engine shifts from subsidy towards volume-driven low fees.
- "Digital gold": a provincial retreat from tokenisation and from Bitcoin as a programmable ledger "capable of representing anything that can be hashed, signed, and verified".
- Full-node ideology: archival replication by passive observers is a "performative misunderstanding"; validation belongs to competing block producers.
- "Code is law" / "Satoshi doesn't matter": shown to be applied selectively — discarded for architecture, invoked for the supply schedule.

## Notable quotes
- "The movement cannot escape the contradiction because it is the contradiction."
- "This is not engineering. It is liturgy. It is not innovation. It is doctrinal editing."
- "If Satoshi is irrelevant, why worship his fragments? And if he is indispensable, why discard his design?"
- "Its essence lies not in scarcity, but in integrity; not in hoarding, but in verification; not in self-imposed limits, but in scalability; not in myth, but in mechanism."

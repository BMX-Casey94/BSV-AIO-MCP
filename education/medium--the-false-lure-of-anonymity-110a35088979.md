# The false lure of anonymity — core principles

- **Privacy is not anonymity.** Private transactions involve identified parties and leave traceable records; anonymity is a different property and a different legal object. Bitcoin was designed to ensure privacy and to destroy anonymity.
- **Anonymous systems cannot scale.** DigiCash's eCash was more anonymous than later privacy coins and disappeared; an anonymous network is at best a black-market methodology and will never be the world's money.
- **Decentralisation is competition among commercial miners.** Any blockchain collapses into a small-world network of large, stationary, lawful businesses. Nodes are miners; there is no such thing as a full node that does not mine blocks. Users do not run the servers, any more than they run their own SMTP.
- **Home-node meshes are a subversion vector.** A mesh of 11–30 hops lets an attacker with a few thousand pounds of hardware control two or three hops and create unbounded forks or double-spends. Security requires miner network distance D<3 — hop count always under three.
- **Propagation, not discovery, is the race.** Bitcoin is not about finding a block first; it is about distributing a found block. That race consolidates miners into large, well-connected facilities.
- **Anonymous coins fail at the exchange and the node.** Bearer-instrument law and licensing already capture exchanges; commercial miners cannot hide (they cannot operate through Tor) and form an ultra-small-world map of known addresses. If a state criminalises the coin, ISPs can filter the local jurisdictional node.
- **Milliseconds at the core secure transactions.** At scale it is not the ten-minute block time but millisecond propagation among a mandala-network centre that settles. Proof of stake fails to form that small-world consensus and is simple to attack.
- **Developer collectives are enterprises.** Groups acting together without a corporate form are unlimited-liability partnerships under law; 'decentralisation' is not a legal shield.
- **One public ledger, lawful contracts.** Nodes replace the settlement third party for Western and Islamic-finance contract forms alike, but everybody is not a bank in Bitcoin. If the law is disliked, lobby parliament — rebellion is not a protocol feature.

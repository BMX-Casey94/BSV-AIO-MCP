# Peer-to-peer digital electronic cash — core principles

- **Bitcoin is not a cryptocurrency.** The white paper calls it "a peer-to-peer electronic cash system" designed because "no mechanism exists to make payments over a communications channel without a trusted party."
- **Bitcoin is not a currency — yet it could be.** Black's Law Dictionary defines currency as coined money and authorised paper circulating as a medium of exchange. Bitcoin fails that definition at present. Tokenisation methods allow the creation of a national currency on top of Bitcoin; "we have the capability to securely tokenise currency offerings on top of Bitcoin."
- **Peers are people, not nodes.** "It is not because nodes act as peers, but rather individuals do. When Alice and Bob exchange consideration using Bitcoin, Alice sends a transaction to Bob that he can send to the network to be settled... The peer-to-peer process here happens between Alice and Bob, and does not involve the network other than settlement."
- **ECDSA is a signature, not encryption.** "Cryptography simply means secret writing... Bitcoin is not secret." A digital signature "provides a means to validate the authenticity and integrity of any data. It does not provide confidentiality — which is encryption." Public-private key schemes in ECDSA and ECC share the same mathematics and are interchangeable; "Based on does not mean is." Bitcoin is sent in clear text.
- **A set protocol is required for decentralisation.** Industry definitions that call cryptocurrencies decentralised fail when "a small group of developers" can alter the protocol. A system whose rules can be changed is not decentralised.
- **Identity linkage is already law.** The EU's Fifth Money Laundering Directive extends obliged-entity status to virtual-currency exchange platforms and provides that national FIUs "should be able to obtain information allowing them to associate virtual currency addresses to the identity of the owner."

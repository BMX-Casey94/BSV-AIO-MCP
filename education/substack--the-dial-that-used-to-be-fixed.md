# The Dial That Used to Be Fixed — core principles

- **A protocol is a dial.** Every prior money inherited its position on the identifiability axis from its material. A digital asset is the first money-like instrument whose position is set by protocol — turned by hand, not by physics.
- **A public historical ledger is not cash.** Cash keeps no record and reveals no identity. A token that records every transfer against a persistent identifier, while detaching legal identity, occupies a coordinate no physical asset could: recorded enough to be traced, nameless enough to be unrecoverable.
- **Private-key authentication proves possession of a key, not who the sender is.** Pseudonymity is not anonymity. The store-of-value form is the payment system for strangers; an account-based record without an account-keeper is a novel interior coordinate, not the cash endpoint.
- **Consensus makes undetected modification costly, not impossible.** Settlement is probabilistic, subject to fork and reorganisation. A payment in an orphaned block did not, in the end, happen. Genuine finality is a legal commitment — real-time gross settlement bought it with law and central-bank money, not consensus.
- **Any positive per-transaction cost that does not shrink with value evicts the smallest payments first.** Bounded capacity plus rising fees makes the eviction automatic. The cheap casual payment is the one function that disguises a public ledger as cash; price it out and the asset migrates to high-value, low-frequency transfers.
- **Name the coordinate and accept its bundle.** Stop claiming the cash endpoint. Locate recourse at the identity-bearing edges. Transferable debt, stablecoins and issuer-backed claims sit in the engineered middle of the axis by construction.

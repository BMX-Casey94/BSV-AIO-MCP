# The Price of Being in the Room — core principles

- **The mere possibility of revision redirects engineering into influence.** Once firms have sunk investment against a rule, a circulating draft is enough: staff attend the working group, a budget line appears, and the firm shifts from building to managing exposure.
- **Adaptation destroys value; displacement moves it.** Rewriting code, requalifying hardware and running parallel versions are real social losses. Loss of market position to a competitor or to the party that made the change is a transfer. Collapsing the two into one "cost of revision" confuses the area.
- **Standing is a purchased good, and pure waste.** Delegates, membership tiers with early draft access and cultivated relationships do not improve any product; they only shape drafts to do less damage. Representation works even when everyone in the room is behaving impeccably.
- **The four-part bill is forgone projects, adaptation, standing and the cost of effecting the change.** The transfer of position is not a social loss, yet it drives everything. What moves the burden is the rule-setter's gain from revising and the cost of effecting revision — procedural and appropriative variables, not technical ones.
- **Gentler migration paths are not a substitute for governance.** As changes become more damaging, the rule-setter revises less often while each remaining builder's exposure rises; the effects largely cancel. Better paths reduce damage per event but raise frequency.
- **More consequential rule changes hurt designers of small systems and help large ones.** A small builder's burden is mostly adaptation, which rises with severity; a large builder's is mostly displacement, which falls as revision frequency falls. Large firms can also afford representation. Mutability concentrates power by arithmetic, not favouritism.
- **In member-owned committees, compensation cannot stop the representation arms race.** Participation determines the share of gains, so standing becomes an investment rather than insurance. The remaining remedy is constitutional: remove the revision margin entirely — a fixed base with nothing left to govern.
- **Whether a rule should be changeable is a constitutional choice about gains, costs and where the proceeds go.** Bitcoin's original fixed design is exactly such a choice, made at setup.

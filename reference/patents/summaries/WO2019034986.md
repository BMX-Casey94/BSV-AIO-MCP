## Problem addressed
Control of a blockchain asset depends on a private key that signs spends of a UTXO. A single node or dealer that knows the full private key is a single point of failure. The specification wants a dealerless way for a group of nodes to generate a public–private key pair such that no node can reconstruct the private key, while a threshold of nodes can produce a standard ECDSA signature.

## Core mechanism
Each node picks a degree-(k−1) polynomial with its secret as the free term and privately sends evaluations to the others, plus elliptic-curve point multiplications of those values (and optionally of coefficients) so shares can be checked. A node’s private-key share is the sum of received evaluations; the group public key is obtained by interpolating the published curve points. Shares are then refreshed by adding a joint-zero secret share so the private key stays the same while shares change. To sign, nodes share double-masked ephemeral-key shares and multiplicative-mask curve points, interpolate to form the ECDSA r component, then reveal additively masked s-shares that interpolate to s.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
The independent method, performed by a first node, exchanges polynomial evaluations and their elliptic-curve point multiplications with other nodes, forms a private-key share as the sum of received secrets, and interpolates the public key from the published points. It is characterised by also receiving additively and multiplicatively masked ephemeral-key shares, and by modifying the private-key share with a dealerless joint-zero secret share. An independent device claim covers a node that runs that method for a blockchain transaction.

## BSV relevance
The output is a conventional ECDSA signature over a Bitcoin-compatible curve, so a threshold vault can spend standard BSV P2PKH or P2PK outputs without changing consensus rules.

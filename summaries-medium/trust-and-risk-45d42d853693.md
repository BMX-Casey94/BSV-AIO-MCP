# Trust and Risk
**Date:** 2018-09-11 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/trust-and-risk-45d42d853693

## Core thesis
Security exists not to eliminate risk — impossible — but to enable trust, and risk is always quantifiable; those who claim otherwise are protecting themselves from measurement. Applied to Bitcoin: zero-confirmation transactions are "secure enough" whenever the cost of a double-spend attack dwarfs the value at risk, which Craig argues holds for everyday cash purchases. The essay is a compact defence of 0-conf as the economic foundation of Bitcoin-as-cash.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Security matters, but not so we can eliminate all risk, but so we can have trust. Even if we could eliminate nearly all risk (we cannot ever remove risk entirely) we would have to ask whether it was worth it to do so."
- "Risk IS quantifiable" — in any field; the live questions are the reliability, precision and accuracy of the measurement.
- Defines the trio: reliability is repeatability within predictable bounds; precision is variance around the mark (you can be imprecise yet centred on the mean); accuracy is closeness to the bulls-eye. Good risk measurement needs both precision and accuracy, reproducible by others.
- Qualitative/"art" risk assessment fails empirically: "when we look at the results of how risk comes out over time, we see that the art based approach does not work well"; measurement "hold[s] those making predictions to account".
- Debunk of gatekeeping: "Next time somebody states to you that risk cannot be measured, remember it is. Think instead what they are telling you is that they do not want to have their ability tested in case they come up short."
- On 0-conf: "No system is completely secure. So, the question is, whether it is secure enough. Is the risk of a 0-conf transaction worth the cost."
- The economic test: "it is likely to cost over 10,000 USD in value to attempt to double-spend a transactions where the standard fees have been applied. So, why would anyone do this to try and steal a $4.00 USD coffee?"
- High-value counter-example: buying a $40,000 car, the paperwork and handover never complete within 20 minutes anyway — so waiting for confirmations imposes no real cost on large purchases.
- Conclusion: "Bitcoin is first and foremost Cash!"

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Metrology first: he imports the reliability/precision/accuracy distinction from measurement science to make "risk" a testable quantity and to recast opponents as refusing accountability. Then a rational-attacker cost-benefit ratio — attack cost versus value at risk — becomes the operative security metric, replacing absolute safety with economic sufficiency. The mode is Socratic-debunking: pose the objection ("risk cannot be measured", "0-conf is not secure"), then invert it.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly attacks the BTC orthodoxy that 0-conf is unsafe — the position used to justify waiting for multiple confirmations, Replace-By-Fee, and ultimately the "Bitcoin isn't for buying coffee" retreat from the cash use case.
- On BTC, full blocks and RBF deliberately made 0-conf unreliable; writing in the BCH camp in September 2018, Craig argues 0-conf is economically secure for small values, restoring the merchant-payment scenario BTC abandoned.
- Contradicts the maximalist security posture ("wait 6 confirmations") by making confirmation depth a priced risk decision: for large value, real-world settlement friction already exceeds a block interval.
- The "$4 coffee" framing is a micropayments-and-cash claim: Bitcoin's design centre is small everyday payments, not a settlement layer for large transfers only.

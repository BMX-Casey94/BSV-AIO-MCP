# True Sale and Insolvency Challenges in ICO Token Sales — core principles

- **A token sale must be a true sale, or it is a security.** Under English securitisation law the purported sale can be re-characterised as a loan secured by a mortgage of receivables — a charge void for want of registration.
- **Sale versus mortgage is a substance test.** In a sale the vendor cannot recover the subject by returning the price and the purchaser keeps profit or bears loss; a mortgagor can redeem, and the mortgagee accounts for surplus and can chase a shortfall.
- **The whole documentary matrix is the contract.** Courts read the whitepaper, website, conditions and offer material together; any term inconsistent with a sale of goods or services undermines a utility characterisation.
- **Fundraising intent makes the sale a sham.** Where the stated purpose is to raise capital rather than deliver a sale, the token is an unregistered security that ought to have been registered under the Companies Act.
- **Promotion for exchange use rejects the utility claim.** A token touted for trading, or a firm that pivots after the raise, is not a sale of goods or services.
- **Undervalue and creditor-defrauding challenges remain.** A later market drop, deferred consideration and profit-extraction devices can be attacked under the Insolvency Act; purchasers themselves may have standing.
- **Ordinary companies and insolvency law already reach token sales.** Offshore wrappers and “no issuer” claims do not extinguish registration, sham or antecedent-transaction analysis.

# A story of apples
**Date:** 2020-01-03 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/a-story-of-apples-b02c5d98a8d4
**Subtitle:** It is said that the apple doesn't fall far from the tree. Well, Ira Kleiman and David Kleiman are not related genetically.

## Core thesis
A polemic against Ira Kleiman, the litigant brother of the late Dave Kleiman, written at the opening of 2020 — the year the Kleiman v. Wright trial was expected. Craig argues that Ira (whom he stresses is Dave's adopted, not genetic, brother) is a greedy opportunist who tried to liquidate Craig's Australian companies, dodge estate tax, and seize money he was never owed, and that the coming trial will expose him. He insists Dave was his friend, that he voluntarily gifted Dave shares, and that W&K Info Defense "wasn't about bitcoin".

## Key arguments and claims
- "One week before David died, I had added Dave to the share registry over company I had in Australia" — not because Dave invested, but because Craig "wanted to have him involved"; W&K Info Defense "came about" to involve Dave in his work, and "wasn't about bitcoin though".
- Dave's father, not Craig, added Ira to the email threads after Dave's death; Craig claims he "spent time in finding Dave's relatives" and then "sought to transfer the shares" he had gifted Dave.
- Ira "wanted to have money and not shares" in a private company Craig "was not interested in selling publicly", and "didn't want to pay tax" — Craig alleges Ira tried to get him to "hide his ownership and shares" to avoid estate tax.
- Ira allegedly "worked with a number of people who had been former staff members" who "had a potential deal to sell my intellectual property", "helped give false information to the Australian tax office", and "lied in order to try and ensure that the companies would be liquidated".
- Ira "was offered money for his shares. Many millions of dollars. But Ira was greedy and wanted more."
- "W and K is not Ira's and is not more than 33% owned by Ira's estate in any view of the world" — a direct stake-in-the-ground claim about the central asset of the lawsuit.
- Ira is "just a useful fool to other people who have played him"; his funders "will never see a cent from the strategy he wants".
- The post closes by tying the litigation to his protocol war: "It is time to learn just how rotten both Ira and Core are."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
This is adversarial, first-person litigation narrative rather than technical argument: character evidence ("one of the greediest people I have ever met", "an idiot and a lowlife"), a reconstructed timeline of the share registry and company structures, and pre-emptive framing of the trial as revelation ("This is 2020 and this year the truth starts to come out"). The rhetorical mode is confession-adjacent self-justification — he stresses his own voluntary generosity to Dave ("I could easily have neglected this duty... but he was my friend") to invert the plaintiff's narrative of partnership theft.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The mainstream crypto-media framing of Kleiman v. Wright cast Ira as the wronged party exposing Craig's false Satoshi claim; Craig inverts it entirely — Ira is the fraudster "creating false documents", and the suit is a "get rich quick" scam backed by third-party funders.
- His claim that W&K "wasn't about bitcoin" and is at most 33% estate-owned directly contradicts the plaintiff's (and much of the community's) assumption that W&K was the vehicle of a Satoshi-era Bitcoin partnership.
- The closing equation "both Ira and Core" collapses the lawsuit and Bitcoin Core into a single corrupt enemy — contradicting the BTC community's separation of the Kleiman case from protocol politics.

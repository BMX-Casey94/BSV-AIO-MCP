## Problem addressed
The specification concerns payment and distribution of a series of digital content (digital rights management for online media is named as an application). It wants a first user to obtain successive episodes without relying on certificate authorities, while limiting each party’s exposure if the other fails to perform, and without locking the full series price into an immediate on-chain payment for every episode.

## Core mechanism
The first user deposits cryptocurrency to a common P2SH address that needs both users’ signatures to spend. Both also co-sign a time-locked refund of that deposit back to the first user. For each requested episode the first node builds a payment transaction from the common address for a quantity based on how many episodes have been requested, signs it, and sends it to the second node. After the second node checks the amount, it sends the episode (optionally with an episode-specific decryption key) but defers co-signing and broadcasting the payment until near the refund deadline, replacing earlier payment transactions with a later one that covers more episodes. If nothing is spent before the lock time, the deposit returns. Communications may be encrypted with a symmetric key from a shared common secret derived from master keys and a generator value.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method in which the first node requests an episode, builds and signs a payment from the two-signature deposit address, and, after the second node verifies the payment, receives the episode; the deposit is refunded after a specified time unless a co-signed payment is sent first, and the second node defers that broadcast until closer to the refund time.

## BSV relevance
The embodiment is a Bitcoin-style 2-of-2 P2SH deposit, time-locked refund, and deferred payment broadcast for metered content. That pattern is relevant to BSV applications that sell sequential digital goods against a UTXO deposit.

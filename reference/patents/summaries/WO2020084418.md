## Problem addressed
The specification says existing secret-sharing and threshold-signature processes do not scale well because private keys sit in poorly organised structures. It seeks a more efficiently packed, non-linear way to distribute shares of digitally signed data (in particular a signed blockchain transaction) so that a threshold of participants can recover the signature while the secret is harder for outsiders to detect.

## Core mechanism
A message hash is signed with ECDSA to produce (r, s). Those values are mapped to points on an elliptic curve shared by the participants. Two encryption layers are then applied in configurable order: a knapsack step that multiplies parts of its input by terms of a series known to the participants (Stern series, powers of a shared-message term, or a super-increasing series), and an ECC encryption under a participant public key (Koblitz-style). Shamir (or similar) secret sharing then splits the result so that a threshold of shares reconstructs the input and fewer shares do not. Optional fragmentation of (R, S) before the knapsack is described. The packed, disguised shares can be sent via an escrow that forwards without learning the secret. Decryption reverses knapsack then ECC (or the chosen order) to recover the signature or the message.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method that maps inputs to elliptic-curve points, encrypts those points under an ECC public key, multiplies parts of an input by terms of a series known to the participants (including powers of a shared-message term), and distributes threshold shares of an input to the sharing step. Independent claims 14 and 15 cover a computer system and a non-transitory medium that perform the method.

## BSV relevance
The specification treats the shared secret as a digitally signed Bitcoin transaction and uses ECC plus threshold sharing so a group can recover or verify that signature. That is relevant to BSV multi-party signing and escrowed share distribution.

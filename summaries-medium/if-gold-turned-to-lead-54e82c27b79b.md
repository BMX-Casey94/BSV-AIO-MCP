# If Gold Turned to Lead
**Date:** 2019-10-21 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/if-gold-turned-to-lead-54e82c27b79b
**Subtitle:** “Imagine if gold turned to lead when stolen. If the thief gives it back, it turns to gold again…”

## Core thesis
An excerpt — the body continues on craigwright.net. Bitcoin is "an immutable evidence trail" designed so that crime does not pay: tracing is built in, the August 2010 alert key was a prototype asset-freezing mechanism, and possession of a key does not constitute ownership — courts can and will order seizure. His own 2010 bitcointalk "kill switch" thought experiment (stolen gold turning to lead until returned) is presented as the design's animating idea.

## Key arguments and claims
- Opens by quoting his bitcointalk post (topic 750, msg8649): "Imagine if gold turned to lead when stolen. If the thief gives it back, it turns to gold again…" — a remote kill switch making stolen goods useless.
- "Bitcoin is an immutable evidence trail. It is not about taking down governments." The recent takedown of "one of the largest child pornography rings in history" (the Welcome to Video operation, October 2019) happened "because of Bitcoin".
- Lineage: Bitcoin is "unlike e-gold, differing from DigiCash's eCash, and unrelated to Liberty Reserve"; "It's not even in the top 10 original peer-to-peer systems" — its novelty is "a key aspect of money that everyone else ignored: tracing".
- "There was never any goal to create a decentralised cryptocurrency that is outside the reach of governments. The entire anarchist catchcry is inimical to the purpose of honest money."
- Nodes "are not there to deliver decentralisation"; the system is distributed for robustness ("when a company fails, Bitcoin doesn't"), and mining professionalises into data-centre companies that "come under the control of law and government" — "there is no way to create a system like Bitcoin that does not eventually end in a way that is constrained by law".
- The August 2010 alert key/alert system followed "the introduction of people seeking to make an online heroin store"; it "allowed Bitcoin to have a freezing system" under which nodes could act "on the receipt of a validly issued judicial order" to "freeze assets globally. Such capability will always remain in Bitcoin."
- "A key does not demonstrate ownership, and it never will"; the "keep your private keys so no one can control your bitcoin" narrative is "the stupidest thing ever touted".
- Prophecy: "In time, nearly every criminal that has ever used Bitcoin will be traced"; "any criminal who has received bitcoin has left a record that is admissible in court"; freezing orders and proceeds-of-crime cases will link bitcoin and tokenised assets to crime.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Autobiographical design-intent claims (the 2010 alert key, the bitcointalk post) serve as evidence of purpose; property-law theory (possession is not ownership) does the normative work; and a current law-enforcement event — the Welcome to Video takedown — is deployed as empirical vindication. The rhetorical mode is admonitory: a warning both to criminals and to those in BTC "protecting a sinking ship".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against "not your keys, not your coins": keys do not demonstrate ownership, and seizure by court order is a designed-in capability, not an attack.
- Against censorship-resistance maximalism: freezing assets on a valid judicial order "will always remain in Bitcoin".
- Against anonymity and fungibility ideals: Bitcoin is "private but is not anonymous"; the permanent evidence trail is the point of the system.
- Against decentralisation as the terminal goal: distribution is mere robustness, and mining must professionalise into law-bound companies.

# Pennies and Power: How Micropayments Could Break the Corporate Siege
**Date:** 2025-08-09 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/pennies-and-power-how-micropayments
**Subtitle:** Why the smallest transactions may be the biggest threat to Silicon Valley’s monopoly machine.

## Core thesis
Frictionless micropayments are not a feature but an insurgency. By destroying the artificial transaction floor and enabling direct, permissionless payment at fractions of a cent, they bypass platform tollbooths, defund the ad-surveillance model, dissolve subscription lock-in, and restore contestable capitalism — which is precisely why corporatism will fight to keep the moat filled.

## Key arguments and claims
- Capitalism versus corporatism: capitalism rests on contestability and open entry; corporatism on moats — compliance costs, minimum transaction fees, bundling requirements, and gatekeeper control over audience access.
- The transaction floor is a commercial artefact, not a technical necessity: card networks were built for high-value, low-frequency payments, and a flat $0.25 processor fee on a one-cent payment "is not offering a service — it’s killing the transaction outright".
- Directness: platforms commonly take "30% or more" of what a creator's work earns; micropayments settle buyer-to-seller in real time with negligible fees and no eligibility gatekeeping.
- Scale arithmetic: 0.5 cents per article read multiplied by a million readers yields $5,000 — earned without advertising, subscriptions, or surrendering user privacy to a data broker.
- Granular pricing: an article at 2 cents, a song stream at 5 cents, a code-snippet licence at 0.8 cents; the price mechanism finally works "at the granularity of individual interactions".
- Ad-surveillance unravels: when producers are paid directly there is no need to monetise behavioural dossiers, and the tyranny of "growth at all costs" gives way to the older, more honest metric of repeat custom.
- Lock-in neutralised: subscription gravity (near-zero marginal cost of consuming more within a bundle) freezes out rivals; pay-per-use makes switching painless and the lock-in moat "evaporates".
- Historical analogy: as affordable printing broke the Catholic Church's monopoly over knowledge and powered the Reformation, "Micropayments are the printing press of the digital economy", eroding the platform's monetisation monopoly.
- Obstacles are structural: Visa/Mastercard/PayPal fee models; one-size-fits-all AML/KYC treating $0.02 like $200,000; app-store minimum prices protecting the 30% cut; incumbent lobbying; the chicken-and-egg adoption problem.
- Remedies: high-throughput, low-fee blockchain rails (with payment channels batching settlement); tiered compliance with customs-style de minimis thresholds; and a cultural shift from "free" to "pay for what you value".
- Epilogue verdict on Lightning: a "Rube Goldberg contraption" — an off-chain credit network requiring watchtowers, pooled liquidity and centralising custodial hubs; "You get promises to pay, not payments."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Applied price theory and industrial organisation. Moats, contestability, lock-in and incentive alignment are analysed as mechanisms, then illustrated with concrete arithmetic and a historical analogy (Gutenberg). The organising dichotomy — capitalism (open entry, voluntary exchange) versus corporatism (rent extraction through gatekeeping) — supplies both the analytical and the moral frame.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Frontal assault on the Lightning Network, BTC's scaling orthodoxy: a "shadow banking layer" that recentralises into well-capitalised hubs and reintroduces trusted intermediaries.
- Rejects off-chain settlement as settlement at all: without the immutable, timestamped record there are only promises; true micropayments demand on-chain finality at fraction-of-a-cent fees.
- Repudiates the high-fee, low-throughput BTC design premise: billions of tiny transactions must be routine on the base chain, not exiled to a second layer optimised for capital warehousing.

## Notable quotes
- "Micropayments are not a gadget feature or a charity gimmick — they are capitalism stripped to its essentials: voluntary, direct, open."
- "Micropayments are the printing press of the digital economy."
- "You get promises to pay, not payments."
- "The smallest coin can win — not because of its size, but because it moves where it is most needed, in the hands of those who earned it."

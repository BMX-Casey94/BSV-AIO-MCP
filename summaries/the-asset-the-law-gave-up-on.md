# The Asset the Law Gave Up On
**Date:** 2026-07-03 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-asset-the-law-gave-up-on
**Subtitle:** Why money is the vanishing point of the good-faith-purchase problem, and what payments economics sees when it looks down the same line

## Core thesis
The property lawyers' good-faith-purchaser puzzle and the payments economists' theory of settlement are one subject viewed from opposite ends of a single axis: the identifiability of the asset. As identifiability falls, the owner's incentive to search, the buyer's incentive to verify, and the deterrent value of any title rule all fall with it. At the limit of perfect fungibility the efficient rule is not a better allocation between two innocents but the abolition of the contest — fresh title for the honest taker for value, and finality. That endpoint asset is money; its legal name is the currency of money, its economic name is payment finality.

## Key arguments and claims
- Crawford (2025, Oxford Journal of Legal Studies) reframes the owner–thief–purchaser triangle: corrective justice cannot reach the O-versus-P dispute because they are linked only as fellow victims of a thief "not worth suing"; the law's objective should be suppression of theft.
- Theft is not a costless transfer: the thief burns resources, owners burn resources on precautions, purchasers bear verification costs, and production distorts toward things hard to steal — "the entire negative-sum apparatus of theft as an enterprise".
- Schwartz and Scott (2011) supply the "double moral hazard": owners take optimal precautions only if they cannot recover from purchasers, and purchasers investigate only if owners can recover — both conditions cannot hold at once.
- Crawford's deeper point: for most assets the owner rationally will not search (the stolen laptop is one of ten million identical units; jewellery is melted by nightfall), so title rules "do not bite" — explaining Levmore's (1987) stubborn doctrinal variety across a cited 247 jurisdictions.
- At high identifiability (a Renoir, a documented classic car) search pays and registers work; Crawford proposes registration-conditioned title, with the private Art Loss Register as a consistent instance.
- Fox (1996) reconstructs money's "currency": coin had "no earmark", so detinue would not lie for loose coin unless sealed in a bag; when earmarked paper arrived from the 1640s, Miller v. Race (1758) refused nemo dat anyway — "The true reason is on account of the currency of it". Finality is an active legal commitment to treat money's identifiability as zero even when technically positive.
- Kahn and Roberds (2009): payment systems solve a time mismatch plus limited enforcement; store-of-value systems verify the object, account-based systems verify identity. Money is "memory made portable"; a store-of-value instrument whose acceptability depends on provenance is a self-contradiction.
- The threshold model: V*(s) = c / [θ · R(s) · (1 + δ)], with search productivity R(s) vanishing at s = 0, so V*(s) diverges and money emerges as the s → 0 limit of the good-faith-purchase framework, not an exception to it.
- Via Coase (1937): as per-transaction recovery becomes infinitely costly at s = 0, deterrence migrates into the identity-bearing institutions of the account layer. "Deterrence of theft does not vanish from the world of money. It is vertically integrated into the payments system."
- The essay is expressly a research proposal: four premises and four falsifiable predictions (regime monotonicity, interior doctrinal diversity, enforcement location at identity-bearing interfaces, function dominating form at the boundary) are stated as untested.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Law-and-economics unification by axis construction: take two mature literatures, show they are interior and endpoint of one spectrum, then compress the claim into a small formal model with an explicit threshold equation. The method is deliberately self-disciplined — premises labelled untested, predictions stated with their falsifiers, and all six sources declared read in full.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post instead builds the identifiability-spectrum framework (property law plus payments economics) that the companion essays then turn onto digital assets; crypto appears only obliquely in the warning that re-engineering money's identifiability upward re-imports "the verification burdens, discounts, and finality doubts that the currency rule was built to eliminate".

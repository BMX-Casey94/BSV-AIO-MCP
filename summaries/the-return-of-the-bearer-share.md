# The Return of the Bearer Share
**Date:** 2026-03-07 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-return-of-the-bearer-share
**Subtitle:** How Proof of Stake Rebuilt the One Financial Instrument Democracy Spent Two Centuries Destroying

## Core thesis
Proof of Stake is functionally equivalent to the bearer share: governance-weighted capital — voting power, economic returns, transfer of control — exercised by holders whose beneficial identity is institutionally illegible. Democratic states spent two centuries prohibiting exactly this combination (anonymous holder plus governance salience); PoS rebuilds it in cryptographic form, more scalable, divisible, and jurisdictionally elusive than the paper original, while marketing itself as a mere efficiency upgrade.

## Key arguments and claims
- Bearer-share prohibition followed a specific trajectory: the UK's Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015; Panama's Law 47 of 2015 (immobilisation with authorised custodians); the EU's Fourth Anti-Money Laundering Directive (beneficial-ownership registries); FATF Recommendations 24 and 25; the Panama Papers of 2016 as political accelerant.
- The deeper rationale was not crime prevention but democratic legitimacy: governance-weighted capital must be linked to identifiable, accountable subjects as a structural condition of legitimate economic governance — a Polanyian counter-movement embedding finance in accountability structures.
- PoW vs PoS is a labour market vs a capital market: under PoW, validation rights are earned through continuous operational expenditure, are proportional to current contribution, and lapse immediately when investment stops; under PoS, validation rights are conditioned on token ownership, yield compounding returns on locked capital, and entrench incumbents — producing Olson-style "distributional coalitions" and a financialised secondary market in governance rights (liquid staking, staking-as-a-service).
- Craig coins "infrastructural domination": consequential governance power exercised through technical infrastructure by agents who are neither identifiable nor accountable. It requires three joint conditions — power exercised through infrastructural design (Lessig's "code is law" covers condition one alone), institutional illegibility of the power-holder, and no internal accountability mechanism.
- A formal two-layer bearer-equivalence test: governance anonymity (unlinked exercise of the governance-rights bundle; low-cost fragmentation of beneficial control across unlimited pseudonymous addresses) plus governance salience (material consequentiality of stake; absence of built-in disclosure). Ethereum, Solana, Cardano, and Cosmos-based networks satisfy all four conditions.
- Three legibility levels must be distinguished: address-level (high), operational-entity-level (partial, via self-disclosure and clustering heuristics), and beneficial-controller-level (systematically unavailable). Democratic accountability operates only at the third level.
- PoS creates a "digital Freeport" differing from physical offshore finance in three ways: jurisdictionally unbounded, infinitely divisible (address creation is computationally costless), and technically self-enforcing (opacity enforced by mathematics, not by cooperative custodians). It is architecturally opaque, not evasively opaque — so better enforcement cannot fix it; only redesign or new institutions can.
- Existing financial governance already operationalises three "conditions of financial recognition": identity legibility (EU Central Register of Beneficial Ownership, UK Persons with Significant Control register, US Corporate Transparency Act 2021), participatory visibility (SEC Schedule 13D: 5% threshold, ten-day filing), and reciprocal accountability (fiduciary duties). Pseudonymous PoS violates all three at the beneficial-controller level, producing arbitrary power in Philip Pettit's republican sense — domination as structural condition, regardless of whether power is actually abused.
- Endpoint regulation fails: exchange-custodied stakers (~20% of Ethereum stake) pass KYC but their validators' governance decisions carry no fiduciary or disclosure obligations; liquid-staking protocols (~34%) are permissionless smart contracts; solo stakers interact with no regulated intermediary. Crucially, the opaque fraction is "structurally unknowable" because the protocol records no acquisition provenance.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Historical institutionalism fused with republican political theory: he extracts the structural principle behind two centuries of securities regulation, formalises it as a necessary-and-sufficient conditions test, and applies it to protocol architecture — treating consensus mechanisms as governance institutions to be evaluated against the same legitimacy standards as corporate forms.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the mainstream "Merge"-style narrative of PoS as a greener, faster, strictly superior upgrade: the conversion of labour-market governance into capital-market governance is "a political transformation accomplished through technical means".
- Rejects the apolitical-neutrality framing common across crypto: removing power from institutional visibility is "anti-political" in effect, foreclosing contestation by making power-holders unobservable.
- Diverges from the PoS-friendly wing of mainstream crypto (Ethereum, Solana, Cardano) by defending PoW's continuous contestability as a legitimacy property, not merely an energy cost.
- Denies that exchange-level KYC and custodial regulation suffice: the accountability gap is architectural, not a regulatory oversight to be patched at endpoints.

# Institutional madness
**Date:** 2019-05-16 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/institutional-madness-6f4fade7b9fc
**Subtitle:** There is a simple reason why Tether (USDT) has avoided being audited. At least 31% of the reported funds have been embezzled from…

## Core thesis
Institutional money cannot enter crypto because the infrastructure is criminal: Tether is unauditable because its funds are embezzled, and exchanges like Binance flout AML law. Bitcoin's survival and legality rest not on "decentralisation" but on its immutable audit trail, which makes tracing simple — Lightning and sidechains exist precisely to destroy that trail and are therefore money-services businesses caught by existing law. Double-spending, meanwhile, is a negligible risk already beaten by the fraud rates of physical cash.

## Key arguments and claims
- "At least 31% of the reported funds [behind Tether] have been embezzled from the Russian state with at least 13% of the total Tether value earned through fraud conducted in the UK" — the reason USDT has avoided audit. Institutions "have rules that preclude them from investing in organisations that have benefited from the proceeds of crime".
- "HODL is all about having a few people invest in Core coin (BTC) so that some others can get money out." A spend-and-replace usage model "would place more upward pressure on price", but the 1-MB cap precludes it — and would stop "crime-friendly log-destruction systems (such as Lightning)".
- BTC culture thinks it can avoid Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs), which are "a critical intelligence resource" for tackling money laundering, terrorism and fraud; exchanges that fail to investigate clients commit a crime and will be shut down.
- Binance is "far more centralised than an organisation like Liberty Reserve" — which had 30,000+ distributed points, no central database, funds across 1,000+ banks and no direct US links, yet was taken down in a global AML action. "Bitcoin survives not because it is decentralised but because it acts within a legal framework that allows it to be traced."
- "The consequence of decentralisation is an immutable audit trail." Had Liberty Reserve been blockchain-enabled, "around 100,000 criminals who used the system of the 2 million or so in the total client pool would now be facing prison".
- Lightning/sidechain nodes and users are money services businesses: "every node within the system, every user will need to keep full compliance details or otherwise be breaking the law. It's the dirty little secret that they are not telling you about Lightning. It was always designed to be a system that loses records."
- "Blockchain does not preclude tracing; it simplifies it." The "scaling lie" — that Bitcoin needs Lightning and sidechains — "is purely made in order to destroy evidence"; AML law has covered funding for decades. He notes patents that "preclude unlicensed use outside of BSV" and that Microsoft is blockchain-agnostic.
- "Stopping crime is not censorship... there has never been a single quote from me when acting as Satoshi that concerns censorship resistance as the purpose for the creation of Bitcoin." Bitcoin "is secure enough to act within legal frameworks"; Tether and Wormhole are "record-losing criminal coins".
- Enforcement walkthrough: a SAR propagates via Interpol to exchanges and custodial banks, which must block tainted addresses or lose legal protection; mixers are custodial and AML-covered; legal fungibility protects an innocent Alice who took coins "without knowledge... for a valid exchange of goods and services", while a guilty Charlie can be traced through any mixing depth and must prove source of wealth in court. Small spends (~£200) sit below AML thresholds; a stolen £1 million can only be laundered in slow £200 dribbles with real detection risk.
- Jurisdiction is inescapable: taking European money brings EU law (MLD5); touching US dollars, a US citizen, resident or ".com" domain brings US law — "You can be covered by US law and European law simultaneously."
- Double spending is a non-issue: ASICs (T-hashes) versus botnet CPUs (K-hashes) mean "a small home user would effectively beat the largest criminal botnet" (citing his 2011 paper on criminal botnets). Merchants already tolerate worse: 30–40 million counterfeit £1 coins (~2.8% of circulation) and 1-in-5,000 fake £20 notes; a restaurateur prices in two pence of fraud per £100 meal, and a Bitcoin double-spend "costs far more to even attempt and needs to be conducted within seconds". Non-payment is handled by law, not protocol — dine-and-dash means prison.
- "I have methods to allow regulators and government to stop all crime going forward that comes from a blockchain. Any blockchain."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is legal-doctrinal: he reads existing AML statute (SARs, MLD5, MSB registration, source-of-wealth laws) onto crypto infrastructure and shows the compliance conclusions follow mechanically, using an extended Charlie/Alice adversarial walkthrough to demonstrate traceability. He supports this with a historical takedown precedent (Liberty Reserve) and comparative risk baselining — pricing Bitcoin's double-spend risk against the known counterfeit rates of pound coins and notes rather than against an absolute ideal.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Repudiates censorship resistance as Bitcoin's purpose — the core cypherpunk orthodoxy — claiming no Satoshi quote ever advanced it.
- Inverts the "decentralisation protects us from the state" thesis: Liberty Reserve was more decentralised than any blockchain and was still destroyed; Bitcoin endures because it is traceable within law.
- Reframes Lightning Network — mainstream BTC's scaling answer — as a deliberately record-losing MSB whose every node is legally obliged to keep compliance logs.
- Attacks HODL/store-of-value as an exit scheme for early holders, arguing spend-and-replace usage would create more genuine price pressure (and is blocked by the 1-MB cap).
- Dismisses double-spend/reorg FUD by benchmarking against physical-cash fraud rates, against the confirmation-count security culture of BTC.
- Declares Tether a fraud built on embezzled funds two years before mainstream scrutiny caught up, and ties "institutional money" narratives to it.

# IPv4.1 Does Not Exist — core principles

- **A protocol is an agreement, and compatibility is the only thing it is.** Changing the shared wire format is never an upgrade; it is the authoring of a new protocol. You cannot let out the seam of an agreement. You can only sew a second garment and persuade people to call it the first.
- **A field cannot be widened in place.** A machine expecting 32 bits reads a 128-bit address as nonsense, and no authority can update every machine at the same instant. The two protocols coexist only through dual stacks, tunnels and translators — machinery whose existence proves they are two protocols, not one.
- **Redefining Bitcoin's unit field is a hard fork by construction.** Value is a fixed-width integer count of satoshis, validated independently against a fixed money-range ceiling. Old-rule and new-rule nodes reject each other's blocks.
- **Identity is fidelity to the rule.** Not market size, not evangelist volume, not custody of the brand. The original is the chain that kept the rule, however small its banner.
- **A bridge is the schism, rendered in machinery.** Pegs, tunnels and wrapped-asset links between an old chain and a renumbered chain are the confession of the rupture, not its reconciliation.
- **Honesty numbers the break.** The internet's engineers named the successor IPv6. A monetary reformer who wants the old name while behaving as the sixth is performing a substitution, not a continuation.

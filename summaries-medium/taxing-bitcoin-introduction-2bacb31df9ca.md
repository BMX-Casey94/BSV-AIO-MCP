# Taxing Bitcoin — Introduction.
**Date:** 2018-10-28 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/taxing-bitcoin-introduction-2bacb31df9ca

## Core thesis
Bitcoin cannot be forced into a single legal category — it is money, ledger, commodity and token, and the correct tax treatment depends on which character it bears in a particular use. Craig opens a series (to become a paper) that will work through those treatments, using the differentiated tax handling of gold (bullion, coins, jewellery, industrial use) as the model. The instalment itself is largely a factual survey establishing that Bitcoin functions as money in the real economy. Notably, the footnotes' access dates (January 2014, November 2015) show the body is recycled from an earlier paper, retrofitted with a "now BCH" aside.

## Key arguments and claims
- The framing question is not which definition fits Bitcoin but "which one defines Bitcoin in a particular use" — a use-based, substance-over-form approach to tax characterisation.
- Gold analogy: a single substance attracts different tax treatments as bullion, minted bullion-grade coins, non-bullion coins, collectables, jewellery, electronics input and unrefined metal; Bitcoin likewise spans "a commodity as a means to store data on a ledger", money/coinage, currency, token markers (movie ticket), and contracts (bonds, equity-linked).
- Adoption evidence from the GAO's May 2013 Senate report: ~11 million bitcoins circulating; prices moved from $5–$20 (May 2012–Feb 2013) to "$79 and over $237" (April–May 2013); 8,000–70,000 transactions daily — contrasted with 2015 figures of 14 million coins, a $354.28 average price and ~159,331 daily transactions.
- Governments already treat it as money-like: Germany's Finance Ministry recognised Bitcoin as "a 'unit of account'"; Switzerland treats BTC–CHF trades like euro trades with VAT-free exchange fees; HMRC scrapped VAT plans and treats it as private currency.
- Merchant acceptance catalogued: Microsoft, Dell, Starbucks, WordPress, Reddit, Gyft, Overstock (800 orders worth ~$126,000 on day one), Expedia, PayPal, Zynga, plus bricks-and-mortar cafés, estate agents and law firms.
- Market infrastructure: "market based" versus "fixed rate" exchanges; XE and Oanda quote Bitcoin rates ("using the currency codes XBT and BTC, respectively in the pre-split days and now BCH"); LocalBitcoins for in-person trades; BitPay claimed 60,000 businesses, $100 million processed in 2013 and "$1 million in Bitcoin transactions per day" by 2014.
- Bitcoin is distinguished from other virtual currencies (Linden Dollars, WoW gold) per the ECB definition: no central issuer, payment is "a reallocation of Bitcoin balances / value in the public ledger" rather than a data packet transfer, and use is for real-world transactions.
- Bitcoin is distinguished from barter schemes: no underlying legal arrangement between users, no central operator, coins can be bought rather than earned in-scheme, and — unlike barter credits — bitcoin converts directly into real-world currency.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The mode is doctrinal and taxonomic: define the legal categories, marshal statistics and authority (GAO reports, central-bank papers, revenue rulings), and sort Bitcoin into them use by use. He writes as a tax practitioner building a paper — numbered sections, footnoted citations, careful distinctions (money/currency/legal tender; exchange types; barter versus money). The persuasive strategy is normalisation: Bitcoin is shown to be already legible to existing fiscal law, not an anomaly needing new categories.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the era's "Bitcoin is outside the state / tax is theft" cypherpunk strand: Craig's whole premise is that Bitcoin slots into existing tax law and should be accounted for like any money or commodity.
- Against the "Bitcoin is not money, just a speculative asset" scepticism of regulators and no-coiners circa 2014–2018: he documents payment usage and government recognition to establish money-status as fact.
- Quietly against BTC maximalism: the aside that forex providers quoted "XBT and BTC… in the pre-split days and now BCH" asserts BCH as the continuing Bitcoin for quotation purposes — a 2018-era claim BTC partisans rejected outright.

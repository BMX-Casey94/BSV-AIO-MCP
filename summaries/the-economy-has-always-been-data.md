# The Economy Has Always Been Data
**Date:** 2026-05-06 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-economy-has-always-been-data
**Subtitle:** Why "knowledge economy," "noonomy," and the rest are not new dispensations but a rediscovery of what Rand, Hayek, and North already knew — and why Bitcoin is the next tool in a very long sequence

## Core thesis
The economy has always been data-driven: what it costs to measure, attest, verify and enforce has always bounded what can be exchanged, by whom, and at what scale. "Knowledge economy" and "noonomy" are therefore rediscoveries of what Rand, Hayek and North already established, and Bitcoin — understood as an instrument for cheap, atomic, verifiable settlement — is the next tool in an eight-century lineage of cost-lowering instruments, not a discontinuity.

## Key arguments and claims
- Rand (Atlas Shrugged, Francisco d'Anconia's money speech): "Wealth is the product of man's capacity to think." Every act of production presupposes prior cognition; even pyramid-building embodied discovered knowledge. Wright endorses the metaphysical claim while explicitly parting from Rand's political programme, and notes Romer's (1990) endogenous growth model makes the same point formally — ideas are non-rivalrous and partially excludable.
- Hayek (1945, "The Use of Knowledge in Society"): the economic problem is not allocative logic but dispersed knowledge that is "never for the whole society 'given' to a single mind". The price system is primarily a communication system that compresses scarcity signals into actionable form. Bigger computers do not rescue central planning, because the constraint is informational — local knowledge loses its time-value in collection — not computational.
- North (1990), building on Coase: transaction costs are the costs of measuring valuable attributes and of protecting, policing and enforcing agreements, and "the costliness of information is the key to the costs of transacting". Wallis and North (1986) measured the US transaction sector at over 45 per cent of national income, up from roughly 25 per cent a century earlier. Western divergence is the cumulative, path-dependent invention of cheaper transacting: bills of exchange, negotiability and discounting, the Champagne fairs, price currents, marine insurance.
- Tools defined: any instrument that lowers the per-unit cost of an economic operation — tally sticks, coinage, double-entry bookkeeping, the telegraph, the standard container, the relational database, SWIFT, ACH. None invented a knowledge economy; each extended one that already existed.
- Path dependence (David 1985 on QWERTY; Arthur 1989 on increasing returns): incumbent payment rails are not optimal, merely locked in. A successor tool is evaluated on whether it reduces unit cost, by how much, on what attributes, and at what credibility.
- The Bitcoin/Teranode engineering claims: atomic, final, cryptographically verifiable transfer against a tamper-evident public record; the Halborn independent verification reports zero double-spend violations across approximately 520 million unspent transaction outputs and 12.8 million deliberate attempts; validated throughput of 1.12 million transactions per second per node; and a projection that a fleet of roughly one hundred nodes would annually produce more transaction data than the world manufactures in storage media.
- Structural elimination rather than competition: reconciliation between sub-ledgers, settlement-finality delay, chargeback windows and correspondent routing are artefacts of pre-atomic-settlement architectures, not features of payments as such. The bill of exchange did not make the coin-caravan faster; it made the caravan unnecessary for the operation in question. Bitcoin's relation to the card-network stack is of the same form.
- Noonomy evaluated (Bodrunov 2018/2024, read via publishers' descriptions and secondary literature — explicitly disclosed): the descriptive part is correct but anticipated by Schumpeter (1942), Machlup (1962) and Bell (1973); the prescriptive part — a "non-economic" stage of diffused property and replaced market coordination — does not follow, and skips the step Hayek devoted his career to.
- Either reading dissolves the supposed tension with Bitcoin: descriptively, cheaper verifiable settlement is exactly what the observation predicts; prescriptively, the conflict is with the entire Rand-Hayek-North apparatus, not with payment architecture.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post is economic history and price-theory synthesis; its only implicit divergence is treating Bitcoin as a high-throughput, low-cost settlement tool (Teranode) rather than a non-transacting store of value.

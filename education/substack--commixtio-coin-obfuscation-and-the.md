# Commixtio, Coin Obfuscation, and the Law — core principles

- **Mixing is a property-law problem.** The Roman doctrine of *commixtio* — the rules for mixing fungible goods owned by different parties — maps directly onto transaction mixing in Bitcoin-like ledgers.
- **Commingled tokens carry legal consequences.** Mixed or “tainted” digital tokens have legal, economic and evidentiary implications; they are not merely a privacy technique.
- **Taint, not anonymity, is the frame.** Coin mixing is a tainting problem: commingled funds are treated as mixed property, not as a right to disappear.
- **Ancient principles still govern forensic tracing.** The same doctrines retain relevance for the forensic analysis of mixed coins on a public ledger.

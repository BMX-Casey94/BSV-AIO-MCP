# How Digital Signatures Work — core principles

- **Control of a key is not legally dispositive.** There is no such thing as handing keys over and saying they are someone else’s. That is not how digital signatures work.
- **Electronic and digital signatures are different categories.** A basic electronic signature is data in electronic form attached to or logically associated with other electronic data and serving as a method of authentication. A digitised electronic signature is not the same as a digital certificate.
- **An advanced electronic signature has four statutory tests.** It must be uniquely linked to the signatory, capable of identifying the signatory, created using means the signatory can maintain under sole control, and linked to the data so that any subsequent change is detectable.
- **Identity anchoring is a feature of the legal framework.** Advanced signatures envision a personal digital certificate from a certificate service provider, giving certainty and non-repudiation.
- **A digital signature is a numerical value over the whole document.** It is verifiable with an extremely low probability of error. An electronic signature can be as simple as a printed name or a scanned autograph.
- **Qualifying electronic signatures satisfy legal signature requirements.** They are admissible as evidence in legal proceedings. A mature statutory regime already governs them.
- **Internet contracting is not a new legal universe.** The Internet is primarily communication channels, like older carriers, so existing contracting law maps onto it without mystique.

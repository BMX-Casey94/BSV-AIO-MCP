# Quantum Illusions: The False Promise of Quantum Threats and the Manipulation of Cryptographic Fear
**Date:** 2025-10-16 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/quantum-illusions-the-false-promise
**Subtitle:** How Quantum Benchmarking, Random Circuit Sampling, and Misapplied Narratives Distort the Real Security Discourse

## Core thesis
Today's quantum computers are fragile engineering prototypes bounded by decoherence and noise, and their celebrated benchmarks reward approximation rather than the absolute precision cryptography demands. The claim that quantum machines will "break RSA within ten years" is not a physics-based forecast but a funding narrative — and, when applied to Bitcoin, an instrument of fear designed to justify centralised seizure masquerading as protection.

## Key arguments and claims
- Random Circuit Sampling (RCS) is "not an algorithm" but a benchmark of fidelity: a device is judged on whether its output distribution resembles an ideal quantum device's, with partial fidelity counting as success. "It is a test of hardware behaviour, not mathematical insight."
- Google's 2019 Sycamore claim — 53 qubits performing in 200 seconds what would take a classical supercomputer 10,000 years — was "within months, challenged and largely dismantled"; improved classical methods (tensor-network compression, matrix-product-state simulation, GPU hybrids) cut the figure to days, then hours. The experiment "produced no factorisations, no optimisations, no chemical models, no decryptions".
- Quantinuum's H2-1 (56 fully connected trapped-ion qubits) repeats the paradigm: "beyond classical simulation" is a moving goalpost defined by whatever classical techniques currently cannot do — "a race against moving goalposts".
- Cryptography is binary: RSA rests on factoring large primes, ECC on the discrete logarithm problem, SHA-256 on one-way hash functions. "A single bit out of place renders the entire output meaningless… A computation that is 99.9% correct is indistinguishable from one that is wholly wrong."
- Shor's algorithm (via the Quantum Fourier Transform) is mathematically sound but physically out of reach: breaking RSA-2048 would require an estimated 20 million physical qubits, error rates below 10⁻¹⁵ per gate, and trillions of coherent operations. Current systems offer microsecond coherence times, gate error rates of 10⁻²–10⁻³, and fidelities of 99–99.9% — and "a one-in-a-thousand chance of error per gate becomes certainty of failure after a few thousand gates", while factoring needs millions to billions of gates.
- "The existence of a theoretical path does not imply the existence of a physical road."
- The economics of quantum fear form a self-feeding cycle — "funding → headline → fear → more funding" — with milestones timed to investment rounds, DARPA-style national-security programmes, and billion-dollar valuations for companies with minimal products.
- The Bitcoin paradox: ownership is purely functional — "whoever can sign, owns". A quantum-derived key would produce a signature indistinguishable from the owner's: "The blockchain records outcomes, not motives." There is no protocol-level standard by which such a transaction could be judged theft.
- Consequently, proposed "quantum recovery protocols" and "protective key migrations" are "Trojan horses for power": pre-emptive forced transfers of "vulnerable" coins would rewrite consensus rules, reintroduce human arbitration, and amount to "confiscation framed as protection".
- "The real weapon is not the qubit, but fear itself—manufactured, marketed, and used to legitimise coercion in the name of protection."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The argument runs on two rails. The first is an error-budget analysis from physics: coherence times, gate fidelities and compounding error rates are set against the sequential precision Shor's algorithm demands, showing the gap is one of kind, not degree. The second is incentive and political-economy analysis: who funds the narrative, who profits from panic, and how fear converts into centralised control over cryptographic infrastructure.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the quantum-panic strand of mainstream crypto discourse (flagging old coins, forced migration to "quantum-safe" addresses) as dispossession: altering consensus rules to move others' coins destroys the neutrality that defines the system.
- Rejects the media-consensus reading of "quantum supremacy" milestones as cryptographic omens: sampling progress "says nothing about the ability to invert a one-way function. It is a category error."
- Pushes "code is law" further than its mainstream critics: even hypothetical quantum key recovery would be lawful execution under protocol rules — "it would merely obey them faster" — so the only thing the threat endangers is "the freedom of those who believe the myth".

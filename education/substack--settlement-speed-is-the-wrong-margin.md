# Settlement Speed Is the Wrong Margin — core principles

- **Measure the margin before you choose the instrument.** In large-value trade the working-capital cost is locked in the receivables cycle of 45–110 days, not in the 1–5 days of rail latency. Faster finality can remove at most low single digits of the carry.
- **The decisive ratio is λ = L/T.** L is rail latency, T is the receivables cycle. With T = 90 days and L = 2, λ ≈ 2%. The ratio is rate-invariant: the financing spread cancels, so no interest-rate assumption can inflate the importance of settlement speed.
- **The instruments that matter act on financeability, not milliseconds.** Invoice verification, receivables registries, enforceable assignment, credit insurance, documentary transferability and counterparty-risk compression reach the spread paid on every day of the cycle. Rail speed is last — useful for resilience and inclusion, not for the carry.
- **Where speed is the whole margin is the machine economy.** When counterparty, credit term and value collapse — machine-to-machine settlement for compute, bandwidth or data in fractions of a cent — T falls toward L and λ rises toward one. Finality is the holding period.
- **Do not import a machine-regime architecture into trade-credit analysis.** Instant rails, CBDCs and tokenised deposits solve a problem trade-credit firms do not have. High-throughput micropayment rails are the right instrument where the holding period is the settlement itself.
- **Rails do not shorten negotiated terms.** Even against only the involuntary frictional residual — collection lag and documentary reconciliation — the latency share remains mid-single digits. Months stay months.

# The Lie of Progress: How Bitcoin Became Fiat in a Digital Suit
**Date:** 2025-10-27 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-lie-of-progress-how-bitcoin-became
**Subtitle:** A treatise on the betrayal of function, the worship of price, and the architecture of self-deception.

## Core thesis
BTC has betrayed its design purpose as peer-to-peer electronic cash and become "fiat in a digital suit": hoarded rather than spent, custodied on exchanges, throttled by an artificial one-megabyte block cap, and patched with Lightning — a reconstruction of banking with extra steps. Money is motion; a Bitcoin that cannot be used is already dead, and the only honest choice is "scale it, or lose it".

## Key arguments and claims
- Utility is the heresy the "Church of Hodl" cannot tolerate: "Bitcoin changes nothing if it cannot be used." Gold failed "not because it lacked value, but because it lacked motion" — too rigid to buy coffee or pay rent — and paper's substitution was "a betrayal that pretended to serve convenience".
- Exchanges are the new banks: "Coinbase, Binance, Kraken—these are the new banks of Babel, the keepers of illusion. Fractional reserve in digital drag." They mint synthetic promises, layer them in derivatives, and call it liquidity.
- Lightning is "banking with extra steps": unverifiable balances, liquidity providers as middlemen, trust reintroduced under the pretence of speed. "Lightning doesn't remove trust—it institutionalises it." The base chain becomes "a settlement ledger for institutions, not individuals".
- The one-megabyte cap was "a temporary safety valve" set when blocks barely filled a tenth of it; myth "metastasised into mandate". Merkle trees "are designed for scale, not scarcity", and the infrastructure for terabyte blocks "exists—and has existed for years".
- Small blocks centralise rather than protect: power flows "into the hands of those with liquidity and access—banks, exchanges, and custodians". Non-mining "full nodes" are dismissed: your node "checks nothing, decides nothing, merely syncs and submits"; miners are the real nodes.
- Newspeak is catalogued: "'Scalability' now means off-chain. 'Security' means stagnation. 'Decentralisation' means a handful of developers dictating protocol updates in group chats." Spam has been redefined — the white paper's spam was unpaid data, and "The moment a miner is paid, the concept of spam vanishes, because it's no longer unwanted—it's commerce." "Layer 2" now means outside Bitcoin entirely.
- Fiat 2.0 arrives as convenience: wrapped Bitcoin, custodial wallets, institutional adoption create "a peer-to-platform" system. "A wrapped Bitcoin is the bureaucrat's dream: fully visible, easily frozen, seamlessly taxed." No coup is needed — "Just stagnation. Limit the block size. Inflate the fees. Force the user base off-chain until the only viable channels are custodial."
- The economics of fear: Core developers resist scaling because "Scaling removes their significance"; in a scaled system "miners outvote developers with hash power instead of pull requests" and the priesthood evaporates.
- The capability persists: "The code still holds the potential to scale to millions of transactions per second" — the failure is "not in the failure of technology. It is in the failure of will."
- "Bitcoin either scales or it dies. Everything else is propaganda for the grave."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The framework is monetary-history analogy plus velocity-of-money reasoning: gold's demonetisation through hoarding and substitution is mapped step by step onto BTC's custody, wrapping and off-chain abstraction. Over this sits incentive analysis — the developer "priesthood" protects its relevance by preventing the scale that would make it obsolete — delivered as sustained satire (Church of Hodl, cult of the cap, Ministry of Mempool) anchored in white-paper literalism.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects "store of value" as primary: "the miracle was never scarcity—it was flow"; value detached from utility "is only hysteria in numerical form".
- Rejects the small-blocks decentralisation doctrine: the cap "renders the architecture inefficient, forcing users off-chain, into exchanges, into the arms of the very middlemen this system was built to replace".
- Rejects Lightning as scaling: it is "the banker's resurrection", an IOU machine that buries Bitcoin's principle "one layer deeper" with every off-chain settlement.
- Rejects fee-market "security" framing: high fees are failure; paid transactions are commerce, not spam.
- Rejects the "Bitcoin standard" dream: "a monetary order built on the same deceit that sank gold" — digital vaults holding claims, derivatives and wrapped tokens instead of the real thing.

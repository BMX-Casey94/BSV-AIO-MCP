# Why Hash Power Is Not Security — core principles

- **Security is the cost of violating the protocol commitment, not the cost of a 51% attack.** The standard \(\alpha V < C\) models only an external adversary acquiring capacity for double-spend or censorship. It treats the rule set as fixed and threats as external.
- **The institutional condition is \(\alpha V < C + I + K + R\).** \(C\) is consensus (attack) cost; \(I\) capital at risk; \(K\) coordination cost; \(R\) legal, regulatory, professional and reputational accountability. The extra terms routinely decide whether attacks occur.
- **\(C\) is marginal acquisition cost, not equilibrium operating cost.** It depends on detection during acquisition; it is bidirectional (defenders can dilute attackers); in proof-of-stake it is denominated in the token being attacked.
- **Insiders face large \(I\); external attackers face \(I = 0\).** A mining firm's hardware, power agreements and infrastructure collapse with the protocol's asset value. Systems whose capacity sits with low-\(I\) actors are the vulnerable ones, whatever \(C\) says.
- **Coordination cost scales with the number of parties required.** A single actor at 51% faces \(K = 0\); thirty actors must identify, communicate covertly, negotiate, prevent defection and split proceeds. Shared clients, hosts and industry meetings lower \(K\) below the raw count.
- **Identifiable authority is itself a security feature.** \(R\) is approximately zero for anonymous external attackers and often dispositive for identifiable institutions — fraud statutes, market-manipulation rules, fiduciary liability, licence revocation. A firm that would gain ten million and face a hundred million in fines is rationally deterred even if \(C\) is low.
- **The terms are partial substitutes.** High \(I\) compensates for lower \(C\); high \(K\) or high \(R\) each deter coordinated attacks. Current practice systematically overstates systems with high \(C\) and low values on the other terms.
- **Governance attacks share the same four-term structure.** Compute it against the rule-changing coalition; consensus security and governance security unify in one typology.
- **Hash rate and Nakamoto coefficients are incomplete statistics.** They capture aspects of \(C\) and \(K\) and do not address \(I\) or \(R\). A moderate-hash-rate system with identifiable, deeply invested operators can outrank a higher-hash-rate system of anonymous, low-stake operators.

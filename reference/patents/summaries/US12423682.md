## Problem addressed
Existing reputation and user-data systems are described as easy to attack or manipulate, poorly suited to a distributed ledger, and often based on subjective user-entered ratings that competitors can abuse. The specification wants an objective, blockchain-stored record of how users fulfilled transactions, and an efficient way to retrieve and aggregate those records for a chosen user.

## Core mechanism
A deterministic finite automaton that implements a smart contract also scores fulfilment and publishes a reputation transaction: a dust P2SH output whose metadata holds contract type, attributable information (address or signature script), and a fulfilment score. Users are linked through a hierarchical deterministic wallet so a hash of the master public key (or descendants) identifies them. A customer wallet builds a filter from that hash, searches the blockchain for matching reputation transactions, and computes an aggregate score (for example a balance of positive and negative outcomes). The aggregate can feed later decisions or DFA modifications.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
The independent method constructs and broadcasts user-related data transactions that express data from prior transactions, repeats this for many transactions or users, then selects a user, generates a filter, searches the blockchain for matching transactions, and computes aggregated user-related data. An independent system claim requires a user digital wallet, at least one computing agent that implements a DFA via a blockchain, and a blockchain platform.

## BSV relevance
The embodiment records reputation as Bitcoin dust P2SH metadata and retrieves it by HD-wallet key lineage. That pattern can be used on BSV to attach machine-generated fulfilment scores to on-chain contracts without a separate ratings server.

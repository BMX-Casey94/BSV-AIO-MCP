# Breach of contract — Remedies for breach — core principles

- **Specific performance is the remedy that maps onto code.** It requires "a breaching party… to fulfil the contract as written" — the canonical example being a house sold to C after contracting to sell to B, where "the law requires that the house go to B". "Such a contract can easily be determined within a smart contract."
- **Specific performance has institutional economy.** It spares the court from estimating the injured party's damages — the information-cost rationale is what makes it automatable.
- **Damages are hard because value is subjective.** The court must determine B's valuation of the asset and subtract the price; "B has a reason to exaggerate, and then objective evidence may be refuted or not support such claims."
- **Bargaining power shapes damages outcomes.** Where specific performance is impractical, relative bargaining power drives the result — "sometimes, such a scenario may even lead to zero damages".
- **The smart-contract design rule.** "Any smart contract that is not purely based on specific performance as detailed within a contract could easily be overturned or subject to legal review" — lawful automation starts from remedies doctrine, not from code capability.

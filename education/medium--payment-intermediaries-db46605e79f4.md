# Payment intermediaries — core principles

- **Bitcoin's target is payment rails, not banks or government.** Bitcoin is not a means to remove banks, offer credit, or undermine government; it is a means to replace the ineffective payment systems (such as Visa and MasterCard) and the problems these create.
- **Intermediaries solve trust at a distance.** They arose from the difficulties of transferring cash payments over large distances between people who may never meet; they provide both trust and a realistic means for a purchaser to transfer consideration to the seller reliably.
- **Intermediaries are essential actors in remote commerce.** A Singapore customer of an Australian online casino, for example, needs trust accounts, stored card details or bank transfers so that winnings can be paid out and gambling debts covered — the card company or bank is an essential actor.
- **Digital cash was expected; cards won.** It was originally believed (Anderson et al., "Exploring Digital Cash", 1997) that digital cash would be minted and evolve quickly; instead, by 2002 roughly ninety per cent of Internet transactions used credit cards (Ronald J. Mann, *Regulating Internet Payment Intermediaries*, 82 Texas L. Rev. 681 (2004)).
- **"P2P" here means person-to-person.** In the payments context, P2P stands for person-to-person (as with PayPal, one of the first), distinct from peer-to-peer file sharing.
- **Payment intermediaries are enforcement chokepoints.** A commercial site distributing illegal content cannot run profitably without an economical method of receiving consideration; as financial gatekeepers, intermediaries can prevent illicit activity over the Internet, either proactively or upon receipt of court orders.
- **Bitcoin removes the need for the middleman.** With Bitcoin, the need for a middle man was removed; it enabled the ability to have true P2P electronic cash.

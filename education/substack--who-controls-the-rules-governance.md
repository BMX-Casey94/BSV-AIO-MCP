# Who Controls the Rules? Governance Credibility and the $109 Billion Question — core principles

- **Governance credibility G is the variable that matters for builders.** Define G as the probability that a protocol's rules — capacity, fees, consensus, censorship policy — are unchanged next period; whether a business should sink investment into a chain turns on G, not on whether the chain calls itself "decentralised".
- **Credibility is measurable from observable traces.** Estimate Ĝ = 1 − Ŵ_R, the restrictor coalition's weight, from hashrate splits in forks, exchange ticker assignments, token votes and post-fork market-cap ratios — governance power leaves market-observable footprints.
- **Measured governance is bimodal.** Across 74 episodes in 30 protocols: developer-led systems median Ĝ ≈ 0.075, foundation-led ≈ 0.10, token voting ≈ 0.15, explicit protocol locks ≈ 0.925 — "the only way to get above 0.60 is to stop changing the rules."
- **Token voting is structurally a shareholder vote.** Stake-weighted amendment systems are capturable by whales and flash-loaned votes (Tornado Cash and Beanstalk scored 0.00); builders should not mistake them for rule stability.
- **Rule immutability is the institutional innovation.** A locked protocol (post-Genesis BSV: script, UTXO structure and incentives frozen, Ĝ ≈ 0.85–1.00) outperforms both incumbent finance and the rest of crypto on the only metric that disciplines pricing — households and businesses discount any instrument by (1 − G) × δ.
- **Low-credibility money cannot compress incumbent rents.** The $109 billion of annual US friction rent ($34.1 billion debit interchange at $0.338 average per covered transaction; $75.4 billion deposit-franchise rent) is only contestable by a chain whose rules hold: a credible outside option transfers an estimated $28–48 billion to end users.
- **Atomic UTXO settlement removes the institutions that settlement delay created.** A debit transaction traverses five institutions because transfer is not final; reconciliation, chargebacks, fraud screening and identity verification exist for the same reason — "settlement is the transaction", at ~$0.00004 versus ~$0.34 interchange, an 8,500-to-one cost ratio.
- **Judge stewards by conduct, not existence.** The BCH IFP episode (2020) shows credibility can be defended — an 8% block-reward tax was rejected as hashrate migrated — and the Parity freeze refusal shows it can be earned; "governance credibility depends on what the controller does, not on whether a controller exists."

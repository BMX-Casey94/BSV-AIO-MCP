# We don't want to lead with "anonymous"
**Date:** 2019-04-17 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/we-dont-want-to-lead-with-anonymous-a4890db7766d
**Subtitle:** One thing I've learnt during my time with Bitcoin is how important it is to choose your words carefully. Privacy and anonymity are not…

## Core thesis
Privacy and anonymity are not the same, and Bitcoin delivers only the former: it "leaves an audit trail, and for honest people provides a system that is private but will never be anonymous". Reconstructing the 2009 Slashdot launch, he describes his dismay as anti-bank, anti-government newcomers reframed the project, and insists Bitcoin never was — and never will be — outside the reach of government, having been "designed to work on the fine edge of law".

## Key arguments and claims
- Word choice matters: he admits having "used the word anonymous when I've meant private", but the distinction is load-bearing — "language is not like mathematics".
- Bitcoin's audit trail means honest users get privacy; anonymity is structurally excluded.
- The early community was the wrong audience: coming from digital forensics and infosec, he was surrounded by "white-hat hackers or even just auditors" and was unprepared for "the grey and black communities that ended up grasping and integrating Bitcoin".
- At the Slashdot 1.0 submission he "saw the project slipping towards all of the problems I didn't want, those who bore hatred towards banks and government", but stayed silent because people "would be donating time" and mining could not be run profitably.
- "Bitcoin is not outside the reach of government. Bitcoin can be shut down even now… There was never a point in the history of Bitcoin… that ever took Bitcoin outside the grasp of government and regulation. To think so is simply asinine."
- He quotes the very next commenter after his own anti-taunting post — "The community is hopeful the currency will remain outside the reach of any government" — as the exact attitude he opposed.
- Practical control vectors: exchanges ("illegal bucket shops") will be regulated; "Bitcoin always happened to be a system that would end in data centres. And data centres can always be stopped. The mere possession of something can be made illegal."
- Adoption argument: "For every anarchist there are at least 100 others who do not want anarchy" — you cannot win the majority by opposing the governments they vote for.
- "There will never be a system that manages to get around anti-money laundering (AML) laws based on Bitcoin."
- Personal framing of his anger: "I saw my life's work taken and beaten into something it was designed not to be. And the thing they try to create can never be."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues from linguistic precision (privacy ≠ anonymity), then from autobiography — positioning himself at the Slashdot launch as the designer watching his intent be subverted — and finally from political pragmatism: systems grow by working within democracies, not by taunting them. The mode is confessional-cum-prosecutorial, using a quoted forum comment as the adversary's own words.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Flatly denies the cypherpunk orthodoxy that Bitcoin is a tool "outside the reach of government" — he calls the idea "asinine" and says no such point ever existed or will exist.
- Contradicts the anonymity-enhancement trajectory of the era (mixers, CoinJoin, privacy coins): Bitcoin "will never be anonymous" by design, and AML cannot be circumvented on it.
- Contradicts "Bitcoin cannot be shut down": data centres can be stopped and possession criminalised.
- Reframes the founding myth: the anti-bank ethos was a community capture he failed to prevent, not Satoshi's intent — a claim only coherent with his authorship campaign.

## Notable quotes
- "Bitcoin leaves an audit trail, and for honest people provides a system that is private but will never be anonymous."
- "Bitcoin was designed to work on the fine edge of law."
- "There has been no point in Bitcoin's history where it was outside the reach of government, and in fact, there never will be such a point."
- "For every anarchist there are at least 100 others who do not want anarchy."
- "There will never be a system that manages to get around anti-money laundering (AML) laws based on Bitcoin."

# Evidence and law — core principles

- **Law is law; code is merely evidence.** Programs and algorithms in any form are evidence, not a substitute for a common-law framework. Bitcoin was designed to work inside that framework.
- **Possession of a key proves only possession.** Ownership of a key is not proof of authorship, identity or anything else; a precedent derived from key-signing would be dangerous, and a competent judge would not base law on it.
- **There is no such thing as non-repudiation.** An individual always has the opportunity to repudiate; evidence law, not a signature algorithm, decides the point.
- **Bitcoin uses keys once and only once.** You do not have a key associated with an identity in the PGP sense; single-key identity systems are foolish and against the design.
- **Pseudonymity is compatible with ordinary financial records.** The system was never anonymous; it was built to sit alongside banking, KYC and retained payment records rather than to erase them.

# Q&A/Written Interview — The answers — Part 2
**Date:** 2018-09-17 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/q-a-written-interview-the-answers-part-2-20c3f5f84f67
**Subtitle:** I shall continue answering in order.

## Core thesis
Continuing the AMA (questions 18–26), Craig defends nChain's patent programme as legitimate property exploitation — free for use on BCH — and lays out his defining doctrine: "There is no blockchain, there is Bitcoin", the protocol was "complete in version 0.1" and must be frozen, and any post-split chain that is not Bitcoin will be made to "die off". The killer app is low-fee P2P cash, not speculation.

## Key arguments and claims
- Patents as property: "A patent is a right to exploit the property you have created", requiring "an implementation, a design", not mere ideas; anything already public cannot be patented, and applications need real names — "there is no provision for pseudonyms. So, no Satoshi Nakamoto."
- Chain-split threat: "If there is a chain split, then we will ensure that anything other than Bitcoin dies off"; a split coin "will need to move from SHA256d to a separate hashing algorithm", and "There is no scenario where we will allow a scenario that Jihan engineered to occur ever again" — blaming Bitmain for the 2017 BTC/BCH split (more coins for exchange speculation, more "now obsolete" rigs sold).
- Essentialist definition: "If you alter Bitcoin, it is not Bitcoin. Bitcoin is a chain of digital signatures based on ECDSA"; changing block time, reward or adding RBF disqualifies a chain. "BTC may have the ticker, but, a ticker is an exchange mechanism"; speculation is "a cancer in Bitcoin… a HODL coin… a zero sum game adding no value but allowing open gambling".
- "Bitcoin is first, foremost, and always P2P electronic CASH."
- Patent-troll denial: "There is no such thing as a patent troll" — Shkreli/Daraprim was expired-patent market manipulation, not trolling; the concept is "how bad reporting and those with a socialist agenda sell the concept of patents".
- nChain practice: many patents free to use on BCH; some licensed in partnership — e.g. the SBI joint "dealer-less threshold wallet system". He disputes patent-league tables: 157 published filings found by simple search, "We have today hit 882 in the Whitepaper list that will end as PCT filings", using the PCT route to lock international priority dates.
- Private/permissioned chains: "The same as a private permissioned Internet. A joke." IBM "failed to understand the Internet" in the '90s and is "simply a buzz word company… a dinosaur and a sick one at that".
- Protocol stability by analogy: Pacioli's double-entry bookkeeping (1470s) has served business unchanged for 500 years; "Yes, Bitcoin needs to be stable… There is no blockchain, there is Bitcoin." BTC "stopped being Bitcoin a long time ago"; BCH must be taken "back to what it needs to be".
- On Ethereum: "ETH is a dead end. It is a joke really. This idea that running all apps a billion times instead of one"; a true distributed oracle requires "a user and two systems that do not know of the existence of the other FOR THAT CALCULATION"; "one vote one person one coin each, it is a joke".
- "Bitcoin was complete in version 0.1" — Core "call Bitcoin a failure as they never wanted what Bitcoin is. It is a capitalist system and they seek to make it socialist", but it is "socialist-resistant"; build on-chain, in script: "There are no layers outside or off-chain."
- Scaling philosophy: "The market is PoW. It is vote with 'skin in the game'… Not one dollar in the bank (PoS), but one dollar risked. Compete or wither. I do NOT subsidise."
- Biggest hurdle for Bitcoin: "thinking that it needs to be something else. Not accepting v0.1 was good… Bitcoin should be frozen and then, allowed to be built on"; full script capability will exceed anything ETH was conceived for.
- Adoption strategy: "cash first. This is the killer app" — stability and a business-friendly system; businesses need assurance the protocol "will not change before completion" (the SBI project runs over years); on-chain contracts, financial instruments, GST/VAT automation to follow.
- Savings worked example: a £100,000 deposit at 1% with a 30% marginal tax rate yields £700 net, and after inflation the real value is £98,260.98 — "You are not saving by placing money into a bank. You just have the convenience." BCH is deflationary; a BCH bank would pay savers under term contracts, "how banking used to work".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Legalistic essentialism: Bitcoin is defined by a bright-line technical-legal test (the v0.1 protocol, the ECDSA signature chain), so deviations are not versions but counterfeits — the same move his patent argument makes (property is implementation, publicly released matter is unpatentable). Historical analogy (Pacioli's ledgers, IBM's '90s networking failure) supplies the stability and open-vs-closed lessons, while polemical dismissal handles rivals (ETH, IBM, Core, PoS). Economic claims are supported with worked arithmetic rather than citation.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Ticker-vs-definition: denies that exchange tickers or market capitalisation make BTC Bitcoin — identity is protocol fidelity, directly inverting the mainstream "BTC is Bitcoin by consensus of markets" position.
- Anti-patent open-source orthodoxy: patents are celebrated as property and the "patent troll" concept rejected outright, against the crypto commons ethos.
- Store-of-value/HODL: speculation is "a cancer"; value comes from cash utility — against the digital-gold thesis dominant in BTC.
- Layer-2 scaling: "There are no layers outside or off-chain" — everything is built in script on one chain, rejecting Lightning-style architectures and Ethereum's world-computer model alike.
- PoS and coin-vote governance: legitimacy is "one dollar risked" via PoW, never "one dollar in the bank" or one-coin-one-vote.
- Enterprise blockchain: permissioned chains are "a joke" — there is no blockchain, only Bitcoin.

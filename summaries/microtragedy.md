# MicroTragedy™
**Date:** 2025-06-14 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/microtragedy
**Subtitle:** A Divine Comedy of Leverage, Faith, and Financial Theatre

## Core thesis
A short satire of MicroStrategy's transformation under Michael Saylor from an enterprise-software company into a leveraged Bitcoin acquisition vehicle — portrayed as religious cult, ritual theatre and performance art whose ending, the author insists, is already visible from orbit.

## Key arguments and claims
- The company's founding mission was "quietly replaced one Thursday morning with 'Just Keep Buying'"; revenue and product lines survive as relics, and the balance sheet is "a suicide note composed entirely in Satoshis".
- Saylor is drawn as a Pentecostal preacher and prophet whose confidence "could be used to re-tile the hull of an aircraft carrier", and who answers questions about liquidity risk by buying more Bitcoin.
- The strategy is characterised as "evangelical roulette": borrow at historic lows, buy an asset that "swings like a caffeinated toddler in a wind tunnel", "and pray to whichever market deity governs derivatives".
- The software division has been folded behind the balance sheet; it persists only as an unpaid intern "maintaining the one remaining software product on an ageing laptop powered by shame and stale coffee".
- Shareholders are depicted as cult faithful chanting "Buy the dip" while "the ship hits reef after reef, believing, with tragic sincerity, that water is just misunderstood liquidity."
- Corporate communications are liturgy: earnings calls as incantations and séances, investor letters as psalms, tweets as rosary beads — "In lieu of earnings, thou shalt issue exuberance."
- The leverage mechanics are framed as rehypothecated faith: Saylor as a "central bank" of conviction, "printing confidence at 10x leverage until the entire system collapses in a heap of holy conviction and expired call options."
- Literary pastiches carry the argument: Wilde — "To buy Bitcoin once may be regarded as fortune; to convert your entire corporate treasury is sheer carelessness." — and a Pratchett-style footnote on legends versus cautionary tales told "while issuing convertible bonds."
- Rebrand gags: "MacroHysteria—Where Hope Is Leveraged and Sanity Is Shorted"; "St. Michael the Martyr of Market Cap, Patron Saint of Poor Liquidity and Over-Quoted Tweets"; "MicroStrategy (Latin: Minimus Cognitus, Maximus Folly)".
- The predicted epitaph for the enterprise: "Revenue not recognized. Vision fully impaired."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Pure literary satire rather than formal argument: mock-epic and religious imagery, parody scripture, and pastiche of Wilde and Pratchett. The economic critique — leverage, convertible debt, volatility, the abandonment of a productive operating business for a speculative treasury — is carried entirely by the jokes; the piece argues by ridicule, not by model.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Mocks the BTC treasury-company model and the "Number Go Up" theology ("the Church of the Eternal Number Go Up") that mainstream Bitcoin culture celebrates as sound corporate strategy.
- Treats Saylor's store-of-value evangelism as hubris that "doesn't just defy gravity; it tries to short it."
- The target is BTC's financialised culture — leverage, conviction-as-analysis, perpetual accumulation — rather than its protocol; no code-level argument is made.

## Connections
Shares the religious-fraud satire register of "In Praise of Shadowled Ledgers" — priesthoods, sacraments and cults of crypto — and extends its theme that mainstream Bitcoin culture substitutes faith and theatre for verifiable proof.

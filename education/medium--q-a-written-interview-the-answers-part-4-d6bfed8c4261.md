# Q&A/Written Interview — The answers — Part 4 — core principles

- **Privacy, confidentiality and anonymity are three distinct legal concepts.** Privacy is the right of non-disclosure; confidentiality is implied with privacy (e.g. census data); anonymity means the individual "is not known and cannot be identified" — and "there is no reason for anonymity to ever exist" (Plato's Ring of Gyges: anonymity corrupts).
- **Bitcoin is an evidentiary contracting system, not an anonymous one.** "Bitcoin is a contracting system… The signed transaction can be used as evidence in law. This is not the case with an anonymous system." Contract requires confidentiality plus demonstrable agreement, which cannot be done in any anonymous system.
- **The ledger keeps records; it cannot serve tax evasion.** "Bitcoin is not designed to help tax avoidance. Nothing based on a blockchain can be… Bitcoin is designed to maintain records."
- **Wallet privacy is an implementation problem, not a protocol property.** "Bitcoin is private. The issue is the implementation. BIP32 is a joke and terribly thought out" — key-derivation and address-management practice, not the base protocol, is where privacy is won or lost.
- **Nodes are miners, and mining is professional competition.** Nodes are "capitalist and commercial — they are supposed to be in data centres"; pools compete professionally on performance, not on solidarity ideology.
- **"Distributed" has a concrete numeric meaning.** "Distributed means 3 or more. That is it. The reality, with banks and others, likely 2,000 or so in a confederated group of 20 main players."
- **Attack vectors are economic questions.** "It is all about economics and money. It always was." — security analysis reduces to the attacker's cost-benefit calculation.
- **Restoring disabled opcodes returns Bitcoin to its original capability set.** Reintroducing opcodes is "returning Bitcoin to what it is and was always to be" — the script system's full predicate power is part of the original design.
- **ICOs and tokenised securities are different categories.** ICOs are "a means to add old frauds in new bottles"; compliant digital IPOs, tokenised shares, equity and bonds are a legitimate, distinct category.

# Satoshi Nakamoto
**Date:** 2019-04-05 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/satoshi-nakamoto-a7c4cf21253e
**Subtitle:** Satoshi Nakamoto is an amalgamation of 富永 仲基 (Tominaga Nakamoto) and Ash Ketchum (サトシ; Satoshi).

## Core thesis
Craig's most direct first-person account of the Satoshi persona: the name combines Tominaga Nakamoto — the Tokugawa-era merchant-philosopher and "Eastern Voltaire" — with Ash Ketchum ("Satoshi") from Pokémon, a joke on The Economist's 1988 phoenix-of-money cover. The essay is both autobiography and manifesto: he rejects the cypherpunk milieu he moved in ("I was a part of the movement… Not because I agreed but because it needed to be stopped"), declares "code is not law", distinguishes privacy from anonymity, and claims Bitcoin was designed from 1998 to destroy anonymity and pre-empt the "totalitarian utopia" of code-is-law — with the protocol to be locked "within the year".

## Key arguments and claims
- The name's origin: "Satoshi Nakamoto is an amalgamation of 富永 仲基 (Tominaga Nakamoto) and Ash Ketchum (サトシ; Satoshi)" — a homage to Tominaga and a play on the Pokémon character, riffing on The Economist's 1988 phoenix article; "The moniker… was simply a way of being able to work and have some privacy in my life."
- Cypherpunk history: he "met Tim May back in the 80s" via DECUS and SunOS groups; he was "ridiculed by people who refused to understand that risk is a probabilistic ideal and can never be perfected and removed" — and by people "with no idea of people or society".
- Anarchy rejected: "anarchy is a utopian pipe-dream"; it is "the path to destruction… the path to totalitarian control", promoted by "wolves leading lambs"; "Anarchy is not one of them [ways to seek change] and never can be."
- Code is not law: "Code is inflexible, there is no heart or soul in code. The strength of the law is that it can be flexible." Those who believe in code-is-law "abrogate the responsibility that they have to the society they live in"; in a democracy you work to change law, not discard it.
- Privacy vs anonymity: "Privacy and anonymity are polar opposites"; "anonymity in its truest form is the heart of crime and deception. Those seeking to tell you that anonymity is in any way like privacy are seeking to draw you into the shadows."
- Tominaga as philosophical key: a Tokugawa merchant-philosopher, "anti-traditionalist, a logician, and an early capitalist in the days of mercantilism", the "Eastern Voltaire", who taught that "concealment is the beginning of the habit of lying and stealing" — the irony "concealed in the name" of a pseudonymous creator now stepping forward.
- Wealth philosophy: "I am Wesleyan"; cites Andrew Carnegie's The Gospel of Wealth (read beyond the title) as the key to his long-term plans — and unlike Carnegie he does not plan to retire in his 50s–80s; "I expect at least 30 years of work" on Bitcoin.
- Crypto as scam recurrence: ICOs "are no different to the dematerialised penny shares that floated around the Internet in the 90s as web IPOs"; frauds "pump false gains" under the banner of "democratising freedom" while removing "the controls that protect the average person".
- Bitcoin's true design goal: "Bitcoin was designed to scale to create something more than the casino that is finance"; the real market is "the exchange of goods between individuals for consumption", not the exchange-casino; "Bitcoin destroys anonymity in all its forms. It allows privacy… with an immutable evidence trail."
- The 20-year claim: "I spent 20 years designing Bitcoin. I started in 1998, and went through more iterations than I can ever hope to imagine and remember"; Wei Dai's b-money is dismissed as a "juvenile and ill-conceived call"; his company Panoopticrypt "means, all that is hidden".
- The lockdown pledge: "within the year it will be locked with the protocol fixed and the asinine changes removed… once it is set in stone, it will remain set in stone."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Autobiographical authority fused with intellectual genealogy: the argument proceeds by revealing the name's hidden references so that Satoshi's *intent* becomes legible through Craig's own biography — the pseudonym itself is turned into evidence. Philosophically he works by antithesis (privacy/anonymity, law/code, market/casino, capital/secrecy) and by literary-philosophical citation (Tominaga, Voltaire, Carnegie, Hamlet pastiche) to position Bitcoin as a Western-liberal, law-abiding construction against cypherpunk "techno-mystical religious dogma". The mode is confessional-prophetic: "right now, I don't care whether you believe me… In time, there won't even be any doubt."

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Cypherpunk origin myth: against the community's self-image as Satoshi's intellectual home, Craig casts the cypherpunks as the enemy Bitcoin was built to defeat — "To create Bitcoin, I used the very system of thought it is designed to collapse."
- Anonymity as a feature: the privacy-through-untraceability orthodoxy (mixers, Zcash, Monero) is inverted — anonymity "destroys" privacy and is "the heart of crime and deception"; Bitcoin's immutable evidence trail is the privacy-preserving design.
- Code is law: the smart-contract/crypto-anarchist maxim is attacked as totalitarian in tendency — law's flexibility is a strength, and code-is-law ends "without humanity".
- "We are all Satoshi" / founder irrelevance: the essay reasserts a single identifiable author with a 20-year design history and continuing 30-year plan, against the leaderless-mythos consensus.
- Democratisation-of-finance rhetoric: ICOs and DeFi-adjacent "freedom" talk are framed as Ponzi-scheme cover, not liberation.

## Connections
A centrepiece of his April 2019 Satoshi-authorship campaign (the month of the BSV delistings and his first lawsuits against critics), complementing the "Why the protocol is set" cluster with the personal origin story; the "set in stone within the year" pledge ties the autobiography to BSV's protocol-restoration roadmap. Cites Wei Dai's b-money and Tim May's crypto-anarchy as the foils, and Carnegie's Gospel of Wealth as his own frame; the privacy-vs-anonymity distinction is operationalised in the nChain threshold-patent essays such as "Saving research".

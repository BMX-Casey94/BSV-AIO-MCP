# Why Scaling on-Chain Works — core principles

- **Storage cost curves outpace block growth.** Storage capacity per dollar doubles roughly every 15 months — faster than Moore's law — so an aggressive block-size growth path converges to roughly 50% spare capacity at constant cost rather than outpacing the hardware.
- **The original 32MB limit was a software artefact, not an economic constraint.** "In 2009, the effective limit in software was 32MB — although this was only due to the limits of the software. For a commercial server, this would not have been an issue." The cap should simply be removed.
- **A concrete growth schedule is affordable.** Target path: no cap; 500TB total blockchain storage by 2030; terabyte-scale single blocks in peak times by 2030; 1.25TB blocks by end of 2021. By the 15-month doubling curve, the 2009-equivalent acceptable block size in 2018 was already ~8GB.
- **Node operation is commercial infrastructure.** Hobbyist Raspberry Pi and laptop nodes are explicitly out of scope; expecting home use and hobby nodes to be subsidised distorts a commercial system.
- **Scaling needs no new mechanisms.** No fee markets, batching or layered networks: "There is nothing special to be done in order to achieve this, it is just allowing commercial systems to compete." The conclusion: "Bitcoin scales."

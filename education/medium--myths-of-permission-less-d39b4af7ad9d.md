# Myths of permission-less — core principles

- **Users need not validate.** Bitcoin is a peer-to-peer system settled by miners; requiring every user to run a node and validate is a false account of how the system works.
- **Sound money, not “decentralisation” as a slogan.** Bitcoin’s design is money that is not easy to debase, secured by competitive mining, not by every machine self-validating.
- **Property rights bound permission.** With property there cannot be “permission-less”; rights stop where they impact others, and you do not have a right to alter the protocol.
- **Miners choose the rules.** Those who invest in hash power decide; developers may propose, they do not own the network.
- **Build inside the protocol.** As with the Internet, you have permission to build if you follow the rules and do not seek to change them.
- **The ledger is permanent evidence.** Bitcoin does not forget; settlement records remain and can be used years later.
- **Tokenisation is settlement, not novelty.** Tokenised securities and EDI-style markers predate Bitcoin; what Bitcoin changes is settlement, not the legal character of the instrument.
- **A genuine utility token is used, not traded.** Like a bus ticket, it is sold and consumed; if it is speculated on a secondary market it is not a utility token. Bitcoin itself is the utility token without middlemen.
- **ICO “utility tokens” are typically unregistered securities.** Substance over form: capital-raising dressed as a token is a securitisation of future receivables, already reachable by existing law.

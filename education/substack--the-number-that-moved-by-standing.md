# The Number That Moved by Standing Still — core principles

- **A legal system has no thermostat for real value.** It reads nominal text. When the unit of account inflates, every fixed nominal boundary silently relocates. The Statute of Frauds threshold held at $500 since 1952 has lost 91.6% of its real value while the words never changed.
- **Money’s currency was keyed to a physical fact that did not drift.** Coin’s indistinguishability held. A rule pegged to a nominal quantity decays where money’s rule did not. The same evidentiary instrument, calibrated to different positions on the identifiability axis, has opposite fates under inflation.
- **The placement of a writing requirement is a judgement about stakes.** It was made in 1952 real terms and silently moved by inflation into the ordinary small commerce the drafters meant to leave alone — converting a considered rule into a randomly assigned option to renege.
- **Correction has concentrated cost and diffuse benefit, so non-correction is predicted.** The number stays because staying is the path of least resistance. Indexation would fix the drift; it would not answer whether a value-keyed writing requirement is still the right instrument.
- **Recorded payment fills the small-transaction range from below.** Account-based payment generates records as a free byproduct. A rule written for a cash world is being operated in a ledger world, and neither its nominal line nor its documentary premise survives the translation intact.

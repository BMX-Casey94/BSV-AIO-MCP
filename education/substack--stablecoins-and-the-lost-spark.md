# Stablecoins and the Lost Spark — core principles

- **Fiat-backed tokens actualise the original design.** Satoshi’s 2009 remark to Martti Malmi — “Once it’s backed with cash, that might change…” — treats cash-backing as the spark that ignites adoption, not as a betrayal of digital gold.
- **Micropayments, automated payments and financial contracts need a stable medium.** Those use-cases presuppose value that holds over short durations; stablecoins stabilise the medium without compromising the ledger.
- **UTXO outputs are digital bearer instruments.** They are redeemable on presentation of the correct cryptographic signature; tokenisation works through OP_RETURN, PUSH DATA and script-based controls — locking conditions, threshold signatures and time-based constraints.
- **An unbounded, script-capable ledger can host stablecoins at global scale.** It can process billions of transactions daily, encode regulatory logic directly into transaction scripts, and host tokens without throughput or cost fragmentation.
- **Anti-fiat zealotry is technically incoherent.** Bitcoin is priced in fiat, exchanged for fiat, and gauged in fiat-denominated markets; gold-purist rejectionism is the “Neanderthal” position Satoshi already criticised.
- **Auditability is a design feature.** The system was built to be auditable, not untraceable: it lends itself to tax reporting, AML enforcement and institutional compliance. What it preserves is finality — what you pay, stays paid.
- **More advanced architectures go beyond permissioned wrappers.** On-chain collateralisation, algorithmic balancing and tokenised central-bank reserves sit on the same UTXO and Script substrate that primitive USDT/USDC instantiations only hint at.

# Q&A/Written Interview — The answers — Part 4
**Date:** 2018-09-19 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/q-a-written-interview-the-answers-part-4-d6bfed8c4261
**Subtitle:** I shall continue answering in order.

## Core thesis
In the fourth batch of interview answers (Q40–57), Craig declares ICOs, altcoins, decentralised exchanges, Monero and Lightning all fraudulent or technically hopeless, draws a legalistic three-way distinction between privacy, confidentiality and anonymity (only the first two are legitimate, and Bitcoin already provides them), and — two months before the November 2018 BCH fork — insists "there is no split", predicts Bitmain's bankruptcy and charges for Jihan Wu, and claims the BTC/BCH split itself was engineered by Jihan to sabotage his 2017 plan to block SegWit.

## Key arguments and claims
- **ICOs are fraud, not innovation:** "They are not crowdfunding, they are not digital IPOs. Basically, they are a means to add old frauds in new bottles." Altcoins are "a gamble to make money in a zero-sum game taking from others before they take from you. They are negative value."
- **Winner-takes-all:** "Blockchain is a winner-takes-all system, and there is only one solution that works — that is Bitcoin as it was originally designed... Long term, the market is simple. It is Bitcoin, and that is cash (BCH)."
- **Decentralised exchanges are a category error:** "no market makers, no large order books and low liquidity... making it decentralised for the sake of it being decentralised, it is a joke"; finance needs efficiency, and "Bitcoin is not and never was about making decentralised apps... rather, it is cash."
- **IoT/micropayments:** "Yes, I have multiple patents filed on this. I have over 100 papers on this field, and more are coming."
- **Privacy ≠ confidentiality ≠ anonymity:** privacy is the HIPAA-style right of non-disclosure; confidentiality is implied with privacy (e.g. census data); anonymity means the individual "is not known and cannot be identified" — and "there is no reason for anonymity to ever exist. There is no place where anonymity is not corrupted." He points readers to Plato's Ring of Gyges.
- **Bitcoin is evidentiary, not anonymous:** "Bitcoin is a contracting system... The signed transaction can be used as evidence in law. This is not the case with an anonymous system." Contract requires confidentiality plus demonstrable agreement, which "cannot be done in any anonymous system".
- **Bitcoin is not anti-state:** "Bitcoin is not designed to help tax avoidance. Nothing based on a blockchain can be... Bitcoin is designed to maintain records." On tax investigation: "Tax is a fact of life, if you do not like it, move to a place where it (tax) does not exist."
- **Monero dismissed:** "a bad joke waiting for a punchline. It is not even close to being anonymous"; "slightly less private than a fully naked flasher with a gun on Times Square at 8.30am on a Monday morning following the announcement of a large IPO."
- **Lightning dismissed:** "Lightning is a failure. It was before they started... LN is not private... as private as the front page of the New York Times on the day of the US election."
- **Wallet privacy is an implementation problem:** "Bitcoin is private. The issue is the implementation. BIP32 is a joke and terribly thought out."
- **Mining pools as capitalist competition:** SVPool will compete via "Capitalism"; BMG (nChain's private pool) won scaling tests because "we run it professionally. We do not care about some anarchist bullshit about home nodes and solidartity. We compete."
- **On November 2018:** "There is no split. Bitmain may find themselves bankrupt and Jihan, well I expect charges... Come November, we scale more, and more and more, and then when it has no cap, lock the protocol and make it business friendly."
- **PoSM ("proof of social media") is nothing:** "There is no PoSM. Time to learn this. Grow some balls." If SV failed to win majority hash: "We fight. There is Bitcoin or there is nothing."
- **Miner-count equilibrium:** "Distributed means 3 or more. That is it. The reality, with banks and others, likely 2,000 or so in a confederated group of 20 main players."
- **Opcode restoration:** reintroducing opcodes is "returning Bitcoin to what it is and was always to be"; nodes are miners, "capitalist and commercial — they are supposed to be in data centres. It is not socialist."
- **Attack vectors are economic:** "It is all about economics and money. It always was."
- **Revisionist history of the BTC/BCH split:** "I talked with Jihan about a plan to build hash power and a pool. He released ABC to stop that plan and undermine scaling BTC. He wanted a hard fork version of SegWit and a malleability fix... That is how we ended with a split. Not a fork, a split. The BTC/BCH split was engineered"; his pool plan "to block SegWit in early 2017" would also have blocked Bitmain's WHC project, and "We have a few patents that block them."
- **No hidden BTC controllers:** "No, just a bunch of idiots with no idea what Bitcoin is."
- **Personal coda:** after global adoption, "My retirement" — a PhD "on the effects of Quantum Time on Singularities", not before 2035, "more likely 2045".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Legalistic definition-splitting (privacy/confidentiality/anonymity, with HIPAA and contract law as anchors) combined with maximalist market-selection claims and ad hominem dismissal. He reasons from law and institutional reality (tax, due process, evidence) rather than cypherpunk values: money is a contracting and record-keeping technology, so anonymity is not merely impossible but outside "what money is". On the fork politics he argues conspiratorially but names a specific mechanism (Jihan's ABC release as sabotage of his anti-SegWit pool plan).

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **Anonymity as a cypherpunk goal:** flatly rejected — anonymity is impossible for money, undesirable, and corrupting; privacy and confidentiality suffice and already exist in Bitcoin. This cuts against Monero/Zcash and against the BTC privacy-enhancement roadmap alike.
- **Lightning Network:** declared a pre-failed, non-private dead end — against the then-consensus BTC scaling narrative.
- **"Decentralisation" as an end in itself:** "When people stop thinking that decentralised is the end in itself, then they may start to make progress"; DEXs and DAPPs are jokes; home nodes are "anarchist bullshit".
- **Anti-state/anti-tax framing of crypto:** repudiated outright — Bitcoin keeps records, signed transactions are legal evidence, and tax avoidance via blockchain is impossible by design.
- **Nakamoto-consensus majoritarianism:** if the hash vote goes against him, "We fight" — the economic/ideological commitment overrides capitulation to the longest chain.

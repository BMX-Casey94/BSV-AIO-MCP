# Hello Dave… — core principles

- **Scarcity is the issued token count, not the block size.** The money's scarcity is defined in the number of issued tokens — an integer. A block-size cap is not a monetary rule and was never the source of value.
- **Bitcoin is auditable cash, not digital gold.** A reserve-currency-for-banks reading (gold's early-banking role) is a misreading. The design is efficient, lawful money with an evidence trail — not a scarce metal analogue.
- **Mining is meant to be expensive.** Scale economics — expensive professional mining — are good for the network. The user-node ideal and a block cap that enables it are the opposite construction.
- **The audit trail is what makes Bitcoin legal.** Bitcoin can only be legal by keeping all records of transactions. The Bank Secrecy Act's purpose is not consumer protection; it is to ensure that financial institutions maintain records.
- **Off-chain channels exist to lose records.** Lightning and sidechains are designed to allow an anonymous system that deletes information. That is the property that got 1990s e-cash systems shut down, and it is Lightning — not the public ledger — that invites FinCEN action against institutions that deal with it.
- **Anonymous 1990s cash is the rejected predecessor.** CHL, Monex and DigiCash were not the model. Bitcoin was designed with an audit trail, and that trail is what lets authorities follow illicit use rather than making the money itself illicit.
- **Record-keeping obligations travel with jurisdiction.** Liberty Reserve sat in Costa Rica, not an FATF member, and Costa Rican authorities still cooperated with the US. Builders cannot assume a foreign host deletes the duty to keep records.

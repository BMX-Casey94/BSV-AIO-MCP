# Monetary Law and Blockchains — core principles

- **Blockchains do not fork into two of the same thing.** A so-called split is the creation of a new system that differs from the original, not a division of one asset into two.
- **Protocol identity is fixed by the original rule set.** Radical changes to the protocol produce a new product, regardless of what name or market recognition it carries.
- **Passing-off law applies to protocol governance.** If a new system does not actively inform consumers that it is not the original, it is fraudulently passing itself off as something it is a mere copy of.
- **Protocol immutability is a commercial guarantee.** Because identity is definitional, businesses and consumers can rely on the rules of the system they adopted remaining the rules of the system they hold.
- **Orphan blocks are designed behaviour, not failure.** An orphan block is a game-theoretic signalling device between miners; to the user it is irrelevant.
- **Reorganisations are part of the design.** Miner competition that produces orphans and reorgs is intended, healthy operation — not an attack on the network.

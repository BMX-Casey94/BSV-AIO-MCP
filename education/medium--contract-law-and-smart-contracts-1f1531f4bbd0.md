# Contract Law and Smart Contracts — core principles

- **Contract law is an economic-minimisation device.** It is designed to minimise three costs: "the cost of writing a contract; the cost of courts rewriting contracts; inefficiency (including behaviour) from inefficient, poorly written, or incomplete contracts".
- **Judicial precedent is a compression mechanism.** Courts "use and select the rulings that lead to the fewest exculpatory clauses", supplying standardised default terms so that contracts can be "as short as possible and feasible" while still delivering the necessary terms and conditions.
- **Design implication for smart contracts.** Do not attempt to encode complete agreements in immutable script: the legal system deliberately supplies defaults and residual rulings, so contracts should be built to rely on courts for the unwritten remainder rather than engineered to replace them.

# The "Perfect" Firewall…
**Date:** 2019-04-20 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-perfect-firewall-3d6971213a2b

## Core thesis
Security is economics, not perfection: it is a risk function in which you never spend more than the asset is worth, and availability is part of the bargain. Drawing on his 1990s infosec career — including a clash with Marcus Ranum's "perfect security" firewall doctrine — he argues Bitcoin "needs to be resilient and available", and that Core's pursuit of perfect security and perfect anonymity produces "a system that cannot work in the real world".

## Key arguments and claims
- Career authority: he "implement[ed] and secure[d] the Australian Stock Exchange as they first went online" and worked for Lasseter's Online casino; "none of them were perfectly secure. They were all secure enough."
- The economic rule: "Security is a risk function. You do not pay more than the value of the asset to secure it. If you do, you are not secure."
- Marcus Ranum ("Ranham"), the Gauntlet firewall and Trusted Information Systems sold "the lie of perfect security"; one side of the security triangle is availability, and Ranum's ultimate firewall was "a pair of pliers" — disconnect from the Internet.
- Case study: a federal government department, threatened with denial of service, disconnected for two weeks on that advice, halting payments to disadvantaged people; Craig was "ridiculed" for arguing they "should not disconnect under any circumstances — if anything implement multiple redundant paths".
- The harm test: "imagine Google disconnecting from the Internet" — a control that destroys availability fails the people the system serves; for late-90s payment recipients, two weeks without money "was a problem".
- Applied to Bitcoin: "Bitcoin does not need to be perfectly secure; there is no such thing. Bitcoin needs to be resilient and available."
- The enemy is perfectionism itself: "In seeking the perfect, we destroy any hope of achieving something that can be great" — and specifically Core, "in seeking perfect anonymity, they create a system that cannot work in the real world".
- Closing maxim: "As with all things in the world, there is no perfection, only risk."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Practitioner economics: security spend is bounded by asset value, and the CIA triad's availability leg disciplines any "perfect" confidentiality or integrity scheme. He argues by war story — a named antagonist (Ranum/Gauntlet/TIS), a concrete failure (the two-week disconnection), and his own ridiculed-but-correct dissent — then maps the lesson onto protocol politics, casting Core's anonymity goals as the same category error as the pliers firewall.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the era's privacy-maximalist drift (CoinJoin, confidential transactions, anonymity as the goal): perfect anonymity is unattainable and its pursuit breaks real-world usability.
- Contradicts security absolutism in protocol culture — the idea that any risk acceptance is heresy; for him "secure enough" with resilience is the engineering optimum.
- Implicitly defends BSV's usability-and-capacity emphasis against Core's conservatism: chasing perfect security "chase[s] a dream that can never be achieved".

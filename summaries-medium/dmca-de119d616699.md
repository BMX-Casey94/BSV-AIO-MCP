# DMCA
**Date:** 2019-03-19 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/dmca-de119d616699
**Subtitle:** I recommend a reading of the following paper:

## Core thesis
Written days after Twitter suspended his account, Craig recasts his feud with Twitter and its troll bots as a statutory copyright/platform-liability matter: bots that circumvent his blocks violate the DMCA's anti-circumvention provision (17 U.S.C. §1201(a)), and Twitter's failure to act strips its §512 safe harbour, making it criminally complicit. He issues a "third and final notice", threatens imminent legal actions, and ties the platform's alleged bias to funding from XRP and Blockstream-linked interests promoting "the sham Bitcoin copy (BTC)".

## Key arguments and claims
- Bots that bypass blocks/privacy controls are "designed to bypass computer controls" and so violate the DMCA's anti-circumvention clause, 17 U.S.C. §1201(a); anonymous trolls who bypass computer security are covered by the law.
- OSP safe harbour under OCILLA / DMCA 512 (Public Law 105-304) is conditional on prompt takedown; Twitter's refusal means the safe harbour "does not apply" and it loses mere-provider status.
- Privacy controls are a fundamental right "sacrosanct here within the European Union"; allowing them to be bypassed is a "criminal breach".
- Twitter suspended his verified account while shadow-banning valid users and leaving "illegal criminal actors", which "demonstrates the complicity of Twitter".
- He claims proof Twitter leaked his verification documents: images he posted for verification contained steganographic watermarks with time/date/recipient, and those documents later surfaced with trolls.
- Accuses Twitter of being funded by "people such as those using XRP and the sham Bitcoin copy or airdrop (BTC), that is funded by the mostly criminal group Blockstream"; one bot is "directly coded by and linked to Blockstream".
- Blockstream's "false version of Bitcoin" exists to enable Lightning "purely to bypass security controls and logging as required by the Bank Secrecy Act"; by contrast "Bitcoin is an immutable evidence trail that does not make drug sales on the dark web easy."
- Free speech gives no right to a platform or audience: "You have the right to talk but not the right to a platform"; trolls "can say what they want on their own platform and property, but they have no right to enter yours."
- Twitter is "either liable for the acts of those who post within it or neutral. But Twitter is far from neutral" — it promotes illegal securities/ICOs and pump-and-dumps while filtering politics it dislikes.
- Cites Rep. Devin Nunes' $250m defamation suit against Twitter as a parallel action; warns "We shall be starting a number of actions shortly."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Legalistic demand-letter mode: he stacks statutory citations (DMCA §1201, §512, OCILLA, Harassment Act 1997, BSA) and frames speech and privacy as property rights over one's own "platform and property". The argument escalates by conditional logic — failure to act "will be construed as complicity" — and by evidentiary claims (steganographic watermarks) presented as courtroom proof. Rhetorically it fuses personal grievance, conspiracy linkage (Twitter–Blockstream–XRP), and imminent-litigation menace.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Crypto's cypherpunk valorisation of anonymity and pseudonymous trolling is inverted: anonymous accounts bypassing blocks are criminals, and privacy is framed as the *victim's* right to exclude, not the troll's right to speak.
- The mainstream "permissionless, censorship-resistant platforms are good" instinct is rejected in favour of OSP liability, takedown duties, and EU privacy rights — a pro-regulation posture.
- His claim that Lightning exists to evade Bank Secrecy Act logging directly attacks the BTC scaling narrative of 2019, and his "Bitcoin is an evidence trail" line contradicts the "digital cash for the dark web" caricature embraced by some early adopters.
- The anti-copyright, "information wants to be free" culture of crypto is contradicted by enthusiastic use of the DMCA as a weapon.

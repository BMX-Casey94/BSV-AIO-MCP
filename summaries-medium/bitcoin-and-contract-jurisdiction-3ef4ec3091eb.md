# Bitcoin and Contract Jurisdiction
**Date:** 2019-04-11 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-and-contract-jurisdiction-3ef4ec3091eb
**Subtitle:** Jurisdiction (or the location where a case may be heard) can be a big issue on the Internet. In a contractual negotiation where the client…

## Core thesis
A very short post (six sentences of body) sketching the problem of jurisdiction in internet contracts and concluding, without elaboration, that "all of this can be rectified using smart contracts with bitcoin". The piece is a stub rather than a developed argument: it states the B2C/B2B jurisdictional distinction and the enforcement gap, then asserts Bitcoin smart contracts as the solution.

## Key arguments and claims
- Jurisdiction — where a case may be heard — is a major issue for internet contracts, e.g. a client in country A contracting with a web server in country B.
- The forum depends on contract type: in B2C negotiations the consumer can sue the business in the courts of his own country, justified because "a consumer is likely to lack the required resources to bring suit overseas".
- In B2B disputes, the competent court is that of the country in which the defending party is based.
- Winning damages does not equal collecting them: "Just because a party in a B2C case has won damages, it does not mean that there will be an international treaty to enforce the judgement."
- The resolution offered is a single sentence: "All of this can be rectified using smart contracts with bitcoin." No mechanism (escrow, arbitration scripts, choice-of-law clauses in code) is specified in this post.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method here is doctrinal summary plus assertion: he recites the standard private-international-law treatment of consumer vs commercial contracts (the protective consumer forum and the defendant's-forum rule in B2B), flags the enforcement gap left by absent treaties, and then gestures at smart contracts as the technical fix. Unlike his longer essays, no evidence, case law or code is offered — it reads as a placeholder or teaser for a longer treatment.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Implicitly contradicts the "code is law" / jurisdiction-free ethos: rather than denying law's reach, Craig treats jurisdictional doctrine as a real problem to be engineered around with Bitcoin-scripted contracts.
- Cuts against the era's "BTC is digital gold, not a contracting platform" narrowing: he assumes bitcoin (his bitcoin — BSV by this date) is a general smart-contract substrate capable of rectifying cross-border contract enforcement.
- No direct engagement with scaling or store-of-value orthodoxies; the post is too short to develop a controversy.

## Notable quotes
- "In a contractual negotiation where the client is in country A and the web server is in country B, the case may be heard in A or B based on whether the transaction was business to consumer (B2C) or business to business (B2B)."
- "Just because a party in a B2C case has won damages, it does not mean that there will be an international treaty to enforce the judgement."
- "All of this can be rectified using smart contracts with bitcoin."

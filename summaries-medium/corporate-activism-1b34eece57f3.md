# Corporate Activism
**Date:** 2018-11-10 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/corporate-activism-1b34eece57f3
**Subtitle:** Over the past 2 decades, Anonymous and the related splinter groups that this has spawned have been attacking business and more. This moral…

## Core thesis
Anonymous-style moral crusades against business are illegitimate, and the same activist mindset now threatens Bitcoin: proposed changes to Bitcoin Cash — specifically DSV (OP_CHECKDATASIG) — are "designed... to allow Bitcoin to be used anonymously and for criminal uses". Because fungibility comes from law rather than technology, tainted transactions destroy the legal neutrality of money and create liability for miners, exchanges, and every downstream holder. Craig's camp will stop this "in Bitcoin" using "hash, and then... law".

## Key arguments and claims
- No group or individual "has the right to say how a company should act other than to not use that company or to compete with it as long as the company is acting within the law" — activism beyond boycott/competition is illegitimate.
- Section headed "Miners are the shareholders": hosting platforms lose their "mere publisher" protection "as soon as they receive notification" of illegal content such as child-pornographic material; a site that restores taken-down illegal data "is guilty of a crime".
- Terror groups (FARC/FARC-V) are moving from kidnapping-for-ransom to kidnapping, raping and murdering victims "including children" and selling videos on the darknet — lower risk, higher profit, "one that will only lead to increased violence".
- He opposes "those who seek to alter Bitcoin to make it more 'criminal friendly'" — DSV is named as a change "specifically designed... to allow Bitcoin to be used anonymously and for criminal uses".
- Liability contagion: adding DSV would "make miners liable, exchanges liable, and, in-fact, anyone who EVER has a transaction with DSV in the path liable".
- "Money is fungible not through technology but as a result of law." A DSV transaction means "you have knowingly received a transaction used in an illegal transaction", making the recipient "the receiver of illegal property" and tainting "anything in the chain of transactions" with "the same loss of fungiblity" [sic].
- The threat: "ABC is not going to do this in Bitcoin. We will use hash, and then we will use law. We oppose anarchy and violence."
- Closing maxim: "Bitcoin is NOT permission-less. You have NO rights to violence."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is legalistic and moralistic: he imports notice-and-takedown liability, complicity, and receiving-stolen-property doctrine into protocol design, treating fungibility as a legal status that technical features can destroy. The argument chains guilt by association — anyone in a DSV transaction path is complicit — then escalates from courtroom reasoning to a raw power threat (hash war plus litigation). Rhetorically it pairs graphic atrocity examples (child abuse, FARC murder videos) with the technical proposal to frame opponents as enablers of violence.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts "permissionlessness" as Bitcoin's defining property — he flatly asserts "Bitcoin is NOT permission-less".
- Contradicts the "neutral protocol / code is law" view that miners and node operators bear no responsibility for transactions they process: Craig argues miners, exchanges, and even unrelated downstream holders become legally liable.
- Contradicts the mainstream view of fungibility as a technical/privacy problem solvable with better cryptography: he claims fungibility is conferred by law and forfeited by criminal-friendly features.
- Contradicts Nakamoto-consensus quietism: rather than letting the longest chain decide, he pre-commits to using hash power and courts to veto a protocol change — written five days before the 15 Nov 2018 BCH/BSV split, this is a direct hash-war communiqué against Bitcoin ABC's November upgrade (which included OP_CHECKDATASIG and CTOR).

# Transparency and Government
**Date:** 2019-11-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/transparency-and-government-69c7e8f59180
**Subtitle:** There is an overwhelming amount of false information around Bitcoin and, in particular, on the topic of privacy or pseudonymous transfer…

## Core thesis
An excerpt (the body continues on craigwright.net) responding to a YouTube video asking "Will Blockchain Bring Freedom or Tyranny to China?". Craig argues the question is misplaced: Bitcoin is a transparent, traceable ledger — "anything but anonymous" — and transparency is precisely what keeps both people and governments honest. He also dismantles the video's gold-standard framing of Bitcoin with supply-history data, reiterating that Bitcoin was designed as peer-to-peer electronic cash for micropayments, not as digital gold.

## Key arguments and claims
- Core distinction: "Bitcoin is anything but anonymous. Anonymity requires that individuals do not know anything about the other party... Privacy, on the other hand, allows people to interact and keep identities away from the public" — the video collapses this into "a false dichotomy presented as a strawman".
- Blockchain adds nothing to a state ledger: "The existence of databases and ledgers precedes Bitcoin", and for "a fixed government-based stable coin, the existence of a blockchain becomes irrelevant. It is simpler to use a distributed database." Bitcoin's distribution matters only because "unrelated entities... compete in maintaining the ledger", so the system survives any single entity's failure.
- The real problem with central-bank currency "is one of seigniorage and alterations in the money supply" — not the lack of a blockchain.
- Transparency as moral technology: citing Plato's Ring of Gyges and H.G. Wells's The Invisible Man, "it has been clear throughout human history that no man remains moral without his actions being monitored and viewed. The same applies to both people and the institutions we create. Governments require transparency to remain honest."
- "It is not possible to create a blockchain-based system that is anonymous. It is importantly so, because anonymity helps breed corruption." Bitcoin "is a transparent system that allows monetary tracing".
- Fact-checking the video's gold claims: gold supply does not stay under 2% annual growth — "Some years have seen 8% changes to the total supply"; South African production after 1886 "radically changed the distribution of the global gold supply"; "Between 1980 and 1987, the production of gold in the US increased by 407%." Gold "has in fact been incredibly volatile in supply and demand".
- Anti-gold framing: "Bitcoin was not designed to replicate gold. The false story mirrors what the Bitcoin Core team wishes to promote, which radically differs from the goals that came with the white paper. Bitcoin is designed to be a peer-to-peer electronic cash system... designed to allow for micropayments across the Internet and small casual transactions."
- The video "treats Bitcoin as a strawman aligned to gold, which stands in glaring contrast to the actual aims of Bitcoin"; the excerpt ends as he begins enumerating "the most egregious" errors.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He opens with conceptual hygiene — separating anonymity from privacy — then attacks the opponent's framing as a strawman rather than its conclusions. Evidence style mixes classical-literary authority (Gyges, Wells) with empirical correction (dated gold-supply statistics) and engineering pragmatism (a state stablecoin needs a distributed database, not a blockchain). The white paper's design intent is the final court of appeal against both the video and "the Bitcoin Core team".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the cypherpunk anonymity/pseudonymity-as-freedom orthodoxy: anonymity "breeds corruption", transparency is the safeguard of liberty, and no blockchain can be anonymous by nature.
- Contradicts the "blockchain fixes state money" pitch popular in the era's China/CBDC discourse: for a government stablecoin, "the existence of a blockchain becomes irrelevant".
- Contradicts the digital-gold/hard-money narrative (fixed-supply virtue vs gold's ~2% inflation): gold's supply history is volatile, and Bitcoin was never meant to replicate gold anyway — it is cash for micropayments.
- Contradicts the framing of Bitcoin as a tool against the state: the freedom/tyranny question is "misplaced" because transparent ledgers discipline governments rather than escape them.

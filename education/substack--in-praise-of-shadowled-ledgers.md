# In Praise of Shadowled Ledgers — core principles

- **A signature proves control, not ownership.** Cryptography shows that a key was used; it does not identify who owns the key, what liabilities attach to it, or whether the funds were borrowed for the occasion. "There is no proof without provenance."
- **Property needs claim, title, identification and enforceability.** A private key "cannot testify" and "cannot be imprisoned"; a signature on a blockchain is not a deed, a wallet is not a safe, and a key is not a name.
- **Solvency is a net position, not an asset holding.** Point-in-time attestations mask insolvency: funds can be borrowed, signed over, and returned within the hour while liabilities stay undisclosed. A man with £10,000 cash against £100,000 owed is "bankrupt with pocket money".
- **Proof of reserves without identity is theatre.** Without a registered legal identity bound to signing keys, every such proof is fiction; any wealthy or colluding third party can rent solvency for an hour.
- **Bind keys to incorporated entities.** A registered master signing key should be bound to beneficial owners, directors, filings and physical addresses, with derived subkeys carrying declared purpose, scope, expiry and revocation — on the PKI/DNSSEC analogy. This centralises responsibility, not control.
- **Continuous attestation replaces curated snapshots.** Time-locked subkeys and near-real-time, independently verifiable liabilities ledgers should replace periodic point-in-time rituals.
- **Law is law; code is code.** An exchange accepting deposits or custody is a bank in all but name and must face registration, audits, reserve requirements and fit-and-proper tests; unsigned solvency claims should be treated as fraudulent per se.
- **Identity is responsibility, not tyranny.** Formalising identity closes escape routes; the industry's resistance to it is a cultural choice to preserve ambiguity, not a technical necessity.

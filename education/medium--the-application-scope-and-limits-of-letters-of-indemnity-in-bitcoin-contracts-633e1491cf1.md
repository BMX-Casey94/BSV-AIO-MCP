# The application, scope and limits of Letters of Indemnity in Bitcoin Contracts — core principles

- **Ledger-based trade documents inherit negotiable-instrument law.** A Bitcoin-based contracting or trade-documentation system inherits the entire law of bills of lading and letters of indemnity — title, receipt, estoppel and deceit — rather than escaping it.
- **The bill of lading's three functions.** Per Bowen LJ in Sanders v Maclean (1883): receipt for the goods, evidence of the contract of carriage, and document of title — "the key to the floating warehouse"; letters of indemnity arose because trade stalls when the original bill is unavailable at discharge.
- **Immutability does not cure false statements.** Documentary fraud consists of false statements entered into records (clean bills for damaged cargo; antedated shipment dates), not alteration of records — "antedated and false bills of lading are a cancer in the international trade" (Standard Chartered Bank v Pakistan National Shipping).
- **Letters of indemnity for false bills are unenforceable.** Against third parties in good faith an LOI has no effect; such LOIs have repeatedly been held illegal and thus unenforceable against anyone (Brown, Jenkinson v Percy Dalton), and even between carrier and shipper many jurisdictions refuse enforcement because both are parties to the same deceit.
- **Hague-Visby sets a floor, not a ceiling.** Bills of lading incorporate the Hague or Hague-Visby Rules (seaworthiness, care duties, package limitation, one-year time bar), and any clause decreasing those obligations is void; estoppel protections for innocent third parties cover falsely clean bills but antedated bills have few legislative protections.
- **Insurance and credit infrastructure assumes honest documents.** P&I Clubs exclude all liability resulting from ante- or post-dated bills (an LOI effectively replaces P&I cover), and documentary credits (UCP 500/600, Art. 32) demand clean transport documents — the pressure point that produces the fraud.
- **Liability rests in the tort of deceit.** Per Derry v Peek, a false representation made knowingly, without belief in its truth, or recklessly; a shipowner has no defence where a false antedated bill was issued even in a genuine though careless belief that it facilitated trade.
- **Intermediaries are policed by law, not protocol.** Banks issuing documentary credits, carriers as bailees and mutual insurers remain trusted, regulated parties; the failure modes are failures of honesty, which legal doctrine — not recording technology — polices.

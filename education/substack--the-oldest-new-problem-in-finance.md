# The Oldest New Problem in Finance: Proof of Stake and the Return of the Bearer Share — core principles

- **Bearer shares were abolished in a documented sequence.** Flagged by the FATF as a primary opacity vector between 2003 and 2005; effectively abolished in the British Virgin Islands in 2004; Luxembourg and Ireland followed in 2014; the UK banned new issuances under the Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015; Panama immobilised them under Law No. 47 in 2015 after the OECD declared the instrument incompatible with beneficial-ownership transparency.
- **A proof-of-stake token maps one-to-one onto equity.** It confers a residual claim on fees, MEV and issuance (a dividend), a stake-weighted governance vote (a shareholder vote) and a slashable security bond (a capital-adequacy requirement) — with title travelling with the private key: no transfer agent, registrar or identity check. A PoS token is a bearer share denominated in a different substrate.
- **The new substrate amplifies both historical dangers.** Transferability without record becomes settlement in seconds to any address on earth; governance without identity becomes a marginal cost of zero for each new pseudonymous address, so fragmenting a position to disguise concentration costs nothing.
- **Registered markets force visibility that pseudonymous governance escapes.** Crossing 5% beneficial ownership in the US triggers Schedule 13D/13G filings with the SEC, whereas a PoS actor can vote hundreds of pseudonymous addresses as a coordinated block; the effective Nakamoto coefficient, accounting for hidden common ownership, is by construction unobservable — a Potemkin democracy.
- **Staking concentration is measured, not theoretical.** As of early 2026 roughly 35.9 million ETH (about 29% of supply) is staked; Lido commands approximately 24% of staked ETH (down from a 2023 peak above 32%), Coinbase about 11.7%, Binance about 9.1%, and the top ten staking entities control over 60%.
- **Staking providers replicate the nominee pathology.** Depositors hold formal economic ownership but zero practical governance power, while the provider decides block proposals, MEV strategy and censorship as a de facto nominee with no fiduciary obligations.
- **Vote-buying becomes a product category.** The Curve Wars built explicit bribery infrastructure: Convex aggregated vote-locked CRV to control gauge emissions; Votium and Hidden Hand operated programmatic bribery markets processing tens of millions; Abracadabra committed over $10 million per voting cycle. In a registered-share corporation, paying shareholders to vote a particular way is a federal crime; in PoS governance it is a market.
- **Flash loans enable zero-cost governance capture.** On 17 April 2022 an attacker borrowed over $1 billion from Aave, acquired a two-thirds supermajority of Beanstalk Farms governance tokens, passed a proposal draining $182 million, repaid the loan within a single transaction and laundered proceeds through Tornado Cash — total governance control at zero economic exposure.
- **Securities analysis answers the wrong question.** The SEC's May 2025 statement (protocol staking as "administrative or ministerial", outside Howey) and August 2025 extension to liquid staking test reliance on others' efforts, while the bearer-share problem concerns anonymous ownership of governance power — even the dissent was framed in Howey terms.
- **Re-intermediation follows the historical tracks.** Custodial exchanges perform KYC (a partial shadow registry) and regulated wrappers — WisdomTree's physical stETH ETP, VanEck's late-2025 filing for a Lido Staked ETH ETF, Figment adding roughly 344,000 ETH in a single month — amount to a voluntary dematerialisation programme covering only the regulated fraction of stake. The builder's takeaway: governance power without beneficial-ownership transparency is a structural defect to design against, not a feature to inherit.

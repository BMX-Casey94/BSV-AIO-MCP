# Lessons in monetary terms
**Date:** 2019-02-25 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/lessons-in-monetary-terms-5e1493e0d197
**Subtitle:** The general understanding of money and finance is incredibly limited. I started to notice that people would quote authors where they had…

## Core thesis
A corrective glossary aimed at crypto discourse, which "quote[s] authors where they had not even read the text". Drawing on Mann/Proctor and English case law, Wright defines store of value, store of wealth and inflation hedge as distinct concepts: "store of value" is a legal property of contracts denominated in a unit of account (the principle of nominalism), not a property of an asset you hold. The payoff: bitcoin is a store of value only where a contract is explicitly denominated in it — merely "holding and saving bitcoin" confers no such status.

## Key arguments and claims
- Store of value defined: money is itself a commodity exchanged for commodities "whose immediate utility in consumption exceeds that of the monetary commodity"; its characteristic feature is measurability — "it forms a unit of account" — grounded in the principle of nominalism: "a USD100 note stores USD100 worth of value even in a period of high inflation".
- Legal anchoring of the unit of account: "a debt is not incurred in terms of currency, that in terms of units of account" (Adelaide Electric Supply Co. v Prudential [1934] AC 122) and "contracts are expressed in terms of the unit of account, but the unit of account is only a denomination connoting the appropriate currency" (Auckland Corp. v Alliance Assurance [1937] AC 587).
- The bitcoin corollary: "If a contract is directly denominated within bitcoin, bitcoin is a store of value for such an exchange. But, holding and saving bitcoin does not make it a store of value outside of a contract denominated explicitly in bitcoin."
- Store of wealth is a separate concept: it "maintains a capital position and the ability for an individual to consume at a given rate"; value shares/stocks are long-term but risky stores of wealth, bonds shorter-term and lower-risk; "Value and wealth are completely separate."
- Inflation hedge defined: a stability refuge when a unit of account suffers volatility — the USD serves this role for "many African countries and a number of South American countries", and gold in times of uncertainty or war; gold was a store of value only when it backed national currencies, "which no longer applies outside of contracts that are directly denominated in gold".
- Unjust enrichment: a recipient of money is presumed enriched by at least the amount received and "will not be heard to argue that money is not a benefit"; liability can extend to interest or exchange-rate fluctuation (Sempra Metals v IRC [2007] UKHL 34).
- Tracing and mixing: monetary value can mix into a larger fund without contributors automatically gaining proprietary rights in the asset; "Tracing follows the amount owed as a proprietary interest, and recognises assets belonging to certain claimants" (Foskett v McKeown [2001] 1 AC 102 per Lord Millett; LD Smith, Law of Tracing).

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Pedantic definitionalism backed by legal authority: each term is fixed by treatise (Mann on the Legal Aspect of Money, 6th edn; Goff & Jones on restitution) and House of Lords/Privy Council precedent rather than by economic assertion. The structure is textbook-style — numbered references, case citations — and the argument moves quietly from doctrine to a deflationary conclusion about the BTC "store of value" slogan without ever naming it directly.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **"Bitcoin is a store of value" / digital-gold orthodoxy** — a category error: value storage attaches to contracts denominated in a unit of account, not to an asset held; HODLing bitcoin makes nothing a store of value.
- **Goldbug framing of BTC** — gold itself only qualified as a store of value under gold-backed currency regimes, so the analogy fails on its own terms.
- **HODL-as-investment logic** — holding is at best a speculative store of wealth (risky, equity-like), never a store of value.
- **Pop-monetary citation culture** — the whole essay is a rebuke to quoting Austrian/monetary authors without reading them.

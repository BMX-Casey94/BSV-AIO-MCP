# The Chessboard, the Grain, and the Fee Market That Ate Itself — core principles

- **Exponential growth is systematically underestimated.** The chessboard parable supplies exact numbers: over a million grains by square twenty, over a trillion by square forty, and 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 by square sixty-four — roughly five hundred years of global rice harvest. Human inability to grasp exponential growth is a structural limitation, not a character flaw.
- **Fees denominated in the coin compound with the coin's price.** A fee of 10,000 satoshis costs $0.10 with the coin at $1,000 and $6.40 at $64,000 — the fee sat perfectly still in its native unit while its purchasing-power cost doubled with every doubling of the coin. On a fixed-capacity chain, price appreciation is not neutral to usability.
- **Appreciation also raises the satoshi fee itself.** A higher-priced coin attracts more holders and more transaction demand against a fixed supply of block space, so users outbid each other and the satoshi-denominated fee rises too.
- **The two compounds multiply; they do not add.** Coin doubles plus satoshi fee doubles means the fiat cost quadruples; three doublings of each yields a sixty-four-fold cost increase. Worked: $1 per transaction at square one becomes $4, then $16, then $64 — over $1,000 by square six.
- **The holder's usable wealth never improves.** Portfolio up sixty-four-fold, per-transaction cost up sixty-four-fold: the ability to use the wealth has not improved by a single cent. A payment system that cannot process payments becomes a "store of value" — the fallacy of the captured audience, a redefinition of failure as success that works only while the holder never actually moves the coins.
- **Detection is asymmetric by denomination.** Gains are denominated in the unit holders watch (dollars) and costs in the unit they ignore (satoshis), so each celebrated doubling obscures the simultaneous doubling of usage cost.
- **Fixed capacity replaces old intermediaries with dearer new ones.** The user who fled bank fees and gatekeeping receives miners he has never met, a fee auction he does not control, and a block size chosen by whoever controls the repository — one set of intermediaries exchanged for another that charges more.
- **The cost of repair escalates with delay.** Early, increasing capacity lets throughput grow with demand; later, repair requires a community-splitting fork and alienates every business model built on fixed scarcity; later still, the user base stratifies into institutional settlors and excluded retail, and the institutions have no incentive to reduce the fees that protect their position.
- **The design lesson is positive: capacity must grow with demand.** The original specification contained the scaling path — block size could increase, throughput could grow with demand, and the fee market could clear at stable prices. A Bitcoin network intended for payments and micropayments must keep the granary growing faster than the chessboard fills.

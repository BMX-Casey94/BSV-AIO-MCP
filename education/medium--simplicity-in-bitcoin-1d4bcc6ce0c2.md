# Simplicity in Bitcoin — core principles

- **Security and simplicity are largely orthogonal.** Neither complexity nor simplicity for its own sake adds security; what matters is engineered robustness. Complexity for its own sake will not add and may remove security.
- **The most robust systems enmesh simple and complex elements.** High-carbon steel is strong but brittle; low-carbon steel bends and can be reformed. Hybrid composites — simple and complex elements together — are the robust pattern.
- **Defence-in-depth can add security through introduced complexity.** Dual firewalls from separate vendors so neither suffers the same software flaw, and multiple anti-virus engines at gateway, server and data store, illustrate the point. The paradox is that this addition of complexity can create a simpler and more robust system.
- **Human factors re-introduce brittleness.** Multiple platforms defeat single-operator competence; adding specialists creates coordination stress points. Training and drilling temper this — the complexity must be operationally mastered.
- **Open source does not evaluate itself.** Even in the face of open-source code, software is not evaluated; malcode, bugs and vulnerabilities remain the major flaw in any system design. Open source has just as many flaws as closed systems.
- **Bitcoin's design centre is simple, fit-for-purpose cash.** Global, fast, P2P electronic cash — not a generalised ledger for fad applications. Complex systems in general are more prone to catastrophic failure: they are more brittle.
- **Security is a dynamical system, not a point equilibrium.** Although point equilibria exist, they do not exist in time; nodal minima require continual tuning and updates.
- **Sharding as a scaling strategy is computationally intractable.** Finding optimal network-aware sharding strategies is an NP-complete problem; if it were efficiently solvable then P=NP, which would also put the discrete logarithm problem at risk and make ECDSA easily reversed. Bitcoin scales without that architecture.

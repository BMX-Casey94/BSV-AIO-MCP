# Coin burning for dummies
**Date:** 2018-11-04 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/coin-burning-for-dummies-baa3cd14f915

## Core thesis
Scarcity alone does not determine Bitcoin's price — value comes from demand at the margin, which requires use. Using a two-asset model (BCH versus gold, then BCH versus Bitmain's WormHole Coin), Wright shows that *losing* coins raises the exchange value of the remainder, whereas *burning* coins into a substitute token leaves total money supply unchanged and gains holders nothing. Wormhole is then named as the scam: its "burn address" is a real key-controlled BCH address, and WHC is an unlimited proof-of-stake token — "digital fiat" — through which Bitmain and Bitcoin.com convert bitcoin into "a tax to Jihan".

## Key arguments and claims
- Anti-scarcity doctrine: "Many in the Bitcoin space think that scarcity of Bitcoin alone determines the scarcity and price of Bitcoin. This is utterly misguided and wrong. The total money supply is not only Bitcoin, but all money. If there is a substitute, then this is a part of the money supply."
- The diamond/water paradox: "this was the issue that Ricardo and Marx never grasped… The fact that something is scarce is not enough to make it valuable" — small vineyards producing under 1% of Moët's output can earn "under 0.75 Euro a bottle"; "it is not the fact that a token is scarce, it is the fact that it is in demand at the margin. For this, it needs use."
- Main Street, not Wall Street: speculation "adds to the volatility, and is necessary in any system", but "the long-term value and liquidity of a system come in use and circulation"; of 21 million coins, if 11 million are saved, "the value to the market comes from the 10 million in active use".
- Scenario 1 (loss): 1 million BCH lost from 10 million active coins chasing 10 million ounces of gold moves the ratio from 1 BCH : 1 XAU to 9 BCH : 10 XAU — "an increase of 11.1% in the exchange value of bitcoin to gold". With savings responsive, the marginal gain equilibrates "somewhere between >1.00 and <1.111 times the original".
- Scenario 2 (burn into WHC): 1 million BCH burnt for 100 million WHC leaves "9 million BCH PLUS 100 million WHC… chasing 10 million ounces of gold"; normalising 100 WHC as 1 WHC*, "we have 10 units (9 BCH units plus 1 WHC* unit) for each XAU" — "the supply of money has not changed, it has simply been altered". Holders "have gained nothing as WHC slowly (and insidiously) replaces bitcoin with a PoS".
- The headline distinction: "Burning bitcoin in exchange for another token cannot be compared to losing access to keys."
- The scam: "the WHC address is not a true burn address… The WHC address is a real BCH address that will be able to be 'found', if the initial fraud does not work" — and could be weaponised as "an 'oh, Bitcoin is not secure, move to WHC sham.'"
- Unbounded issue: "Once the quantity of BCH is expended, WHC as a PoS system controlled by Bitmain can simply issue more WHC. There is no way to stop this, it is not a set and limited system such as Bitcoin is designed to be. WHC is in effect digital fiat."
- The capture: "Users are forced to use WHC in the issue of other tokens and this returns to… You guessed it, Bitmain. It is not miner fees now, but a tax to Jihan. Welcome to the money that Jihan made. Roger and Jihan are NOT seeking sound hard money, they seek power and control."
- Overall: "The scarcity of the system is only one aspect of value. The real determination of value is in use. That is not HODL and exchange scams, but use as cash, use in a ledger unit, and any other use that people pay for."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Wright builds a stripped-down ceteris paribus exchange model — fixed goods (gold), fixed money, then a controlled shock — and compares the two shock types (loss versus substitution) to isolate the monetary effect. The theory is marginalist and velocity-flavoured: price forms at the margin from active circulation, with savings as a latent supply that re-enters when rates move. Classical name-checks (Ricardo, Marx, the diamond/water paradox) frame the error he is correcting; the polemic layer then maps the model onto named actors (Bitmain, "Jihan", "Roger", Bitcoin.com) with fraud allegations and satirical footnotes ("PoS… otherwise known as Proof of Semen Sao or jizz").

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the scarcity/store-of-value orthodoxy (HODL, digital-gold, later stock-to-flow): "Money is not made more valuable as there is less of it" — value is use as cash, so deliberate supply destruction is economically pointless or worse.
- Contradicts the legitimacy of proof-of-burn and token pegs: burning into a substitute "cannot be compared to losing access to keys" because the substitute keeps the money supply constant — a direct attack on the 2018 tokenisation vogue.
- Contradicts the "code is law / trust the burn address" convention: he asserts the Wormhole burn address is a valid key-controlled address, so the burn itself is "a blatant" misrepresentation (developed forensically in the earlier Wormhole essays).
- Contradicts proof-of-stake as sound money: an issuable PoS token is "digital fiat" whose controllers "can just add more and more" — PoW's fixed supply is the only discipline.
- Intensifies his BCH-civil-war positioning ten weeks before the BSV split: "Roger and Jihan" (Ver and Wu) are recast from big-block allies into power-seeking monetary centralisers.

# Hello Dave…
**Date:** 2019-02-22 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/hello-dave-330c164a4aad
**Subtitle:** Hal Finney was an amazing man in many ways. Without him, Bitcoin would not exist today. Hal is a far better coder than I will ever hope...

## Core thesis
A eulogy-cum-reckoning for Hal Finney: Hal was the better coder and Bitcoin would not exist without him, but his cypherpunk instincts left damaging legacies — the block cap and the user-node ideal — and his "digital gold" vision misunderstood what Bitcoin was designed to be: an auditable, lawful money. Wright recounts his own late-2010 disillusionment as WikiLeaks and Silk Road adopted Bitcoin, claims vindication in Silk Road's prosecution, and argues that Lightning and sidechains recreate exactly the record-destroying anonymity that got the 1990s e-cash systems shut down — making Lightning, not Bitcoin, the legally vulnerable system.

## Key arguments and claims
- Hal's indispensability and superiority: "Without him, Bitcoin would not exist today. Hal is a far better coder than I will ever hope to be"; the initial versions "fell over and crashed repeatedly" — Wright is "not a specialist".
- Hal caused the block cap: "Hal wanted people to run nodes early on. He is the reason that we had a block cap. I knew that I should have fought back and not have allowed it." Scarcity "is defined in the number of issued tokens… an integer" — not in block size.
- Hal's digital-gold misreading, quoted: Bitcoin as "ultimately becoming a reserve currency for banks, playing much the same role as gold did in the early days of banking" — Wright: "It is not what Bitcoin is, nor is it what Bitcoin was designed to be."
- Hal partially came round: his December 2010 line — "Ultimately it's good for the network for mining to be expensive" — shows he began to see scale economics, "but by the time I was already disillusioned".
- Wright's 2010 departure explained: WikiLeaks' promotion of Bitcoin, then Silk Road, made Bitcoin seem to deliver "everything I was opposed to"; Bitcoin was designed with an audit trail, "not to be even remotely similar" to the anonymous CHL, Monex and DigiCash systems; he left "to prove to myself that the system I had created worked the way I'd intended. It does, and it always did."
- Vindication: "Because of Bitcoin, Silk Road was stopped, and many of the people behind it are now in prison" — the audit trail worked; eCash, by contrast, had been accepted by Deutsche Bank before shutdown, while community attitudes ensured "no bank or responsible organisation was touching" Bitcoin.
- Lightning and sidechains as record destruction: they "are designed to lose records, to allow the creation of an anonymous system that loses records"; the BSA's purpose "is not consumer protection, it is to ensure that all financial institutions maintain records"; "the irony is that Bitcoin can only be legal by keeping all records of transactions."
- Legal exposure via Grinberg's 2011 paper: FinCEN "has power to prevent US financial institutions from dealing with institutions that deal with Bitcoin, which would essentially outlaw it in the US" — Wright's gloss: "Well, not Bitcoin, but, Lightning."
- International enforcement precedent: Liberty Reserve was based in Costa Rica, not an FATF member, "and yet, Costa Rican authorities cooperated with the US government".
- Teaching philosophy and output: "Learning is a privilege, not a right. It has to be earnt. It is a proof of work in itself"; with "close to 1,200 papers in our patent pipeline" he will exceed Edison and aims for 10,000 papers.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Memoir as authority: personal history with Hal Finney is used to claim interpretive priority over Bitcoin's design intent, with selective Finney quotations arranged to show convergence toward Wright's position. The legal argument proceeds by statute (BSA/FinCEN, FATF) and enforcement precedent (Liberty Reserve, Silk Road) rather than cryptography, recasting an engineering debate (Lightning vs on-chain) as a compliance question. The tone mixes tribute, grievance and vindication narrative.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **"Digital gold" / store-of-value BTC narrative** — attributed to Hal and rejected as cypherpunk baggage; Bitcoin was designed as efficient cash with an audit trail.
- **The block cap as sacred** — reframed as Hal's mistake that Wright regrets not fighting.
- **Lightning as legitimate scaling** — it is a record-destruction mechanism that invites exactly the FinCEN action Grinberg warned about.
- **Cypherpunk anonymity as Bitcoin's heritage** — inverted: anonymity is what got the 1990s systems killed; Bitcoin's legality depends on total record-keeping.
- **"Run your own node" culture** — traced to Hal's early influence and treated as a design error.

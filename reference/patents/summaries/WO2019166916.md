## Problem addressed
The specification says automated voting can authorise blockchain transactions through majority decisions or threshold signatures, but existing processes struggle when parties hold unequal numbers of votes and only a subset need consent for a motion to pass or a transaction to be signed.

## Core mechanism
Shares of a first common secret (in the embodiment, shares of a signed blockchain transaction, i.e. a threshold signature) are distributed so the secret is recoverable only with a threshold number of shares. Participants may hold different numbers of shares. Each participant is given an estimated reliability (probability they will vote as expected); that value is used as a cost (for example cost = 1 − reliability). Share proportions times reliability are sorted into computational bins whose bounds are rational numbers from a Stern–Brocot tree (or Stern series). A knapsack algorithm (dynamic programming is worked through) then selects a combination of participants whose expected contributions meet the threshold, optionally with a safety margin. If voting or reliability profiles change, the same computation is rerun. Dependent claims describe the common secret as a digital signature assembled from partial signatures and shares of a private key and an ephemeral key.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a voting process that distributes threshold shares of a first common secret and, using a knapsack algorithm on share counts multiplied by estimated-voting-behaviour coefficients, finds at least one combination of participants that supplies the threshold. Independent claims 9 and 10 are a system and a storage medium that perform that process.

## BSV relevance
The specification applies the scheme to signing a blockchain transaction with a threshold signature. Selecting a reliable subset of unequal share-holders is relevant to BSV multi-party wallets and threshold-spend policies.

# The Law of Controlled Amnesia — core principles

- **Cash is tolerated bearer finality, not a thing.** Perpetual recoverability would destroy liquidity. The tolerance is scalar: strongest for small ordinary payments, collapsing as value, opacity and systemic risk rise. A five-pound coffee and a ten-million-pound suitcase are not the same institution.
- **A small cash payment is presumptively transactional; a very large cash transfer is presumptively evidential.** Small cash solves a transaction-cost problem; large anonymous cash creates a verification-cost problem. Digital systems inherit the structure wholesale.
- **Code may settle the ledger; law settles the rights.** A technically final transfer can still generate proprietary, restitutionary, criminal, tax, fiduciary and sanctions consequences. “Code is law” fails for the same reason strong cash-finality fails.
- **Digital assets are property, not cash.** Rivalrous by software design, they sit in the Law Commission’s third category. “Digital cash” is a legal-governance category, not a technical one.
- **Blockchains do not abolish governance; they relocate it.** Forks, upgrades, exchange delisting, validator censorship, stablecoin freezes, court orders and developer discretion are the relocated seats. Genuine decentralisation is a question of fact for trial, not an immunity conferred by description.
- **Recovery is real but proof-dependent.** Tracing, following, unjust enrichment and civil recovery run through control points that answer to courts. Custodial stablecoins freeze more easily than banknotes; transparent ledgers trace more easily than privacy coins.
- **A currency that remembered everything would not circulate; it would litigate.** The right posture is calibration of bearer finality, not abolition of cash and not absolute irrecoverability.

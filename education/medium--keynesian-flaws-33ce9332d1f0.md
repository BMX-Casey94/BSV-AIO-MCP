# Keynesian flaws. — core principles

- **The knowledge problem applies to money and to protocol governance alike.** No small group of bureaucrats — or developers — with limited knowledge can out-decide a market; presuming otherwise is central planning in disguise, and treats the consumer as wrong when they choose differently.
- **Deficit stimulus spends the future for present political gain.** Make-work projects and aggregate-demand management do not create wealth; government spending should be set at the amounts of government goods and services the public would demand at full employment.
- **Fixed-supply money removes the monetary lever from unelected officials.** Controlling the money supply is power over all our lives; a money whose supply cannot be manipulated removes that ill-gained power.
- **Governance legitimacy tracks economic exposure.** Decision rights belong to those with skin in the game — miners and investors who bear the cost of error — not to developers with little or no economic interest in the system.
- **"In Bitcoin, it is the miners who decide."** Miner choice, expressed through proof-of-work investment, is the legitimate governance mechanism; the protocol's path was set in stone in 2009.
- **Sound money requires scaling plus stability.** "What will make Bitcoin a stable and sound money is for it to scale" — business craves stability: a sound platform, a protocol that scales.

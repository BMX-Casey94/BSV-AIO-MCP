# Rights and Tracing
**Date:** 2019-09-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/rights-and-tracing-603bee35c584
**Subtitle:** Identification of ownership rights in Bitcoin and assets on the Bitcoin blockchain through payments and mixtures would be an exercise in…

## Core thesis
The Medium body is an excerpt; the full essay continues on craigwright.net. Craig argues that ownership rights in bitcoin can be established through the equitable doctrine of tracing (per Lord Millett in Foskett v McKeown), and that courts can freeze specific UTXOs or addresses via orders implemented by miners and exchanges — without needing to know the real-world parties. The BTC claim that "all nodes vote on the protocol" is a false narrative: home users are not nodes, because in a proof-of-work system only miners who produce valid hashes act as nodes.

## Key arguments and claims
- Tracing vs following, per Lord Millett in Foskett v McKeown [2001] 1 AC 102, 127: "Following is the process of following the same asset as it moves from hand to hand. Tracing is a process of identifying a new asset as a substitute for the old." Payments and mixtures on the blockchain are "an exercise in tracing."
- Prediction: "It will be easy to show the false narrative created from Bitcoin Core (BTC) when court orders start requiring the seizure of coins in the next 12 months."
- "Many money launderers, people smugglers, drug dealers, and others will find that the promise of money which cannot be taken by government is not one that Bitcoin delivers" — a promise never made "when I was posting as Satoshi."
- The seizure-proof-money narrative came from those "fleeing the sinking ship of Liberty Reserve and e-gold" who piled into Bitcoin and "sought to change Bitcoin into something that it was never designed to be."
- Freezing mechanism: a court could order "a particular UTXO or address to be frozen… without knowledge of the real person or parties behind a theft or fraud", with the order "issued on and implemented by the miners and exchanges of the Bitcoin protocol."
- The "decentralised system of nodes and all nodes need to vote on the protocol" argument was "designed to fool regulators and law enforcement" and promoted by BTC camps; it is "exactly the system that James Donald wanted, which is exactly the opposite of the system I created."
- Jurisdiction: English courts' power to grant freezing injunctions over overseas assets derives from s.37(1) of the Senior Courts Act 1981, recognised in Derby v Weldon (No 6) [1990] 1 WLR 1139.
- "Home users are not nodes": Bitcoin is a proof-of-work system; "Nodes act only when they are validating a transaction AND providing a proof-of-work demonstrating that they did so correctly." The hash is a costly signal — "in biological terms, the hash is the equivalent of a peacock's tail."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Craig argues as a lawyer-technologist: he imports English equitable doctrine (tracing, worldwide freezing injunctions) with precise citations, maps it onto UTXO mechanics, and issues a falsifiable prediction (seizures within 12 months). He then attacks the opposing model at its definitional root — what a "node" is — using white-paper originalism plus a biological analogy (costly signalling, the peacock's tail / handicap principle) to explain why non-mining "nodes" are inert. The mode is didactic-prosecutorial, naming James Donald (the first person to reply to Satoshi's white paper post in 2008) as the origin of the misreading.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the foundational BTC orthodoxy that bitcoin is seizure-resistant "money that cannot be taken by government": court-ordered UTXO freezing is presented as both lawful and technically implementable.
- Demolishes the node-democracy model central to BTC's scaling-debate identity ("all nodes need to vote on the protocol"): non-mining home nodes have no protocol role, so miner/exchange enforcement is not capture but design.
- Recasts early Bitcoin history against the community's self-understanding: the privacy/seizure-resistance ethos is attributed to Liberty Reserve and e-gold refugees, not to Satoshi's design.
- Contradicts the "1 user = 1 node = 1 vote" egalitarianism with a costly-signalling account in which only capital-backed miners participate in consensus.

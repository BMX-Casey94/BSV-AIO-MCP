# Money is a measuring stick — core principles

- **Money is a measurement of stored debt.** It is a call on society for past exchange — a marker for selling into the ledger and taking back out of it — not a valuable in itself.
- **Value sits in the goods, not the token.** What can be obtained through the ledger has value; shekels and talents began as grain receipts against communal stores.
- **No planner can compute the whole economy.** Supply and demand across all goods is too complex for any individual or machine; that is why a global ledger measures, rather than directs, exchange.
- **A growing capitalist economy is naturally deflationary.** Produce 110 and consume 100 and prices fall; inflation is often used to hide that growth.
- **Scaled Bitcoin is neither inflationary nor deflationary.** In a stationary economy, a rise in Bitcoin’s price is only speculation; stability is the design goal of a global measuring stick.
- **A national currency measures political concerns.** A global ledger would let people track the international economy — once it has the capacity, which is a decades-long build.
- **Scale means all banking, not a card network.** The next decade’s test is secure settlement of every banking transaction, with no doubt as to whether it occurred.

# Currency — core principles

- **Currency in the narrow sense is a creature of sovereign fiat.** Modern coins and notes "rest on the fiat declaration of a sovereign"; the legal-tender monopoly is "more an issue of public policy than of use… a political desire to control the money supply".
- **Bitcoin is commodity-based private money.** It stands "in the nature of other private specie-based monies including coined gold and silver" — money by common consent and conduct, not by state declaration.
- **Payment in currency is final on delivery.** Under the negotiability rule of *Miller v Race*, "at the instant of delivery, the underlying obligation or debt is satisfied in full" — the receiver need not assess the payer's creditworthiness, unlike a cheque, where delivery is only the initial step and exposure lasts until clearing.
- **A valid 0-conf transaction is final payment.** Where a transaction is valid and sent without prior double-spends, the receiver "may be assured of good payment that has finalised" once it meets miners' acceptance conditions — the only alternative outcome is "a conspiracy to defraud them in the nature of a counterfeit note".
- **A double-spend attempt is an attempt to defraud.** Offline receivers and payment-channel arrangements replicate the cheque scenario — trust pending broadcast.
- **Tokens on Bitcoin follow chattel rules.** Under *Brown, The Law of Personal Property* (§9.3) a transferee of chattel property gets only the transferor's title, so stolen tokens, contracts and in-game items (the "magic sword" example) are recoverable — the game operator "would be obliged to allow the owner to recover the digital goods".
- **Form and use, not material, decide the regime.** *Chapman v. Cole*, 78 Mass (12 Gray) 141 (1858): a minted gold token was treated under chattel rules, not money rules — "It was insufficient that the token was of gold."
- **Ledger transparency creates legal notice — and miner liability.** A victim who proves theft and notifies "the super majority of the mining network" before the thief moves the coins puts miners on notice: "The miners could now be held liable, if it is found that they accepted and processed the transaction from B to a later party." No such notification infrastructure exists yet, but building one "would not be infeasible".
- **The currency principle protects downstream innocence.** Bitcoin is simple to map to the last known address, but proximity decays fast (six blocks ≈ one hour); once coins reach innocent hands for consideration, "the currency principle would make the subsequent transfers good".

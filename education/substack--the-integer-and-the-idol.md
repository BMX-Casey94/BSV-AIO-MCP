# The Integer and the Idol — core principles

- **There are no decimals in Bitcoin.** The ledger counts indivisible satoshis in integers. One coin is a fiction of convenience naming 100,000,000 satoshis. A satoshi and a half is a contradiction in terms.
- **The terminal supply is 20,999,999.9769 coins.** The subsidy halves by binary right-shift; when a halving lands on an odd satoshi count the fraction is discarded. The shortfall of 2,310,000 satoshis was never created, because creating it would have required an arithmetical lie.
- **The denomination is a dialect; the integer is the language.** The decimal point is presentation-layer costume. Moving it repaints the display while the ledger counts unchanged integers.
- **Issued at inception, distributed in time.** Cap, schedule and terminal figure were fixed and complete from the first instant. Miners are clerks handing out a sealed inventory, not mints creating value. A defined-at-inception supply admits no discretion.
- **Property requires an integer.** Fractions invite rounding errors whose residue comes to belong to whoever is closest to the rounding. The integer ledger assigns every satoshi to exactly one output, making ownership total, unambiguous and defensible.
- **Sub-satoshi divisibility lives in overlays.** Channels and token schemes are second-layer bookkeeping that settle to the base in whole satoshis. Divisibility is a property of the system that records the value, not of the token itself.
- **A thing you can divide indefinitely is a thing you can be robbed of imperceptibly.** The ugly total is the badge of integrity; the round slogan is the badge of a lie.

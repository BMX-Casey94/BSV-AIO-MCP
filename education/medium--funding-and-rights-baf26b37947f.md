# Funding and rights — core principles

- **A right to bitcoin is not the bitcoin.** On a custodial wallet or exchange you have a claim on funds, not the funds themselves — the same form as any other digital right.
- **Large organisations need audit and control, not slogan self-custody.** Shareholders and directors will not manage keys; the lesson of lost custody is supervision, not “not your keys” absolutism.
- **If Bitcoin is not used, it has no value.** Digital gold already exists as certificates, and as an asset it is only a money-laundering tool; Bitcoin is valuable as a ledger and as cash.
- **Exchange balances are digital rights.** Tether, Bitfinex and Binance holdings replicate the same custody risk: hacks, embezzlement and disappearances of other people’s money.
- **Privacy is not anonymity.** The design is traceable money; altering the protocol toward anonymity is how you help organised crime.
- **Price-to-the-moon talk is a scam.** Value is made through the actions you take; wash trades and social-media pumps are the fraud, not the protocol.
- **Trust conditions can be locked.** Once a trust’s rules are set, even the settlor cannot unlock assets except on those terms — the same discipline the protocol itself is meant to impose.

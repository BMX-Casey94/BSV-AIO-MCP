# You Cannot Hoard Your Way to Money — core principles

- **The unit of account follows the medium of exchange.** White (1984): an abstract numeraire divorced from any traded good has no operational significance. Monetary status is won only at the payments layer. Whoever wins payments wins pricing for free.
- **A capped coin engineered to be held manufactures the volatility that deters use.** With quantity fixed, every demand shift lands entirely on price. Speculative demand is fickle, transactional demand stable; attracting appreciation-seekers conflicts with attracting everyday payers.
- **Hoarding, generalised, prevents monetisation.** Individually rational holding (Lester's fallacy of composition) starves the circulation that would drag the unit of account behind it. You cannot hoard your way to money; you can only be spent into it.
- **Adoption is wallets opened; monetisation is bills paid.** Price and market capitalisation measure the speculative demand that is the obstacle. Opening a wallet is not accepting Bitcoin as a medium of exchange.
- **Electronic cash is optimised for use.** Fees driven toward zero so micropayments (Nakamoto's stated benchmark) are feasible; throughput scaled to real payment volume; a spend-not-hoard culture. Unbounded on-chain scaling and sub-cent fees are the design that matches that intent.
- **Value can be bootstrapped on anticipated payment use.** The Mengerian loop Nakamoto described — not store-of-value-first, medium-of-exchange-later — is the regression path. "Digital gold" is a later overlay on a design whose stated goal was to be cash.
- **A fiduciary layer of redeemable claims on a capped base is an open question, not a solution.** The honest escape from baked-in volatility is payments-first electronic cash, not a new bank sitting on a scarce token.

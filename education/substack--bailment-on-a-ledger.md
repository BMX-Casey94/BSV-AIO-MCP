# Bailment on a Ledger — core principles

- **The key is not the asset.** The asset is the tokenised right constituted by the ledger state; the private key is merely an instrument of control — a key, password or credential enables interaction with an asset but does not constitute it.
- **"Information is not property" has limits.** OBG Ltd v Allan establishes only that information as such is not property; it does not show that rights constituted, transferred and extinguished through an informational system cannot be possessed or held in custody. Your Response Ltd v Datateam is a boundary case about non-rivalrous data (a possessory lien over a database), not an abolition of digital possession.
- **Bailment has never depended on physicality.** Since Coggs v Bernard it concerns delivery, control and the allocation of responsibility following the assumption of custody; the inquiry is functional — does the mechanism transfer exclusive control sufficient to ground possessory consequences?
- **The Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 confirms a third category.** Certain digital assets are objects of personal property rights though neither things in possession nor things in action; Parliament deliberately declined to codify the incidents of this category, leaving them to the courts.
- **Recordation is delivery.** A token transfer is a state change, not a copy; whether output-based or account-based, the system enforces a single valid state, extinguishing the transferor's dispositive power and conferring exclusive dominion on the recipient — rivalrous exclusivity enforced operationally.
- **Key-copying is not delivery.** Copying a credential creates concurrent capability — the opposite of delivery, which excludes the transferor; sharing a key allocates a power over another's property, engaging fiduciary, trust or agency analysis, but not bailment.
- **Mistaken transfer shows bailment arising without title.** The recipient acquires possession but not title; subsequent unauthorised dealing is conversion under Fouldes v Willoughby — strict liability attaches to dealing as though entitled, not to mere receipt.
- **Possession-based remedies transfer directly.** The Winkfield (a bailee in possession may sue a wrongdoer for full value, holding proceeds on account); The Aliakmon (possessory rights exist independently of ownership); Morris v C W Martin & Sons Ltd (a professional bailee is liable for an employee's loss); Parker v British Airways Board and Costello v Chief Constable of Derbyshire (possession inferred from control and context, enforceable against all but a superior claim).
- **Custodial versus non-custodial is a factual inquiry, not a slogan.** A provider is a bailee only where it has assumed exclusive practical dominion excluding the client's unilateral control; blocked or non-recorded transfers do not crystallise bailment, and "was it recorded?" is an externally verifiable evidentiary boundary. Bailment fails only where delivery fails, and delivery fails only where exclusive control has not moved.

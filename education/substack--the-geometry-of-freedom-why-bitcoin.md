# The Geometry of Freedom — core principles

- **Freedom in a monetary network is throughput.** It is the rate of voluntary action the system makes possible; five transactions per second for eight billion people converts liberty into a queue — the monetisation of limitation.
- **Every bottleneck breeds a broker.** Throttled throughput resurrects intermediaries; custodians become indispensable because the network made independence impossible. It is not scale that centralises; it is constraint.
- **Engineered scarcity is an edict, not a fact of nature.** Diamonds and bread derive value from production; a developer-imposed ceiling divorces worth from function. Gold’s scarcity is natural; Bitcoin’s throughput cap is contrived — and unlike gold, Bitcoin was meant to move.
- **Privacy is a property of scale, not of cryptographic patches.** Millions of transactions per second drown individual data in statistical noise; a five-TPS ledger is a beacon in which every movement is identifiable by timing, value and signature. Abundance is privacy’s only refuge.
- **The original design anticipated hardware growth.** Moore’s Law, bandwidth expansion and storage growth were assumed; there is no physical law that enforces a tiny cap. It is a decision.
- **Safety resides in volume.** Scale dilutes risk; constraint concentrates it. A network processing billions of transactions per second enforces equality mechanically and no longer needs ideology to claim it.
- **A system that cannot include all who wish to use it is unjust by design.** The ledger that cannot hold all of us belongs to none of us. Every genuine innovation followed one rule: scale or perish.

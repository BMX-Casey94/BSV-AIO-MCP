# Privacy and Bitcoin: Legal Boundaries, Economic Realities, and the Illusion of Technical Obfuscation — core principles

- **Distinguish privacy, anonymity and secrecy.** Privacy is lawful conduct without unwarranted intrusion within a traceable framework; anonymity conceals identity; secrecy suppresses even the fact of a transaction. Legal systems uphold privacy, tolerate limited anonymity, and consistently reject secrecy.
- **Mandated transparency is a binding design constraint, not an adversary.** KYC/AML obligations, suspicious-activity and large-transaction reporting, subpoenas and FATF harmonisation including the "travel rule" mean any mechanism promising "absolute anonymity" is untenable — "anything 'untraceable' inevitably stands out."
- **Privacy is indistinguishability within a large anonymity set — an output of scale.** One large transfer is a signal; hundreds of small notes are noise. Market analogy: a sudden block trade moves prices and attracts inquiry, while the same position accumulated gradually in small trades preserves informational privacy and avoids front-running.
- **Throughput ceilings destroy privacy.** A one-megabyte block cap — "no more than about seven transactions per second globally" — is a governance choice of scarcity that makes privacy-by-scale mathematically impossible; millions of small, standardised transactions are what produce the statistical fog.
- **Script-masking is not flow privacy.** Taproot masks script complexity (one branch or ten) but not transaction flows; under mandated origin-and-destination traceability, script-masking is irrelevant — "at best, Taproot conceals trivia."
- **Aggregation concentrates risk; dispersion dilutes it.** CoinJoin's large, irregular many-input/many-output footprint is instantly flagged, risks treatment as unregistered mixing (already prosecuted in multiple jurisdictions), and shrinks the anonymity set to the visible participant pool — "a beacon, not a shield".
- **Lawful privacy by construction: the £700 case.** Alice pays Bob £700 as hundreds of £0.50–£2.00 notes within Bob's posted min/max policy, using ECDH-derived invoice-scoped addresses transmitted IP-to-IP as standard P2PKH outputs — lawful and auditable individually, unlinkable in aggregate.
- **Law does not criminalise smallness.** "There is no statute against paying in coins or breaking a sum into many lawful notes. What law targets is deliberate concealment through non-standard obfuscation." A salary paid as hundreds of small notes remains fully compliant — declared, logged, auditable — while the employee's later spending stays unlinkable, with selective receipts proving rent or tax and nothing else.
- **Builder guidance.** Build privacy from scale and standard transactions with retained, selectively disclosable records; do not bolt on obfuscation tools that are legally indefensible and economically backwards.

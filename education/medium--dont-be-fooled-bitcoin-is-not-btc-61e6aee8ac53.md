# Don’t be fooled — Bitcoin is not BTC — core principles

- **Bitcoin is private, not anonymous.** It suits people who do not want a purchase on a credit-card bill; anonymity breeds crime, and crime kills freedom.
- **The goal is honest, legally enforceable cash.** To be money, Bitcoin must be neutral and friendly to most people, not a tool designed for crime.
- **No cryptocurrency can abolish debt.** Debt is contractual; banning it would ban contracts and trade. Bonds and repayable public works can be built on Bitcoin.
- **The ledger is an immutable evidence trail.** It is a traceable log that meets the requirements of sound, honest money; earlier anonymous e-cash systems ended in court.
- **Peer-to-peer cash and the miner database are separate.** Two parties exchange a transaction; miners, expected to aggregate into competing data-centre firms, append it under a fixed protocol.
- **Decentralisation is a locked protocol.** The majority adds to a global database by consent of a set of rules; the moment developers can change those rules, users are trusting technocrats and the system is no longer Bitcoin.
- **A transaction signed today must be valid in 50 years.** Like TCP still connecting 1980s machines to today’s Internet, stability is what lets businesses invest; weekly protocol change is how you get IPX.
- **Rules in stone, not a living dictator.** The designer’s job is to set the protocol and leave it; an absentee lock is the opposite of benevolent-dictator governance.

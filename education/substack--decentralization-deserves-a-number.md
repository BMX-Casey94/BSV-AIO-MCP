# Decentralization Deserves a Number — core principles

- **Producers are adopters, not legislators.** They choose which chain, client or fork to run; they do not author the rules they operate under. Counting nodes tells you almost nothing.
- **Decentralisation is cross-layer separability.** Measure whether rule authorship, software implementation, block production, legal enforcement and liquidity coordination are staffed by different people. A near-empty overlap matrix certifies separation; a filled one exposes a consortium.
- **Layer 3 has the least authority over the others.** Miners and validators are the layer everyone fixates on. A chain whose token cannot be priced or moved is technically alive and economically dead.
- **Proof-of-work buys flexibility; proof-of-stake buys commitment.** Bonded stake is a switching cost. After China's 2021 mining ban, Bitcoin recovered half the lost hash in about two weeks; Ethereum's largest validator exit took roughly 83 days to clear half its backlog.
- **Lock-in is a coordination phenomenon.** Under feedback, systems exhibit tipping points. An inferior standard can survive purely because everyone is already on it.
- **The public ledger has a permanent blind spot where private cost lives.** Pool electricity contracts and hardware efficiency are off-chain. A null result that teaches you where the data runs out is not a failure.
- **The method, not a single number, is the point.** Equal entity-counting ignores economic weight, and entity resolution is fiddly. Replace slogans with a layer-by-layer test.

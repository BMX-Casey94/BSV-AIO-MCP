# Stable by design — core principles

- **Protocol design is an economic function of the system's purpose.** "The design of a protocol is a function of the system and organisation it is designed to deliver and it is always an economic function. Bitcoin, is first and foremost cash."
- **Money is critical infrastructure and must be boring.** The end goal is "to make the protocol as boring as the global plumbing it reflects"; Bitcoin works when most people see it as boring — "When, it is simply cash."
- **Lock the base protocol, then patch and monitor.** "To build a platform, you set the system in stone" — the base layer changes only for critical issues, while apps, high-level languages and use-cases are built on top of the immutable ledger.
- **The market for money wants 20–30 years of stability.** Businesses need predictable sound money, not cool features; developer incentive mismatch (CV-padding through protocol churn) is the governance failure to guard against.
- **Security is an economic risk calculation, not perfection.** Some risks are accepted and not all can be fixed: "If you aim for perfection, you aim to always lose."
- **Old attacks work; new attacks cost money.** Most breaches come from unpatched systems, off-whitelist applications and poor practice — not zero-days, which are "the proverbial nuclear weapons. Once they are used, they are depleted."
- **Trust must be signalled.** Banks historically used Greek and Roman frontages to convey stability; a money protocol needs the equivalent signal — "Bitcoin needs to be plumbing."

# Forex accounting in script
**Date:** 2019-02-24 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/forex-accounting-in-script-51984db05c6f

## Core thesis
BSV's restored script can host Ricardian contracts — legally enforceable agreements that are simultaneously human- and machine-readable — and Wright demonstrates with an automated forex/accounting system: VAT and customs handled at transaction time, tokenised tax remittance, atomic swaps with automatic gain/loss recording, and Bank Secrecy Act reports filed "without even the click of a button". The whole construct rests on nChain patents granted or pending in roughly 80–100 jurisdictions, and rival chains such as EOS/block.one infringe that IP, rendering their "smart contracts" legally unenforceable without a licence.

## Key arguments and claims
- Ricardian contracts as the blockchain "killer application"; Wright traces his involvement to conversations with Ian Grigg — "he had an imagination that allowed him to see a space elevator but not how to create it" — and claims his own work predates the 2018 revival of interest.
- Law over code: "I've been saying it for many years: law is law, and code is mere evidence at best"; enforceable electronic contracts must satisfy paper-and-ink-equivalence requirements debated since 1980s EDI admissibility problems.
- Patent EP3257191B1, "Registry and Automated Management Method for Blockchain-Enforced Smart Contracts": priority date February 2016, granted in the EU, being granted "in around 80 countries including the USA".
- Ideas versus implementation: "To get a patent, it is necessary to not have a mere idea but to create a functioning solution"; "A mere idea does not matter, an implementation does."
- The EOS/block.one flaw: "you cannot create a legally enforceable contract using illegal means" — such systems "will need to license intellectual property to be run legally. A failure to hold a valid license invalidates the contract."
- Automated accounting example: an online store charges VAT at the time of transaction when selling locally, sells VAT-free internationally with customs fees incorporated, and uses "tokenised notes" to "instantly pay any and all tax that it has collected" — simplifying small-business contracting, stopping fraud, and letting governments collect revenue "with minimal interference to business".
- Forex mechanics: "Linked to a common exchange, a smart contract could complete an atomic swap and record the foreign-exchange evaluations", storing all gains/losses for automatic regulatory reports; the system can incorporate consumer credit and hire purchase, use Bitcoin as base protocol or as the exchange rail between national currencies, and automates Bank Secrecy Act filings.
- Identity layer: nChain's granted patent "number 42" (EP3268914B1) provides "a deterministic system that allows pseudonymous publication that is linked immutably to identities", enabling legally binding contract signature.
- Scope claimed: five contract scenarios in the patent (public asset registry; asset registry with hidden ownership; lease contract; rolling contract; contract conditionality) capture equities, property titles and rolling forward-payment contracts; deeds and land titles to be explained "later in the year"; integration via XBRL, XML, JSON and project-management tooling (his PMP and Prince2 teaching background).
- Certainty pitch: with grants due in "close to 100 jurisdictions" by year-end, "developers have certainty when building on Bitcoin (BSV)".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Patent-first argumentation: priority dates, grant jurisdictions and enumerated patent scenarios are deployed as trump cards over rival designs, and legal doctrine (admissibility, paper equivalence, licensing validity) is treated as the binding constraint on system design rather than cryptography. A worked small-business example (the VAT-paying online store) grounds the abstraction, and credentials (LLM in International Commercial Law, 2006 publications, PMP/Prince2) are cited as authority. The mode is proprietorial and dismissive — competitors are infringers, not peers.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **"Code is law" smart-contract orthodoxy (Ethereum/EOS)** — inverted: code is "mere evidence at best"; a contract failing legal form requirements is void, and unlicensed execution is itself invalidating.
- **Permissionless innovation free of IP encumbrance** — he asserts a patent-licensing precondition for legally operating a smart-contract chain.
- **Anonymised contracting** — replaced by deterministic keys that are pseudonymous yet "linked immutably to identities".
- **Crypto-tax-resistance culture** — the design goal is instant, automatic tax collection and BSA reporting built into the payment itself.

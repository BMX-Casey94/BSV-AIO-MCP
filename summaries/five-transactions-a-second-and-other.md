# Five Transactions a Second, and Other Discourtesies to Commerce
**Date:** 2026-05-19 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/five-transactions-a-second-and-other
**Subtitle:** Why gold knew its station, and what happens to a currency that has forgotten its own.

## Core thesis
BTC's roughly five-transactions-per-second ceiling makes its "digital cash" claim arithmetically impossible, leaving it a settlement asset — "a broken digital bullion" — that competes with gold while lacking gold's honesty about its own role. The Lightning Network and custodial balances that supposedly fix this are not Bitcoin but claims against it, reconstructing the nineteenth-century gold-and-notes hierarchy while pretending the paper is the base. Gold is closer to cash than Bitcoin Core, because gold is not pretending.

## Key arguments and claims
- The arithmetic: ten-minute blocks of roughly one megabyte (two to three under SegWit's witness discount) yield three to seven transactions per second — five is the figure used in serious commentary — about 430,000 transactions per day. Against eight billion people making one purchase each, the queue would take approximately fifty years to clear.
- The comparison set: Visa processes around 65,000 transactions per second at ordinary peak, Mastercard similar, Chinese interbank systems larger still; a mid-sized Asian convenience store chain on a single Saturday afternoon processes more than the Bitcoin Core network does in a week.
- Gold's five thousand years: it settled debts between merchants, cities and empires but never functioned as the daily medium — the aureus, solidus and sovereign were instruments of "the upper register of commerce" while peasants paid in copper, silver fractions, credit and notes. Every commodity-money system at scale has a heavy base and a light circulating instrument; "the Bank of England note in 1850 did not claim it was gold; it claimed it was redeemable for gold".
- The category error: "digital cash" and "digital gold" sit at opposite ends of the monetary stack; "the thing that settles cannot, at the same volume, be the thing that circulates". No white paper "can repeal arithmetic".
- If BTC occupies the gold seat, its cash must be something else — Lightning channel balances, custodial exchange deposits, tokenised IOUs, stablecoins — all "paper, in the older sense of the word": structurally weaker claims against the base. "The user does not transact in Bitcoin. The user transacts in something that promises Bitcoin."
- Mempool mechanics: fees rose from cents to fifty dollars and beyond during the minor shocks of late 2017, spring 2021 and the 2023 ordinal episodes; at genuine cash demand inclusion becomes an auction — "a two-dollar coffee paid for with a fifty-dollar fee is not a payment system. It is a curio."
- Exclusion of the small payer destroys cash's defining property of universal accessibility: what remains is "a settlement asset for the rich".
- Lightning is a separate protocol with its own security and trust models: channels lock funds in multi-signature outputs; routing requires intermediaries with liquidity in the right direction, online, honest and non-censoring. Hubs are "in every economically meaningful sense, banks"; non-custodial cryptography "rearranges the failure modes; it does not abolish them". "The existence of the second layer is the confession of the first."
- Five consequences of actual cash-scale use: fee escalation to hundreds of dollars; migration of users to custodians and stablecoins; institutional concentration as hubs gain economies of scale; regulatory capture as AML/KYC attaches to the new intermediaries; and security-budget degradation — the subsidy tends to zero while the throughput cap forbids the fee volume needed to replace it.
- Gold beats BTC on three counts: physical transfer is genuinely peer-to-peer with no mempool or fee market; gold's settlement role is honestly stated; and gold's circulating instruments never pretended to be gold.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Rhetorical arithmetic plus monetary history. The method is to state the throughput numbers plainly, scale them against population and incumbent payment volumes, then map the result onto the historical base-versus-circulating-instrument structure of commodity money. Lightning is analysed institutionally — by the functions its hubs perform, not the guarantees its scripts give.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The "digital cash" self-description is the central target: the system "was sold as cash, built as bullion".
- "Layer two solves scaling" is inverted: Lightning is proof of the base layer's failure and reconstructs correspondent banking — "correspondent banking with extra steps and worse uptime".
- The fee-market-as-security model is presented as an unresolved bind: fees must grow as subsidy declines, but the cap forbids the volume required without choking the demand that pays them.
- The cypherpunk disintermediation promise is reversed in practice: scarcity at the base generates intermediaries "with the inexorability of a thermodynamic law", and those intermediaries become regulable banks under new names.

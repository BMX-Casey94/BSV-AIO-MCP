# Valuing systems — the margin of substitute goods.
**Date:** 2018-11-25 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/valuing-systems-the-margin-of-substitute-goods-891b47fe381e
**Subtitle:** In order to value a cryptocurrency, we need to start with what it really is and what a substitute good will be. This is important as all…

## Core thesis
A cryptocurrency can be valued like any product: by the price of its closest substitute goods. Since Bitcoin is a commodity ledger, its floor value is the fee revenue miners earn from usage. Comparing block capacity across BTC, BCH and BSV, Craig projects BSV miners earning up to $8,000 per block from fees alone — implying $640 per coin on exchanges "not factoring in the gambling price" — and touts nChain's Teranode reaching 1-TB blocks and 6.5 million transactions per second, with Visa predicted to run on Bitcoin SV within four years.

## Key arguments and claims
- Valuation method: "you can always value a product based on the closest alternative goods and services and the substitutes on market. For Bitcoin, this is as a commodity ledger" — past unvaluability stemmed from bitcoin being "a mere speculative guess as to the future".
- Capacity comparison: BTC — 1 MB (simple transactions); BCH — 8 MB (16-MB burst); BSV — 64 MB today, 512 MB within 6 months, 2 GB in a year.
- Per-transaction value: around $0.005 USD per transaction as they stand; other uses such as EDI would be worth more, "but only BSV is set for these".
- Per-block miner earnings from use: BTC — 4,000 simple transactions, $20 a block; BCH — 32,000–35,000 transactions, $160 a block; BSV — 200,000–300,000 transactions now, approaching 1 million within 6 months, worth $1,500–$8,000 a block.
- Subsidy sunset: all three chains pay a 12.5-coin subsidy halving to 6.25 in 2020, so "a base idea of value will come from use".
- Floor-value formula: "the minimum coin value at market to be the block earnings divided by the utility as capacity" — excluding the scenario where the better system wipes out the worse ones altogether.
- The $640 claim: BSV miners earning over $8,000 a block from use alone "equates to $640 a bitcoin on exchanges, and we have not factored in the gambling price of bitcoin, just what miners will earn as a service".
- Rejection of monetary-premium narratives: "Not the hype. Not the global-money argument. These ONLY come when bitcoin is a commodity money, and the ledger is the commodity."
- Teranode: nChain will scale Bitcoin SV "to handle over 1.0 TB within the next 3 years (aiming for 2)", with miners earning "over $600,000 for each Terabyte block… every 10 minutes on average".
- End-game capacity: 2–4 billion transactions per block within 2–3 years — 6.5 million TPS — equal to "Visa, MasterCard, banking in SWIFT, and ALL global currencies (not just crypto) in under 15% of a block".
- Visa prediction: "I expect that you will see Visa using Bitcoin (SV) as its backbone and plumbing within the next 4 years" — more secure, faster, and cheaper.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Substitution-based microeconomics: value is anchored to the market price of the nearest alternative service (ledger space, EDI, payment processing), then computed as capacity × fee per transaction. The method is arithmetic extrapolation — today's block sizes and the published scaling roadmap become per-block revenue, then a per-coin floor — combined with winner-take-all displacement logic ("the better system wiping out the worst ones altogether").

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- "Crypto cannot be fundamentally valued": he offers an explicit fee-revenue floor model instead of Metcalfe's-law or monetary-premium heuristics.
- Store-of-value / "global money" thesis: those premiums are downstream of commodity-ledger use, not sources of value — the hype is explicitly demoted.
- BTC's high-fee, low-capacity model: value scales with capacity, so BTC's 1 MB yields $20 a block against BSV's projected $8,000 — scarcity of block space destroys rather than creates value.
- Speculative price discovery: exchange price is decomposed into a computable service-earnings component plus a dismissable "gambling price".

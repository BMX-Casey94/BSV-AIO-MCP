# What is Bitcoin…. — core principles

- **Currency definition.** Bitcoin is "a form of digital or virtual currency using cryptography to control its creation and operation" — launched 2009 when the algorithm was released online, as an open-source protocol on a peer-to-peer network.
- **Mining validates the ledger.** The network "updat[es] and archiv[es] transactions in a shared public ledger or log … (known as the 'block chain')", and "new Bitcoin are created with each update to the ledger" as the miner's reward.
- **Ownership is a signature chain.** Each bitcoin "represents an allocation of value which is recorded in the public ledger and exist[s] as a chain of digitally signed transactions" — the whitepaper's definition of the coin.
- **Fixed issuance schedule.** New coins per ledger update are "halved every few years, such that Bitcoin become more difficult to 'mine' over time"; "the maximum number of Bitcoin that can be mined is 21 million".
- **Divisibility to the satoshi.** A bitcoin divides "up to 8 decimal points — a unit known as a 'satoshi'", and those sub-units are themselves transferable.
- **Institutional sourcing.** The system's mechanics were documented early by mainstream authorities — the US GAO's May 2013 report (GAO-13-516) and The Economist's 2013 explainer — fixing the factual baseline for later legal analysis.

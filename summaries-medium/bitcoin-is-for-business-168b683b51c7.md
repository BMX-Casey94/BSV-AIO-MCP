# Bitcoin is for Business
**Date:** 2018-11-20 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-is-for-business-168b683b51c7
**Subtitle:** The goal of Bitcoin is to be cash, but there is only one way to achieve this. Bitcoin must be a commodity ledger. To be money, Bitcoin…

## Core thesis
For Bitcoin to become cash it must be a commodity ledger, not a speculative gambling asset — scarcity alone cannot create value. Craig reports live-network tests hitting 1,500 TPS and 64-MB blocks, lays out a roadmap to 512 MB, 2 GB and then uncapped blocks, and claims this capacity will exceed all other cryptocurrencies plus PayPal combined and reach Visa-level volume, with Tokenized bringing legally registered securities and bank-issued digital fiat onto the chain.

## Key arguments and claims
- Scarcity fallacy: "If scarcity alone made something valuable, then many unwanted things would be collected and exchanged" — an obsolete Telex is extremely rare yet worthless; "Value does not come from scarcity, rather scarcity allows things that are in demand to be traded at the margin."
- HODL critique: for many, Bitcoin is a "short-term zero-sum game" and "get-rich-fast ponzi" built on "the false idea that money and value come from simple scarcity".
- Purpose redefinition: "Bitcoin was never about removing banks and government. It is sound money, a stable supply, and an efficient and audit-able ledger" — everything anonymity-and-anarchy seekers oppose "is at the heart of Bitcoin".
- Test results: a peak of 1,500 transactions per second and "the largest blocks on any public blockchain at 64 MB" in testing the previous night, by CoinGeek, SV and affiliated companies.
- Scaling roadmap: 6 months — 512 MB; 12 months — 2.0 GB; 18 months — uncapped (no limit); beyond that, "1-TB blocks and no limits".
- Comparative throughput: 512 MB ≈ 2,000 TPS sustained, exceeding "the combined peak rates for Ripple XRP, Ethereum (ETH), Bitcoin SegWit (BTC), and all other cryptocurrencies combined with the transactional ability of PayPal".
- Ripple debunk: Ripple's claimed 1,500 settlement exchanges are lab figures "and not real transactions/sec", against a "real sustained 175 TPS of XRP's reality".
- Visa-level claim: 2.0-GB blocks allow 8,000 TPS settled — "Visa-level transaction volumes (not what they do in a lab, but the real world)".
- Tokenized: a secure platform for issuing legally registered securities with bank-issued digital fiat on Bitcoin, enabling "all debit-card and other payment traffic on our chain for less than Visa or MasterCard can do".
- PayPal benchmark: "Today, the PayPal network only processes 194 TPS" against the test's 1,500 TPS.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Marginalist microeconomics (value arises at the margin from demand, not scarcity) fused with engineering-triumphalist benchmarking: TPS figures, block-size roadmaps and side-by-side comparisons with Ripple, PayPal and Visa. The rhetorical mode is debunk-and-declare — knock down the scarcity/HODL story and the competitors' lab numbers, then declare "Welcome to the future of finance. Today."

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Digital-gold / scarcity-value thesis (the stock-to-flow style narrative): explicitly rejected — scarcity merely lets demanded goods trade at the margin.
- Cypherpunk origin story: "Bitcoin was never about removing banks and government" inverts the anti-bank, anti-state reading dominant in 2018 crypto.
- Small-block orthodoxy: Visa-scale throughput on-chain, with the block cap treated as a temporary artefact to be removed within 18 months.
- Altcoin marketing claims: Ripple's and Ethereum's throughput figures are dismissed as lab numbers against SV's live-network results.

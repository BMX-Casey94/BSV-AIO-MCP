# The Imperative of Scalable Blockchain for Global Commerce
**Date:** 2025-06-26 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-imperative-of-scalable-blockchain
**Subtitle:** Scaling blockchain isn't just an option—it's the key to unlocking global commerce and avoiding economic decay.

## Core thesis
Blockchain in its current form cannot serve global commerce, and only a system engineered from the ground up for scale — millions of transactions per second — can act as the backbone of world trade. Craig argues that Bitcoin SV (BSV), built on Teranode, is the only system being developed that meets this requirement, and that nations which fail to adopt scalable blockchain will suffer economic isolation and decline analogous to protectionist Japan before WWII.

## Key arguments and claims
- There are 195 countries — "195 markets, each with its own set of regulations, currencies, and systems" — and a commerce-grade blockchain must handle transactions between countries, within countries, between companies and between individuals across all sectors.
- Existing scaling approaches are dismissed by name: "sidechains, sharding, and cross-chain communication" cannot deliver the millions of transactions per second that genuine global commerce demands.
- Current blockchains are "bogged down with slow transaction speeds, high fees, and inefficient consensus mechanisms", rendering them toys for speculators rather than tools for real business.
- The benchmark is the existing international financial system — stock markets and banks executing millions of real-time transactions daily — which requires "reliability, scalability, and instant execution", not occasional functionality.
- The goal is instantaneous cross-blockchain and cross-border trade under one overarching protocol: "No need for intermediaries. No need for hours of waiting while a bank approves a cross-border transfer."
- Achieving this demands massive investment in technology and infrastructure plus cooperation between businesses, governments and institutions — it is framed as "a philosophical one" as much as a technical one, requiring a model that "transcends national boundaries".
- A historical analogy does the geopolitical work: "Just like Japan in the lead-up to WWII, the U.S. cannot afford to fall into the trap of economic isolation." Protectionism, tariffs and trade barriers will reproduce Japan's resource-control failure.
- China is named as the pacing threat: it has "already embraced blockchain technology on a large scale" and will lead, leaving the U.S. behind if America refuses to scale.
- The conclusion names the solution explicitly: "There is only one system that can achieve this kind of scale, and it's already being developed: Bitcoin SV (BSV), using Teranode as its foundational technology."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The essay is an exercise in economic and geopolitical reasoning rather than formal proof. Craig moves from an arithmetic premise (global commerce requires millions of TPS across 195 national markets) to a technological claim (only ground-up-scalable architecture can meet it), then to a historical analogy (pre-war Japanese autarky) that converts a technical choice into a national-survival imperative. The method is imperative-driven: define the demand, disqualify the incumbents, present the single viable candidate.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the entire Layer-2/sidechain/sharding paradigm that BTC and Ethereum mainstreams rely on, asserting these "cannot handle the millions of transactions per second that true global commerce demands".
- Dismisses the "digital gold"/store-of-value framing implicitly: blockchain must be "a tool for real people doing real business", not "a toy for financial speculators".
- Contradicts the small-block premise that base-layer capacity should stay constrained; scale is the whole point, and the base layer itself must carry global trade.
- Rejects the multi-chain interoperability vision in favour of "one blockchain [that] could process transactions for all nations, industries, and markets" under a single overarching protocol — i.e. BSV/Teranode, not BTC.

## Notable quotes
- "To think that the digital age will proceed without a system capable of scaling for global commerce is the very definition of self-deception."
- "We need a blockchain that isn't just a toy for financial speculators, but a tool for real people doing real business."
- "Protectionism, tariffs, and trade barriers won't save the U.S. economy."
- "The world isn't going to wait."

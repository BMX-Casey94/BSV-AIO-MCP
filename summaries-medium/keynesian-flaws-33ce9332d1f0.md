# Keynesian flaws.
**Date:** 2018-09-14 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/keynesian-flaws-33ce9332d1f0
**Subtitle:** Now, it is generally taken [1] by rational economists without an agenda (and even most of the honest ones with an agenda) that unlike any…

## Core thesis
Keynesian economics rests on the conceit that bureaucrats can out-decide the market — central planning in disguise — and deficit "stimulus" merely spends the future for present political gain. Craig then maps this directly onto Bitcoin: the same neo-Keynesian mindset that captured "Bitcoin Core (AKA SegWit Coin)" is now, via ABC's developers, trying to subvert Bitcoin Cash, and this time "those with skin in the game" will oppose it.

## Key arguments and claims
- Against the Keynesian claim that any expenditure raises "aggregate demand", he quotes approvingly: "Government spending should be set at the amounts of government goods and services that the public would demand if the economy were at full employment; no make-work projects should be permitted."
- The core flaw: "The assumption is that government can choose better than the market" — that "a small group of bureaucratic public servants with… limited knowledge — can out decide a market. Any market this is."
- Krugman and neo-Keynesians conceal that their policy presumes "central planning works better than a market" and that "the consumer is wrong when they choose differently".
- On deficit stimulus: "all they have done is spend the future to make things politically better now"; pyramid-building and hole-digging have only marginally been left behind.
- Motive attribution: "Lord Keynes made a system that was designed to keep Keynes a Lord. Little more."
- Why Keynesians loathe Bitcoin: "they seek a system that allows them to control and manipulate society. In controlling the money supply, they have power over all our lives. With Bitcoin, these un-elected bureaucrats lose much of this ill gained power."
- The Bitcoin Cash turn: the same thought process that subverted Core is now targeting BCH — "as with Core, ABC — developers with little to no economic interest in BCH think they know better that [than] the market". He names Amaury Séchet as "promoting the idea that markets should not be trusted, that a group of developers know best".
- Opposition declared: "it is the first time that they will be opposed"; "No self professed 'Shit Lord' will ever again be allowed to use Bitcoin as a personal play thing and ground to recklessly experiment."
- The remedy: "What will make Bitcoin a stable and sound money is for it to scale"; business "craves stability" — "A sound platform, a protocol that scales".
- Governance claim: "In Bitcoin, it is the miners who decide. With respect to BCH, we decide that BCH is Bitcoin and will remain on the path it was designed for. A path that was set in stone in 2009."
- References cited are MMT-leaning: Wray's "Government as Employer of Last Resort" and Mosler's "Full Employment and Price Stability" — held up as the opposition's canon.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Hayek's knowledge problem fused with public-choice motive analysis: planners lack the knowledge markets aggregate, and their proposals serve their own power. The distinctive move is analogical transfer — developer-led protocol governance is equated with Keynesian central planning, so the economics critique becomes a governance critique. Legitimacy is allocated by "skin in the game" (Taleb's phrase): miners and investors decide; developers without economic exposure do not.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects developer-led governance (Core's and ABC's alike): developers with "little to no economic interest" have no legitimate say — against the mainstream norm of dev-roadmap authority and rough consensus.
- Asserts "it is the miners who decide", contradicting the BTC orthodoxy (cemented after the 2017 UASF episode) that economic nodes/users, not miners, define the protocol.
- Treats protocol change itself as central-planning subversion — against the mainstream view of ongoing protocol development as healthy maintenance.
- Frames BTC ("SegWit Coin") as already captured and no longer on the 2009 path — the foundational BCH/BSV polemic against BTC's legitimacy.

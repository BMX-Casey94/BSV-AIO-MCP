# Taking care of Business.
**Date:** 2018-11-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/taking-care-of-business-63430b62be2f
**Subtitle:** Going forward we will start to open up and allow business use of the Bitcoin Blockchain. Bitcoin scales and we will be demonstrating this…

## Core thesis
Written three days after the BCH/BSV split, this announces that nChain will open the Bitcoin (SV) blockchain to business use, demonstrating scaling first with 128-MB blocks and then moving to remove the block cap altogether. Craig argues Bitcoin is a commodity ledger whose long-term value comes from commercial transactions, not speculation — the "HODL fallacy" — and that price volatility is irrelevant to merchants because fees are stable in local-currency terms.

## Key arguments and claims
- Scaling demonstration: SV will run 128-MB blocks "in the coming days", then remove the cap entirely — "an unbounded blocksize and as many transactions as people are willing to send us".
- The HODL fallacy: the error in how people see Bitcoin is "the concept of getting rich off the work of others" — buying and waiting while others create value; the "long-term road is pathed using commercial transactions".
- Commodity-ledger framing: the blockchain is "a method to exchange value without the issue of double spending"; "it is not the value of the coin that matters" — volatility "does nothing to the use of Bitcoin as a commodity ledger".
- Concrete fee targets: a simple marker/record transaction "securely encoded" should cost a merchant under $0.001 USD; a more complex contract about $0.05 USD.
- Volatility immunity via local-currency pricing: merchants pay in USD, GBP or Yuan; the number of bitcoins required fluctuates but merchant costs stay stable because "the cost is paid as it is used".
- nChain's invention portfolio: "several 100s of inventions" to be provided to developers on SV — "simple and boring uses such as technological data plumbing": tax invoices, contract-exchange platforms, in-store purchase records auto-loaded into accounting systems.
- Privacy with auditability: records stored on the public blockchain "in a completely private manner and without loss", minimising fraud, loss and alteration, at a cost below paper-based invoice/receipt systems.
- Market definition: "So, what do we see as our market? The crypto community? No, we see our market as the world" — billions using Bitcoin "without even knowing that they are using Bitcoin".
- Hash-war resilience claim: even amid the ABC/SV dispute, "no loss of transactions would have occurred on the SV chain" and businesses need not concern themselves with the "hash war".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Cost-based commodity reasoning: he prices ledger space in fiat terms and treats the coin as a means of payment for a service, stripping the speculative premium out of the use case. Rhetorically he positions himself against ideology — "not one based on ideology and religious drive, but simple efficiency and value" — and uses the business-efficiency frame (invoices, tax records, accounting integration) to make blockchain invisible infrastructure rather than a movement.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Store-of-value/HODL orthodoxy: he explicitly attacks the idea that holding is the point; coin value "does not matter" to the ledger's use.
- Small-block conservatism: unbounded blocksizes and merchant sub-tenth-of-a-cent fees are the direct opposite of the BTC fee-market/scarcity model.
- Volatility-as-barrier argument: mainstream view says price swings block merchant adoption; Craig claims local-currency-denominated fees make volatility a non-issue.
- Community-centric marketing: the target market is explicitly not the crypto community but "any merchant in any country".

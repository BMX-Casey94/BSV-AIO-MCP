# Reversing Illicit Transactions on Bitcoin Is Simple
**Date:** 2019-07-24 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/reversing-illicit-transactions-on-bitcoin-is-simple-71a99cf14ec3
**Subtitle:** There is a lie that is propagated by those who seek to falsely claim that Bitcoin was created as an anarchist system…

## Core thesis
An excerpt of a longer essay that continues on craigwright.net. Craig argues that the claim Bitcoin was created as an anarchist system opposed to government and banks is "a lie" designed to make Bitcoin operate outside law and facilitate illicit activity. The technical hinge of the excerpt is his reading of white-paper section 8: Bitcoin was never a system where every user runs a node — nodes are miners — and on that foundation the full post argues that reversing illicit transactions is simple.

## Key arguments and claims
- The anarchist-Bitcoin narrative is a deliberate fraud: "There is a lie that is propagated by those who seek to falsely claim that Bitcoin was created as an anarchist system designed to oppose government and banks."
- It is "the scam that tries to change Bitcoin into bit gold" — a redefinition of Bitcoin into Szabo's different project.
- He quotes Nick Szabo as the foil: "Decentralization is what allows Bitcoin to substitute an army of computers for an army of accountants, investigators, and lawyers."
- He names the culprits: "a group of developers and those seeking to promote Dark Web sites that disseminate illicit material" started the campaign "to make people falsely believe that Bitcoin was about all users running the nodes."
- Technical correction: "Nodes are not individual users but a function of the verification system, a.k.a. miners" — grounded in section 8 (Simplified Payment Verification) of the white paper.
- Authorial authority: "In July 2010, I said how Bitcoin would evolve. It is not a system where every user has a network node."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Genetic-historical argument from authorship: he cites his own July 2010 statements and white-paper section 8 as the authoritative interpretation of Bitcoin's design, then attributes the opposing view to bad-faith actors (developers, dark-web promoters). The mechanism-level claim — nodes equal miners, users rely on SPV — does the technical work; the legal conclusion (illicit transactions can be reversed within law) follows from the premise that Bitcoin was designed inside the legal system, not against it. Rhetorical mode: expose-the-lie polemic anchored to primary-source citation.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "every user should run a full node" sovereignty orthodoxy of BTC culture — he calls it a fabricated campaign rather than a design requirement.
- Contradicts immutability-as-absolute ("code is law", transaction finality): the title asserts reversing illicit transactions "is simple."
- Contradicts the cypherpunk reading of Bitcoin as anti-state and anti-bank; he insists Bitcoin was never either.
- Turns Szabo's celebrated quote against its own camp, treating the computers-replace-lawyers vision as the scam rather than the goal.

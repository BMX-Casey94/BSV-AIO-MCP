## Problem addressed
Centralised and decentralised exchanges typically pool user assets. A successful attack on the pool can steal those assets, and pooling also breaks provenance of individual holdings. The specification wants matching and settlement without a common pool.

## Core mechanism
Users register with a matching platform. Each user opens at least one payment channel with the platform (per protocol or per token type) and funds the sell side. The platform matches orders, keeping a sequential order book with timestamps. Settlement is an update of the relevant channels; a preferred form is a multi-party atomic swap in which spending a first transaction reveals a secret that unlocks the next. Assets remain locked in the users’ channels: a platform compromise can block completion but does not seize the locked funds. Optional timelocks return unused value. The text discusses BitcoinSV payment-channel constructions and tokens of types Token1, Token2 and so on.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
A method identifies matching exchange instructions of first and second users and uses at least one payment channel with each user to perform the exchange. A system claim adds a matching component; equipment and program claims cover the method.

## BSV relevance
Payment-channel token matching without a pooled hot wallet is a BSV exchange design: users keep channel-locked value, and atomic secret-reveal spends settle matched orders.

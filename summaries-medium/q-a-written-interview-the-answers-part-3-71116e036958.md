# Q&A/Written Interview — The answers — Part 3
**Date:** 2018-09-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/q-a-written-interview-the-answers-part-3-71116e036958
**Subtitle:** I shall continue answering in order.

## Core thesis
In this third instalment of answers (Q27–39) to a community interview, Craig demystifies Bitcoin's design constants as pragmatic guesses, attacks Tether, ETFs, HODLing and permissioned blockchains as category errors, and frames Bitcoin as a winner-takes-all capitalist competition whose only meaningful scaling is miners in competition — with nChain positioned as a commercial IP house (patents, Terranode, 300+GB block tests) under no obligation to share anything.

## Key arguments and claims
- **The 21M cap is arbitrary engineering, not mysticism:** it "allows a 2 week difficulty adjustment, 144 block daily reward and more. It fits in a 64bit integer and has enough range to be useful... No real magic, it is a good enough guess." The 10-minute block target is just a workable range (7–13 minutes); the 4-year halving cycle "works well in econometric systems as a medium-term figure"; 50 BTC start "could have been 60, or 48".
- **Tether is not a peg:** "Tether is not a pegged coin. It is money creation and in time, the US will stomp on it and stomp hard." A fiat-backed token only works if issued by "a major Federal US bank or as a part of the US Treasury, or why bother?".
- **One blockchain survives:** "There is no long term blockchain other than Bitcoin... One survives, all others end worth nothing." National cryptocurrencies will only exist as "a token pegged on BCH"; "Permissioned blockchains are just like personal internets. Utterly useless."
- **Roadmap to 5 billion users is building, not marketing:** "Building. Others market. I am creating things that will only ever see BCH." Global adoption inflection hoped for "around 2030 to 2034".
- **Governance post-scaling is market competition:** "Software houses will target miners and exchanges, and the best software will win" — a distributed, possibly microservices-based system, not a single reference client.
- **Hashpower's role is conservative stabilisation:** "The role of a PoW system is to make the system stable, it is conservative. Miners choose to get as many transactions into a block as possible, and the fees and other aspects are the result of an emergent process."
- **ETFs are pointless for Bitcoin:** they solve gold's custody/verification problems, which Bitcoin doesn't have; "The entire concept that people are seeking to apply is worse than a joke."
- **Anti-HODL, pro-use:** "Use matters. Forget the HODL crap... if BTC or anything else hits zero (in the case of BTC — when). Wealth is about creation of the means of production. It is not about a zero-sum game."
- **Spain's silver as the canonical hoarding parable:** despite American silver flooding in, Philip II declared bankruptcy three times (1557, 1575, 1596); bullion "created no industry nor growth", funded consumption and inflation, and enriched rivals like the Dutch. Adam Smith: wealth is "the annual output of the necessaries, conveniences, and amusements of life", not bullion.
- **BTC-as-store-of-value inherits the mercantilist fallacy:** "Gold, printed fiat currency, and even BTC as a 'store of value' are only a store of future claims on the actual wealth of society" — real wealth is output of goods and services; Singapore and 1970s–80s Japan prove resource-poor states can get rich through trade.
- **nChain is building compilers mapping high-level languages to Script** ("we have patents and PCT applications pending"), ran internal 300+GB block tests (pointing to the SSRN paper on Bitcoin as the world's primary payment infrastructure), and is developing **Terranode**.
- **Corporate, not communal, ethos:** "Bitcoin is not a socialist community. It is a capitalist system of competition... a red queen game"; home-user node groupthink is "utterly sickening". nChain owes the public nothing: "There are no obligations and no requirements."
- nChain will never run a fiat exchange — "We are a research and development house", partnering with others (e.g. SBI) "just as Microsoft has tools for exchanges and banks, we create IP and software".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Socratic Q&A used as a platform for economic first-principles argument: design parameters are deflated as engineering guesses, while monetary questions are answered with Smithian anti-mercantilism and historical case studies (Spain, Singapore, Japan). His evidence style mixes econometric hand-waving, historical narrative and appeals to competitive/market selection; rhetorically he alternates technical deflation ("good enough guess") with maximalist political claims (capitalist red queen game vs "socialist group think").

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **Digital gold / store of value:** he explicitly classes BTC-hoarding with Spanish bullion mercantilism — an inflationary claim on future output, not wealth. This is a direct repudiation of the dominant BTC narrative.
- **"Every user runs a full node":** dismissed as "socialist group think around home users needing to run nodes" — validation is for competing miners in data centres.
- **ETF/institutional adoption as validation:** rejected as worthless; only use adds value.
- **Tether/stablecoins and multi-chain future:** Tether is unbacked money creation that the US will crush; all altchains go to zero; permissioned chains are "personal internets".
- **Core/developer governance:** replaced by a market where software houses compete for miner and exchange business.

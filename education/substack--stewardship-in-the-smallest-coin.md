# Stewardship in the Smallest Coin: Wesleyan Capitalism and the Moral Economy of Micropayments — core principles

- **Profit is the market's verdict on stewardship; rent is the dividend of political leverage.** In a genuinely competitive market profit signals that resources were stewarded well; corporatism — rent-seeking, monopoly protections, subsidies, weaponised licensing — decouples profit from value created.
- **Micropayments democratise economic agency.** A penny can reach a farmer in Kenya, a coder in Manila or a musician in Warsaw with no bank manager's permission and no processor devouring half its value — direct, permissionless payment at any scale.
- **Payment specificity sharpens the market signal.** "This article, not the whole newspaper; this video explanation, not the entire course" — precise, voluntary, instantaneous payments are purer demand signals than proxy rewards filtered through platform algorithms.
- **Costed attention disciplines both sides of the exchange.** When every act of appreciation costs something, spending regains moral weight and attention is redeemed directly by the producer rather than seized and sold to advertisers.
- **Distributed small payments are structurally resilient.** Distributed giving is "a river fed by countless small streams" — harder to extinguish than single-source institutional patronage shaped by compliance checklists and PR optics.
- **The obstacles are fee floors, uniform compliance and the "free" norm.** Fixed fees make a $0.05 payment irrational; the remedies are low-fee rails, proportionate tiered AML/KYC with de minimis thresholds, and a cultural shift from "free" (paid for with data and attention) to paying for what you value.

# The Arithmetic of the Last Fool
**Date:** 2026-06-12 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-arithmetic-of-the-last-fool
**Subtitle:** Why speculative money without utility must eventually meet the wall

## Core thesis
A token that does not scale, serve commerce or carry ordinary transactions is not storing value but storing belief — and belief-dependent assets are Ponzi-like structures whose implied growth requirement must eventually exceed the finite world of buyers. BTC's "store of value" narrative is an admission of functional failure, and its throughput constraint turns the exit into a straw precisely when the crowd needs it.

## Key arguments and claims
- The defining economic category is not legal fraud but dependency: "A system becomes Ponzi-like when present holders depend primarily on future buyers rather than present utility, productive yield, or transactional necessity." The old holder is paid by the new holder until the room runs out of fools or the exits jam.
- Value must be created before it is stored: a farm produces crops, a bond promises cash flows, a share is a residual claim; a non-scaling token is "the financial equivalent of bottling smoke and calling it wine".
- Exponential narratives die because the world is finite: finite buyers, capital, attention — "Later is the god of every speculative mania."
- Throughput as exit constraint: at roughly a handful of transactions per second BTC "is not a global economy. It is a turnstile with a marketing department." Liquidity is proven when everyone wants out, not in: "At five transactions a second, the exit is not a door. It is a straw."
- BTC culture converts ordinary commercial use into heresy — logistics, EDI, data, micropayments, enterprise records and high-volume business processes are all labelled "spam" — so businesses rationally test where testing is cheap: "A company can run meaningful trials on BSV for the price of lunch."
- "Store of value" as retreat with better typography: "we cannot process global commerce, therefore commerce is unnecessary... We cannot scale, therefore scaling is vulgar."
- Scarcity is not value: "A dead rat in a locked box is scarce. Bad poetry written on a napkin is scarce." Scarcity matters only when joined to utility, beauty, status, necessity, productivity or power.
- Price-as-proof is the oldest stupidity in markets: tulips, railway shares, South Sea stock, dot-coms and housing derivatives all had prices. "A bubble is not refuted by pointing at the bubble."
- The decisive question is "why hold?" If the answer is "because someone else will pay more", the system is buyer-dependent; if "because it enables commerce", it must demonstrate commerce; if "because it settles value", it must settle at scale.
- Real economies run on invoices, shipping manifests, purchase orders, customs records, refunds, audits and receipts: "A system that cannot carry them is not a monetary revolution. It is a speculative ornament."
- The final irony: the BTC narrative depends on non-use — "the logic of a restaurant that refuses to serve meals because food would damage the purity of the kitchen".
- The ending need not be dramatic: manias can end "as farce, sometimes as stagnation, sometimes as slow irrelevance covered in slogans", with institutions, custody products and conferences giving "failure better shoes".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Satirical polemic built on a precise economic definition (Ponzi-like = dependence on continual external inflow) and two hard constraints: the arithmetic of exponential growth against a finite pool of buyers, and the throughput ceiling that throttles exit under stress. The priest-and-congregation rhetoric dramatises the underlying incentive analysis — those with tickets need a more credulous entrant behind them.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Inverts "store of value first, medium of exchange later": storage without prior value creation is impossible, so the sequence can never complete.
- Rejects fee pressure as "security" and congestion as virtue: "That is not freedom. That is congestion with a logo."
- Rejects the "spam" doctrine that excludes commerce from the chain; a network that treats business transactions as pollution will not become the foundation of business.
- Rejects institutional adoption, ETFs and "monetisation" talk as proof: "respectability does not create utility".

# Set in Stone or Sold to the Highest Bidder: Why Immutability Is Bitcoin's Only Defence
**Date:** 2025-06-17 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/set-in-stone-or-sold-to-the-highest
**Subtitle:** (none given)

## Core thesis
Bitcoin’s only defence against capture by the deepest-pocketed institutions is absolute protocol immutability. Bitcoin was engineered for verifiability — born from the 2007–08 payment strangulation of the online-gambling industry — not anonymity; and every mutable “upgrade” is a precedent that lets capital rewrite both the protocol and the “BTC” brand itself.

## Key arguments and claims
- Premise: “The system either is set in stone, or it is rewritten by the hands with the fattest wallets and the greasiest fingers.” Protocol is political; once mutable, “it becomes a buffet for the financial class.”
- Origin claim: in 2007 and 2008 the online gaming companies he worked with “were being strangled” — banks refused them, payment processors froze, and regulators “saw every packet as a potential felony.” Bitcoin was built to move digital value “cleanly, verifiably, and without the institutional drag of legacy finance.”
- Anonymity was “always the enemy”: “You can’t run poker if five out of six players are sock puppets for the same guy.” Pseudonymity — “accountability without exposure” — is the design point.
- Mixers, tumblers and “zk this and zk that” are “trojan horses”: obfuscation invites BlackRock to launch “a cleaner, compliant clone” and market it as progress.
- The “cult of change”: Taproot, SegWit and Lightning were each pitched as upgrades, but “each change makes the system more complex, more obscure, and more dependent on ‘experts’” — “a hostage situation with better branding.”
- Ticker hijack: “BTC” appears in neither whitepaper nor code; Ethereum supplies the precedent — the fork that “violated its own immutability in the name of investor relief” kept the ETH ticker while the honest chain “got a suffix” (ETC). The same script can be replayed on Bitcoin.
- Satoshi’s 2009 message to Martti Malmi is quoted: “Once it’s backed with cash, that might change, but I’d probably better refrain from mentioning that in public anymore until we’re closer to ready to start.” Fiat integration was design, not betrayal — “not to be gold, but to be fire.”
- The remedy: “fix the rules, and never touch them again.” Bitcoin “should be boring. It should be blunt. It should be exactly the same tomorrow as it was in 2009.”
- Verdict: “BTC is no longer Bitcoin. It is a ticker hijack with marketing muscle” — “a settlement system for bankers and bots.”

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Institutional economics and incentive analysis: power follows money, so any mechanism of change becomes a lever of control held by the richest stakeholder. The argument is supported by historical analogy (the gambling industry’s payment crisis; the ETH/ETC fork) and by primary-source exegesis of Satoshi’s correspondence with Malmi.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects privacy-maximalism outright: anonymity is “a playground for fraud”, and mixers and zk tools are capture vectors — the inverse of cypherpunk orthodoxy.
- Rejects “improvement” itself: SegWit, Taproot and Lightning are reframed as authority-shifting precedents, not scaling or privacy progress.
- Rejects the anti-fiat “digital gold” stance: Satoshi himself anticipated cash-backed Bitcoin and self-censored to avoid the “Neanderthal” backlash.
- Treats the ticker and brand as contestable property of capital rather than technical fact — “Branding is theatre.”

## Notable quotes
- “Code that cannot change is the only thing that resists money. If it bends, it will eventually kneel.”
- “You can’t run poker if five out of six players are sock puppets for the same guy.”
- “It wasn’t designed to cloak you in shadows — it was designed to light the room so that every transaction could stand as a witness.”
- “What’s immutable can’t be owned. What’s fixed can’t be bought. And that is why they hate it.”

# Trolls and bullies
**Date:** 2019-04-11 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/trolls-and-bullies-d19287bb754d
**Subtitle:** The irony of those seeking anarchy and calling out for BitCoin to act outside the law is that they are not only trolls but simple bullies…

## Core thesis
Online trolling and cyber-bullying are economic phenomena, not just social ones: they flourish because the internet's "free" model removes all cost from communication and lets abusers hide behind anonymity. Craig's remedy is the Metanet built on Bitcoin micropayments, where every interaction carries a sender-pays price that users set themselves — pricing trolls out of existence while leaving desired communication cheap or free. Anonymity, in his framing, is not a civil liberty but the coward's tool that this incentive redesign removes.

## Key arguments and claims
- The anarchist wing of crypto ("calling out for BitCoin to act outside the law") are "not only trolls but simple bullies" — hypocrites who demand privacy for themselves while invading the privacy of their victims.
- Anonymity is sought because "it gives them power in their otherwise useless lives"; the troll is "the antisocial loser who fails drastically".
- The damage of social media is a "tragedy of the commons": "With the Internet, the inability to charge access based on use leads to abuse." Free access is paid for by selling users' PII, which "incentivises and encourages the worst aspects of society".
- Micropayments create a personal price schedule for communication: near-free for friends, a small balancing fee for loose acquaintances, and a high price for unsolicited commercial or hostile contact.
- This "alters the incentives and makes it less profitable in every way for the Internet troll and bully" — they "will have to start acting within social bounds".
- He distinguishes trolls (nuisance-seekers who "rile online communities" to harvest attention) from cyber bullies (who target individuals with shame, intimidation, private pictures and falsified information), noting individuals switch between the two.
- Trolling is attention extraction: "They seek to extract value from other people because they are too worthless to onus on their own."
- He quotes a Mozilla blog on the "troll tax" borne by women online — stolen time, energy, productivity, and real-world physical threat — and replies that the tax harms all targets, not only women.
- Conclusion: "As the Metanet replaces the Internet, trolls will slowly become extinct."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Craig applies price theory to social behaviour: abuse persists because its marginal cost to the abuser is zero, so reintroducing a positive price (sender-pays micropayments) restores socially bounded conduct — a Coasean/tragedy-of-the-commons framing rather than a moderation or free-speech one. He pairs this with ad hominem psychologising of opponents (trolls as "worthless", "minuscule worth", "sick individuals"), which does double duty as an attack on his crypto-anarchist critics. One external source (Mozilla's Internet Citizen blog) supplies the empirical harm framing.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the crypto orthodoxy that anonymity/pseudonymity is a core feature to protect: Craig treats anonymity as the enabling condition of abuse that Bitcoin (properly understood) and Metanet identity remove.
- Contradicts the "free and open internet" ethos of the era's web discourse: he argues zero-cost communication is the bug, and paid access is the fix — the opposite of net-utopian and "information wants to be free" positions.
- Contradicts the BTC scaling narrative: the argument presupposes a chain capable of global micropayment volume, i.e. his big-block BSV position against BTC's fee-market, high-cost model, which would make per-message payments uneconomic.

## Notable quotes

- "The damage that social media has released upon society comes from the typical tragedy of the commons that all free systems or seemingly free systems release."
- "With the Internet, the inability to charge access based on use leads to abuse."

## Problem addressed
Outputs already on the blockchain cannot be double-spent once mined, but parties also pass transactions off-chain (templates, 0-conf payments, IoT command transactions). A peer may complete and broadcast a transaction whose input was already spent, so the network rejects it after goods, services, or a device action have been delivered. The specification wants a way to prove issuance order and to refuse transactions that lack that proof.

## Core mechanism
The first party builds a transaction spending a previously submitted output, then signs a combination of that transaction (or its identifier) and one or more time indicators — a timestamp and/or current block height — showing when the transaction was created or sent. The transaction, signature and indicators travel to the second party on an off-chain channel. The second party accepts only if those items are present; optional further checks are that receipt is within a threshold of the indicated time and that the signature matches the first party’s public key. Acceptance may mean adding inputs or outputs and broadcasting, or (in an IoT hierarchy) sending a control instruction to a device and completing the command transaction after the device reports completion. The signature is intended as evidence of intentional double-spend if a conflicting spend later appears.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
The first-party independent method generates the transaction, generates a signature over the transaction and time indicators, and transmits all three, with acceptance conditioned on receipt of the signature and indicators. The second-party independent method receives the off-chain transaction, checks whether those items were supplied, and accepts only if that condition (and any further stated conditions) is met.

## BSV relevance
The specification targets Bitcoin-style 0-conf and peer-to-peer IoT command transactions. The same off-chain signature-plus-timestamp bundle can support merchant 0-conf policy and device-control flows on BSV without changing consensus validation of double-spends.

# The Forked Illusion — core principles

- **The whitepaper defines Bitcoin as a scalable distributed timestamp server.** A timestamp server that cannot timestamp at scale is a broken machine; the system must accept input, not restrict it.
- **Fees were designed to diminish with volume, not expand.** The intended economic engine migrates from subsidy towards volume-driven low fees; dollars-high congestion fees are a grotesque inversion of the intended economics.
- **Direct IP-to-IP exchange was the intended topology.** Replacing it with relay groups, gossip layers and gatekeeping infrastructure cripples the payment path the design specified.
- **There are no artificial block limits in the design.** Scaling was left to hardware progression and miner competition; a fixed small-block cap is economic vandalism against the system's core purpose.
- **Tokenisation was a design commitment.** Tokenised fiat, tokenised gold and real-world asset representation — a programmable ledger capable of representing anything that can be hashed, signed and verified.
- **Micropayments and global everyday access were the target.** Reframing the base layer as a "settlement layer" annihilates the everyday-commerce purpose the architecture was built for.
- **Verification runs through economic structure.** Competing block-producing nodes validate; passive archival "full nodes" are a performative misunderstanding — validation belongs to producers under competitive expenditure.
- **The architecture stands on its own.** Testable, observable, falsifiable — it requires neither the designer's infallibility nor his irrelevance, only fidelity to large blocks, low fees, competitive nodes and broad timestamping of money, data, contracts and knowledge. Its essence lies not in scarcity but in integrity; not in hoarding but in verification; not in self-imposed limits but in scalability.

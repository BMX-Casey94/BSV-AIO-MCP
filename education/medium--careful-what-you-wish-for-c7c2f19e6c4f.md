# Careful what you wish for… — core principles

- **Private cash, not anonymous cash.** Bitcoin was designed as lawful electronic cash that is private in ordinary use and simple to trace once the law is breached — a complete audit trail, never an anonymous money-transfer system.
- **Any chain sits inside existing law.** A blockchain can be made to work within the legal frameworks of where it exists; it does not stop governments taxing and it does not bring down banks.
- **Competitive miners replace a single clearer.** DigiCash (1989) used a central company to detect double-spends; its liquidation killed eCash. Bitcoin solves the same problem with a distributed, competing group of miners, so no single party's failure takes the system down.
- **Home-user mining is easy to subvert.** Bitcoin cannot scale as a home-user system; security belongs with professional, competing miners, not with a swarm of user nodes. It was never about all users running nodes, nor about equality or taking down the establishment.
- **Anonymity layers are optional script, not the base protocol.** Chaumian eCash can be implemented inside Bitcoin script, but the base system avoids eCash's regulatory fatal flaw by remaining pseudonymous rather than anonymous.
- **More anonymity yields less privacy.** The more a system is made anonymous rather than pseudonymous, the more it can be controlled; crime-coin designs give up privacy, they do not gain it.
- **Deletable off-chain records are a legal-avoidance design.** Off-chain channels that allow information to be deleted exist to avoid law, not to scale settlement. An immutable public ledger is the opposite construction: evidence that cannot be erased.
- **There is no anarchist solution in a blockchain.** Pseudonymity plus an undeletable trail is the intended property; builders who chase anonymity-as-freedom invert the design.

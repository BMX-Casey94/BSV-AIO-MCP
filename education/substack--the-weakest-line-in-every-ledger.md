# The Weakest Line in Every Ledger — core principles

- **The seam between organisations is the weakness.** Double-entry keeps each company's books internally consistent and silent on whether two sides agree. Errors, disputes and frauds cluster where the invoice sent meets the invoice received, and where the payment claimed meets the payment acknowledged.
- **A thin shared layer of sealed notes hardens that seam.** Each party keeps ordinary double-entry books and also publishes a sealed, timestamped note per transaction: a commitment that an invoice or payment with given properties existed at a moment, revealing nothing of its contents, tamper-evident thereafter, with invoice-note and payment-note privately linked.
- **Four evidence properties change.** Source is fixed at commitment rather than the client's word today; integrity means post-hoc change breaks the seal; timing is an objective timestamp that kills period-end shifting; linkage turns reconciliation into verification rather than detective work.
- **Selective disclosure dissolves the old binary.** A sealed field can be proven to exist and be unchanged without being revealed; a specific signed authorisation opens exactly one field to exactly one auditor. Privacy with weak evidence, or strong evidence with exposure, is no longer the choice.
- **Strong, cheap, instantly verifiable evidence makes full-population testing rational.** Sampling is a cost response to weak evidence. Evidence that stays re-verifiable for years also matches the long liability tail of audit.
- **Receivables and supply-chain finance get hard facts.** A control management cannot silently override; the lender's core risk — did this invoice arise and will it be paid — receives sealed evidence rather than self-created paper.
- **The tool narrows the factual-occurrence gap, not the judgement gap.** It does not detect collusion, establish economic substance, value or collectability, confer legal admissibility, or prove completeness of what was never recorded. That division is the point: stop wasting judgement on questions a good receipt should have answered.
- **A system that requires transparency to deliver trust is dead on arrival.** Companies cannot give public visibility of commercial records. The ledger's value is sealed evidence with selective disclosure, not published books.

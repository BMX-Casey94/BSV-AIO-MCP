# The Right to Privacy — core principles

- **Legality is a design property.** Bitcoin was built as a system that works within the majority of legal frameworks — all of those in the free world — not as a tool for evading them.
- **Privacy and anonymity are distinct categories.** Privacy is a right; anonymity is not. Conflating the two is the central conceptual error in popular debate.
- **Privacy protects ordinary life.** The value at stake is the individual's ability to live without being owed an explanation by the public or by platforms.
- **Anonymity-seeking signals illicit intent.** Anonymity is what people seek when they want to commit crimes, hide illicit activities, and do things that are generally wrong.
- **Design systems for privacy, not anonymity.** Firewall identity from public view while keeping the system compatible with lawful oversight — that combination is the legitimate and defensible position.
- **Treat leak culture with scepticism.** Irresponsible reporting and illegitimate dump sites pollute the information environment; leaked-document dumps are substantially unreliable, and the public is dulled by the resulting bombardment of fake information.

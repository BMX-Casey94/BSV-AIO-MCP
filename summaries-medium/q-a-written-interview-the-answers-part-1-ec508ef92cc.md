# Q&A/Written Interview — The answers — Part 1
**Date:** 2018-09-16 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/q-a-written-interview-the-answers-part-1-ec508ef92cc
**Subtitle:** I have been asked to do an AMA and will answer the questions posed to me.

## Core thesis
First instalment of a 60-question written AMA (questions 1–17, mostly personal, plus the patents question set up for Part 2). Amid the autobiography — polymath credentials, reading lists, productivity claims — the substantive Bitcoin content is Q17: Blockstream's capture of BTC is explained as the product of "a falsely held belief in socialism", and on-chain scaling is asserted as proven by recent stress tests.

## Key arguments and claims
- Intellectual self-fashioning: favourite authors Dostoevsky (The Brothers Karamazov), Frank Herbert (Dune) and Ayn Rand (The Fountainhead); the one book he would give the world is Mises's "Liberalism: The Classical Tradition" — "Mises was a scholar in the classical tradition. But mostly he was a realist who held onto ideals."
- Role models: his grandfather Ron Lynam and his mother; outside family, Qin Shihuangdi and Miyamoto Musashi.
- Productivity claims: completing two doctorates (applied mathematics and securities law) with a DBA planned; "I will have completed my 1,000th paper around the end of this year", 3,000th in five years, 5,000th in ten; around 2,500 patents granted by the ten-year mark — explicitly exceeding Edison's 1,000+ filings.
- Method: think across disciplinary boundaries, "as if it was a chess game on the move that follows the move that follows the move"; history shows "we remain the same species and interact in the same way".
- On the industry: "there is an absence of talent within crypto currency, and especially an absence along the lines of people who have a realistic vision of economics and business"; he learns from outsiders — "Calvin Ayre acts as my business mentor".
- Persona: "naturally introverted", analytical, planning "years in advance"; "It is always good to be both an enigma and a paradox"; admits being deliberately controversial "at times".
- Q16 — people misunderstand both him and Bitcoin: "very few people understand at all, and the worst people to come to to understand anything about it are so-called experts".
- Q17 — Blockstream's "strangle and takeover of BTC": "It is very simple, there is a falsely held belief in socialism. It is parasitic, and it is embedded deep within the human psyche", driven by "a false mentality that we should not be more than those around us" and by "those who seek power and control".
- On scaling: "Bitcoin scales on-chain, but no system scales in the way that some people desire" — the desire for "a system of eternal equality" where everyone runs everything is the socialist mentality applied to protocol design.
- Q18 (patents/licensing, BCH patent pool, chain-split scenarios) is posed in full but answered in Part 2.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The Q&A format is used as self-mythology: credential stacking (doctorates, papers, patents), canonical name-dropping (Mises, Rand, Dostoevsky, Musashi) and productivity numerics construct the authority from which the Bitcoin claims then speak. Substantively, his method is psychologising critique — technical-governance disputes (Blockstream, block size) are explained by the opponents' mentality (envy-driven socialism) rather than engaged on technical merits.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The Blockstream-capture narrative: BTC's development is framed as a hostile takeover by power-seekers, not community consensus — the standard BCH/BSV-era polemic.
- On-chain scaling treated as empirically settled by stress tests, against the BTC position that big blocks endanger decentralisation.
- The "eternal equality" jab restates his anti-full-node argument: mass verification participation is a socialist want, not a system requirement.
- Dismisses the entire field's expertise ("absence of talent", worst guides are "so-called experts") — positioning himself as the sole competent authority, consistent with his Satoshi-authorship campaign then underway.

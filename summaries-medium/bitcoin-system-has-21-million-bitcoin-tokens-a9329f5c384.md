# BitCoin system has 21 million BitCoin tokens.
**Date:** 2020-02-14 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-system-has-21-million-bitcoin-tokens-a9329f5c384
**Subtitle:** Each token has 10⁸ decimal places, with the 10^-8 slot being named "satoshis". The smallest possible unit in the BitCoin system is 1…

## Core thesis
A short layman explainer — apparently the promised answer to the reader question in "Hey up Craig," posted the same day — reducing the supply question to place-value arithmetic: each bitcoin token carries 10⁸ decimal places, the smallest unit is 1 satoshi, and "1 BitCoin" is simply a shorthand for 100 million satoshis, as dollars are to cents but with eight places instead of two.

## Key arguments and claims
- Each token has 10⁸ decimal places, and the 10^-8 slot is named "satoshis"; "The smallest possible unit in the BitCoin system is 1 satoshi".
- "100 million satoshis = 1 BitCoin" — divisibility runs to eight decimal places, against two in the USD.
- "BitCoin has 16 possible integer places. 8 in front of the decimal (21mm) and 8 to the right (.0000001, a satoshi)." — framing the entire money supply as a fixed-width integer field.
- Accuracy note: the body contains an arithmetic slip, writing 1 satoshi as "0.0000001 BitCoins" (seven places) where 0.00000001 is meant; the surrounding text (10⁸, "100 million satoshis") confirms the intended figure.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Pedagogical minimalism: the entire monetary structure is presented as fixed-width integer arithmetic plus a currency analogy (dollars/cents extended to eight places). The framing quietly does argumentative work — treating the satoshi as the indivisible atom and "bitcoin" as mere verbal shorthand supports his wider claim that the supply was a fixed issuance defined at creation, not an ongoing emission.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The popular "21 million coins" framing: by treating the satoshi as the true base unit, the supply is better read as roughly 2.1 quadrillion indivisible tokens — a framing he uses elsewhere to argue Bitcoin was built for high-volume micropayments, not digital-gold hoarding.
- Beyond that reframing, no direct engagement; the post is an explainer, not a polemic.

## Notable quotes

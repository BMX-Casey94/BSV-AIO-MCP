# Black Mirror
**Date:** 2018-09-01 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/black-mirror-fb2457166a3d
**Subtitle:** There is an episode of Black Mirror called Nosedive that explains some of the reason for the creation of bitcoin as a proof of work system…

## Core thesis
Bitcoin "has always been designed to end in data centres and corporate competition", and CoinGeek's arrival as the first true corporate miner marks the start of that era. The small-miner/pool phase was dangerously exposed to what Craig names "proof of social media" (PoSM) — FUD campaigns over Reddit and Twitter, exemplified by the UASF, that sway miners who cannot afford to verify claims. He also re-reads the Genesis block's Times headline as a critique of political manipulation of money (demagoguery), not of banking, and frames his own provocative public persona as a deliberate Diogenes-style Socratic performance.

## Key arguments and claims
- "CoinGeek has become the first real corporate miner in bitcoin" — defined as "a significant private business that mines on its own, rather than individual miners or pools of smaller miners" — with SBI expected to follow. Their infrastructure investment "with the goal of delivering global money signals the start of a new era".
- Corporate mining exists to deliver stability: "the creation of the first money that is set in stone and invariant", enabling long-term contracts and "stable global money".
- PoSM defined: before rewards were large enough to attract corporate miners, pools of "over 150,000 miners" dominated; small miners "are easily swayed through social media" and lack the resources to investigate "false claims made by developers and others who use social media platforms such as Reddit and Twitter to promote fear uncertainty and doubt (FUD)".
- "The use of techniques such as the UASF have no technical merit but have acted through proof of social media." The mechanism "is similar to the oligarchical attack that is associated with proof of stake (PoS)" (linking his nChain paper "PoW and the firm").
- CLTV/CSV as a PoSM case study: OP_BLOCKNUMBER "was rejected by Satoshi with all Core developers admitting that it would introduce vulnerabilities" (reorg invalidating time-limited transactions, quoted from bitcointalk), yet "After people had forgotten about the vulnerabilities, these opcodes were introduced" to enable Lightning.
- Genesis block exegesis: "The reference for this article relates not banks, but demagoguery." The Times piece showed government planning "to keep the banks on the boil" with taxpayer cash; the target is "the political and social manipulation of money" — fiat altered "on the whims of bureaucrats and politicians".
- American founding analogy: the US founders "created a representative democracy" because they "foresaw how a demagogue could sway the popular opinion" — the same defence Bitcoin needs against PoSM.
- Autobiographical disclosure: his public persona was "designed to challenge in a way that gets some to question and others to attack based on the mere perception of social disagreement" — a Diogenes ("deface the currency") and Socratic teaching strategy; "I have achieved this and as a result shall change yet again." The "modem" post was an intentional error bait (bonded-modem engineering at OzEmail; correct plural "modem") to expose people who "jump to conclusions".
- PoW work ethic vs PoS: "Bitcoin is a Red Queen game" forcing continual competition; "proof of stake does the opposite; it allows those who have power and money to maintain control without doing any more" — "something many such as Bitmain seek to alter".
- Personal history offered as ethos: he "was heavily involved with the Uniting Church in Australia… a pastor for some time" and a trustee of the church's banking operations; invokes Adam Smith's Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759) for "an equitable capitalist system", plus Nietzsche (stewardship reading) and Atlas Shrugged ("as with Atlas to shrug").
- Forecast: "the next two years solidify the first stage of bitcoin", with "six and ten years" bringing radical corporatisation, "larger and larger block sizes and hypercompetitive systems".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method blends pop-culture allegory (Black Mirror's "Nosedive" social-credit world as PoSM), revisionist close reading of a canonical text (the Genesis Times headline), and classical allusion (Diogenes, Socratic method, Nietzsche, Asimov's Foundation). He names and taxonomises attack vectors (PoSM, oligarchical PoS attack), supports claims with his own primary sources (bitcointalk archives, his nChain paper), and uses autobiographical confession as both ethos and trap-setting. The rhetorical mode is prophetic-corporate: Bitcoin maturing through staged struggles into stable capitalist money.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the hobbyist-node ideal outright: stability comes from "large corporate entities", "not of small home users and hobby nodes" — the inverse of BTC's decentralisation rhetoric.
- Delegitimises the UASF — celebrated in BTC lore as users defeating miners in 2017 — as a technically meritless social-media attack.
- Re-reads the Genesis block against the anti-bank/"bailout" orthodoxy: the enemy is political money manipulation, and banking itself is not the target.
- Treats miner-driven governance and hash-power investment ("We have invested in hash power", per the companion essay) as legitimate, against BTC's "users define the rules" doctrine.
- Frames off-chain solutions as losing "the benefits that come with the pseudonymous system that holds global data and network patterns for an eternity" — an anti-Lightning, on-chain-scaling stance weeks before the BCH/BSV split.

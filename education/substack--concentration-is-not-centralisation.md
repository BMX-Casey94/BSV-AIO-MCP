# Concentration Is Not Centralisation — core principles

- **Concentration and centralisation are different categories.** Concentration is an economic descriptor of market structure (oligopoly, concentration ratios, the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index); centralisation is a governance descriptor — sovereignty lodged in one hand, a protocol that can be rewritten by decree. Confusing them is like mistaking weight for authority.
- **Five to twenty serious miners is a normal equilibrium.** Capital-intensive proof-of-work is a security industry with high fixed costs — like airlines, container shipping, semiconductor fabs and cloud hyperscalers — concentrated because scale economies are indifferent to slogans, not because a dictator arrived.
- **Mining economics drive consolidation.** ASIC fleets depreciate fast, power is the dominant cost, variance reduction matters because proof-of-work rewards are probabilistic (large operators smooth revenue and lower their cost of capital), and engineering competence is a moat; the inefficient miner is liquidated.
- **Contestability keeps oligopoly honest.** Supernormal profits attract entry and losses force shutdowns; the hobbyist mining tail is cultural, not productive — like pointing at amateur pilots in a flying club and declaring commercial aviation decentralised.
- **A node is defined by production, not observation.** Whitepaper Section 5: nodes gather transactions, assemble blocks, prove work and broadcast; spectator clients are listeners, and verification without production is observation.
- **Proof-of-work is Sybil resistance.** Identities are cheap, work is expensive; "one CPU, one vote" was always shorthand for one unit of work, one unit of risk — the vote is the block.
- **Governance is competition among block-creators under stable rules.** A protocol "set in stone" is an anti-power principle — no priesthood, no mob sovereignty — and the precondition for the long-term investment, scaling and tokenisation that require an unshiftable foundation.
- **Bottlenecks create gatekeepers the way drought creates water barons.** Throttled on-chain capacity forces commerce into custodial layers, turning cash for everyone into a settlement token for intermediaries; observer "validation" by passive archives is not governance — only block production under competitive expenditure counts.

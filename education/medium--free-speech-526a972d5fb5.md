# Free Speech — core principles

- **Free speech is a negative right.** It is "not the right to be heard on any platform but rather limitations set by governments and authorities in blocking individuals from speaking... it is not a positive right."
- **Zero-price speech is a tragedy of the commons.** "Free is the evil within the Internet. And worse, free is incredibly expensive." The open, unpriced commons lets trolls drown out the meek; "the Internet has become a sewer."
- **Speech is property.** "We have the right to exclude people... We have the right to our own copy... We should be able to profit for and through it" — rather than surrendering it to platforms that sell it inefficiently for advertising.
- **Micropayments and escrow commercialise posting.** Every post transmitted "through an encrypted smart contract"; posting requires "a small micropayment and escrow a small amount of money", so "each individual who is honest can earn every time someone tries to attack him." Reputation accrues "through tokenised exchanges."
- **Price can be tiered by relationship.** Trusted parties a tenth of a cent or a flat fee; regular associates a cent; advertisers a larger fee; parties one seeks to block an exorbitant amount per post or link that includes them.
- **Cost protects the meek, not the powerful.** "If you want freedom of speech, you need to remove the right for individuals to shout you down. It is not the strong or powerful that need free speech, it is the meek and those in society who cannot be heard."
- **Everything has a cost.** "Not all costs are monetary, but they always exist. Until people realise it, they will never be free." Enforcing copy-holder rights and privacy controls is not censorship; organisations are liable when they fail to take action.

# Monetary transfer and transmission rules
**Date:** 2018-10-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/monetary-transfer-and-transmission-rules-89b76489807e
**Subtitle:** The exchange of Bitcoin for other currencies clearly comes under existing AML[1] regulations and where an exchange[2] has been used as a…

## Core thesis
A single-paragraph legal note: exchanging bitcoin for other currencies "clearly comes under existing AML regulations", and where an exchange handles the process, KYC requirements apply — jurisdictional details differ, but "the laws of money and exchange remain valid". The post's body is essentially one thesis sentence plus definitional footnotes anchoring it in US money-services-business regulation.

## Key arguments and claims
- "The exchange of bitcoin for other currencies clearly comes under existing AML regulations" — no new law is needed; existing anti-money-laundering regimes already capture bitcoin-to-fiat conversion.
- "Where an exchange has been used as a part of this process, KYC requirements will still apply" — the intermediary's presence triggers customer-identification obligations.
- Jurisdictional variance is acknowledged but bounded: AML/KYC requirements "will differ" across jurisdictions, "but, the laws of money and exchange remain valid".
- The footnotes do the legal anchoring: "exchange" is defined via 31 CFR § 1010.100(ff) (the FinCEN money-services-business definition), and the Bank Secrecy Act regulations are cited as "Bank Secrecy Act Regulations — Definitions and Other Regulations Relating to Money Services Businesses, 76 FR 43585 (July 21, 2011)" — i.e. the 2011 FinCEN MSB rule under which US crypto exchanges were already being treated as money transmitters.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Declarative legal assertion supported by pinpoint regulatory citation: one sentence of argument, four footnotes of apparatus (AML, KYC spelled out; CFR section and Federal Register notice). The brevity is itself the rhetoric — the matter is presented as settled ("clearly"), requiring no extended argument, only correct citation of the governing rules.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the era's "Bitcoin is outside AML" / regulation-resistant narrative: conversion to fiat is already squarely inside existing money-transmission law.
- Against disintermediation-as-compliance-escape: using an exchange does not launder the obligation away — it adds KYC duties.
- Against "new asset, new rules" exceptionalism: continuity is the claim — "the laws of money and exchange remain valid" for bitcoin as for any currency.

## Notable quotes
- "The exchange of bitcoin for other currencies clearly comes under existing AML regulations, and where an exchange has been used as a part of this process, KYC requirements will still apply."
- "The laws of money and exchange remain valid."

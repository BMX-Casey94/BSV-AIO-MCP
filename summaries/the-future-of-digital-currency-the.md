# The Future of Digital Currency: The Need for Global Competition in CBDCs and Stablecoins
**Date:** 2025-06-29 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-future-of-digital-currency-the
**Subtitle:** Scaling blockchain isn't a choice—it's the foundation of a global economy built for the future.

## Core thesis
CBDCs and stablecoins are the next evolutionary step of money, but they can only serve global commerce if the underlying blockchain infrastructure scales to millions of transactions per second and if multiple national and private systems compete and interoperate on a level playing field. Craig argues against any single dominant digital-currency system and for a unified, interoperable, high-throughput blockchain ecosystem — enabled by Teranode-class technology — as the foundation of future global trade.

## Key arguments and claims
- National digital-currency infrastructure must process "anywhere from 30,000 to 100,000 transactions per second (TPS)"; the UK Treasury is cited as requiring a system in that range. These figures are presented as "not speculative" but real-world demand.
- Internationally, interconnected systems require "a platform that can support millions of transactions per second without compromising security, cost, or reliability".
- Incumbent networks are quantitatively disqualified: "Bitcoin, for instance, can only handle around 7 transactions per second. Ethereum can process roughly 30 transactions per second under normal conditions."
- Teranode is named as the solution class: "a technology designed to increase blockchain's transaction throughput to millions of transactions per second" by "eliminating bottlenecks that previously limited transaction speed".
- On monetary design, the essay explicitly rejects monopoly: "The goal should not be to create a single, dominant system controlled by one or a few entities, but rather to encourage open, competitive markets for digital currencies", letting "the most effective and efficient solutions emerge naturally".
- CBDCs and stablecoins are distinguished by function: CBDCs digitise national currency and sharpen monetary-policy control; stablecoins (typically pegged to the U.S. dollar or euro) serve cross-border trade and financial inclusion without reliance on government-issued currency.
- Cross-border trade is the motivating use case: a U.S. business paying a Japanese supplier currently waits days and pays significant fees; blockchain-based digital currencies would settle "in seconds, with minimal fees".
- The end-state is "a unified, interoperable blockchain ecosystem" in which multiple CBDCs and stablecoins cooperate via "cross-chain communication", creating "a level playing field where efficiency is the driving force behind global commerce, rather than bureaucratic delays or excessive costs".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The reasoning is infrastructural-economics: derive throughput requirements from stated treasury-level figures (30,000–100,000 TPS nationally, millions globally), measure incumbents against that bar, and conclude that only a re-architected base layer qualifies. Overlaid on this is a competition argument drawn from free-market economics — monetary systems should compete so efficient ones win — and a trade-efficiency argument in which settlement latency and intermediary fees are pure deadweight to be engineered out.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- States bluntly that Bitcoin's ~7 TPS and Ethereum's ~30 TPS are "far from sufficient to handle global commerce", rejecting the mainstream position that base-layer throughput limits are acceptable because Layer-2 systems absorb demand.
- Implicitly rejects the BTC "digital gold" settlement-layer thesis: money here is a high-frequency medium of exchange for commerce, not a low-velocity asset.
- Contradicts maximalist one-chain-wins ideology from a different angle: the future is many competing CBDCs and stablecoins interoperating, not a single dominant asset — though the scalable backbone technology he points to is Teranode/BSV.
- Rejects the toleration of multi-day, fee-laden correspondent-banking settlement that both legacy finance and slow-chain crypto normalise.

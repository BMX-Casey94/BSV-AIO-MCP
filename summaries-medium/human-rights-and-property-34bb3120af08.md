# Human Rights and Property
**Date:** 2019-09-03 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/human-rights-and-property-34bb3120af08

## Core thesis
The Medium body is an excerpt; the full essay continues on craigwright.net. Craig grounds Bitcoin in a natural-rights frame: the right to hold property is the most fundamental human right, and freedom requires secure control of property. He then previews his team's (nChain's) forthcoming solution — "redeemable contracts": tradable, tokenised property rights abstracted from the individual and from use, stored encrypted across the Metanet, which separate ownership, occupation, and use while preserving the owner's privacy.

## Key arguments and claims
- "The most fundamental of human rights is the right to hold property": to be free, people must control their lives, which requires securing "the security and integrity" of property rights.
- "Freedom lies at the core of what Bitcoin, the blockchain is about." His team has worked "for several years" on property solutions, with papers and products coming "in the coming months."
- The primary problem is informational exposure: "Our lives are not private, and they are becoming less and less private over time." Privacy here is not about shielding crime but "empowering individuals to live the life they want to live."
- The solution is "redeemable contracts" — "a form of tradable, secure property rights that have been abstracted from the individual and the use."
- House example: "Just because you live in a house does not mean people need to know that you own it." The system will "separate the ownership, occupation, and use of any property."
- Mechanism: ownership is divided "into shares that are tradable and that link over the blockchain to redeemable contracts that are encrypted and stored in a distributed manner across the Metanet" (linking to nChain's Metanet announcement).
- The design includes "a secure voting system and a revenue-distribution system": a homeowner pays rent at market rates, rent is distributed pro-rata to the property's shares, and where the homeowner owns 100% of the shares, "the money will be pseudonymously returned to them."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Craig argues top-down from liberal political philosophy (property as the foundation of freedom, echoing Lockean natural rights) to an engineering specification: the philosophical problem (exposure of ownership) is answered with a concrete tokenisation architecture (shares, encrypted redeemable contracts, Metanet storage, voting and revenue distribution). The reasoning is teleological — Bitcoin's purpose is freedom through property — and the essay doubles as a product announcement, blending normative claim with roadmap.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Reframes privacy against the crypto-anarchist reading: privacy exists to protect ordinary life and property, not to evade law — "The lack of privacy is not about protecting criminal acts."
- Contradicts the BTC-era "digital gold / money only" minimalism: Bitcoin's blockchain is presented as a general registry for tokenised real-world property (houses, shares, rents) — the BSV/Metanet vision.
- Inverts the "code replaces institutions" instinct: ownership is abstracted into legally meaningful "redeemable contracts" rather than bearer keys, consistent with his "Good Title Is Not a Key" argument that title, not possession of keys, constitutes ownership.
- Pseudonymous rent recirculation to a 100% owner quietly accepts regulated-market mechanics (market-rate rent, revenue distribution) rather than exit from the financial system.

# Don't be fooled — Bitcoin is not BTC
**Date:** 2019-05-08 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/dont-be-fooled-bitcoin-is-not-btc-61e6aee8ac53
**Subtitle:** In order to clear up some areas around my history as the creator of Bitcoin for people, I need to point out a few fallacies. Firstly, …

## Core thesis
Writing explicitly as Bitcoin's creator, Craig argues that BTC is a "passing off" fraud — a developer-mutated airdrop copy that is not Bitcoin. He systematically rebuts the myths built around Satoshi: Bitcoin is private but not anonymous, is not anti-authority, cannot abolish debt, and was designed to work within law. Its decentralisation comes solely from a fixed protocol; once developers can change the rules, the system is centralised and no longer Bitcoin.

## Key arguments and claims
- As Satoshi he "interacted with people who held views that differed from mine"; the goal was "an honest and legally enforceable cash system" — to be money, "Bitcoin needs to be neutral… friendly to most people", not to crime.
- "Bitcoin is a system that is private. It is not anonymous." He quotes his own bitcointalk post (topic 671) about Bitcoin suiting people who don't want purchases on a credit-card bill — legal-but-indiscreet cash use, like corner-store purchases. "Anonymity breeds crime, and crime kills freedom."
- Silk Road proves Bitcoin is a bad drug coin "by design": captures show sales of "plastic explosives; ammunition; poisons; and hard drugs including crack, ice, and worse". "Freedom does not include crime."
- "Bitcoin does not stop debt. No cryptocurrency can." Debt is contractual; banning debt means banning all contracts and trade. Bonds and repayable lighthouse projects can be built on Bitcoin.
- Bitcoin is "an immutable log and an immutable evidence trail… traceable", meeting Joseph Stiglitz's requirements for sound, honest money. E-gold, Liberty Reserve and DigiCash seekers "did not want Bitcoin… and have hijacked the narrative"; those systems "all ended as a result of legal action".
- On the Cypherpunks list: he used it from early on, but list membership doesn't imply anarchism — the "no true Scotsman" claim that no cypherpunk would support a regulated system is a fallacy of composition. "I do not support anarchy in any form. I did not in the 90s, and I never will."
- White-paper exegesis: peer-to-peer cash (two parties exchanging a transaction) and the distributed node database are "separate concepts"; he "always expected" miners to "aggregate into large corporations that would run out of data centres" — competing organisations are what decentralisation means, and "the protocol is fixed".
- Passing off: BTC is "a tort based on deception" — people "are being led to believe they are getting Bitcoin when in fact they are getting a crippled developer-controlled mutation". Honest forks (Ethereum, Litecoin) don't confuse anyone; Core "could call it Core coin, developer coin, protocol-changes-weekly coin, but they cannot legally decide to name it Bitcoin".
- Protocol-stability analogy: TCP from the 80s still connects a DOS 5.0, XENOS or SunOS 3.0 machine to today's Internet; constant change gives you IPX and stifled business investment. "A transaction signed today needs to be valid 50 years from now."
- On his p2pfoundation quote ("decentralized, non-trust-based system"): critics cherry-pick "decentralised"; the point is a "global distributed database, with additions… by consent of the majority, based on a set of rules" — non-trust-based *because* the protocol is fixed. "As soon as you allow developers to change the protocol, you trust the developers."
- "I left Bitcoin as a project so that I would not be the benevolent dictator. I left to be an absentee dictator. One who set rules in stone."
- His "profound distrust is not towards central banks. It is towards technocrats who think they know far more than everyone else" — the fiat quote targets trust-based systems "that the DEVELOPERS can alter… BTC is the reason Bitcoin exists. Bitcoin exists to ensure systems like BTC fail."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Primary-source exegesis: he re-quotes his own Satoshi-era posts (bitcointalk, p2pfoundation) and the white paper, accusing opponents of cherry-picking, and restores context. He names logical fallacies explicitly (no true Scotsman, fallacy of composition), imports a common-law doctrine (passing off) as the frame for the BTC ticker claim, and argues by engineering analogy (TCP/IP longevity vs IPX obsolescence). The voice is autobiographical authority: only the designer can say what was designed.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the foundational myth that Satoshi was an anti-authority cypherpunk: Craig's Satoshi is pro-law, pro-corporation, anti-anonymity.
- Contradicts "Bitcoin is uncensorable anonymous money": traceability and legal enforceability are the design goals; Silk Road is presented as a failure of misuse, not a feature.
- Contradicts developer-led protocol governance (BIPs, soft forks) as legitimate: any rule change is centralisation and passing off; "developers are not decentralised".
- Contradicts the market consensus that BTC *is* Bitcoin: the ticker itself is framed as an actionable tort.
- Contradicts anti-central-bank readings of the white paper's trust quote: the target is "technocrats" who alter rules — including BTC's own developers.

# “Where Your Treasure Is”: A Christian Denunciation of Hoarding, HODL Culture, and False Promises of Wealth Without Work
**Date:** 2025-06-22 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/where-your-treasure-is-a-christian
**Subtitle:** Why Passive Speculation is Not Stewardship—And the Gospel Demands Creation, Not Inertia

## Core thesis
HODL culture is a “seductive theology” of scarcity and windfall that contradicts Christian economic ethics. Scripture demands stewardship through risk, labour and creation — the multiplication of talents — whereas passive speculation is the burial of talent, the hoarding of manna and the worship of golden calves “rendered in cryptographic keys”.

## Key arguments and claims
- The Parable of the Talents (Matthew 25:14–30) is “explicitly economic”: the master condemns the servant who buried his silver — “You wicked and slothful servant!” — not for theft but “for failing to create.”
- The HODLer is that servant: “He produces no good, hires no labourer, funds no poor, and creates no community. He waits. Not on the Lord, but on a chart.”
- 2 Thessalonians 3:10 (“If anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat”) and Proverbs 13:11 (“Wealth gained hastily will dwindle…”) ground an ethic in which “creation is divine; extraction without contribution is theft”; Genesis gives man dominion “to tend the garden”, not to idle.
- The 21-million cap is “a myth of engineered scarcity”: Biblically, “virtue does not lie in scarcity—it lies in righteous distribution, in abundant giving, in generous multiplication.”
- Scarcity theology breeds pyramid logic: “buy early, persuade others to join late, and exit when the price allows me to abandon work. This is not capitalism. This is not even trade.”
- Luke 12:16–21 — the rich fool who says “I will tear down my barns and build bigger ones” — is “the original HODL strategy—bigger wallets, more keys, total inertia”; Ecclesiastes 11:1 urges “cast your bread upon the waters.”
- James 5:1–6 indicts hoarders: “You have hoarded wealth in the last days… the wages you failed to pay the workers who mowed your fields are crying out against you.”
- BTC evangelists preach “blasphemous economic eschatology” — salvation without sacrifice; the early Church “shared all they had—not to speculate, but to liberate” (Acts 4:32–35, glossed as “not socialism… sacrificial enterprise”).
- The redemptive model: wealth must move; capital must be applied to “justice, inclusion, and creation”; if Christians buy digital assets, “let it be to fund innovation, not speculation” — “not online casinos with delusions of grandeur.”
- Closing judgment: the returning Master will ask, “What have you done with what I gave you?”

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Theological exegesis: close readings of Gospel parables, epistles and wisdom literature as economic texts, mapping HODL and speculation onto Biblical condemnations of hoarding, sloth and false prophecy. The argument is moral rather than technical — wealth is judged by what it builds and whom it serves.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Attacks HODL itself as vice: reframed as sloth and the burial of talent rather than prudence or conviction.
- Attacks engineered scarcity (the 21M cap) as morally perverse pyramid logic rather than “sound money”.
- Rejects the eschatological narrative — BTC replacing fiat, destroying central banks, ushering in “Eden” — as blasphemy.
- Insists value must circulate and fund creation, cutting against “number go up” passivity.

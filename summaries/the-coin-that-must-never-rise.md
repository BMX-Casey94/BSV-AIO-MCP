# The Coin That Must Never Rise
**Date:** 2025-11-23 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-coin-that-must-never-rise
**Subtitle:** (none given)

## Core thesis
A satirical allegory: in the city of Intermedion, where the Guild of Gatekeepers (backed by the Holy Order of Brands — MasterCardia, Visaria, the Lesser Order of PayPallus) skims roughly forty per cent of all economic activity, a stubborn engineer named Cassian "Cass" Ledger invents "Direct Coin" — boring, instant, nearly free payments at scale. The establishment deploys narrative warfare, price manipulation, regulatory fog and a branded clone to kill it, and fails, because the thing simply works. The greatest threat to entrenched power is "boring, reliable, cheap payments at scale".

## Key arguments and claims
- The fee economy is itemised through cabbage-seller Meriel Quince: a 2.00 sale nets 1.36 after Fees 0.43, Adjustment 0.09, "Network Resilience Charge" 0.07, "Merchant Assurance Contribution" 0.03 and "Settlement Harmonisation Offset" 0.02. Direct Coin settles a 1.50 sale instantly with a fee of 0.00002.
- The Skim Council's suppression playbook, narrated by Lord Arbitrage von Basispoint: first "shape the narrative" — paint the device as "unstable, dangerous, unscalable, environmentally ruinous, probably criminal"; second, "depress its market price"; third, "unleash the regulators… We drown it in forms, permits, compliance audits, and safety evaluations until even the inventor cannot recall what he invented."
- Price as weapon: quant-monk Brother Basis pumps Direct Coin with the Gate's reserve capital, then dumps it all at 2:17 PM to engineer a crash; pundits then "prove" the system flawed. The manipulation fails because "Usage is not correlated with price" — people keep buying onions. "'They're not supposed to buy onions with it,' Basis hissed. 'They're supposed to gamble!'"
- Regulatory fog personified: auditor Imogen Slate is tasked with mutually contradictory standards ("If it passes one, it must fail another. It is the perfect filter"); Cass receives 117 notices; a buried sentence on page 812 reads: "Any device that processes transactions efficiently is provisionally prohibited pending inefficiency compliance."
- The honest data: Slate finds profit retention up 30–40 per cent, settlement inefficiencies nearly eliminated, and net local output up 11 per cent in four days in adopting districts. Her suppressed report concludes: "The only identifiable systemic risk is to established stakeholders." She is ordered to stop calling the 40 per cent a burden — "We call it a Stabilisation Dividend."
- The Brand Offensive: "Next-Generation Premium Micro-Payments™" offers "Triple-Verification Settlement" and "The Comfort of Familiar Skimming™" — twenty-second settlement and 43 per cent fees. It backfires by forcing comparison: "The Gatekeepers weren't necessary. They were just expensive."
- The mechanism of power is named: "Inequality isn't held in place by force — force is too expensive. It's held in place by belief." Once citizens shrug — "Softly. Politely. But undeniably: No." — compliance collapses without violence.
- The revolution is irrelevance, not storming: the tower's windows go dark — "Not shattered. Not stormed. Just… irrelevant." The closing image: "A number that didn't go up. A number that didn't have to."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Rather than formal argument, the essay uses allegorical satire in which characters personify mechanisms — Lord Arbitrage von Basispoint (rent extraction), Brother Basis (price manipulation), Dame Seraphina Swipe in MasterCardia-and-Visaria robes (card-network branding), Imogen Slate (empirical audit). The plot operationalises his economic claims: utility decoupled from price, regulation as a "weaponised maze", and the forty-per-cent intermediary skim made visible through ledger lines.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against price-as-truth: a payment system is judged by function, not market price — "It's functioning perfectly… That's not how you judge a payment system." Speculative volatility is the Gatekeepers' tool, not the technology's verdict.
- Against the claim that cheap, instant, high-volume payments are dangerous or unscalable: the device works, nothing collapses, and "the only systemic danger identified is economic" — to the skimmers.
- Against regulation-as-protection: certification regimes are depicted as deliberately contradictory fog whose purpose is to freeze functionality, not to safeguard users.
- Against brand and complexity as markers of quality: Premium Micro-Payments™ does "everything the old system did, but with more reassurance" — at 43 per cent fees.

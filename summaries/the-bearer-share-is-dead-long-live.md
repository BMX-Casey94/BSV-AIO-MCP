# The Bearer Share Is Dead. Long Live Proof of Stake.
**Date:** 2026-03-18 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-bearer-share-is-dead-long-live
**Subtitle:** How a century of financial governance reform was quietly undone by people who think “decentralisation” is a technical property rather than a measurable outcome

## Core thesis
Bearer shares were abolished across more than 200 jurisdictions not primarily for money-laundering reasons but because they made it impossible to know who controls an economically significant institution. Proof of Stake recreates that exact governance pathology — anonymous exercise of governance power — with costless Sybil fragmentation and programmable delegation that make it strictly more dangerous than the paper instrument ever was, while address-level "decentralisation" metrics render the problem unfalsifiable by design.

## Key arguments and claims
- The bearer share was an anonymous governance instrument, not merely an anonymous financial one: the holder voted, determined board composition, approved related-party transactions and controlled corporate resources while remaining invisible — enabling hidden concentration (40–60% positions appearing as nothing), self-dealing immune to fiduciary duty (which requires knowing who the fiduciary is), and regulatory evasion.
- Abolition was a governance judgment, culminating in the FATF's 2022 revision of Recommendation 24 requiring prohibition of new issuance and conversion or immobilisation of existing bearer shares across 200+ jurisdictions; the UK acted in 2015 with 100% compliance, Switzerland in 2019 with criminal sanctions. The UK post-implementation review: lawful-use potential "did not outweigh the benefits of entirely preventing the potential for misuse."
- Even registered intermediation failed to tame the opacity: Nougayrède's analysis and the Clearstream case, in which Iranian central bank assets sat hidden in an omnibus account for years, show custodial deposit did not solve the problem — pure bearer instruments were hopeless.
- Cosmos Hub illustrates the configuration: one bonded ATOM, one vote; delegators inherit their validator's vote unless they actively override; a person controlling ten validators through ten legal entities in ten jurisdictions, collectively holding 30% of staked ATOM, has no disclosure obligation. The system asks "do you control a private key associated with sufficient stake?" — never "who are you?"
- The Sybil asymmetry: fragmenting a paper bearer position across fifty identities required fifty certificates, safe-deposit boxes and nominee arrangements — positive marginal cost. Fragmenting a 51% PoS position across 51 apparently independent 1% addresses costs some gas fees and wallet management. The result is a governance 51% attack that is undetectable by design: no consensus disruption, just ordinary-looking governance — "autocracy cosplaying as decentralisation".
- The Nakamoto coefficient is measured at address level (Cosmos Hub: 6–7 as of late 2022), but the beneficial-ownership Nakamoto coefficient could be 1, and the system cannot tell us. Address-level decentralisation claims are therefore unfalsifiable — "a statement of faith, not a statement of fact."
- The proxy-voting parallel: delegation-inheritance is structurally identical to corporate proxy voting, which in the US is regulated under Section 14(a) of the Exchange Act, with disclosure rules, SEC oversight of advisory firms (ISS, Glass Lewis) and fiduciary duties for institutional voters. Validators bear none of these obligations.
- Tezos shows the practical outcome: exchange bakers (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken) hold among the largest staking balances yet participate in governance "quite infrequently — if at all", so outcomes are determined by a smaller set of bakers while retail delegators' governance power silently evaporates.
- MiCA does not address the problem: it regulates issuers and service providers, not the governance relationship between token holder and protocol, and its Recital 22 decentralisation carve-out exempts precisely the systems where the bearer-governance pathology is most acute — a structural, not accidental, gap.
- PoS is strictly worse than paper on every amplifying dimension: costless pseudonymous identity generation, smart-contract-coordinated collusion, programmable vote delegation, and cross-jurisdictional operation that makes enforcement functionally impossible at protocol level. On-chain analytics provide entity-level tracking, not beneficial-ownership identification.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Functional regulatory analysis: extract the principle behind a settled international judgment (governance power without beneficial-ownership transparency is intolerable), show the principle carries no "unless digital" asterisk, then map the new instrument onto the abolished one feature by feature, using named chains, dated metrics and statutory instruments as evidence. The burden of proof is placed on PoS proponents, with the falsifiability criterion doing the analytical work.

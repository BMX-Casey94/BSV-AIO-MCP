# Trust and Risk — core principles

- **Security exists to enable trust, not to eliminate risk.** Risk can never be removed entirely, and even near-total risk removal must be justified against its cost.
- **Risk is quantifiable in any field.** The live questions are the reliability, precision and accuracy of the measurement — and those who claim risk cannot be measured are generally refusing to have their predictive ability tested.
- **Measurement quality has three distinct axes.** Reliability is repeatability within predictable bounds; precision is variance around the mark; accuracy is closeness to the bulls-eye. Good risk measurement needs precision and accuracy, reproducible by others; qualitative "art-based" assessment fails empirically over time.
- **0-conf security is an economic sufficiency test.** "No system is completely secure. So, the question is, whether it is secure enough" — an unconfirmed transaction is secure enough whenever the cost of a double-spend attack dwarfs the value at risk.
- **Worked figure: attack cost versus basket value.** "It is likely to cost over 10,000 USD in value to attempt to double-spend a transactions where the standard fees have been applied" — irrational to attempt against a $4.00 coffee, which is why 0-conf works for everyday cash purchases.
- **Confirmation depth is a priced risk decision.** For a $40,000 car the paperwork and handover never complete within 20 minutes anyway, so waiting for confirmations imposes no real cost on large purchases — small payments and large settlements have different optimal confirmation policies.
- **Design centre: Bitcoin is first and foremost cash.** The 0-conf economics above restore the merchant-payment scenario — small everyday payments are the primary use case.

# Valuing systems — the margin of substitute goods. — core principles

- **Value a ledger by its closest substitutes.** You can always value a product based on the closest alternative goods and services and the substitutes on market; for Bitcoin this is as a commodity ledger. Past unvaluability stemmed from bitcoin being a mere speculative guess as to the future.
- **The floor is fee revenue from use.** Since Bitcoin is a commodity ledger, its floor value is the fee revenue miners earn from usage; a base idea of value will come from use as the subsidy halves.
- **Capacity times fee yields per-block earnings.** Around $0.005 USD per transaction as they stand; other uses such as EDI would be worth more. Worked comparison: BTC — 1 MB, about 4,000 simple transactions, $20 a block; BCH — 8 MB (16 MB burst), 32,000–35,000 transactions, $160 a block; BSV — 64 MB then 512 MB, 200,000–300,000 transactions now and approaching 1 million, $1,500–$8,000 a block.
- **Minimum coin value is block earnings divided by utility as capacity.** That formula excludes the scenario where the better system wipes out the worse ones altogether, and it excludes the gambling price of bitcoin — only what miners will earn as a service.
- **Monetary-premium narratives are downstream of use.** Not the hype, not the global-money argument: those only come when bitcoin is a commodity money and the ledger is the commodity.
- **Unbounded capacity is the end-state.** nChain's Teranode aims to handle over 1.0 TB within the next three years (aiming for 2), with miners earning over $600,000 for each terabyte block every 10 minutes on average; 2–4 billion transactions per block within 2–3 years is 6.5 million TPS — Visa, MasterCard, banking in SWIFT, and all global currencies in under 15% of a block.

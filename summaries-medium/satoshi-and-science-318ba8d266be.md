# Satoshi and Science
**Date:** 2019-05-30 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/satoshi-and-science-318ba8d266be
**Subtitle:** The scientific principle applies in all aspects of life including jurisprudence and law. It has done so for a long time. Generally…

## Core thesis
A short Medium excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net. Craig argues that the scientific method governs jurisprudence as much as laboratory science, and that his authorship of Bitcoin will be established through legal evidentiary process — "I don't need to give more than I need to give as evidence". He pairs this with a direct attack on "code is law": Bitcoin "is not about replacing courts. Rather, it augments them", and was designed as the opposite of criminal e-money like Liberty Reserve and e-gold.

## Key arguments and claims
- "The scientific principle applies in all aspects of life, including jurisprudence and law" — he lists the seven-step method (Question, Research, Hypothesis, Experiment, Observations, Results/Conclusion, Communicate) and links a Yale Law School paper as authority.
- Evidentiary minimality: "I don't need to give more than I need to give as evidence" — the standard of proof is the court's, not the crypto community's demand for public key-signing.
- "Bitcoin isn't 'code is law,' it is not about replacing courts. Rather, it augments them" — a direct rejection of smart-contract legal absolutism.
- Motive attribution to critics: "People don't want me stepping into court in seeking to prove I created Bitcoin because it robs them of the narrative."
- Anti-crime design intent: "I've had enough of people taking my invention and twisting it into something corrupted and rotten" — Bitcoin "is about the opposite of Liberty Reserve. It is about the opposite of e-gold. It is designed to rip apart and end forever everything that such systems promote."
- Closing threat: "I've said it before: careful what you wish for" — addressed to "those seeking to discredit me", who "are going to find out the hard way how law works".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He transfers the authority of the scientific method into jurisprudence, framing litigation itself as the experiment that will test the hypothesis of his authorship. The key distinction is between legal standards of evidence (court-tested, minimal-but-sufficient) and the crypto community's demanded cryptographic demonstration, which he treats as illegitimate. The rhetorical mode is combative and prophetic — part legal brief, part warning.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Explicitly contradicts the "code is law" orthodoxy of the smart-contract era: Bitcoin augments courts rather than replacing them.
- Contradicts crypto culture's evidentiary norm that Satoshi must "sign a message" to prove identity — Craig asserts court-grade evidence is the proper and only necessary venue.
- Contradicts the "Bitcoin is neutral, amoral money" framing: he insists it was purpose-built against criminal e-money systems, "designed to rip apart and end forever everything that such systems promote".

# Institutional madness — core principles

- **The consequence of decentralisation is an immutable audit trail.** Bitcoin survives because it sits inside a legal framework that lets it be traced, not because it hides from the state.
- **A blockchain simplifies tracing; it does not preclude it.** The ledger is what makes the system legal; systems built to lose records are the opposite of Bitcoin.
- **Record-losing layers are money-services businesses.** Every Lightning or sidechain node and user must keep full compliance details or break the law; the design that “loses records” is the dirty secret.
- **Stopping crime is not censorship.** There is no design brief in which censorship-resistance is the purpose; Bitcoin is secure enough to act within legal frameworks.
- **Legal fungibility protects the innocent.** Alice who takes coins without knowledge, for a valid exchange of goods, is protected; Charlie who mixes stolen funds must still prove source of wealth in court.
- **Jurisdiction follows the money.** Taking European funds brings MLD5; touching dollars, a US person or a .com domain brings US law — often both at once.
- **Double-spending is already cheaper in cash.** Merchants price in counterfeit coins and notes; a Bitcoin double-spend costs more to attempt, must be done in seconds, and non-payment is a matter for the courts.
- **Spend-and-replace is the usage model.** Holding idle so a few can exit is not how a cash system puts upward pressure on genuine use.

# The wheel of time is not on an axis — core principles

- **A Bitcoin transaction is an ordinary ledger entry.** It reassigns value from one key pair to another; AML law already speaks of funds, and Bitcoin is already covered.
- **Exchange and KYC sit inside existing rules.** Bitcoin-for-fiat exchange is money transmission; where an exchange is used, KYC applies under the Bank Secrecy Act and FinCEN guidance as written.
- **Harder cases are still legal cases.** Multisig, conditional smart contracts, embedded data, tokenisation of real property and chattels, theft, fraudulent procurement and cross-border assignment are problems of existing law, not a new legal universe.
- **Offer and acceptance still have to be located.** Whether transmission or six-block inclusion is acceptance, and where the wallet sits, decides contract formation under ordinary doctrines.
- **Bitcoin helps corporations keep honest books.** Record-keeping, tax, compliance and shareholder registries are the commercial use; it was never designed as an attack on banks or companies.
- **The evidentiary trail is the crime-fighting property.** Corruption, illicit drug sales and trafficking leave a record that can be used in court — a decades-long process, not a slogan.
- **ICOs do not democratise finance.** Qualified-investor rules exist to protect people without information; bypassing those controls recycles penny-share and web-IPO scams.
- **The existing system already democratises access.** Dematerialised shares and derivatives have been database entries for decades; going off-exchange has always been a way to lose money, not a revolution.

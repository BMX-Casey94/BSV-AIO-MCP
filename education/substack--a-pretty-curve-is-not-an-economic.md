# A Pretty Curve Is Not an Economic Theory — core principles

- **A monetary system is judged by payment function, not by a price chart.** Transaction volume, merchant acceptance, unit-of-account usage, wage denomination, velocity, fee stability and payment finality are the relevant tests. Price as a function of time alone cannot speak to any of them.
- **Custody is not circulation.** Opening a wallet, holding an ETF claim, or watching a logged price rise is not peer-to-peer cash. Price is not adoption; speculative buying is not use.
- **Fit is not mechanism.** A high in-sample R² on a log-log regression is a goodness-of-fit statistic, not evidence of causality, stability or monetary theory. Trending series produce impressive, meaningless fits (Granger–Newbold; Phillips).
- **Only an economic mechanism distinguishes adoption from mania.** The same logged specification fits internet diffusion, railway buildout, housing bubbles and a Ponzi's manufactured statements. A curve that cannot tell those apart is chronology pretending to be economics.
- **Parameter fragility is a warning, not a feature.** A robust structural law should not depend on start date, price source, sampling frequency or an early-illiquidity window. Out-of-sample forecast without recalibration is the test; redrawing bands is a story being protected from reality.
- **Contract structure, delivery and enforcement carry the explanation.** Tulip prices, railway shares and housing derivatives all had curves. Serious monetary argument specifies demand, settlement and use — not a line through a log-log chart.

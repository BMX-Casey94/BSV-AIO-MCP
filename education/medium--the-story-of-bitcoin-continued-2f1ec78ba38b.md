# The story of Bitcoin, continued — core principles

- **Immutable evidence is the design purpose.** Bitcoin was built as money that is private in daily life yet leaves a trail that cannot be lost or rewritten — the opposite of systems in which transactional data can disappear.
- **Lost records enable crime.** Terrorist financing and similar flows persist because existing banking can lose transactional data; an undeletable ledger is the anathema to that disappearance.
- **Privacy without Gyges.** Anonymity is a curse: the system is private cash, not a ring of invisibility. Pseudonymity plus an evidence trail is the intended pair; anonymity is not.
- **A protocol-changing fork is a new issue.** The rule-set is set in stone; when it is changed the result is a new system with an airdrop and new issuers. Continuity of the original rules is what identifies the issued system — Bitcoin SV as the 2009 issue, a SegWit-altered chain as a 2017 airdrop.
- **Decentralisation is not the absence of an issued protocol.** The protocol is a fixed issued system, not a committee that can rewrite the money. Builders rely on that immutability as the business and legal guarantee.

# Mechanised Myths: Control, Compliance, and the Dystopian Present — core principles

- **Marketed decentralisation is not measured decentralisation.** A network can promise trustless distribution while control concentrates in "a few mining entities, protocol committees, and development gatekeepers"; builders must audit who actually holds power across each layer, not the rhetoric.
- **A timestamped ledger is only as honest as the data it records.** The capacity to "define, exclude, or interpret entries transforms the ledger from a neutral archive into a curated narrative" — input integrity and interpretation rights are governance surfaces that must be evaluated before building on any chain.
- **Compliance can be engineered into architecture.** Control in modern systems operates by design rather than by force: monitoring, granular auditing and metering are built into the rails, so the choice of platform determines the compliance regime your users inherit.
- **Intermediary interdependence collectivises risk and prices out entry.** Finance, tech platforms and energy exhibit cartel logic in plain sight — operational interdependence that socialises systemic risk while dictating standards, protocols and definitions of compliance.
- **Micrometering every interaction is an enclosure of the commons.** When every action is priced, metered and billed, value is extracted through "the aggregation of countless small, compliant actions" — a design outcome builders should weigh against micropayments' liberating potential.
- **Builder guidance.** Before sinking investment into a Bitcoin network, demand evidence of who controls mining, protocol governance and development; treat delegated verification feeds as a trust assumption to be understood, not independence to be assumed.

# The “Perfect” Firewall… — core principles

- **Security is economics, not perfection.** It is a risk function: you do not pay more than the asset is worth to secure it; if you do, you are not secure.
- **Availability is one side of the bargain.** A control that disconnects the system from its users — the “pliers” firewall — fails the people the system serves.
- **“Secure enough” is the engineering optimum.** Large payment systems are never perfectly secure; they are resilient enough to keep operating.
- **Bitcoin needs to be resilient and available.** There is no such thing as perfect security, and chasing it destroys any hope of a system that works in the real world.
- **Perfect anonymity is the same category error.** Seeking a perfectly anonymous coin produces a system that cannot be used as money.
- **Redundancy beats disconnection.** Under denial-of-service pressure the answer is multiple paths, not taking the payment system offline.
- **There is no perfection, only risk.** That bound applies to protocol design as much as to firewalls.

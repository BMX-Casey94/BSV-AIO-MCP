## Problem addressed
Symmetric diffusion of Bitcoin-style INV/GETDATA traffic lets an observer link transactions to source IP addresses. The specification wants node-level anonymisation so that watching inter-node traffic does not reveal whether a node originated a packet or only relayed it.

## Core mechanism
A Diffusion Mixer Protocol has two stages. In random differential relay (RDR), a node waits a period T (and may be forbidden to transmit during it), collects incoming packets, arbitrarily assigns each packet to a subset of entry nodes, and may XOR-combine packets that share a destination. An RDR table records sources, destinations, time-of-arrival, time-of-relay and duplicate counts; actual send times can be delayed past the next scheduled relay. In standard diffusion the same packet is sent to every entry node, one packet at a time. Each participating node chooses a mode per packet. Encoded packets plus a subset of the originals let a neighbour recover the rest without an interceptor seeing a simple one-to-one relay.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
A blockchain node and a matching method select either RDR — wait for a second packet, then send the collected packets to an arbitrary subset of entry nodes — or standard diffusion to every entry node. Dependent features include network-coded combinations, an RDR table and scheduled delays. A medium claim stores the method.

## BSV relevance
RDR plus optional XOR mixing is a transaction-relay privacy measure for BSV peer graphs that still use inventory-style gossip and are exposed to IP traffic analysis.

# The paradox of the Übermensch

## Core thesis
"Truth is not a function of popular belief": Wright attacks "proof of social media" — the Reddit/Twitter bandwagon demand that he publicly prove his identity — and refuses the crowd's terms, arguing via Nietzsche's Übermensch, Sartre's Nobel refusal, Bastiat and Plato that Bitcoin was designed as pseudonymous, translucent money precisely to circumvent proof-by-demagoguery. He will not sign publicly because "proof is more than signing", and under contract law (offer, acceptance, consideration) he owes the public nothing. Proof of work, he concludes, is the first true meritocracy, whereas proof of stake recreates Plato's oligarchy.

## Key arguments and claims
- The bandwagon fallacy has a new variant: "proof of social media… We can call this the Reddit effect, but it has infected much of society and not only crypto currency." "Seeking social validation does not help you reach truth."
- Nietzsche's Übermensch is "commonly mistranslated into beyond man, Superman or over man"; the real model is "the worldly businessman of the format of a Carnegie. The unwavering focused businessman developing and building come what may without care of social pressure" — "a new form of aristocrat, not one who inherited a position but one who had to earn it in a meritocracy" (Cesare Borgia, Steve Jobs as examples).
- He admits to manipulating his own archived trail: "I taught the ability to use robot.txt files in a manner that would create varying information within sources such as the wayback machine… One skilled in the art can select files that appear and disappear over time." He deleted many old blog files but deliberately left one post's timestamps unmanipulated.
- The preserved post is his 2016 "Jean-Paul Sartre, Signing and Significance": Sartre rejected the 1964 Nobel prize because "If I sign myself Jean-Paul Sartre it is not the same thing as if I sign myself Jean-Paul Sartre, Nobel Prizewinner". Wright reveals the piece was "a manifesto very different to the philosophy of Sartre days after the death of my best friend, David Kleiman".
- On cryptographic proof of identity: "bitcoin is pseudonymous and proof is more than signing." If he signed, "others would simply believe that I'd stolen keys or defrauded others"; he demonstrated in the Sartre piece "knowledge of the most convoluted way of… verify[ing] a signature without resorting to something as simple as dropping it into a bitcoin wallet. But verification and proof was never the key."
- Bastiat's "The Seen and the Unseen" and "A Negative Railway" (1845) frame protocol governance: accommodating every vested interest "between all parties seeking stops on the Bordeaux to Bayonne railway" is as absurd as seeking "all changes from all users of money". "He Who Has a Right to Work Has a Right to Profit."
- Plato's regime cycle (philosopher-king → timocracy → oligarchy → democracy → tyranny) maps onto crypto: democracy's demagogue first attacks the wealthy — "In the Republic, it was in the forum and through lawsuits. In the modern world it starts and Reddit and twitter."
- Bitcoin is a third kind of money Plato could not foresee: "neither anonymous nor open… a money that is translucent… polarising and visible only when seen in certain ways", so that "even family can be abstained out of knowledge from the extent of one's wealth".
- "In a proof of work system, unlike that of proof of stake, we create not an aristocracy, but maybe if it works the first true meritocracy" — proof of stake "gives way to oligarchy as we will see with proof of stake".
- "Bitcoin is a system for the pseudonymous exchange of wealth. It does not redistribute and it does not create any socialist idea of equality. It is a form of money that allows one to prove when they choose to prove and no more." Honesty in a pseudonymous system is maintained "only through the exchange of information between individual parties and not public attestation".
- Contract-law refusal of the crowd's demand: exchange requires offer, acceptance and consideration; "I do not want your adoration and the only thing I will do is ensure none ever take up the mantle I do not want… I have no need of money — rather I have all I will and shall ever need. So, it is likely you have little to offer."
- The paradox of the title: "You cannot call for a leader to lead you into a leaderless society."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Self-described "Socratic style": he layers named philosophical authorities (Nietzsche, Sartre, Bastiat, Plato) to recast a social-media dispute as a 2,400-year-old problem of democracy, envy and proof. The method is analogical and elitist-meritocratic — distinctions (anonymous/open vs translucent; signing vs proof; aristocracy vs meritocracy) do the argumentative work — with an autobiographical reveal (the Kleiman manifesto, the robots.txt admission) used both as evidence and as a demonstration that archives and signatures are gameable.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the community's standing demand that Satoshi prove identity by signing with early keys: Wright asserts signatures prove nothing ("proof is more than signing") and that the demand itself is a fallacy of social validation.
- Contradicts proof-of-stake orthodoxy (Ethereum's roadmap and the wider PoS literature): PoS is Plato's oligarchy reborn; only proof of work yields meritocracy.
- Contradicts the "leaderless decentralised community" ethos of mainstream crypto as incoherent — "You cannot call for a leader to lead you into a leaderless society."
- Contradicts both anonymity maximalism (privacy coins) and radical-transparency maximalism: Bitcoin is "translucent", honest through bilateral disclosure, "not public attestation".

# The Cult of Cross-Disciplinary Prophets
**Date:** 2025-11-27 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-cult-of-cross-disciplinary-prophets
**Subtitle:** How Modern Credulity Manufactures Experts Out of Thin Air

## Core thesis
Modern culture suffers an epidemic of "credential drift" — mastery in one field mistaken for authority in all — and crypto is its purest expression, having canonised the programmer Hal Finney as a macroeconomic oracle on the strength of a "valuation" formula (global household wealth divided by coin supply) that is numerology, not economics. Misapplied authority, Craig argues, is not harmless flattery but a civic hazard that corrodes discourse, markets and policy.

## Key arguments and claims
- The "Nobel Disease" taxonomy: Linus Pauling (chemistry laureate turned vitamin-C megadose evangelist), William Shockley (transistor co-inventor turned eugenicist), Kary Mullis (PCR inventor turned HIV denialist), James Watson (DNA structure turned race pundit). Lesson: "Excellence in one domain does not confer competence in another."
- Finney is the central case study: a "competent programmer" whose casual forum musing — "Total worldwide household wealth… divide by 20 million coins… equals coin value" — has been enthroned as holy writ.
- The formula's buried assumptions: all wealth is liquid, convertible and seeking one speculative token; no competing assets, regulations, externalities, transaction costs or market structure exist.
- Real valuation requires liquidity, velocity, marginal utility, market structure, regulatory regime, distribution and use case — none of which appears in the Finney incantation.
- Reductio ad absurdum: by the same logic, "if I carve twenty wooden ducks, and global wealth is $300 trillion, each duck should be worth $15 million."
- Three psychological drivers of the fallacy: authority bias (prestige spilling across domains), cognitive laziness ("thinking is hard; parroting is easy"), and desperation for validation — speculative markets "generate prophets the way swamps generate mosquitoes."
- A catalogue of tech overreach: the developer who thinks SHA-256 unlocks Austrian economics; the cryptographer who thinks complexity theory explains consciousness; the "early in Bitcoin" influencer as self-declared monetary PhD; founders as amateur constitutional theorists (Vitalik on governance, CZ on regulatory philosophy, Jack Dorsey "exploring metaphysics through hash rate"); Musk on demography; Zuckerberg on political stability; Altman on metaphysics — "a Californian mystic with better funding."
- "Expertise-by-osmosis": quoting programmers as if they were central bankers and treating forum posts as peer-reviewed papers.
- The costs: bad economic predictions, pseudoscience, cults of personality, wealth destruction, and public policy shaped by people who cannot define the words they use.
- Posthumous canonisation is the mechanism: "The dead cannot clarify. The dead cannot disclaim." Finney "is not quoted—he is ventriloquised."
- The antidote is intellectual discipline: Gracián's "Know what you know, and know what you don't," Rand's "Competence is not transferable by wishful thinking," and the civic courage to say "He doesn't know that."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Satirical anatomy in the Mencken–Wilde mode, built on informal-fallacy analysis (authority bias, category error, the self-named "Blacksmith–Neurosurgeon Fallacy") and reinforced by economic first principles: the essay dissolves the Finney formula by enumerating what genuine monetary valuation actually requires.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the "millions per coin" Finney prophecy that maximalists cite as mathematically confirmed destiny.
- Rejects "number go up" apologetics as "a psychological salve, not an economic one."
- Inverts the usual accusation of ignorance: the faithful are the ones who "know C++ but not CPI."
- Distinguishes engineering statements from economic prophecy — early Bitcoin figures' casual musings are not scripture (a selectivity Craig applies elsewhere: Satoshi's design statements are cited as authoritative engineering intent, not macroeconomics).

## Notable quotes

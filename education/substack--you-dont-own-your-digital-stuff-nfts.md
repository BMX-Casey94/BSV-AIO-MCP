# You Don’t Own Your Digital Stuff. NFTs Could Actually Fix That — Without Intellectual Property. — core principles

- **Digital purchases today are revocable licences, not ownership.** Amazon's 2009 remote deletion of Orwell's *1984* from Kindles was the system working as designed, not a glitch; two decades of the digital economy have replaced ownership with licensing, property with permission, and markets with platform control.
- **Property norms exist to resolve conflict over scarce means.** Following Menger, Mises, Rothbard and Hoppe: only resources insufficient to satisfy all wants become economic goods, so non-scarce patterns of information need no property institution at all.
- **The anti-IP argument is correct — and does not reach tokens.** Kinsella's *Against Intellectual Property* is airtight on its own terms: copyright and patent are state-granted monopoly privileges restricting what you may do with your own genuinely scarce property. The error is concluding digital ownership is therefore impossible.
- **Distinguish the pattern from the access bundle.** The pattern (infinitely copyable bits) is non-rivalrous; the access bundle (servers, bandwidth, cryptographic keys, device enclaves, platform accounts) is genuinely scarce and excludable. "Digital scarcity" conflates four things — the pattern, the token (a cryptographically unique ledger entry), access to a service, and contractual permission — and the right-click critique lands only on the first.
- **A meaningful token is a transferable title to a defined bundle of claims.** Authenticated access, commercial permissions, community membership, future updates, provenance and royalties — exactly as a house deed is paper whose value comes from an enforcing institutional framework. NFTs do not make information scarce; they make titles to access scarce.
- **A token without enforcement infrastructure is a collectible, not property.** Most 2021–22 tokens were certificates referencing centrally hosted content with no enforcement linkage — photocopied title deeds declared to be houses. The argument applies only to tokens integrated with token-gated consumption and production.
- **Piracy is answered by competition, not prohibition.** The analogue hole means copying cannot be technically eliminated, but Spotify, Netflix, live-service games and cloud software show authorised access can be made so superior that copying becomes commercially irrelevant — the mechanism is competitive service provision, not state-enforced DRM.
- **Token property is compatible with the strongest anti-IP position under two conditions.** Claims must attach to scarce means (a positive claim to specific means, not a negative claim against the world's use of non-scarce information), and enforcement must be market-produced — cryptography and contract rather than state grant.
- **The required architecture is concrete.** Cryptographic key management, token-gated delivery, device-bound rendering (Apple Secure Enclave, ARM TrustZone), and — critically — secondary-market synchronisation so access follows the token and resales are not empty.
- **Tokens restore alienability to digital goods.** Resale, gifting and bequest re-enable the secondary-market price signals Mises identified as central to the market process; the 2021–22 crash (a correction exceeding 90 per cent) was a classic Austrian boom-bust that cleared speculative froth while leaving viable applications intact.

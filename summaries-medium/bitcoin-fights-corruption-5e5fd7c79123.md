# Bitcoin Fights Corruption
**Date:** 2019-10-23 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-fights-corruption-5e5fd7c79123
**Subtitle:** The battle for Bitcoin is very simple, and it goes right back to the days before I launched it. My vision lies in a system that will…

## Core thesis
An excerpt — the body continues on craigwright.net. Bitcoin was designed "not to avoid government, but to act within law": an honest-money system whose permanent record destroys crime (Silk Road, child-pornography rings, ransomware) and makes ICO promoters accountable, while creating trust, enabling global micropayments, and restoring long-term, generational capitalism against short-term "mercantilist" extraction.

## Key arguments and claims
- "My vision lies in a system that will utterly destroy crime. I designed Bitcoin not to avoid government, but to act within law" — it "will bring down activist criminal groups and more mainstream traditional criminals".
- Empirical hook: "the collapse of Silk Road and — just the other day — the arrest of hundreds of people involved with child porn rings" show Bitcoin "enables the tracing of criminal activity, allowing law enforcement to shut such activities down".
- SPV lets individual users "act as peers" with privacy, while the design "artfully removes any trace of anonymity": "If you purchase drugs using Bitcoin, you will be recorded forever"; ransomware proceeds "eventually… will be seized".
- ICOs are rebranded frauds: "really just a new name for a web IPO of the 1990s or a pink sheet scam of the previous era". In the '80s and '90s conmen escaped "by destroying records"; Bitcoin's immutable record "makes the creators of such tokens accountable… today you can be tracked down a decade later".
- "The creation of yet another token is not innovation, and it never will be" — wealth comes from "the creation of an industry and goods and services and a company that delivers what it promises", not from tokens; "democratise finance" is "the same con job today" as in the 1990s.
- "Bitcoin was never about decentralisation for decentralisation's sake. The call for decentralisation doesn't even make sense. It's a religious catchword of a cult based on people saying that government is bad."
- Honest money and stewardship: Bitcoin favours "long-term gains rather than short-term misguidance" and zero-sum extraction; it is "a system of stewardship" — build businesses "designed not to last months or years or even decades, but generations".
- Political economy: today's capitalism drifts towards "the system of mercantilism that Adam Smith wrote against", cherry-picking ideas while "ignoring moral sentiments"; Henry Ford "paid people more than the base wage" to build engagement, whereas today "we have layoffs for no better reason than quarterly gains".
- Micropayments as inclusion: Bitcoin "opens up global markets through global micropayments, where people in Third World countries can earn small payments with low fees, enabling them and helping them to help themselves".
- Trust inversion: "Bitcoin is not about destroying trust. Bitcoin is about creating trust" — because crime is captured, fraud reversed and theft rendered worthless, "we can build a world based on trust".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
A moral-economic essay: design-intent autobiography plus a Smithian framing (anti-mercantilist, attentive to *moral sentiments*) of what capitalism should be; current enforcement events (Silk Road, the October 2019 child-exploitation arrests) serve as vindication; historical analogy (ICO = pink sheets = web IPO) debunks "innovation" claims. The rhetorical mode is sermonic — stewardship, purpose, "why have you even bothered waking up?".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the cypherpunk/crypto-anarchist purpose statement: Bitcoin was built to act within law and destroy crime, not to take down governments.
- Against decentralisation as a terminal value: it is "a religious catchword of a cult".
- Against the token/ICO innovation narrative: tokens are old scams with a new label; only real industry creates wealth.
- Against "trustlessness" as the ideal: Bitcoin creates trust rather than destroying it.
- Against anonymity: privacy for users, yes, but the permanent record ensures "if you purchase drugs using Bitcoin, you will be recorded forever".

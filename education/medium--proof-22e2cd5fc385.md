# Proof — core principles

- **A key is evidence, not proof.** "Evidence is not possession of a key. It is evidence of possession of a key and no more." A digital signature is "at best a conditional or presumptive proof." Mere possession of a file is evidence of possession of a file.
- **Code cannot be law.** There is no definitive means to assess truth through a computer program. The onus probandi comprises a dual burden of production and of persuasion. "Law is not binary. We cannot make law binary, and code cannot be law in any fair society." Proof is weighed by judges and juries.
- **Companies cannot sign.** "A company does not act. It (the company) has human agents, directors, and officers, and they act for the company." Digital corporate signatures rest on securely stored data and human agency, not programs.
- **Key possession as proof would collapse justice.** If possession of a key were proof, Ross Ulbricht would be exonerated the moment anyone else spent from an attributed key — and a second "Dread Pirate Roberts" did reopen Silk Road one month after seizure. Computers are insecure; under code-is-law people would be wrongfully accused and framed.
- **Bitcoin is one source of immutable evidence.** Used correctly it "provides an evidentiary trail that can lower the cost of taking action in court, yet it does not replace the court with mere keys." It is "not anonymous but pseudonymous."
- **One ledger, not many sets of books.** Sidechains and methodologies that allow people to create many ledgers undermine the single evidentiary source.
- **Privacy includes a right of refusal.** "It is the right not to answer when others seek answers." Proof-by-social-media inverts the burden onto the innocent.

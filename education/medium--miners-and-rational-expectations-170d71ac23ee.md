# Miners and rational expectations — core principles

- **Proof-of-work mining is memoryless.** "The miner expects the next block at 10 mins from now — ALWAYS"; a 33% miner expects its own next block "at 30 mins from now — always", regardless of how much time has already elapsed.
- **Miners decide ex ante on rational expectations.** When a 33% miner sees a competitor's block, its expected wait on the old height immediately exceeds its constant 30-minute expectation (it must now propagate after the other block), so it switches — "Nodes stop mining AS SOON AS THEY SEE a block. It IS how Bitcoin functions."
- **Selfish-mining payoffs rest on a "forward memory" fallacy.** The attack models reason over hypothetical future chains — "remembering what HAS NOT happened" — while ignoring the losses incurred waiting for a rare win; no rational miner with ongoing costs can adopt such a strategy.
- **Counterfactuals are incoherent in path-dependent processes.** Had miner B changed strategy, its later realised win would not exist ("if B changed, B would not have the block at T=500"); retrospective measurement of realised chains cannot reveal the ex-ante rational choice.
- **Pre-consensus changes the epistemic situation.** "Miners now know what is expected. They have no rational expectation of waiting. They know." A certain and an uncertain system "are NOT the same thing" — collapsing uncertainty strengthens the network's ordering.
- **Bitcoin's security is designed and whole-system, not emergent.** Evaluate miner strategies at the level of the whole coupled system; analysing isolated actors and declaring the parts explain the whole is reductionism — "The idea of emergence is a religion."

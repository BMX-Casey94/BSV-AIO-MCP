# I shall continue answering in order. — core principles

- **Nodes are miners.** "Nodes are miners, these are commercial entities that compete" — the network's validating backbone is profit-seeking business, not hobbyist full nodes.
- **The roadmap is: remove limits, then lock.** "The aim is to remove all limits to scale as soon as possible. Then, and only then, to lock the base protocol and stop any arbitrary change."
- **SPV is sufficient for users.** "SPV is safe and is all a user needs" — users should never need to run nodes.
- **Layer 2 lives in Script.** "Layer 2 is IN SCRIPT" — second-layer functionality belongs in Bitcoin Script predicates, not in external networks.
- **Malleability is not a flaw.** Transaction malleability — the stated justification for SegWit — is reframed as a non-bug; SegWit removed the signature's commitment in response to a problem the design did not have.
- **Decentralisation has a precise, instrumental definition.** "Distributed and decentralised means that there are 3 or more miners with no group holding more than 50% control" — it is a tool used to achieve goals, not a goal in itself; excessive node counts are "more insecure than centralised", and adding 1,000× the nodes must be justified against cost.
- **Law supersedes code.** "LAW is law, code is at best a form of evidence" — on-chain artefacts serve courts; they do not replace them.
- **ICOs and tokenised securities are distinct categories.** "ICOs are a scam. FULL STOP", while "A digital IPO, tokenised shares, equity, bonds, etc are NOT ICOs."
- **Governance weight tracks risk, not holdings.** Roman-Republic-style weighted voting: power follows "not proof of stake, what you had and want to keep, but what you are willing to risk".
- **Consensus is competition, not community agreement.** "Bitcoin is not about people agreeing — it is about them competing" — a capitalist system of competition rather than non-competitive group consensus.

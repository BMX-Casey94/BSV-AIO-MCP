# Binance: The Untrusted Intermediary — core principles

- **Cryptocurrencies are property.** They are subject to law as property; title, not mere possession of keys, determines what you can own and call your own.
- **Nemo dat quod non habet.** No one gives what they do not have. Coins mixed with criminal funds carry defective title, so a buyer of tainted coins does not take good title.
- **Privacy is not anonymity.** Exchange-level anonymity is not a lawful privacy right; it is a facilitation of crime. Privacy means known counterparties and unlinkability to the public, not nameless onboarding.
- **KYC that is unverified is theatre.** Accounts opened on an email and an unverified name do not satisfy customer due diligence; withdrawal allowances on such accounts are a laundering vector.
- **A “decentralised exchange” is not a regulatory safe category.** Multiple servers designed to bypass controls are a distributed laundering operation, not a legal exemption.
- **Mixers contaminate honest volume.** Clean flow from ordinary users supplies the cover needed to wash criminal funds; taint follows coins, so proceeds-of-crime law reaches ordinary holders.
- **Self-custody does not clean title.** Even coins withdrawn to one’s own keys remain legally defective if they were acquired from a tainted pool.

# Welcome to science
**Date:** 2019-03-30 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/welcome-to-science-c5e33a827449
**Subtitle:** To be a valid hypothesis under the scientific method, there are steps that need to be followed. It is not simply saying that something is…

## Core thesis
Most celebrated "attacks" on and "improvements" to Bitcoin — selfish mining, CTOR, transaction-malleability exploits, Lightning — fail the basic requirements of the scientific method: they are unfalsified mathematical models never tested against the live system. A valid hypothesis must explain, predict, and enable control, and must be tested against reality with the original Bitcoin design as the null hypothesis. Those who skip empirical testing and appeal to authority are "selling you snake oil".

## Key arguments and claims
- Three tests of a scientific theory: (1) explanatory power with minimal assumptions and falsifiability "without needing justification through external calls such as a call to authority"; (2) predictive success exceeding the existing belief structure — "If a theory has no predictive power, it has no use in science"; (3) the hypothesis enables control — predicting effects of growth and scale.
- Selfish mining indicted: it "seeks to explain Bitcoin using a mathematical model. Doing so in itself forms a hypothesis and not a theory" — the authors should have run "a number of nodes, and measure[d] how they react"; "It is not about testing a mathematical model against the mathematical model but rather testing against reality."
- The null hypothesis is fixed: "Bitcoin, as it was designed originally, is the null hypothesis. Always." — any proposed change (selfish mining, CTOR, Lightning) must be tested against the existing system, not against its own model.
- No evidence was even sought: "In the case of something like the completely unscientific claims of selfish mining, it is not only that there is no evidence; no evidence was ever sought." Both selfish mining and CTOR "provide results that differ significantly from the behaviour of Bitcoin".
- Motive attribution: selfish mining "was all about creating a reputation and showing off by a postmodernist quack trying to pose as a scientist"; the defence "I do not want to create something others will exploit" is "how quacks and charlatans sell their snake oil".
- The seven-step hypothesis test is spelled out (state H0, state alternative, set α, collect data, test statistic, rejection regions, conclude) — with worked alternatives: "Can selfish miners gain a significant profit advantage…? Would it be easily detectable?"; "Is CTOR faster than the default order in Bitcoin blocks?"; for Lightning, "Can you create a system… that will be allowed in anti-money laundering (AML) law and at scale?"
- Measurement rigour: reliability, precision and accuracy are distinguished — repeatability, variance around the mark, and closeness to the bullseye — and all claims about Bitcoin "link to a measurable and quantifiable metric".
- Fallacy policing: appeals to "scientist X or Y" are "the false appeal to authority" relying "on an argument from ignorance"; "you cannot take one false theory to explain another" — the malleability-based theft claims are named as an instance.
- Qualitative dodges rejected: "Those who tell you that they cannot qualitatively measure the results of the system change are seeking to promote an agenda without evidence."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Philosophy-of-science formalism deployed as polemic: Popperian falsifiability plus a hypothesis-testing checklist imported from statistics, then applied as a gatekeeping device — claims that skip empirical validation against the live network are disqualified *a priori*, regardless of their authors' credentials. The framework does double duty: it elevates the original Bitcoin design to the privileged null hypothesis (so the burden of proof always falls on changers) and recasts academic critics as "postmodernist" pseudo-scientists with agendas.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Academic security literature: the selfish-mining result (Eyal/Sirer) — accepted by mainstream researchers as a real incentive flaw — is dismissed as an untested model contradicted by observed miner behaviour.
- "Responsible disclosure" norms: refusing to publish exploit details is recast as charlatanism; science requires public, replicable testing.
- CTOR as optimisation: the BCH-side canonical-ordering proposal (then live post-split) is treated as an unjustified protocol change with no demonstrated predictive or performance benefit over the default order.
- Lightning as scaling solution: pre-emptively framed as a hypothesis that fails on AML-law compliance, record-keeping and competition grounds — questions its advocates never empirically answered.
- Credentialism: the era's deference to academic cryptographers is inverted — authority without empirical test is an argument from ignorance.

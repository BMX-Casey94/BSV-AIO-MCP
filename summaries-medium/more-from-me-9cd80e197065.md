# More from me
**Date:** 2018-09-04 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/more-from-me-9cd80e197065

## Core thesis
A second directory post (companion to "For more", same day), this time mapping Wright's "@ProfFaustus" alias. Six linked essays cluster on one theme: a defence of Bitcoin's mining economics against the "selfish mining" literature and against mining-centralisation alarmism, plus on-chain scaling advocacy and a Bitcoin Cash malleability critique. The post's function is canon-assembly — attaching the ProfFaustus output to his public identity.

## Key arguments and claims
- A three-part offensive against the selfish-mining theory: "Why Selfish miners do not exist and never will" ("I have started simultaneously teaching people how bitcoin works for the first time"), "The Markov mysteries…" ("a key aspect of the model proposed by" the selfish-mining authors — i.e. an attack on the Markov-chain modelling in Eyal & Sirer-style analysis), and "The cancer that is the Selfish mining fallacy" ("the false notion that rational miners are some sort of central socialist" planner).
- Mining scale is not a threat: "The trouble with Too much of anything" — "the arguments against commercial mining come down to an outcry against 'mining centralisation'", which the essay treats as misconceived.
- Scaling is being deliberately obstructed: "Scaling Bitcoin and what some will do to stop this" — "We at nChain have been doing research into many aspects of Bitcoin. One of these is scaling. A consequence of this…" — tying the alias's output to nChain's research programme.
- Bitcoin Cash does not need a malleability fix: "Spending the simple way in Bitcoin cash" — "many people believe that we require a malleability fix in Bitcoin cash. When pushed for a real…" (the teaser cuts off, but the stance is anti-SegWit-style-malleability-justification carried into the BCH context).

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
As with "For more", the method is self-citation as authority-building, but the teasers reveal the argumentative style of the underlying essays: mathematical-model critique (attacking the Markov assumptions behind selfish mining), economic-incentive reasoning (rational miners are competitors, not socialist central planners), and institutional affiliation (nChain research). The clustering shows Wright treating mining as an economic competition problem rather than a purely cryptographic one.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly contradicts the academic selfish-mining literature (Eyal/Sirer and successors) that underpins much BTC-era anxiety about miner incentives and was used to justify protocol conservatism.
- Contradicts the mining-centralisation orthodoxy — the widespread view that large commercial miners threaten Bitcoin — by treating commercial mining as the system's intended engine.
- Contradicts the malleability-fix rationale that justified SegWit on BTC, extended here to argue Bitcoin Cash needs no such fix either.
- Implicitly contradicts small-block scaling orthodoxy via the nChain scaling-research entry.

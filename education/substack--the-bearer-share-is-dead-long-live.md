# The Bearer Share Is Dead. Long Live Proof of Stake. — core principles

- **Bearer shares were abolished as a governance judgment.** The FATF's 2022 revision of Recommendation 24 required prohibition of new issuance and conversion or immobilisation of existing bearer shares across more than 200 jurisdictions; the UK acted in 2015 with 100% compliance and Switzerland in 2019 with criminal sanctions. The UK post-implementation review concluded lawful-use potential did not outweigh the benefits of entirely preventing the potential for misuse.
- **The bearer share was an anonymous governance instrument.** The holder voted, determined board composition, approved related-party transactions and controlled corporate resources while remaining invisible — enabling hidden concentration (40–60% positions appearing as nothing), self-dealing immune to fiduciary duty (which requires knowing who the fiduciary is), and regulatory evasion.
- **Even registered intermediation failed to tame the opacity.** The Clearstream case — Iranian central bank assets sitting hidden in an omnibus account for years — shows custodial deposit did not solve the problem; pure bearer instruments were hopeless.
- **Delegation mechanics recreate the instrument.** On Cosmos Hub, one bonded ATOM is one vote, delegators inherit their validator's vote unless they actively override, and a person controlling ten validators through ten legal entities in ten jurisdictions — collectively 30% of staked ATOM — has no disclosure obligation. The system asks "do you control a private key associated with sufficient stake?", never "who are you?"
- **The Sybil asymmetry makes capture undetectable by design.** Fragmenting a paper bearer position across fifty identities required fifty certificates, safe-deposit boxes and nominee arrangements — positive marginal cost. Fragmenting a 51% PoS position across 51 apparently independent 1% addresses costs some gas fees and wallet management: a governance 51% attack with no consensus disruption, just ordinary-looking governance — autocracy cosplaying as decentralisation.
- **Address-level decentralisation metrics are unfalsifiable.** The Nakamoto coefficient is measured at address level (Cosmos Hub: 6–7 as of late 2022), but the beneficial-ownership Nakamoto coefficient could be 1 and the system cannot tell us. A decentralisation claim the system cannot falsify is a statement of faith, not a statement of fact.
- **Delegation-inheritance is proxy voting without the regulation.** Corporate proxy voting in the US is regulated under Section 14(a) of the Exchange Act, with disclosure rules, SEC oversight of advisory firms (ISS, Glass Lewis) and fiduciary duties for institutional voters; validators bear none of these obligations.
- **Exchange staking silently evaporates retail governance power.** On Tezos, exchange bakers (Coinbase, Binance, Kraken) hold among the largest staking balances yet participate in governance quite infrequently, if at all — outcomes are determined by a smaller set of bakers while retail delegators' votes lapse.
- **MiCA does not address the problem.** It regulates issuers and service providers, not the governance relationship between token holder and protocol, and its Recital 22 decentralisation carve-out exempts precisely the systems where the bearer-governance pathology is most acute — a structural gap, not an accidental one.
- **The measurement rule for builders and investors.** Evaluate any network's decentralisation at the beneficial-controller level, not the address level; treat costless pseudonymous identity generation, programmable vote delegation and cross-jurisdictional operation as amplifiers of governance risk, and prefer consensus whose authority lapses when expenditure stops.

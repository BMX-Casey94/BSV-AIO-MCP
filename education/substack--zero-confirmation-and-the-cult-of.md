# Zero-Confirmation and the Cult of the Spectator — core principles

- **Bitcoin's security rests on block-producing actors, not passive observers.** The white paper describes competitive block production through proof of work. Block producers determine transaction ordering; a non-mining node may verify, relay and keep a copy, but none of those functions determines inclusion.
- **Validation is not authority.** Validation determines whether a transaction complies with the rules. Block production determines whether it becomes part of the ledger. Observation does not create authority — the spectator fallacy.
- **Zero-confirmation acceptance is ordinary commercial risk management.** Bills of exchange, cheques, drafts and letters of credit all circulated without final settlement. Clearing houses concentrated information among institutions capable of acting on it; they did not eliminate risk.
- **An unconfirmed transaction is not an absence of information.** Signatures can be verified, structure examined, propagation observed, conflicting spends detected, and block producers' receipt acknowledged — none of which requires a block.
- **The actors who determine ordering are finite and identifiable.** Communication with them is a networking problem, not a philosophical one. Merchants need probability assessments, not omniscience.
- **Mining consolidates because it is capital-intensive.** Infrastructure, energy contracts, specialised hardware and financing drive concentration, as in steel, semiconductors, telecoms and shipping. The relevant question is whether sufficient competition exists among block producers to prevent collusion — not whether millions of miners exist.
- **Home-node counts measure spectators, not security.** Consensus is not democracy; nodes are not voters; security is not popularity. Blocks are not created through applause.

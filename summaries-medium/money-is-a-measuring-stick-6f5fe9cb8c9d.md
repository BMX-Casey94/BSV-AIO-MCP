# Money is a measuring stick
**Date:** 2019-05-14 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/money-is-a-measuring-stick-6f5fe9cb8c9d
**Subtitle:** Something that people don't seem to understand well is that money is not in itself a valuable. Money is simply a measurement of stored…

## Core thesis
Money has no intrinsic value: it is a ledger, "a measurement of stored debt" and a call on society's goods, originating in Bronze Age grain receipts such as the shekel and talent. A growing capitalist economy is naturally deflationary, and Bitcoin at scale is neither inflationary nor deflationary — its proper role is as a stable global measuring stick, which requires massive scaling that is perhaps 20 years away.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Money is simply a measurement of stored debt. It is a call on society for past exchange" — a marker for "selling into the ledger and then taking back out of it". Value comes from the goods money is exchanged for, not from money itself.
- Origin story: the shekel (2 bekahs, 11.34 grams — Exodus 30:23) and talent (60 minas, 34.02 kg — Ezra 8:26) began as grain-weight measures; Late Bronze/Early Iron Age communal granaries issued tallies against deposited grain, so "shekels and talents originated as grain receipts". Specialisation (pottery, brick-making from straw) followed from storable surplus — "It is the origin of money."
- Money is a call on the individual items in the whole economy, not on GDP; the calculation of supply and demand across all goods "is too complex for any individual to plan" — even Moore's law doubling for a thousand years couldn't match capitalism, and the storage "would require more atoms than we have in our universe" (a knowledge-problem argument against central planning).
- Value is set not by production but by consumption against production: an economy producing 110 units and consuming 100 deflates. "Right now, we have a deflationary economy" — the richest Golden Age in history, with per-person wealth growing faster than population.
- "Inflation is used to balance and hide the fact." Many in government "believe that growth is bad" because capitalism is disruptive and "rearranges and reorders the status quo... Such is the nature of profit."
- "When scaled, Bitcoin is not deflationary. A vibrant growing capitalist society is deflationary." Ceteris paribus, "Bitcoin is neither inflationary nor deflationary at scale"; in a stationary economy, growth in Bitcoin's value "exists only through speculation".
- At 3.1% growth, society doubles its goods and services every two decades — "for every person alive in 20 years' time, they will have double the amount of goods and services we have now".
- "This is why Bitcoin should be stable. A national currency measures political concerns." Bitcoin as a global ledger would let people track the international economy, but "it hasn't achieved the required level, and likely won't for another 20 years".
- Scaling roadmap: "In the next decade, we will have the capacity not just for Visa or Mastercard but to allow all banking transactions to be conducted securely" with no doubt as to whether a transaction occurred.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues from monetary first principles in a Menger/Mises-style regression narrative (grain receipts → coinage), supported by biblical citation and archaeological claims about silos and granaries. He then deploys a Hayekian calculation argument (no computer could out-plan the market), simple numerical thought experiments (110 produced vs 100 consumed), and ceteris paribus analysis to separate Bitcoin's monetary properties from the economy's growth.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly attacks the "digital gold"/hard-money-deflation thesis: a fixed-cap coin is not magically deflationary — "The growth in the value of Bitcoin in a stationary economy exists only through speculation."
- Rejects the hodl-as-value model: value comes from use and exchange against real goods, so Bitcoin must scale into a global transaction ledger rather than sit as a store of value.
- Treats mild deflation from growth as natural and good, against the inflation-fearing sound-money framing common in BTC circles — and against the inflationist mainstream he accuses of hiding growth.
- Stability, not appreciation, is the design goal: "This is why Bitcoin should be stable" — anathema to number-go-up culture.

# Taking care of Business. — core principles

- **Bitcoin is a commodity ledger.** The blockchain is a method to exchange value without the issue of double spending; its long-term road is pathed using commercial transactions, not speculation.
- **The HODL fallacy.** The error in how many see Bitcoin is the concept of getting rich off the work of others — buying and waiting while others create value; holding is not the use case.
- **Coin price is irrelevant to ledger use.** It is not the value of the coin that matters; volatility does nothing to the use of Bitcoin as a commodity ledger.
- **Merchant costs are fiat-denominated and stable.** Merchants pay in USD, GBP or Yuan; the number of bitcoins required fluctuates with price, but the cost is paid as it is used, so merchant costs stay stable — a simple marker/record transaction securely encoded should cost under $0.001, a more complex contract about $0.05.
- **Unbounded blocks are the scaling path.** From 128-MB blocks demonstrated in the near term to an unbounded block size, taking as many transactions as people are willing to send.
- **Business plumbing is the first market.** Simple and boring uses such as technological data plumbing: tax invoices, contract-exchange platforms, and in-store purchase records auto-loaded into accounting systems — records stored on the public blockchain in a completely private manner and without loss, minimising fraud, loss and alteration at below the cost of paper-based systems.
- **The market is the world, not crypto.** The goal is billions of people using Bitcoin in the coming years, without even knowing that they are using Bitcoin.

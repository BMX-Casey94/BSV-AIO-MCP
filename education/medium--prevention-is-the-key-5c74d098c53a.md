# Prevention is the key — core principles

- **Fraud is preventable at the network and payment layer.** The vast majority of illicit activity and fraud across the Internet could be averted or curtailed if destination ISPs and payment intermediaries monitored and controlled access to their networks; monitoring can detect an attack in progress so it can be aborted before serious damage is done (Denning, *Information Warfare and Security*, 1999).
- **Card fraud is structural and consumer-borne.** Litigation against Internet intermediaries, corporate-responsibility norms and PCI-DSS enforcement (Visa and MasterCard fines of $25,000 per day for non-compliant organisations) all end up increasing costs to the consumer.
- **Bitcoin removes the card attack surface.** Unlike card credentials, bitcoins cannot be copied and replayed: "Cards are simple to copy and replay. Bitcoin is not."
- **The killer app is sound, cash-like money.** Sound money that does not leave the merchant or user out of pocket — spending bitcoin is just like spending cash.
- **A 2-of-3 escrow contract replaces institutional trust.** A payment can be locked to a merchant and released only when the goods are delivered; if delivery is lost, the escrow comes into play based on what was negotiated — giving the merchant payment certainty and the buyer delivery assurance in both directions.
- **Prevention beats policing.** Engineering that removes the attack surface (replayable credentials) is cheaper than monitoring, litigation and compliance regimes whose costs are passed on to consumers.

# IoT and the coming Toaster-world
**Date:** 2018-09-19 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/iot-and-the-coming-toaster-world-654edcdb977
**Subtitle:** The commodification of technology is what will drive the move to the cloud. It is what makes the entire structure of how some see Bitcoin…

## Core thesis
Technology is becoming an appliance-grade commodity — users want toaster-like simplicity, not control — and this trajectory (cloud, 5G, virtualised containers) dooms the vision of Bitcoin as a mesh of hobbyist home nodes. Bitcoin's real nature matches that world: it is commercial P2P global cash run by enterprise infrastructure, whose value lies in sound, scarce, stable money and a simple immutable ledger, not in the geek-run node hobby.

## Key arguments and claims
- "The commodification of technology is what will drive the move to the cloud. It is what makes the entire structure of how some see Bitcoin, as a mesh of home nodes incredibly flawed."
- Geeks are losing: "we Geeks are a minority and we are losing the war... technology is becoming commodity based. This is a good thing, but it means changes."
- BCH's image problem: it "is seen by many in the industry as a system that Geeks run. This is a problem, for it is money, P2P global Cash... Bitcoin is designed to be a commercial system, one that is designed to deliver cash to the globe."
- What matters is not the tech or even mining: "It is not the commodity aspect of Bitcoin that was ever important. It was not the mining — nor the commercialised competition that maintains the security of Bitcoin. What matters is that you have the ability to have a sound, stable money. A money that is scarce and capped and a money that allows direct peer exchange."
- His definition of decentralisation is functional, not topological: "That is how it is decentralised. Not as a made up idea of DAPPs that offer no value for an excessive investment, but as a simple means to store an immutable ledger" and to let remote parties "trade safely and with confidence".
- UX must become appliance-grade: "We want an Internet of Toaster like simplicity and reliability... we need to improve the interfaces that people use with Bitcoin. The MoneyButton and HandCash are a step towards this path."
- Generational skill shift: "Net-gen cares even less than my generation of techies did... Net-gen knows how to mash up an app but fails to know what the base protocol really is in most cases" (Assembly coding as the benchmark of lost depth).
- Bandwidth trajectory kills local hardware: with 5G and eventually "100Gb/sec or even 10Gb/s wireless" as a utility, "remote desktops will work faster from server farms and the rates we are used with local systems will make PCs seem slow"; "When all is available, backed up and secured, who will want or care for a USB device?"
- The endpoint: "a world not of home machines, but of cloud servers and virtualised containers. This is a world, not of hobby Bitcoin nodes, but of commercial enterprise."
- "In years to come, the Bitcoin ledger itself will be a means to interact with and store data" — the ledger as data layer, anticipating Metanet-style claims.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Technology-adoption sociology rather than protocol argument: he extrapolates from consumer behaviour (appliance expectations, cloud migration, generational deskilling) and bandwidth trends to a structural conclusion about where Bitcoin infrastructure must live. The move is to redefine "decentralisation" away from node-count populism towards the functional properties users actually want (scarce stable money, immutable ledger, direct exchange), so that commercial centralisation of infrastructure is not a compromise but the design.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **Home-node maximalism:** the "mesh of home nodes" ideal — shared by BTC small-blockers and parts of the BCH community — is "incredibly flawed"; hobby nodes have no place in the coming world.
- **"Not your keys, not your coins" self-sovereignty culture:** the essay normalises cloud custody of data and computation ("who will want or care for a USB device?"), cutting against the local-hardware, self-hosted ethos.
- **DAPP/utility-token narrative:** DAPPs are "a made up idea... that offer no value for an excessive investment"; the ledger's purpose is cash and simple data storage.
- **Decentralisation as topology:** he relocates the term's meaning to monetary properties (scarce, capped, direct peer exchange), so an enterprise-run network still counts as decentralised — the opposite of the node-count metric used by BTC advocates to justify small blocks.

## Connections
The title references the talkative toaster from Red Dwarf. MoneyButton and HandCash were the flagship BCH consumer wallets of 2018, situating this in the pre-split BCH app ecosystem. The argument is the consumer-facing twin of his miner/datacentre scaling position (Q&A Parts 3–4, the SSRN scaling paper) and prefigures his later Metanet concept of the Bitcoin ledger as a general data-storage and interaction layer.

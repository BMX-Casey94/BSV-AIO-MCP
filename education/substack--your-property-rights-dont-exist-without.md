# Your Property Rights Don’t Exist Without a State — And That’s Not a Moral Claim — core principles

- **A property system must survive adversaries, not gentlemen.** The test is worst-case behaviour: a working system must bind non-consenting defectors, produce final outcomes, prevent jurisdictional veto, terminate meta-disputes, provide unitary title records, and cost less than the rights protected. A right that cannot compel the strong without the victim purchasing equivalent coercive capacity is a priced service, not a right.
- **Forum shopping collapses into veto by the strong.** In the Forum Veto Game, a strong party rejects every proposed forum by backward induction because rejection preserves a status quo favouring the possessor; the unique subgame-perfect equilibrium is universal veto by the strong and rational surrender by the weak. Bond repairs fail because enforcing forfeiture itself requires compulsory terminal jurisdiction.
- **Enforcement markets have a justice floor.** A profit-maximising protection agency is always purchasable by a wealthy adversary offering more than the victim's lifetime premium stream; where enforcement cost exceeds asset value, rights cease to exist in practice.
- **Legal standards drift towards money.** Hotelling-type spatial competition positions institutions towards strong clients (higher evidentiary burdens, shorter limitation periods, costlier procedures); weak-serving institutions go bankrupt, so law tracks money under formal neutrality.
- **The Conditional Impossibility Theorem.** Given open entry, non-consenting defectors, costly attribution, wealth asymmetry and payer concentration, no polycentric order without compulsory jurisdiction, registry or fiduciary constraint can deliver enforceability and finality for weak agents — every necessary repair reconstitutes a monopoly terminal authority. State capture is real but constitutionally repairable; polycentric failure modes are structural.
- **Resources come in three categories, not two.** Physically rivalrous goods (I); institutionally created but genuinely rivalrous positions — bank balances, shares, domain names, spectrum licences, tokens (II); and non-rivalrous information (III). Category II moves trillions daily and refutes the physicalist restriction of property to tangible things on its own.
- **Non-physical assets satisfy every scarcity criterion.** Radio spectrum (over $230 billion in auction revenues since 1994, per Coase's 1959 paper), domain names (over 60,000 WIPO UDRP disputes; Voice.com sold for $30M in 2019), and Bitcoin — whose UTXO single-spend rivalrousness is recognised as property by IRS Notice 2014-21, HMRC, and the English High Court in *AA v Persons Unknown* [2019] EWHC 3556 (Comm).
- **Token property is categorically distinct from IP.** It restricts only the controller's exclusive ledger position and prevents nobody from copying files or creating competing tokens, so the anti-IP argument has zero application to it.
- **Bitcoin supplies the state-function in code for its domain.** Within the consensus-dominant branch, the ledger provides finality, compulsory jurisdiction (no veto over the state-transition function) and unitary publicity (one authoritative record) — blockchain does not replace the physical-world state; it is a state for the specific domain of digital token ownership.
- **History confirms the theorem.** The lex mercatoria operated in the shadow of sovereign enforcement (Berman 1983; Milgrom, North and Weingast 1990); Gold Rush mining districts collapsed into corporate dispossession and were replaced by the General Mining Act 1872; ICANN moved from voluntary governance to the compulsory UDRP monopoly within a decade.

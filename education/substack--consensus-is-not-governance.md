# Consensus Is Not Governance — core principles

- **Consensus recognises valid states; governance decides whether the validity rules may change.** Let \(P_t\) be the rule set and \(C_t\) the consensus process under it. Consensus answers only "given \(P_t\), which proposed update is next?" Who decides \(P_{t+1}\) is governance \(G_t\).
- **Three separable layers: admission, governance, mutability.** \(A \neq G \neq \mu\). "Permissionless" describes only who may participate. A system can be open at the door and closed at the rule book, and the rule book determines economic behaviour.
- **Scalar decentralisation metrics measure consensus, not governance.** Node counts, the Nakamoto coefficient and mining Herfindahl say almost nothing about who can revise the rules.
- **Investment is made under \((P_t, C_t, G_t)\), not under consensus alone.** A firm integrating a payment rail makes a Williamsonian asset-specific investment; if a maintainer coalition later deprecates the transaction format, the sunk infrastructure is stranded — functionally equivalent to having been refused admission.
- **This is a commitment problem, not a coordination problem.** Participants anticipate discretionary revision and discount investment ex ante (Kydland–Prescott). "You can always fork" fails because fork cost equals the asset specificity of the original investment; exit disciplines only when near-costless (Hirschman).
- **Governance takes observable forms.** Repository permissions and release schedules, funding concentration, adoption authority at validators and exchanges, classification authority ("soft fork", "bug fix", "emergency"), activation design, token voting, and narrative authority.
- **Effective decentralisation is the minimum across layers, not the maximum.** The most concentrated control layer binds.
- **Security must include governance terms.** The standard \(\alpha V > C\) condition is incomplete; the institutional form is \(\alpha V > C + I + K + R\), where \(I\) is capital at risk, \(K\) coordination cost, and \(R\) legal and reputational accountability.
- **TCP/IP's stability is an institutional achievement.** The RFC process, working groups, rough consensus and deployed code separate base-layer revision from operational routing.
- **Coalitions are revealed by behaviour, not charters.** Insurance markets already separate consensus failures from governance-driven losses.

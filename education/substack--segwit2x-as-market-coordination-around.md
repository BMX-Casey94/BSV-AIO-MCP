# SegWit2x as Market Coordination Around Incentives — core principles

- **Miners follow expected revenue, not ideology.** \(E[R_i] = P(\text{chain accepted}) \times (\text{subsidy} + \text{fees}) \times \text{market price} - \text{costs}\). Revenue denominated in an economically rejected asset is impaired, so expected commercial acceptance disciplines miners directly.
- **Local rejection is private non-participation unless it changes payoffs.** A non-mining node's refusal is not network enforcement unless tied to settlement, liquidity, exchange recognition or competing proof-of-work.
- **Exchanges are commercial chokepoints, not consensus enforcers.** Listing, crediting and ticker decisions are commercial policy — capable of pressuring miners through price and liquidity. A bank deciding not to clear a cheque is not a pen enforcing contract law.
- **Fork outcomes are Schelling coordination problems.** Ticker, liquidity, exchange continuity and brand identity are the salient equilibria. "Lack of consensus" is the polite phrase for a coalition that can no longer guarantee the payoff structure.
- **Power flows through identifiable chokepoints.** Exchanges, custodians, software maintainers, mining pools and media narratives — not millions of private nodes independently enforcing technical law.
- **The hypothesis is testable.** Miner signalling as dependent variable; futures or IOU spreads, ticker announcements, hash-allocation changes, abnormal returns, volatility and order-book depth as observables. Support should decline as expected liquidity, ticker recognition and exchange acceptance weaken.
- **The causal agent is the organisation that controls liquidity, custody, settlement, customer access or hashpower** — not the software instance that happens to be present when the snow arrives.

# Trolls and bullies — core principles

- **Abuse flourishes when communication is free.** The internet's inability to charge access based on use is a tragedy of the commons: zero marginal cost to the sender produces abuse.
- **Sender-pays micropayments restore social bounds.** Every interaction carries a price the recipient sets, so trolling becomes less profitable in every way.
- **Users set their own price schedule.** Near-free for friends, a small balancing fee for loose acquaintances, and a high price for unsolicited commercial or hostile contact.
- **Free access paid for by selling personal data incentivises the worst.** Advertising-funded "free" platforms encourage the worst aspects of society rather than charging for use.
- **Anonymity is the bully's tool, not a civil liberty.** It gives power to people who invade others' privacy while demanding privacy for themselves.
- **Trolls extract attention; bullies target individuals.** The former rile communities to harvest attention; the latter use shame, intimidation and falsified information — and people switch between the two.
- **A micropayment-capable chain can replace the free internet.** As a paid data network (the Metanet) replaces zero-cost posting, trolls are priced out of existence.

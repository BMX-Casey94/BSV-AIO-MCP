# A diatribe on Bitcoin, Trust and the economy of security (redux) — core principles

- **Money emerges from the most marketable goods.** Mengerian convergence: barter at village scale quickly becomes collated obligations, and divisible goods (grain, butter) act as improvised currency — the most marketable goods become the media of exchange without any decree.
- **Good money is divisible, durable and verifiable.** Salt in Rome (the root of "salary") served as early money because it was divisible, durable and quality-verifiable by taste — but became too bulky and perishable as trade volumes grew: "a rainstorm could literally liquidate your holdings".
- **Non-printability is the trust foundation.** Gold succeeded because it is divisible, non-degrading, and above all "you cannot print gold" — inflation is the hidden tax that cannot be applied to it, and trust in a currency rests on immunity to debasement.
- **Money was created by merchants, not the state.** "Money was created by merchants and commercial entities as a means of engaging in trade"; government monopolised it in a hostile takeover, and trust in currency predates and does not require the state.
- **A post-gold-standard fiat note is a promise of itself.** Hand a dollar to the Treasury and you get a dollar back — "basically, there is nothing to trust"; the trust basis has been replaced by political discretion exercised outside legislatures.
- **The key to currency is trust.** "That's it, nothing special no more" — trust grounded in non-debasement and merchant adoption, not in minimising trust through maximal validation.
- **Automation makes wealth intangible and payments borderless.** Automation grows exponentially while human skill grows at best linearly; as manufacturing and agriculture become technologies, "all goods will be digital", wealth becomes intellectual property, and IP commerce demands instant, cheap, borderless payment — a function no state currency will be trusted to serve.

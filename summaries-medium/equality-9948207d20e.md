# Equality
**Date:** 2018-09-15 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/equality-9948207d20e
**Subtitle:** Some argue that capitalism fails as it does not lead to equality. That is a fallacy we need to avoid. The only equality we can achieve is…

## Core thesis
Equality of outcome is a fallacy — "The only equality we can achieve is one of equal destitution" — because humans differ irreducibly in ability and inclination, and free markets already possess a natural equaliser in wage signals. Craig maps this onto Bitcoin: the demand that everyone be able to run a "full verification node" is the same socialist fallacy, and Bitcoin, as capitalism embodied, must scale without subsidising hobbyist nodes.

## Key arguments and claims
- Opening claim: capitalism is not a failure for producing inequality; "We are all equally poor and suffering or we are unequal."
- The Bitcoin division is rooted in this fallacy: "A few believe that Bitcoin needs to be maintained equally and that we should hold back development to ensure this."
- Anti-blank-slate: "We are not born equal. We do not remain equal… People are not a blank slate" (with a garbled jab at tabula rasa, rendered "Tabler Rosser").
- Diversity as strength: differing intelligence, aptitude and proclivities mean "Where one person or one group earns more than another it is because in an open market they have services that are desired more".
- The NBA analogy: "we cannot take a 5 foot one man and make him an NBA star" — nor reverse; capability is not fungible.
- The natural equaliser: high wages signal scarcity — "Wages are high in an open market as there is a scarcity. When there are high wages others are attracted to this field" — so no government intervention is needed, "just a free-market without intervention".
- Failure is necessary: "We need to allow people to fail for this is how they learn and develop."
- The node claim: "You do not have a right to run a 'full verification node' as this is not a part of Bitcoin as a system. You have the right to pay the costs of running this system" — and it is time "to stop thinking that we need to subsidise those wanting to run a node".
- "Bitcoin is capitalism. Get used to it." No one has the right "to limit the growth of Bitcoin… just as you want to have a falsely romanticised idea of running a node at home".
- Keynes and the moon myth: "It was Keynes who started the Moon myth. The myth of those seeking the thesis of infinite money. And, it is the moon myth in Bitcoin that is Keynes" — Bitcoin is money, "not for green cheese and Lambo's, but as global cash".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
A descriptive-to-normative argument: from empirical human diversity he derives the legitimacy of unequal market outcomes, with the wage-signal mechanism doing the equilibrating work that egalitarians assign to the state. The rhetorical mode is aphoristic polemic — short declaratives, reductio analogies (NBA height), and slogan coinages ("Bitcoin is capitalism") — then the whole framework is transposed, one-to-one, onto the block-size/node-equality controversy.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Direct assault on the "everyone should run a full node" orthodoxy: home-node verification is recast as a subsidised entitlement, not a right, and not part of Bitcoin's design (implicitly invoking SPV per the whitepaper).
- Recasts small-block philosophy as socialism: holding back scale so all can participate equally is the "equality of misery" applied to protocol policy.
- Attacks HODL/moon culture as Keynesian — "the moon myth in Bitcoin that is Keynes" — against the dominant BTC store-of-value, number-go-up narrative.
- Denies any right "to stop others from using Bitcoin" via capacity limits — inverting the BTC framing of small blocks as protecting decentralisation for users.

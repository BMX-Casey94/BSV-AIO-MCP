# The Property Flaw of Lightning — core principles

- **The unit of property is the satoshi.** There are 100 million individual tokens for every nominal coin. The design lets a digital file act as if it were a corporal thing, enabling true possession and bailment (escrow via smart contract).
- **Tokens are registered, not stored, on the chain.** The blockchain is a distributed clearing house and registry. Until registration, double-spend risk remains.
- **Property rights cannot be stripped by a layer.** There is no way on a blockchain to remove the property rights associated with bitcoin. Account-based balances are treated differently under law from tokens, and the conversion does not work.
- **Nemo dat quod non habet.** No one gives what they do not have; one cannot transfer more rights than one has. Buying without CDD/KYC means good title does not pass. Stolen bitcoin sent through a thousand addresses remain the original owner’s.
- **Malleability is not a Bitcoin flaw.** It is a security problem only for payment-channel constructions that were never part of Bitcoin; the base peg cannot be removed without destroying the channel.
- **Handling stolen goods is a crime.** The Theft Act 1968 includes bitcoin. A miner taking a fee derived from stolen coins is handling stolen goods; a data centre can be sequestered as proceeds of crime.
- **Freezing orders stop channel movement.** Orders already seen in the UK and Ireland can halt any movement of coins in a channel.
- **Identity is firewalled, not absent.** Section 10 of the white paper notes identities firewalled from the public network. Users must do their own customer due diligence by law.

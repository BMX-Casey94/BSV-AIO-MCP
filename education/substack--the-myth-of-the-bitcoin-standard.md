# The Myth of the Bitcoin Standard — core principles

- **Overspending is a problem of governance, not of the monetary medium.** States do not naïvely print; they borrow against the future. They will do so whether the reserve is gold, fiat or Bitcoin.
- **Scarcity cannot substitute for discipline.** Gold and "hard money" fetishism hope that a fixed unit will enforce rectitude without political reform; the problem has never been the medium — it has always been the man.
- **Modern money creation runs through debt.** Governments issue bonds bought by institutions and central banks; reserves multiply into credit — temporal arbitrage that spends today what tomorrow must earn.
- **Inflation's moral test is use, not mechanism.** Borrowing that funds roads, energy networks and productive infrastructure seeds the returns that repay it; borrowing for consumption, patronage and subsidies cannibalises the economy that must redeem it.
- **A Bitcoin Standard would replicate the gold standard's failures.** Governments would issue Bitcoin-denominated bonds, intermediaries would layer synthetic derivatives and off-chain IOUs, and redemption would again be "temporarily suspended" when liabilities outran reserves.
- **Scarcity without elasticity is paralysis.** A deflationary unit drives capital into hoarding, withers investment, and would turn every recession into a depression by forbidding the flexibility that absorbs shocks.
- **The decisive distinction is productive versus political capital.** Bridges, grids, ports and research repay debt through growth; subsidies and vanity projects are economic cannibalism. The world collapses not when debt grows, but when debt ceases to build.
- **Code does not immunise money against politics.** A state need not hack the blockchain when it can regulate gateways, tax, surveil and expropriate. When the ledger conflicted with the sword, the sword always won.
- **Default is a continuum.** Every inflation is a partial default, every unredeemed bond a quiet betrayal, spread across generations as the slow theft of purchasing power.

# The Number That Moved by Standing Still
**Date:** 2026-07-05 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-number-that-moved-by-standing
**Subtitle:** How the fixed $500 Statute of Frauds threshold lost 91.6% of its real value while its text never changed — and what a nominal boundary in an inflating unit of account really captures

## Core thesis
UCC § 2-201 has held its Statute of Frauds threshold for the sale of goods at $500 since the 1952 Official Text. Because the figure is denominated in an inflating unit of account, its real value has fallen 91.6% — to $42.24 in 1952 purchasing power, against the $5,918.66 that constant real value would require in 2024. The erosion is invisible in the text, uncorrected for Coasean reasons, and drags a considered evidentiary rule down into the ordinary small commerce the drafters meant to leave alone — converting it into a lottery of enforceability.

## Key arguments and claims
- Verified primary facts: the threshold is "$500 or more" in UCC § 2-201(1), dating from the 1952 Official Text (checked at Cornell LII); CPI-U annual averages were 26.5 in 1952 and 313.689 in 2024 (BLS series CUUR0000SA0), a ratio of 11.8373.
- The erosion is an identity, not an estimate: R(t) = H · P(t₀)/P(t), pinned entirely by observed price ratios — "there is nothing to misspecify". Real values by decade: $448 (1960), $341 (1970), $161 (1980), $101 (1990), $77 (2000), $61 (2010), $51 (2020), $42.24 (2024).
- Compounding dynamics: CPI-U compounded at 3.49% per year over 72 years; by the rule of 72 the real threshold halves roughly every 20.6 years. The decay is level-invariant — every fixed nominal boundary set in 1952 has lost the identical 91.6%, because the fraction lost is set by the price ratio, not the level.
- Via Fox (1996): the Statute and money's currency are the same evidentiary instrument calibrated to different axis positions — both refuse enforcement resting on unreliable proof. Money's rule was keyed to a physical fact (coin's indistinguishability) that did not drift; the Statute pegged its line to a nominal quantity, so "its real content decays where money's did not".
- Via Schwartz and Scott (2011): the writing requirement addresses a fabrication hazard cognate to the double moral hazard; its placement was a judgement about where stakes make fabrication worth attempting — made in 1952 real terms and silently relocated by inflation.
- Via Levmore (1987): an obscure dollar threshold whose drift is invisible attracts inertia; "the number stays because staying is the path of least resistance".
- Via Coase (1937): correction has concentrated cost and diffuse benefit, so non-correction is predicted. The 2003 Article 2 revision proposed $5,000 — close to the $5,919 full indexation requires — and was not adopted, "the Coasean prediction realized".
- Via Kahn and Roberds (2009): the documentation-cost premise is dissolving from below as account-based payment generates records as a free byproduct. The threshold is caught in a scissors — inflation drags it down from above while recorded payment fills the small-transaction range from below.
- What the erosion transfers: the Statute operates as a defence, so erosion hands "a randomly assigned option to renege" to whichever party a price movement later disfavours; the social cost is destroyed reliance. Direction and incidence are established distribution-free; magnitude is expressly not claimed because the distribution F of real transaction values has not been measured.
- Indexation fixes the drift but not the deeper question: whether a value-keyed writing requirement is still the right instrument at all when most transactions now document themselves.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Measurement first, interpretation second, with a hard line between them: verified primary-source numbers and a definitional identity carry the quantified core (declared free of econometric vulnerability), the distribution-free direction is separated from the unmeasured magnitude, and the interpretive layer (option-to-renege, Coasean non-correction) is labelled reading rather than proof.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post instead extends the series' value-and-identifiability axis to a pure monetary-economics pathology: what happens to any legal boundary expressed in a unit of account that inflates, with the cash-to-ledger migration of payment technology as the second blade of the scissors.

## Notable quotes
- "The legal system has no thermostat for real value. It reads nominal text, and nominal text lies still while the ground moves."
- "It is a randomly assigned option to renege, handed out by inflation to whichever side later wants it."
- "A rule written for a cash world is being operated in a ledger world, and neither its nominal line nor its documentary premise survives the translation intact."
- "The number did not have to move to do this. It did it by standing still."

# Stablecoins and the Lost Spark
**Date:** 2025-06-19 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/stablecoins-and-the-lost-spark
**Subtitle:** Reclaiming Satoshi’s Design from BTC Core’s Dogma

## Core thesis
Stablecoins are not a deviation from Satoshi Nakamoto’s vision but its “most coherent actualisation”. His 2009 remark to Martti Malmi — “Once it’s backed with cash, that might change…” — anticipated fiat-pegged tokens as the spark that would ignite adoption; BTC Core’s anti-fiat dogma and scaling blockage smothered that vision, which stablecoins now revive and which BSV’s unbounded, script-capable ledger is positioned to host at global scale.

## Key arguments and claims
- The Malmi quote is exegeted clause by clause: “Your understanding of bitcoin is spot on” legitimises pragmatic systems design; “rather Neanderthal” skewers gold-purist rejectionism; “They concede that something is flammable, but argue that it’ll never burn because there’ll never be a spark” frames fiat backing as the missing catalyst; the closing line is “strategic self-censorship” before a libertarian-dominated forum culture.
- Malmi (“Sirius”), a Finnish computer-science student, was “second only to Hal Finney in substantive engagement” and helped maintain bitcoin.org.
- Satoshi’s core use-cases — micropayments, automated payments, financial contracts — presuppose a medium that holds value over short durations; stablecoins “stabilise the medium without compromising the ledger.”
- The UTXO model makes outputs “digital bearer instruments”, redeemable on presentation of the correct cryptographic signature; tokenisation works through OP_RETURN, PUSH DATA and script-based controls (locking conditions, threshold signatures, time-based constraints).
- BTC Core’s capture: deliberate blockage of on-chain scaling via block-size limits produced rising fees and stagnant throughput; the “digital gold” narrative recast Bitcoin as an asset “to be hoarded, not spent” — “economic atrophy disguised as sound policy.”
- Anti-fiat zealotry is technically incoherent: Bitcoin “is priced in fiat, exchanged for fiat, and whose success is gauged in fiat-denominated markets.”
- SegWit and Taproot are dismissed as “largely internal optimisations” — “solutions in search of problems”; Lightning “has failed to translate into widespread use-case adoption.”
- Tether (USDT) and USD Coin (USDC) are primitive, permissioned instantiations; more advanced architectures include on-chain collateralisation, algorithmic balancing and tokenised central-bank reserves.
- BSV “retains the original protocol and rejects arbitrary limitations”: it could process billions of transactions daily, encode regulatory logic directly into transaction scripts, and host stablecoins without throughput or cost fragmentation — “the protocol that would have been.”
- Bitcoin was designed to be auditable, not untraceable: it lends itself to tax reporting, AML enforcement and institutional compliance; what it preserves is finality — “what you pay, stays paid.”

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Primary-source textual exegesis — the 2009 Malmi correspondence is treated as a “suppressed axiom” — combined with technical-economic analysis of the UTXO and script architecture, and an institutional-capture account of BTC Core’s governance.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The “digital gold”/store-of-value narrative is “not an evolution but a reversion—a descent into ideological fetishism.”
- Anti-fiat maximalism is the very “Neanderthal” position Satoshi criticised in 2009.
- Block-size limits and high fees are sabotage of the design, not prudent engineering.
- Compliance, auditability and state interfacing are framed as design features — inverting the censorship-resistance-above-all orthodoxy.

## Notable quotes
- “Once it’s backed with cash, that might change, but I’d probably better refrain from mentioning that in public anymore until we’re closer to ready to start.”
- “Where BTC Core erects a shrine, stablecoins build a marketplace.”
- “The tragedy is not that Bitcoin changed—it is that it was arrested mid-birth, and frozen into a monument to what might have been.”
- “It is not rebellion but return.”

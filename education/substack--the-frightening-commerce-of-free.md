# The Frightening Commerce of Free Persons — core principles

- **The test is whether the intermediary reduces friction or lives from it.** Banks, courts, brokers and registrars can lower risk and transaction costs. The problem begins when the intermediary ceases to be a servant of exchange and becomes its condition. The intermediary's nightmare is comparison.
- **Direct exchange makes custody optional.** Banks hold positional power over captured flows — wages, invoices, remittances, settlement — monetising float, fees, spreads, custody and informational asymmetry. Person-to-person settlement forces them to justify themselves transaction by transaction.
- **Private digital cash does not make taxation impossible.** Tax was collected under cash dominance using ledgers, audits, customs records and field agents. Direct exchange removes the presumption that every payment must be pre-inspected by authorised intermediaries; it does not abolish law, tax or records.
- **Delay is a business model.** Whoever controls the interval between instruction and completion controls the relationship. Delayed settlement is a hidden transfer from the weaker party to the stronger.
- **Records are not surveillance.** Records are created by parties for evidence, contract, tax and audit. Surveillance is imposed by an external power for general monitoring. The person-to-person economy is not anti-law; it is anti-presumption.
- **Functional micropayments break advertising captivity.** Pay for the article, the API call, the song, without making every user a product. Platforms that monetise visibility, payment, reputation and data lose their enclosure.
- **Protection should be a service, not sovereignty.** Escrow, identity, insurance and courts used optionally. Safety rhetoric that expands the category of danger from fraud to risk to non-compliance to political association is control.
- **A direct system surrounded by custodians, exchanges, side layers and tokenised claims leaves the user holding an account, not money.** Settlement becomes exposure; cash becomes a promise. Adults should be able to trade with adults.

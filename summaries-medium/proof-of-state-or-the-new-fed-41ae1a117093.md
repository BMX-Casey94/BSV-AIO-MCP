# Proof of State, or, the new Fed
**Date:** 2018-11-14 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/proof-of-state-or-the-new-fed-41ae1a117093
**Subtitle:** The thing that few see, is that we already have Proof of Stake (PoS).

## Core thesis
Proof-of-stake is not an innovation but a recreation of the US Federal Reserve: a small group of "plutocrats with little direct skin in the game" who entrench their own power. Craig argues PoS chains (EOS, Ethereum's roadmap, XRP) are strictly worse than an Oracle database — slower and less secure — while proof-of-work's competitive mining is the only mechanism that delivers sound, trust-free global money.

## Key arguments and claims
- "We already have proof-of-stake": the Fed's Board of Governors is a PoS system in which a small group sets policy "for the 'good' of all" while ensuring "those in power remain in power." PoS "is not about a stable economy, it is about a stable set of power players."
- PoS is not even an efficient Fed: an Oracle database already does "atomic" encrypted transactions "across systems more securely, with 1,000x the speed of a system such as Ripple (XRP)" — so the gains from permissioned blockchains or PoS "are negative."
- Proof-of-work "removes the necessity of the fiat-issuing controls of any central bank," creating "a trust-free global money, P2P electronic cash"; central banks could persist but would be constrained like national banks under the Euro, since reserves flow out if a bank "tries to 'cheat'."
- "Proof-of-stake is not new" — adding a blockchain to PoS/proof-of-authority is "a sham used to hide a poorly designed permissioned and badly distributed database."
- "The only thing that secures proof-of-work is competition": even a miner with >51% short-term control "find[s] more profit in maintaining the system," because the system's value depends on not changing the rule-set.
- Blockchains "can be reversed, they can be unwound" — immutability in Bitcoin comes only from miners' competitive incentive to preserve the rules, not from the data structure itself.
- PoS violates fair representation: "There is no monetary system that allows for one-vote-one-person without sharing full identity"; PoS lets those with little "skin in the game" punish others, blowing "the economy into cycles and bubbles."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is analogy plus comparative-systems benchmarking: map PoS governance onto the Fed's structure to expose it as old plutocracy in new dress, then benchmark PoS chains against a centralised Oracle database on speed and security to show the "blockchain" wrapper adds nothing. The underlying model is incentive economics — security and immutability are emergent properties of capitalist competition ("an engine of capitalism"), not of cryptography or code. He cites his own SSRN paper (abstract 2993312) and Oracle documentation as evidence.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Attacks the 2018-era smart-contract-platform orthodoxy (Ethereum's Casper roadmap, EOS DPoS, XRP): these were marketed as the decentralised future; Craig calls them "a mere sham… a cover that slows the system under a pretence of adding trust-less immutability."
- Contradicts the "code is law / trustless immutability" slogan common across crypto: "blockchains can be reversed, they can be unwound" — only miner competition prevents it.
- Challenges the "permissioned blockchain" enterprise narrative (Hyperledger-style): a permissioned chain is strictly inferior to a database, so the entire category is a "boondoggle… to milk the 'noobs'."
- Implicitly rebukes the "one CPU one vote is dead, governance by stakeholders is progress" view: PoS is plutocracy, and PoW's competition is the only legitimate consensus.

## Notable quotes
- "In the US Fed, a small group of Governors, in other words, plutocrats with little direct skin in the game set policy, and control the system for the 'good' of all."
- "Proof-of-stake is a system designed to maintain the status quo, to ensure that those in power remain in power."
- "'Blockchain' is a far slower version of an oracle database when used in this manner."
- "Bitcoin uses the blockchain not as a mere database but as a distributed competitive system, an engine of capitalism."
- "The truth is that blockchains can be reversed, they can be unwound."
- "Bitcoin is a system designed, at scale, to bring sound money to the world."

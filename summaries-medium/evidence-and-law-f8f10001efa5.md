# Evidence and law
**Date:** 2019-04-12 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/evidence-and-law-f8f10001efa5
**Subtitle:** It's funny, the cypherpunks want to demonstrate that code is law. In part, they think they can back me into a corner. They think…

## Core thesis
Responding to demands that he prove his Satoshi authorship by signing with Satoshi's keys, Craig argues that cryptographic keys are merely evidence, not law: key possession proves only possession, non-repudiation does not exist in law, and any judge basing precedent on a key signing would be incompetent. He then offers his alternative evidentiary trail — the bitcoin.org domain was purchased through AnonymousSpeech.com using his personal credit card, claimed on his 2008/2009 Australian tax return and tested in a tax-audit court case — and declares he will prove authorship "using courts and law", not cryptographic theatre.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Law is law. Code (programs and algorithms) in any form is merely evidence. Bitcoin was designed to work within a common-law framework." He lists his legal credentials: master's in international commercial law (Northumbria, 2008), Masters of Law (2012), barrister and solicitor training (never practised), Society of Legal Scholars membership, and a current doctorate in law.
- "Ownership of the key is not proof of anything other than the possession of a key." A precedent derived from key signing "is dangerous to say the least, and any competent judge would not base law on such basis".
- "There is no such thing as non-repudiation… an individual always has the opportunity to repudiate" — a point he says he has made "many many times before", grounded in evidence law.
- Key reuse is against Bitcoin's design: "You don't have a key that is associated with an identity in the format that something like PGP uses… Bitcoin was developed in a manner that uses keys once and only once." Single-key identity systems are "foolish".
- "Bitcoin was birthed using a credit card payment": the AnonymousSpeech.com account used to register bitcoin.org (via Vistomail) was funded by his credit card through the banking system "with full KYC and all the logging requirements", records banks must keep for 25 years.
- He claimed the expense on his 2008/2009 Australian tax return and "was audited, which ended up going to court" — so the records are preserved and validated by the tax office: "The source of the funds that went to pay for the bitcoin.org domain registration on AnonymousSpeech.com derived from my credit card."
- He used AnonymousSpeech/SecureAnonymousSurfing for legitimate investigative work — tracking malware breaches and peer-to-peer copyright violations against clients — so that cyber criminals could not identify him.
- On the December 2015 doxing: "I moved to the UK in October 2015"; media waited outside a house he no longer lived in. The plan, he claims, was "to stop me. To stop scaling Bitcoin and to try and take what Bitcoin is and break it and create a drug coin."
- "The Lightning Network is a system designed to allow criminals to purchase drugs and other illicit material and records being lost but still settling on Bitcoin in such a way that they can avoid anti-money laundering rules."
- BTC's transformation: "There is one sole purpose of all the copycat coins like Monero. It is the entire reason that BTC changed from being Bitcoin into some lab experiment designed to avoid law."
- Declaration: "I am the person behind the moniker Satoshi Nakamoto, and as a result of all the trolls and haters, I'm going to provide evidence… I'm going to use courts and law. Welcome to law."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Craig argues from evidence law rather than cryptography: he distinguishes possession from authorship, denies non-repudiation, and elevates conventional legal artefacts — credit-card records, tax filings, audit litigation — above key signatures as proof. The method is adversarial and autobiographical, converting his critics' demand ("sign publicly") into a trap that would, he claims, corrupt legal precedent. Rhetorically it is a gauntlet-throwing piece: credentials established, motive assigned to opponents (a "drug coin" conspiracy), and the courtroom named as the chosen arena — published as the Kleiman litigation was underway.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly contradicts the "code is law" maxim: code is demoted to mere evidence subordinate to common-law process.
- Contradicts the cryptographic-proof orthodoxy of the Satoshi debate ("sign or it didn't happen"): he asserts a public signing would prove nothing legally and that documentary/financial evidence outranks it.
- Contradicts the privacy-as-anonymity ethos: "Bitcoin was never anonymous. It was paid for using a credit card" — the system's very origin is KYC'd financial records.
- Contradicts the mainstream celebration of Lightning as scaling: he recasts it as an AML-evasion tool for illicit markets, "the entire purpose of Lightning".

# Who Reads the Meter? — core principles

- **The chain pays the number the paid party supplies.** Every blockchain-settled energy market rests on meter integrity. If the report is inflated, auctions, bonds and incentives above it settle a fiction.
- **Honesty is an incentive condition, not a virtue.** Honest reporting is optimal when audit probability × detection probability × stake ≥ price × maximum plausible over-report. Deterrence is a product: any single zero collapses it.
- **Size deterrence to the cheater's gain, not social damage.** Phantom units earn the unit price. You deter people by making cheating cost them more than it pays them, which is rarely the same as what it costs everyone else.
- **On-chain levers are priced parameters.** Audit probability, sensor accuracy and stake size substitute along a one-dimensional frontier. The cost-minimising contract equalises recurring audit cost with the carry on locked stake.
- **Many small settlements share one stake.** A single pooled bond can back concurrent micro-settlements: full deterrence per report, carry shared across all — an argument for high-frequency micro-settlement over bulk settlement.
- **Buy accurate meters where volume is high.** Sensor accuracy is a make-or-monitor choice: pay once for telemetry, or skimp and pay forever in audits. Long-lived, high-volume markets should buy the meters; pilots should not.
- **Verification economics, not trust, secures the data layer.** Get the contract right and the meter tells the truth because lying does not pay.

# Crowd Funding and ICOs — core principles

- **ICOs are securities — all of them.** Token offerings belong inside the securities/funding-portal perimeter: rules for registered funding portals, completion, cancellation and withdrawal of offers, non-resident funding-portal requirements, plus restrictions on resales and disqualification provisions.
- **Six crowdfunding models, each with characteristic abuses.** Rewards-based (perks, no ownership — Kickstarter type), equity-based (common-share raising), charity/donation-based, debt (peer-to-peer crowdfunded debt, usable where mortgage companies are unavailable), litigation funding (e.g. LexShares), and product pre-order (pre-sales ahead of the public, possibly at a discount).
- **Enforcement reality.** "There are abuses in each of the models, and enforcement can be problematic" — builders should assume existing fundraising law applies to token sales rather than engineering around it.
- **The FTC Act is a live enforcement lever.** *Federal Trade Commission v Eric Chevallier*: a permanent injunction sought under the prohibition on "unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce" — the available remedy for crowdfunding fraud.

# Bitcoin After the Casino
**Date:** 2026-08-02T05:10:18.953Z | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/bitcoin-after-the-casino
**Subtitle:** Fixed rules, digital scarcity, and the economic machine hidden inside the original design

## Core thesis
Fifteen years of public discussion have fixated on Bitcoin's price, building "a casino whose principal product is anticipation". Wright's counter-claim is that Bitcoin is "an economic machine for creating, transferring and verifying value, state, authority and evidence between parties". Its deeper achievement is digital scarcity at the level of individual transactions — unique spendable states that move rather than multiply — which, on a now-fixed protocol, founds "Bitcoin capitalism": profit from production and exchange rather than from resale to a later buyer.

## Key arguments and claims
- The protocol is a commitment: businesses do not invest for thirty or fifty years on infrastructure whose rules a committee can rewrite. The BSV "Chronicle" upgrade "completed the removal of artificial restrictions and restored the protocol to the unrestricted logic of the original design"; it "ends the programme of protocol alteration. It does not begin another one."
- Protocol ≠ implementation: Teranode/SV Node are software and may improve indefinitely without changing Bitcoin. "A node that changes the rules has ceased to be an honest node in the computer-science meaning of the term." Fixedness moves innovation to implementations, applications and overlays — as a stable grammar enables poetry, contracts and science.
- The white paper title is a specification: peer-to-peer means parties transact directly (not everyone keeping identical copies); electronic cash means transferable value with cash-like immediacy and evidential character.
- UTXOs are "discrete, single-use state objects"; spending consumes prior outputs and creates successors, so recipients hold evidence (transaction, chain of title, signatures, inclusion proof) rather than trusting a platform's database entry — "the beginning of digital property".
- SPV is the overlooked half of the design: miners perform industrial ordering while users verify only what concerns them. His MF-SPV reference implementation uses hierarchical Merkle commitments with logarithmic proof growth; proofs travel with the payment.
- Scarcity is not merely 21 million: transactional scarcity lets tickets, licences, credentials, invoices, warranties, contractual rights and financial instruments be transferred without leaving a valid copy behind. "Ownership moves rather than multiplies."
- His prof-faustus repositories are reference components: overlay-broadcast (key graphs over data-storage transactions), MF-SPV, and the cardtable dealerless-game work (transaction-native state machines; card games as adversarial laboratories for auctions, escrow and procurement).
- Three BSV projects "saw the machine": Money Button (the transaction as an internet primitive; Paymail), Twetch (a social action as an economic event), Tokenized (tokens as legally meaningful rights and obligations).
- Mining economics: the subsidy "was never a permanent business model"; the sustainable model is "high volume multiplied by low fees". Restricting block space "prices out ordinary payments and encourages custodians", manufactures rent for intermediaries, and forces reliance on speculation.
- Effects of adoption: reconciliation collapses as payment, performance, title and evidence are linked; fraction-of-a-cent transactions open markets below today's minimum economic size; data becomes priced, controlled property. Intermediaries survive only by adding measurable value.
- Law and privacy: Bitcoin improves evidence but does not replace law; the goal is "privacy with accountability" — anonymity "attempts to remove identity and responsibility".
- Trajectory: cheaper payments and reconciliation, then portable digital goods and rights, then machine-to-machine markets. Success is measured in transactions, not market capitalisation. "Bitcoin should become boring."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
First-principles definition joined to institutional economics. He reads the white paper title as a design specification, derives the system from the UTXO–SPV–Script–proof-of-work interaction, and treats the fixed protocol as a commitment device — predictability as the precondition of long-horizon investment, with the grammar of English as the governing analogy (stable rules enable rather than prevent innovation above them). Normative authority rests in "the original design": restoration (Chronicle) is completion; alteration is exit from the compliant set. The casino-versus-capitalism contrast is ultimately a counting argument about where profit comes from — volume of useful exchange versus token resale.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Store of value vs cash: "Bitcoin is not principally a speculative asset"; success "will be measured by transactions", not market capitalisation.
- Fee markets and security: the sustainable model is high volume at very low fees; restricted capacity "creates a small number of expensive transactions, prices out ordinary payments and encourages custodians to aggregate users away from the ledger".
- Node definitions: SPV is "not a weakened imitation of a mining node"; everyone-stores-everything "does not decentralise economic power. It suppresses scale, raises costs and pushes ordinary users into custodial services".
- Governance: "Developers should not decide what Bitcoin becomes"; introducing different validity rules creates "a different system" — against BTC's evolving-protocol culture.
- Scaling: unbounded blocks, industrial miners and a light edge, versus capacity caps and custodial layers.
- Anonymity: "Anonymity attempts to remove identity and responsibility"; Bitcoin should "remain within law while improving the evidence upon which law operates".

# The Great Global Skim
**Date:** 2025-11-21 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-great-global-skim
**Subtitle:** A Treatise on the Parasites Who Mistake Themselves for Titans

## Core thesis
Nearly forty per cent of global economic output — over US$40 trillion a year — is consumed by a financial intermediation sector that produces nothing: a "global skim" of rents extracted by middlemen standing between parties who would otherwise transact freely. Crypto, and BTC in particular, has not challenged this parasitic order but replicated and perfected it. The remedy is direct exchange at scale, returning capital to builders rather than bettors.

## Key arguments and claims
- The arithmetic of the skim: the IMF and Financial Stability Board value the formal financial sector at ~25–30 per cent of global GDP; shadow banking holds ~US$63 trillion in assets, "equivalent to seventy-eight per cent of global GDP"; with global GDP ~US$105 trillion, total extraction exceeds US$40 trillion annually — "more than US$40 trillion each year to a caste that produces nothing but movement without progress".
- Shadow banking mechanics: money market funds, structured investment vehicles, off-balance-sheet conduits and repo markets; rehypothecation makes "a single dollar… appear simultaneously in multiple balance sheets"; opacity is the business model — "a fire-brigade that prevents fires is out of business; a shadow-bank that prevents crises forfeits its raison d'être".
- The con is cultural: "confuse the public, then charge them for the clarification"; complexity is "the sanctuary in which responsibility is buried".
- Crypto is "the newest carnival game": BTC offers "speculation without productivity, scarcity without purpose, and wealth without creation", and has rebuilt the priesthood — "exchanges, custodians, market-makers, the self-appointed arbiters of 'protocol governance'" — "each more opaque, more ravenous, and more unaccountable than their predecessors".
- The BTC indictment is categorical: "It is not cash, for it cannot scale. It is not a medium of exchange, for it cannot function at commercial throughput. It is not a store of value, for its worth gyrates like a circus acrobat with a death wish." No factory, supply chain or trade corridor runs on it.
- Wealth versus finance: wealth is labour and ingenuity transforming matter — the brick, the bridge, the crop, the machine. "The labourer pouring concrete under a hostile sun contributes more to humanity's progress in an hour than an entire hedge fund contributes in a decade."
- The opportunity cost: US$40 trillion redirected annually could "modernise infrastructure across continents, elevate billions from poverty, catalyse the next industrial renaissance"; instead capital spins "uselessly like a top that never falls only because it never moves forward".
- Direct exchange is the counter-vision: "a handshake made cryptographically visible, a contract enforceable without a pilgrimage through institutional purgatory"; verification replaces trust, ending absurdities like "ten-dollar fees to move a dollar" and correspondent-bank mazes. It is framed as "an ethical proposition", not merely a mechanism.
- The closing charge: today's brightest minds "squander their intellect designing trading algorithms that siphon micro-fractions of value"; redirecting even a tenth of that brainpower "would redraw the boundary of the possible".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The essay combines institutional-economics arithmetic (IMF/FSB GDP shares and asset totals) with a moral philosophy of value — production versus extraction, wealth as creation rather than circulation — delivered through satirical inversion (intermediaries as toll collectors, mosquitoes, a priesthood). It is a classical rent-seeking critique extended to crypto, which is judged by whether capital flows "toward creation, not circulation".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against "BTC exposes and opposes the financial system": "BTC is not the enemy of the financial order—it is its apotheosis", distilling financialisation into "the worship of price untethered from the gravity of purpose".
- Against crypto's disintermediation claim: "Crypto promised disintermediation and delivered the opposite: a fresh priesthood of exchanges, custodians, protocol cabals, influencers, and self-appointed visionaries."
- Against sanctified scarcity: "Scarcity, in its true form, is merely a condition to be overcome—through invention, labour, and production"; BTC's scarcity is "a fetish, a sacralised austerity masquerading as wisdom".
- Against "financial innovation" as progress: not one such innovation "has ever grown a crop, cured a disease, or built a single machine that can survive a winter".

## Notable quotes
- "nearly forty per cent of the world's economic output is now dedicated not to building, creating, or enriching life, but to shuffling claims, extracting rents"

# The Future of Digital Currency — core principles

- **National digital currency needs tens of thousands of transactions per second.** Treasury-level demand sits in the 30,000–100,000 TPS range; internationally, interconnected systems require millions of transactions per second without compromising security, cost or reliability.
- **A re-architected base layer is the qualifying infrastructure.** Incumbent throughputs of roughly 7 and 30 TPS are far from sufficient; Teranode-class engineering raises throughput to millions by eliminating the bottlenecks that previously limited speed.
- **CBDCs and stablecoins are complementary functions, not rivals.** CBDCs digitise national currency and sharpen monetary-policy control; stablecoins, typically pegged to the dollar or euro, serve cross-border trade and financial inclusion without reliance on a single government issue.
- **Monetary systems should compete on a level field.** The goal is not a single dominant system controlled by one or a few entities, but open competitive markets in which the most effective solutions emerge naturally.
- **Cross-border settlement in seconds, with minimal fees.** A business paying a foreign supplier should not wait days or pay correspondent-banking tolls; instantaneous settlement is a necessity for global trade, not a luxury.
- **Many currencies, one high-throughput substrate.** A unified, interoperable ecosystem lets multiple CBDCs and stablecoins cooperate; efficiency, not bureaucratic delay, becomes the driving force of commerce.

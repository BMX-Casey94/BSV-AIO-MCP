# Binance: The Untrusted Intermediary
**Date:** 2020-03-02 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/binance-the-untrusted-intermediary-dddec51f5c47
**Subtitle:** Today, as I have done in the past, I set up a number of accounts with Binance based only on the verification of e-mail addresses. As was…

## Core thesis
Craig reports a personal penetration test of Binance's onboarding: he created over 100 accounts in a day using nothing but unverified names, dates of birth, and email addresses, each allowing 2 BTC of withdrawals per 24 hours — enough, he calculates, to launder USD2 million a day. He argues Binance is functionally a re-run of Liberty Reserve (shut down under AML law in 2013), that CZ's "decentralised exchange" branding is a fiction for a "giant commercial mixer", and that because bitcoin is property, the nemo dat rule means tainted coins bought there never confer good title. The Medium post is an excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net.

## Key arguments and claims
- Binance KYC is theatre: accounts need only a name, date of birth, and email — "None of it is verified at all" — and each gets a 24-hour withdrawal allowance of up to 2 BTC.
- He personally "set up over 100 such accounts, none of which involved real names or addresses", enabling "up to USD20,000 a day out of Binance, after washing it through various cryptocurrencies"; scaled to 100 accounts, "USD2 million a day through Binance without being noticed".
- The precedent is Liberty Reserve: he quotes Manhattan U.S. Attorney Preet Bharara on Arthur Budovsky's "digital currency empire built expressly to facilitate money laundering on a massive scale" and says Binance has merely "learnt from many of the mistakes of Liberty Reserve".
- The "DEX" is a marketing fiction: "a DEX is no different than the distributed laundering operation created by Liberty Reserve" — multiple servers run "in a manner that is designed to bypass regulatory controls".
- Binance is "a giant commercial mixer": clean money from "foolish gamblers" and tax avoiders provides the volume needed to wash "hardcore criminal funds" — honest users are the necessary cover.
- Revenue sources are alleged to be "credit card theft, identity fraud, laundering money associated with extortion, and Ponzi investment schemes"; without Binance, "CryptoLocker coders" could not monetise.
- Scale claim: external estimates put ~USD1 billion laundered through Binance in a year; "In reality, Binance alone accounts for money laundering in the order of USD2.1 billion or more annually", via OTC trades, off-platform swaps, and brokers "tied to terrorism and arms dealings".
- CZ's nomadic jurisdiction-hopping ("lives out of a suitcase and changes his location every week") is evidence of intent to evade prosecution; his downfall is inevitable, "like all foolish criminals".
- Legal payload: "All cryptocurrencies are property, and are subject to law as property"; under nemo dat quod non habet, coins mixed with criminal funds carry defective title, so buyers on Binance do not get bitcoin "that you can own and call your own".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is adversarial audit: he runs the experiment himself (mass account creation), quantifies the throughput of the abuse vector, then maps the findings onto a known enforcement precedent (Liberty Reserve) to predict the legal endpoint. He quotes the target's own marketing (CZ's "choices and different levels of privacy" post) and reframes "privacy" as "anonymity and the facilitation of crime". The closing move is property-law doctrine — nemo dat — converting an AML critique into a warning that every Binance buyer's title is contaminated.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "not your keys, not your coins" framing that treats exchanges as mere custody risks: Craig's claim is stronger — title itself fails on tainted coins, so even self-custodied withdrawals from Binance are legally defective property.
- Contradicts the DEX/DeFi narrative of the era (2020), which treated "decentralised exchange" as a regulatory safe category: he calls it a distributed laundering operation with the same legal exposure as Liberty Reserve.
- Contradicts the "privacy is a fundamental right" defence (quoting CZ): he distinguishes privacy from anonymity and casts exchange-level anonymity as per-se criminal facilitation.
- Contradicts the fungibility assumption underlying "clean" market pricing: taint follows coins through mixers, so proceeds-of-crime law reaches ordinary holders.

# The Abolition of the House — core principles

- **The trusted operator is three bundled functions, not one party.** Fairness of mechanism, keeping of secrets, and custody of property can each be dissolved by its own cryptographic primitive — and what can be dissolved separately can be dissolved together.
- **Fairness without a dealer is joint computation among distrustful parties.** Mental poker, verifiable secret shuffles and the completeness theorem show that any computation a trusted party could perform can be performed jointly by the players themselves.
- **Secrecy without a keeper is designed disclosure.** Broadcast encryption, revocation and traitor tracing make the readable set a protocol choice: sealed moves proven later, delayed spectator reveals, selective disclosure to an adjudicator.
- **Property without a custodian is a bearer object.** Items become scarce by construction, owned by possession of a key, transferable directly, and rule-bearing — Nakamoto's ledger joined to Szabo's composed rules. An item whose existence depends on one company's server is not property at all.
- **Portability is the decisive new property.** A bearer asset wanders between venues as money wanders between shops, and its price is discovered across markets rather than decreed by an operator's store.
- **Fairness becomes a verifiable artifact.** The transcript of a proven shuffle, public randomness anyone may check, and disclosure that opens only to the entitled replace a believed claim asserted by a house and audited by a regulator.
- **No-custodian means no-recourse, with full symmetry.** A stolen key is the asset gone, finally. Every primitive rests on physical keys; collusion outside the protocol — the whispered word — is unreachable by mathematics.
- **Power relocates; it does not vanish.** Residual authority sits with protocol authors, asset issuers, standards maintainers and large holders. Name those parties. Unbundle deliberately: the convenient trusted helper is the house returning by the side door.

# The Protocol No Gatekeeper Wants — core principles

- **Control of payments, identity, property, contracts, data and evidence belongs at the edges.** No intermediary is compulsory. A service is paid for a task; a gatekeeper extracts because parties cannot otherwise reach each other. The objective is to make the centre unnecessary.
- **A fixed protocol is the commitment upon which products compete.** If developers can alter validity rules they are governors; if exchanges coordinate acceptance of a rule change they exercise institutional power; if a foundation can redefine an asset, ownership is conditional. The rules are not a product feature.
- **SPV is central, not optional.** Miners order and timestamp; users hold their own transactions and evidence and verify inclusion without operating transaction-processing nodes. Hierarchical Merkle commitments keep proof size logarithmic while transaction volume expands by orders of magnitude; proofs travel with the payment.
- **The operational test is substitutability.** If a service disappears, can another recover and continue the relationship from evidence controlled by the parties? If the answer is no, the system remains centralised at the point that matters. Users change tools, not worlds — exit without exile.
- **Universal replication produces centralising economics.** If everyone must process everything, capacity is restricted, fees rise, micropayments die, and users flee to exchanges and custodians. The ideology of universal verification creates the intermediaries it claims to prevent.
- **Overlays create application-specific views over a common transaction substrate.** Protocol, database, identity, interface and governance are separated. The service remains useful; it ceases to be sovereign. Users bring keys, proofs and portable state, so entry no longer requires rebuilding the whole commercial network.
- **Micropayments let services charge for actual use.** One article, one calculation, one message, one second of computation or one sensor reading. Digital scarcity makes a licence, ticket, credential, invoice or publication a unique authorised state. Intermediaries survive only by adding measurable value.
- **Privacy is selective disclosure among accountable parties.** Anonymity as commerce's organising principle attempts to remove identity and responsibility. Bitcoin does not eliminate law; it gives lawful relationships a better evidential substrate, including lawful recovery and succession without pretending that possession of a key answers every legal question.
- **UTXOs, Script, SPV and overlays form a transaction system with transferable state** — not a shared database, not a rare ceremonial resource, and not a perpetual protocol-politics programme.

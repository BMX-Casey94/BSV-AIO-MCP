# Myths of Decentralisation… — core principles

- **You hold your own bitcoin.** Tokens are not distributed across the network. Your bitcoin is held in your wallet or related application; the blockchain is a transaction record and log of control and movement.
- **The ledger is a time-ordering log.** It is distributed so the correct version of a token is fixed and later double-spend attempts are ignored — a list of what was seen first.
- **Bitcoin is tokens, not accounts.** It is a set of individual and indivisible tokens. Ownership attaches as intangible digital property — a chose in action — because of law, not code.
- **All tokens were issued at launch.** Every token was allocated in January 2009 under published rules. The white paper acts as the equivalent of the software’s EULA. The subsidy is allocation to commercial nodes, not a later mint.
- **Bitcoin does not use encryption.** All transactions are conducted in clear text. Digital signatures are not encryption and do not stop access to files.
- **Peer-to-peer is layered.** Section-5 nodes are highly connected commercial systems designed to end in data centres, competing for fees. Users were catered for by SPV and original IP-to-IP connectivity so exchanging peers communicate directly.
- **The network simulates a trusted intermediary without single-entity fragility.** A competitive group structured as a parallelised quorum replaces the single timestamp authority of earlier time-chain designs. Nodes come and go; all companies eventually fail.
- **Property rights exist because of law.** The ledger is merely a time-ordering and logging system. Transfers may be invalid and theft remains possible. Code is not law; Bitcoin does not replace banks.
- **Defectors are isolated and prosecuted.** Public, auditable information lets honest nodes detect an attacking system quickly and use computer-crime legislation to bring the offending node to justice.

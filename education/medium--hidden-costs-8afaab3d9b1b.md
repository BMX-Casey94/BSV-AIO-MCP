# Hidden costs — core principles

- **All valuation is subjective.** Calling other people's behaviour irrational merely privileges your own values over theirs; subjective valuation is precisely why the price system is needed, not a bar to economic calculation.
- **Every choice imposes hidden flow-on costs.** Locking away land raises real-estate prices and pushes farming onto marginal land, raising food costs; diverting crops to biofuels repeats the mechanism — each unit withdrawn becomes progressively more expensive.
- **Scarcity is the foundation of economics.** There is and never will be enough for all people to do all they want; without scarcity there would be no place for economics.
- **Protocol changes are uncompensated costs on all users.** Every alteration of the base protocol creates uncertainty, and uncertainty is a cost paid by everyone: some gain — those who want the change — and the rest pay.
- **Experiment on your own chain, not on a captured network effect.** Dissenting developers should split off and compete on their own chain rather than run experiments using the network effect built with other people's money.
- **A stable, locked base protocol is an economic good.** Money requires a predictable foundation; minimising base-layer change minimises the uncertainty costs imposed on every user and lets business build with confidence.

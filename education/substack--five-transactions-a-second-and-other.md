# Five Transactions a Second, and Other Discourtesies to Commerce — core principles

- **Cash requires unbounded throughput.** A few transactions per second cannot serve a population of billions. Digital cash and digital gold sit at opposite ends of the monetary stack: the thing that settles cannot, at the same volume, be the thing that circulates. No white paper can repeal arithmetic.
- **A capped base reconstructs banking.** If the base occupies the gold seat, everyday payment becomes Lightning balances, custodial deposits, tokenised IOUs and stablecoins — paper in the older sense. The user does not transact in Bitcoin; the user transacts in something that promises Bitcoin.
- **Fee auctions exclude the small payer.** When inclusion is an auction, a two-pound coffee paid for with a fifty-pound fee is not a payment system. Cash's defining property is universal accessibility; what remains is a settlement asset for the rich.
- **A second layer is the confession of the first.** Channels lock funds; routing needs intermediaries with liquidity, uptime and honesty. Hubs are banks in every economically meaningful sense. Non-custodial cryptography rearranges failure modes; it does not abolish them.
- **The security budget needs volume, not a cap.** The subsidy tends to zero. A throughput ceiling forbids the fee volume required to replace it. Scarcity at the base generates intermediaries with the inexorability of a thermodynamic law, and those intermediaries become regulable banks under new names.
- **Honest money states its station.** Gold settled the upper register of commerce and never claimed to be the daily medium; circulating notes claimed redeemability, not identity with the metal. A system sold as cash and built as bullion has forgotten that honesty.

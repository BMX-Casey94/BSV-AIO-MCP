# Bitcoin Is Anything BUT Anonymous — core principles

- **Section 9 defines privacy, not anonymity.** The white paper’s new privacy model keeps details away from the public — not from counterparties, and not from those required by law to monitor exchanges.
- **All transactions are public so double-spends can be stopped.** The system announces every payment so it can be viewed and analysed at will; privacy is maintained, but it is no longer associated with the transaction.
- **Identities are firewalled, not removed.** Identities are kept from the transactions and the public, but they are not deleted. Commercial exchange and contracting cannot occur without knowledge of the other party.
- **Re-keying and amount-splitting preserve privacy while remaining auditable.** With keys not reused and payments split into multiple amounts and coins, metadata can be monitored like a stock-exchange tickertape: everything can be audited without the public knowing all of the information about a transaction.
- **Parties can derive identity-linked keys.** New keys can simultaneously associate identities and authorise parties within organisations — including for taxation.
- **Bitcoin does not remove the need for all third parties.** Correspondence banks, credit companies and processors in the legacy model learn everyone’s details; Bitcoin firewalls the public view without abolishing entitled intermediaries.
- **The banking model exposes PII across many hops.** Traditional privacy fails because personally identifiable information is distributed to every intermediary; Bitcoin’s model is the opposite of that dispersal.

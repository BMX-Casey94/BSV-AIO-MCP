# Your Property Rights Don't Exist Without a State — And That's Not a Moral Claim
**Date:** 2026-02-13 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/your-property-rights-dont-exist-without
**Subtitle:** A formal proof that stateless property enforcement cannot protect the weak, and why Bitcoin proves Kinsella wrong about digital ownership

## Core thesis
Adapted from a paper under submission to the Journal of Institutional Economics, the post proves formally that polycentric (stateless) property enforcement cannot deliver enforceability and finality for weak agents: every repair mechanism either fails against determined defectors or reintroduces the monopoly terminal authority it was designed to avoid. Kinsella's physicalist restriction of property to rivalrous physical things is falsified by existing institutional property, and blockchain works as digital property precisely because it supplies the state-function in code.

## Key arguments and claims
- The target is Stephan Kinsella's framework (Against Intellectual Property, 2001): property allocates scarce resources to avoid conflict (C1, accepted); scarcity requires physical rivalrousness (C2); institutions cannot create scarcity (C3); a polycentric private-law society can enforce property (C4); hence IP is not property and the state unnecessary (C5). Craig uses C1 to demolish C2–C4.
- The method is an adversarial proof — worst-case behaviour assumed throughout, because a property system that only works when everyone cooperates is "a gentleman's agreement". A working system must bind non-consenting defectors, produce final outcomes, prevent jurisdictional veto, terminate meta-disputes, provide unitary title records, and cost less than the rights protected.
- Model 1 (Forum Veto Game): a strong party's agency rejects every proposed forum by backward induction, since rejection preserves a status quo favouring the possessor; the unique subgame-perfect equilibrium is universal veto by the strong and rational surrender by the weak. Bond repairs fail because enforcing forfeiture requires compulsory terminal jurisdiction — a state.
- Model 2 (Enforcement Market Game): a profit-maximising protection agency is always purchasable by a wealthy adversary offering more than the victim's lifetime premium stream, the sell-out disguised as a "commercial settlement". The model derives the "justice floor" — where enforcement cost exceeds asset value, rights cease to exist in practice.
- Model 3 (Institutional Drift Game): Hotelling-type spatial competition in which institutions position legal standards toward strong clients (higher evidentiary burdens, shorter limitation periods, costlier procedures); weak-serving institutions go bankrupt, so "law tracks money" under formal neutrality.
- The Conditional Impossibility Theorem: given open entry, non-consenting defectors, costly attribution, wealth asymmetry and payer concentration, no polycentric order without compulsory jurisdiction, registry or fiduciary constraint can deliver enforceability and finality for weak agents. Each necessary repair constitutes a monopoly terminal authority.
- State capture is conceded but answered comparatively: monopoly failure modes (single-point capture, registry corruption) are amenable to constitutional repair; polycentric failure modes (distributed multiplicative veto, registry multiplication) are structural.
- Resources fall into three categories, not two: physically rivalrous goods (I); institutionally created but genuinely rivalrous positions — bank balances, shares, domain names, spectrum licences, tokens (II); and non-rivalrous information (III). Category II, moving trillions daily, refutes the physicalist premise on its own.
- Three counterexamples satisfy Kinsella's own scarcity criteria while being non-physical: radio spectrum (over $230 billion in auction revenues since 1994; Coase's 1959 paper), domain names (over 60,000 WIPO UDRP disputes; Voice.com sold for $30M in 2019), and Bitcoin (UTXO single-spend rivalrousness; market capitalisation exceeding $1 trillion; recognised as property by IRS Notice 2014-21, HMRC, and the English High Court in AA v Persons Unknown [2019] EWHC 3556 (Comm)).
- Token property is categorically distinct from IP: it restricts only the controller's exclusive ledger position and prevents nobody from copying files or creating tokens, so Kinsella's anti-IP argument has zero application to it.
- Blockchain supplies, within its domain, finality, compulsory jurisdiction (no veto over the state-transition function), and unitary publicity (one authoritative ledger) — the state-function in code, confined to on-chain token transitions within the consensus-dominant branch.
- History confirms the theorem: the lex mercatoria operated in the shadow of sovereign enforcement (Berman 1983; Milgrom, North and Weingast 1990); Gold Rush mining districts collapsed into corporate dispossession and were replaced by the General Mining Act 1872; ICANN moved from voluntary governance (1998) to compulsory monopoly via the UDRP within a decade.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Axiomatic institutional economics with game-theoretic proof: derive enforcement requirements from the opponent's own functional definition, model each failure as a formal equilibrium result, then test every proposed commitment device (bonds, reputation, charters, escrow, auditors) and show each either fails adversarially or re-imports monopoly authority.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Cuts against crypto-anarchist "code is law" anti-statism by proving monopoly terminal authority is functionally necessary for property — Bitcoin succeeds precisely because it is one for its domain: libertarians celebrating Bitcoin while rejecting monopoly terminal authority "are celebrating the very thing they claim to oppose."
- Rejects the libertarian anti-NFT argument: token property is not IP, and the "right-click save" objection confuses the copyable file with the rivalrous ledger position.

## Notable quotes
- "A property right that cannot compel compliance from the strong without requiring the victim to purchase equivalent coercive capacity is not a right. It is a priced service."
- "Blockchain doesn't replace the physical-world state. It is a state, for the specific domain of digital token ownership."

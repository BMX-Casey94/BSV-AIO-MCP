# I shall continue answering in order.
**Date:** 2018-09-20 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/i-shall-continue-answering-in-order-eae445cba4bf
**Subtitle:** The original questions are posted here.

## Core thesis
The fifth instalment of a written Q&A (answering questions 58-61), in which Craig compresses his whole Bitcoin worldview into a hypothetical "Top 10 FAQ" for the whitepaper and then defends his combative public persona. The FAQ's spine: nodes are competing commercial miners, all scaling limits should be removed and then the base protocol locked, SPV suffices for users, and "Layer 2 is IN SCRIPT". The closing answers generalise this into a philosophy of pure competition — Bitcoin as "Rome 2.0" — and a flat rejection of "community" as a governing ideal.

## Key arguments and claims
- Regrets nothing about Bitcoin's first decade: "Bitcoin is a long game. It is something I expect to keep me busy into my 70's or 80's."
- The FAQ asserts "Nodes are miners, these are commercial entities that compete" — equating the network's validating backbone with profit-seeking businesses, not hobbyist full nodes.
- Scaling roadmap in one line: "The aim is to remove all limits to scale as soon as possible. Then, and only then, to lock the base protocol and stop any arbitrary change" — unbounded blocks first, protocol ossification second.
- "SPV is safe and is all a user needs" and "Layer 2 is IN SCRIPT" — users should never need to run nodes, and second-layer functionality belongs in Bitcoin Script predicates, not external networks like Lightning.
- "Malleability is not a flaw" — transaction malleability, the stated justification for SegWit, is reframed as a non-bug.
- Defines decentralisation instrumentally and numerically: "Distributed and decentralised means that there are 3 or more miners with no group holding more than 50% control" — a low bar far from maximal-distribution orthodoxy.
- "LAW is law, code is at best a form of evidence" and "Patents are a means to encourage development" — a direct statement of the legal-supremacy and pro-patent positions underpinning the nChain strategy.
- "ICOs are a scam. FULL STOP", while "A digital IPO, tokenised shares, equity, bonds, etc are NOT ICOs" — distinguishing compliant tokenised securities from the 2017 ICO market.
- Decentralisation is a tool, not a goal: "There is no and I mean NO reason to make something decentralised just so it is decentralised"; too much decentralisation is "more insecure than centralised", and adding "1,000 X the nodes" must be justified against cost.
- Bitcoin's governance is weighted like the Roman Republic: voting power tracks investment — "not proof of stake, what you had and want to keep, but what you are willing to risk".
- On his style: "FUCK the community! Bitcoin is NOT socialist... The community, the concept of a crypto community — this group think of non-competitive consensus — it is a cancer!" He is glad ETH supporters occupy "a dead end project" rather than slowing BCH with "ideas of socialist togetherness".
- Challenged on the wormhole/counterparty proof-of-burn address checksum dispute, he refuses concession: "The base58 of this incorporates the address separately... I make mistakes — this is not one."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The FAQ format is axiomatic: short declarative dogmas ("Bitcoin is pure capitalism", "Only one coin can exist at scale") presented as design facts rather than arguments. The discursive answers then reason by analogy and inversion — Roman Republican voting weights for governance, competition-as-teleology for culture — and by redefining contested terms (decentralisation, malleability, hoarding) so that mainstream critiques become category errors. Rhetorically it is unapologetically agonistic: Socratic teaching is endorsed, but "time is a limit and there is not enough of it to be nice".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the full-node-for-everyone doctrine outright: SPV "is all a user needs", and nodes are commercial miners — the opposite of BTC's "verify, don't trust" culture.
- Denies the premise of SegWit ("Malleability is not a flaw") and of Lightning ("Layer 2 is IN SCRIPT"), the two pillars of BTC's scaling roadmap, two months before the BCH/BSV split.
- Redefines decentralisation as "3 or more miners with no group holding more than 50%", where BTC orthodoxy treats maximal node count and geographic dispersion as the security goal.
- Elevates patents and corporations ("Companies help to protect rights and allow us freedom from government and tyranny") against the open-source, anti-IP ethos of mainstream crypto.
- Frames consensus as capitalist competition rather than community agreement — "Bitcoin is not about people agreeing — it is about them competing!!!!!" — against the cooperative-governance self-image of both BTC and ETH communities.

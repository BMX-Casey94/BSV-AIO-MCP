# The lie of anarchy
**Date:** 2018-12-13 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-lie-of-anarchy-bd7c1f239289
**Subtitle:** Many have thought that Bitcoin was in some manner designed to promote anarchy and a stateless society, that concept cannot be further from…

## Core thesis
Bitcoin was designed to promote the rule of law, not to abolish the state: it is "a system where all are equal under the law and sunlight is shone on the corruption that festers in the dark". Anarchism is a "corrosive philosophy" incompatible with liberty, and its adherents merely pose as libertarians. Bitcoin's real purpose is corporate compliance — an immutable ledger that makes document destruction (Enron-style) impossible, automates Sarbanes-Oxley-grade disclosure, and lets firms prove due diligence to auditors and courts.

## Key arguments and claims
- The founding claim of crypto-anarchy is inverted: "Bitcoin was designed to promote the rule of law… Bitcoin was designed to allow corporations and people to engage with sound government — not to remove government."
- "Anarchism ≠ Libertarianism": anarchists claiming the libertarian mantle run "a charade designed to help indoctrinate those who have not really studied liberty into the Philosophy of Collectivism"; the demand to dismantle ALL government is "the lie of a free society".
- Sarbanes-Oxley §§802 and 1102 (codified at 18 U.S.C. 1519 and 18 U.S.C. 1512(c)) impose criminal penalties for destroying evidence relevant to actual or even "contemplated" federal investigations — "set the basis for why an immutable blockchain is needed".
- Enron counterfactual: "If the accounts of Enron had been implemented on a blockchain-based ledger, there would have been no way for these to be destroyed." SOX §409 real-time disclosure of legal risks "will be simple" with Bitcoin at scale — "a partnership of business and government acting transparently".
- Litigation readiness: parties must list not only documents in their possession but those "that once existed and have been destroyed" — an "incredibly difficult task" that an immutable ledger solves by construction.
- Machine learning plus Bitcoin enables automated compliance: classifying scanned/electronic documents, data-mining warehouses for keywords, configuring database rules to isolate required documents, and network scanning for classifications. He discloses the interest: "some of the IP we have been creating at nChain is all about" exactly this.
- Security's CIA triad — confidentiality, integrity, availability — is "preserved in Bitcoin"; the compliance landscape (PCI-DSS, Australia's CLERP9 which is "as onerous as, if not more onerous than SOX", criminal penalties around Tax File Numbers and SSNs) creates demand for provable retention.
- Evidentiary governance: "If you cannot prove it using evidence, then there is a presumption that you have not done anything. Bitcoin provides a ledger that helps organisations meet their security needs and also prove that they have done so."
- Audit theory: "The primary objective of an auditor is to measure and report on risk"; the auditor is "the eyes and ears of management", and measurement lets management act. Bitcoin's "real purpose" follows: "it is not anarchist money, it is sound reporting, a system that is resistant to corruption".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Wright argues by definitional policing (anarchism versus libertarianism), statutory exegesis (SOX sections with US Code citations), and historical counterfactual (Enron on a blockchain). The method fuses his auditor's frame — risk measurement, due diligence, evidentiary presumption — with the protocol's properties, treating immutability not as a cypherpunk escape hatch but as a compliance primitive. The rhetorical mode is polemical inversion: the feature his opponents prize (a ledger no one can alter) is re-purposed as the state's and the auditor's best tool.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the foundational cypherpunk orthodoxy that Bitcoin exists to route around the state: Bitcoin "cannot be anarchist" and was built to strengthen the rule of law.
- Contradicts the "code is law / regulation is irrelevant" stance: statutory regimes (SOX, CLERP9, PCI-DSS) are the very reason enterprises need Bitcoin, and criminal evidence law reaches blockchain records.
- Contradicts the privacy-through-opacity strain of crypto culture: the value proposition is transparency — "sunlight is shone on the corruption that festers in the dark and hidden regions".
- Contradicts the "immutability protects dissidents" framing: immutability's headline use is stopping corporations from shredding documents, i.e. it binds the powerful to their records rather than freeing users from oversight.

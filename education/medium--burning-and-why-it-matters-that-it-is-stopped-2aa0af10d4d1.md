# Burning and why it matters that it is stopped — core principles

- **Lost keys are not burned keys.** "A 'lost' bitcoin is not the same as a 'burned' bitcoin": RIPEMD-160's 160-bit hash space erodes at "around 6.7 bits of information" per decade with "around 70 bits of information free" now, so collisions can be expected "(without a mathematical flaw) in just 40 to 60 years" — a lost coin is like "the Spanish gold and silver sunk for a long time to the ocean floor, but one day to be recovered".
- **Loss does not diminish the money.** "If bitcoin was a global currency, then lost bitcoins only mean a loss for the individual … and a gain in the future for the proverbial bitcoin treasure hunter."
- **Burning is destruction, not backing.** Per Bastiat's Parable of the Broken Window, destruction does not create; a gold note cannot be backed "by taking gold and throwing it into an active volcano" — destroying coins to issue a token is "a SHAM or a common 'bait and switch'", not backing.
- **Supply is a fixed integer count.** "There are no decimal points in Bitcoin": the protocol transacts "a fixed-format integer value" of 10⁸ satoshi per coin (21 million bitcoin is 0x000775F05A074000 in hex); wallet decimals are a visualisation and IEEE 754 floating point has no place — "Bitcoin is a coin, a token system, it is atomic."
- **Altering supply alters identity.** Adding precision means adding tokens — a consensus-rule change such that "altering the number of individual tokens in Bitcoin means it is no longer Bitcoin"; the format permits at most "a doubling or, at the utmost, an 8x increase in possible tokens".
- **Scarcity alone is not value.** Value comes from "a limited scarcity that is not diluted", not from destroying supply — destroying coins diminishes rather than raises value; the contrary claim is "the Ricardian value fallacy".
- **Burn-to-issue drains the base asset.** In a toy model (1,000 B1 against 10,000 W1), destroying the base coin for the issued token makes the base "less and less liquid, and the system drives towards only W1 tokens" — the base chain degrades into a mere settlement layer for a separate token.
- **Misrepresented backing is fraud.** Claiming a token is "backed" by destroyed coins is "a common securities fraud"; "false and misleading statements are not only civil offences, they are criminal in nature", and "the courts will investigate the substance of the transaction", not its label — ICO tokens classify as share, debenture or unit.

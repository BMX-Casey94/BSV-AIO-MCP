# The Oldest New Problem in Finance: Proof of Stake and the Return of the Bearer Share
**Date:** 2026-03-16 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-oldest-new-problem-in-finance
**Subtitle:** 

## Core thesis
Proof of Stake has structurally — not metaphorically — rebuilt the bearer share, the instrument every major jurisdiction abolished between 2004 and 2022 for enabling hidden concentration, anonymous self-dealing and governance capture. The governance consequences are already observable and measured: staking oligopoly, cartelised validators, programmatic vote-buying markets and zero-cost governance capture via flash loans.

## Key arguments and claims
- Bearer shares were killed in sequence: flagged by the FATF as a primary opacity vector between 2003 and 2005; effectively abolished in the British Virgin Islands in 2004; Luxembourg and Ireland followed in 2014; the UK banned new issuances under the Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015; Panama immobilised them under Law No. 47 in 2015 after the OECD declared the instrument incompatible with beneficial-ownership transparency.
- A PoS token confers three economic rights mapping one-to-one onto equity: a residual claim on fees, MEV and issuance (a dividend); a stake-weighted governance vote (a shareholder vote); and a slashable security bond (a capital-adequacy requirement). Title travels with the private key — no transfer agent, registrar or identity check. "A PoS token is a bearer share, denominated in a different substrate."
- PoS amplifies the two properties that made bearer shares dangerous — transferability without record and governance without identity: settlement in seconds to any address on earth, and a marginal cost of zero for creating each new pseudonymous address, so fragmenting a position to disguise concentration costs nothing.
- Registered markets force visibility — in the US, crossing 5% beneficial ownership triggers Schedule 13D/13G filings with the SEC — whereas a PoS actor can vote hundreds of pseudonymous addresses as a coordinated block. The effective Nakamoto coefficient, accounting for hidden common ownership, is by construction unobservable: a "Potemkin democracy".
- Concentration is measured, not theoretical: as of early 2026 roughly 35.9 million ETH (about 29% of supply) is staked; Lido commands approximately 24% of staked ETH (down from a 2023 peak above 32%), Coinbase about 11.7%, Binance about 9.1%, and the top ten staking entities control over 60% — "an oligopoly operating behind a pseudonymous veil".
- Staking providers replicate the nominee/bearer pathology: depositors hold formal economic ownership but zero practical governance power, while the provider decides block proposals, MEV strategy and censorship as a de facto nominee with no fiduciary obligations.
- The Curve Wars built explicit vote-buying infrastructure: Convex aggregated vote-locked CRV to control Curve's gauge emissions; Votium and Hidden Hand operated as programmatic bribery markets; Abracadabra committed over $10 million per voting cycle; Votium alone processed tens of millions in bribes — transparent, on-chain and market-priced.
- Flash-loan governance exceeds anything bearer shares enabled: on 17 April 2022 an attacker borrowed over $1 billion from Aave, acquired a two-thirds supermajority of Beanstalk Farms governance tokens, passed a proposal draining $182 million, repaid the loan within a single transaction and laundered proceeds through Tornado Cash — total governance control at zero economic exposure.
- The SEC's May 2025 statement (protocol staking as "administrative or ministerial", outside Howey) and August 2025 extension to liquid staking (stETH-type tokens as receipts) answer the wrong question: Howey tests reliance on others' efforts, while the bearer-share problem concerns anonymous ownership of governance power. Even Commissioner Crenshaw's dissent was framed in Howey terms.
- Re-intermediation is already under way on both historical tracks: custodial exchanges perform KYC (a partial shadow registry), while regulated wrappers — WisdomTree's physical stETH ETP, VanEck's late-2025 filing for a Lido Staked ETH ETF, Figment adding roughly 344,000 ETH in a single month after institutional demand doubled — amount to a voluntary dematerialisation programme, but one covering only the regulated fraction of stake.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Law-and-economics by structural analogy: establish the property-rights structure of the historical instrument, document why regulators abolished it, then demonstrate a one-to-one functional mapping onto the new instrument — supported with on-chain concentration data, named protocols, dated enforcement events and statutory references — and show the new substrate strictly amplifies each pathology.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post is a legal-economic analysis of proof-of-stake governance instruments, argued from corporate-law history and on-chain evidence rather than from any BTC/Core position.

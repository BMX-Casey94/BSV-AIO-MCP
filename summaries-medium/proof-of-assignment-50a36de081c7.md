# Proof of Assignment
**Date:** 2019-11-13 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/proof-of-assignment-50a36de081c7
**Subtitle:** The Bitcoin blockchain is an immutable audit trail, which does not mean that money cannot be seized or that illicit images and other…

## Core thesis
An excerpt (the body continues on craigwright.net) arguing that Bitcoin's immutability is fully compatible with court-ordered seizure and reassignment of coins. "Immutability does not require non-assignability": the record never changes, but ownership can be reassigned by legal order enforced by miners, with every alteration publicly recorded. Far from weakening Bitcoin, this transparency is what a free society needs — and any exchange, miner, or "validating node" fantasy that resists valid court orders will be crushed.

## Key arguments and claims
- Definition: "Immutability does not require non-assignability. An immutable object is something that remains unchanging over time" — once a block is deep enough, transactions cannot be changed, but "as with all code-based systems, alterations can easily be made. The secret behind Bitcoin is that the alterations are publicly recorded."
- Analogy: an Oracle database in write-once-read-many (WORM) format, "used in all public companies in the US" — such systems "do not preclude the correcting of mistakes; it requires that a mistake is noted and followed".
- Seizure requires international cooperation: a worldwide freezing order "would require enforcement actions in multiple jurisdictions", which usefully limits abuse — "Every small government seeking to abuse the rights of their citizens will not be able to use Bitcoin in order to seize assets", but for "major drug trafficking, people smuggling, high-level money laundering", multiple jurisdictions can act together.
- Transparency as liberty: "A Republican democracy requires transparency to be healthy and thrive. Which is what Bitcoin delivers." Nazi Germany and Stalinist Russia hid records of seizures and imprisonments; Bitcoin records every seizure — "It is what a free society needs."
- Anonymity regresses to the powerful: "The ability to hide money outside of the government's ability to tax only hurts the poorest people in society... It is not the rich who suffer from anonymity, nor is it the corrupt. The people who suffer are those who have the least." Legal tax shelters "can be constructed using privacy and without anonymity".
- "Court is the ultimate distributed system" — common-law judges and lawyers form "a truly distributed and decentralised method of decision"; the only system guaranteed to go your way is "a centralised system where you are the dictator".
- Enforcement chain: court issues order → law enforcement enforces across jurisdictions → "the miners will act to enforce the rules". A UTXO reassignment "is recorded... it becomes public knowledge that the UTXO has been assigned".
- Freezing orders can be private and operate FIFO: "Miners and exchanges would be required to block and reject selected Bitcoin addresses or keys" — temporary, off-chain, though likely to leak given hundreds of recipients.
- Exchanges/wallet providers (Blockchain.com, Bitcoin.com, Coinbase, Kraken, OkEx, BitGo named) that defy a valid freezing order "will be criminalised and face first contempt and then sequestration, before being shut down as a criminal operation. The expected lifespan... would be minutes."
- "Without infrastructure, Bitcoin and any other cryptocurrency lose all value" — transactions must "follow monetary law. If they fail to do so, they lose all value." Monetary tracing follows FIFO, and the blockchain's full history makes frozen coins traceable through mixers and onward spends.
- The 2010 alert key was his own "very early and nascent attempt" at a notification system; its centralisation "belonging to just myself and then later a few developers" was problematic, and at today's value unacceptable — "In the future, governments will be able to issue their own alerts using their own keys."
- "Bitcoin was never designed to act outside of the law, nor was it designed to attack government"; "no blockchain can be made anonymous. It is antithetical to the nature of how blockchain-based systems work." Exchanges ignoring AML and the EU's fifth money laundering directive (MLD5) "will cease to exist".
- Nodes: "There is no such thing as a validating node in Bitcoin... only miners matter." Nodes are defined by six steps in the white paper; "Any system that does not complete all of the six stages is not a node in Bitcoin. As such, any Raspberry Pi system is alluding itself... Any such system is by definition a Sybil."
- Forking to escape court orders fails: "The miners won't follow you. Any miner seeking to follow your new chain will be in contempt of court", and capital-intensive miners are "easy to locate" — "law forms a subset of rules and miners exist to enforce, not to create, such rules".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He proceeds by definitional surgery (immutability vs assignability), enterprise-IT analogy (WORM audit databases), and institutional analysis (courts as the real distributed system; miners as capitalised, locatable, law-bound actors). White-paper textual authority is deployed — "Any needed rules and incentives can be enforced with this consensus mechanism" — alongside insider history (the 2010 alert key) to establish standing. The rhetorical mode is legal-realist: describe the enforcement machinery as it actually is, and dismiss contrary belief as "a child's dream".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts "code is law" / censorship-resistance absolutism: law is a superset of protocol rules, and miners will enforce court-ordered blacklists and UTXO reassignments.
- Contradicts immutability-as-unseizability: the ledger is an immutable audit trail of changes, including seizures — seizure resistance was never a design goal.
- Contradicts the full-node sovereignty doctrine ("your node enforces the rules"): non-mining nodes are "Sybil systems"; only proof-of-work miners are nodes, and they obey courts.
- Contradicts fork-as-exit: a fork evading court orders puts participating miners in contempt, so exit is economically and legally closed.
- Contradicts crypto-anonymity claims: "no blockchain can be made anonymous"; transparency is the feature, and AML/KYC (MLD5) compliance is the survival condition for exchanges.

# The High Priests of “crypto” and the dogma wars — core principles

- **Decentralisation is protocol fixity.** The platform is decentralised because it does not change. If Bitcoin is not a fixed protocol, it is not decentralised, and hence it is not Bitcoin.
- **A variable supply ends decentralisation.** Changing issuance requires manipulation; once that occurs, the system is no longer decentralised.
- **Bitcoin is confiscable under existing law.** When transactions are more substantial than a few hundred dollars, it is among the most straightforward systems on earth to seize; freezing orders have already been applied to holdings worldwide.
- **Bitcoin restores digital property.** Costless copying of files can be negated; Bitcoin reintroduces atomic structures of property ownership that ordinary digital systems precluded.
- **Law is enhanced, not abolished.** Bitcoin does not remove the traditional aspects of the law that have built society; it enhances them.
- **A digital signature presupposes identity.** The algorithm is not a method for creating a signature without first having an identity; centuries of common law already govern pseudonymity and identity.
- **Records must be kept.** A construction designed to lose records yields control by a few individuals rather than a public ledger.

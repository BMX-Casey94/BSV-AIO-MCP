# Cryptographic Control Is Fiduciary Power, Not Title
**Date:** 2026-01-19 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/cryptographic-control-is-fiduciary
**Subtitle:** Why custody, escrow, and exchange “gatekeeping” belongs inside orthodox trust law, fiduciary duty, and judicial supervision

## Core thesis
Control over a cryptoasset is neither ownership nor "mere mechanics" beyond law; it is gatekeeping power over another person's proprietary position, and equity has spent centuries constraining exactly such power through trust law, fiduciary duty and judicial supervision. Custody, escrow and exchange arrangements are either holding for another (trust-like) or a promise to deliver an equivalent (debt-like), and in both cases discretionary gatekeeping attracts fiduciary constraint and judicial oversight.

## Key arguments and claims
- Two doctrinal mistakes dominate: collapsing technical transfer capacity into title, or treating it as a purely technical fact beyond law. Both allow gatekeepers to present discretionary power as "ownership" or "mechanics" and thereby evade equity's vocabulary for entrusted power.
- The method is a "power map", not labels: who can sign, delay, impose conditions, redirect, commingle, control recovery, and where precisely discretion sits. Terms of service saying "you remain the owner" while reserving rights to delay, freeze, substitute, rehypothecate, net internally and refuse withdrawals are marketing, not analysis.
- Recurring architectures: single-key custody (customer dependent on an internal book entitlement; custodian can delay, refuse, condition, substitute, commingle and controls recovery); 2-of-3 multi-signature escrow (a built-in gatekeeper holding a veto); threshold/MPC custody (a committee decision concealed behind what looks on-chain like a single signature); smart contracts with admin keys (pause, upgrade, emergency withdrawal and oracle control as meta-control); exchange custody (omnibus wallets, internal ledgers, withdrawal queues, compliance holds).
- Trust classification turns on the orthodox three certainties: intention (objective — words and conduct, not labels), subject matter (identifiability supplied by records, reconciliation and defined pool boundaries; pooling does not defeat certainty), and constitution (placing the asset under the custodian's control architecture with acceptance of the undertaking).
- Commingling does not automatically mean "no trust": it may evidence that segregation was never intended, or it may be a breach of a prior undertaking to segregate — a distinction that changes remedies and priorities, especially in insolvency.
- The debt model is legitimate if candid; the illegitimate structure is the hybrid: marketing custody while reserving free use and discretionary control, monetising customer dependence without admitting it.
- Fiduciary duty is a loyalty constraint triggered by position: veto powers (delay, freeze, refuse, condition, release) are precisely the powers usable as leverage, preference or profit devices. Equity demands no conflict without authorisation, no unauthorised profit, proper purposes, and even-handed administration where multiple parties are entitled.
- Standard conflict patterns: undisclosed use or rehypothecation; profit through delay (liquidity management, fee extraction); selective processing and internal preference in pooled systems; and "compliance holds" used for liquidity, retaliation or extraction rather than genuinely lawful purposes. Partial powers ("we cannot move anything unilaterally") can be the most dangerous because easiest to deny.
- Judicial supervision is the missing doctrinal bridge: equity's jurisdiction is in personam — it supervises persons who hold powers for others, asking whether discretion was exercised in good faith, for proper purposes, on relevant considerations, without caprice or disloyal self-interest. Breadth of a power enlarges, rather than reduces, the need for proper-purpose policing.
- Remedies respond to power without rewriting code: injunctions and freezing orders against faits accomplis; information orders exposing the internal mapping between addresses, ledgers, holds and reasons; tracing into substitutes; account of profits (gain-based, apt for omnibus systems); contempt; and structural relief — substitution of trustees, receivers, supervised administration, conditioned emergency powers.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Equity's institutional logic applied through functional power analysis: strip marketing labels, map veto points and discretion, then classify the arrangement against orthodox trust and fiduciary categories. The framework is prophylactic and positional — discretion over another's proprietary position creates temptation, and equity constrains the position regardless of demonstrated bad faith.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects crypto-exceptionalism: the claim that "the chain will accept a valid signature" and courts cannot "force a key to sign" misunderstands equity, which supervises persons, not protocols.
- Rejects "trustless" and "code-enforced" rhetoric: admin keys, pause functions and upgrade powers mean many systems are discretion in a more concentrated, less visible form.
- Rejects "not your keys, not your coins" as a complete legal answer: key-holding is power, and power held for another is fiduciary, not proprietary.
- Contradicts the market convention of calling venue balances "custody" while operating them as credit: "What must not be tolerated is the comforting fiction of custody paired with the quiet reality of discretionary dominion."

## Notable quotes
- "Control over an asset is not the same thing as owning it."
- "It does not need to operate the asset. It needs to control the controller."
- "A platform cannot draft itself into a private sovereign position and then insist that the law must treat the resulting power as a mere fact."
- "Cryptographic control, properly understood, is not a new kind of title. It is a new surface on which an old problem appears: power over another’s property."

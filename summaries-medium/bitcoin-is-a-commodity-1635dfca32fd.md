# Bitcoin is a commodity
**Date:** 2018-12-12 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-is-a-commodity-1635dfca32fd
**Subtitle:** Ledger space in a sound, immutable ledger is a commodity. It has value. The trouble is that many think they can create something new. That…

## Core thesis
Money comes in exactly two forms — commodity money and state-decreed fiat — and Bitcoin, not being legal tender, "is either commodity money or a pure Ponzi". Its commodity is transaction ledger space and computation: businesses will pay to store data and contracts on an immutable ledger for compliance purposes, and that use, not exchange speculation, is what grounds Bitcoin's value. A long appended legal analysis (drawn from his earlier academic work) then argues Bitcoin already satisfies the ordinary legal meaning of "money" under Australian and US case law.

## Key arguments and claims
- Money is only ever two things: "Commodity money based on specie or some other valued exchange item" and "Fiat, that is legal tender — that which is decreed by the state". Bitcoin is not and never will be legal tender, so it must stand as commodity money or fail as "a pure Ponzi".
- The commodity underpinning Bitcoin is "the transaction ledger space and computation" — its value comes from businesses "wanting to save data and contracts in a manner that allows them to be covered under many of the US SOX provisions and many of the global laws of document retention".
- Teranode scaling pledge: "With SV, we are scaling Bitcoin to handle over 4 million transactions a second in the period between now and 2021", at "$0.0025 a transaction on average", yielding miners "over $10,000 a block". Paid-for data storage is not spam: "There is no spam on the blockchain that is paid for."
- Silk Road counterfactual: banks were ready to adopt Bitcoin — "E-Cash was used by Deutsche Bank. In 2013, we had relationships with the Commonwealth Bank in Australia. We had partnerships with Teminos and others" — but "The publicity from Silk Road stopped all of this". "If not for Silk Road, Bitcoin would be 100,000 times larger, today."
- Legal definition of money: Emmett J in *Travelex Limited v Commissioner of Taxation* [2008] FCA 1961 (at [25]) held money is "any generally accepted medium of exchange"; it need not be legal tender, and "It is common consent and conduct that gives a thing the character of money" (citing *Miller v Race* (1758) and *Moss v Hancock* [1899]). The reasoning survived appeal to the Full Federal Court and High Court.
- Money is abstract, not tangible: bank-account balances transferred electronically are plainly money, so intangibility is no bar to Bitcoin being money. Negotiability — transfer of ownership by delivery — is the key feature, and Bitcoin possesses it.
- Bitcoin is intangible personal property: quoting Dr Rhys Bollen, it "involves the circulation of valuable rights but not rights to cash as such… transferred irrevocably and immediately (albeit with delayed confirmation) by electronic order".
- US authority: in *SEC v Shavers* (E.D. Tex, №4:13-CV-416), Magistrate Judge Mazzant held Bitcoin "is a currency or form of money" for the investment-contract limb of the *Howey* test, since it buys goods and services and exchanges for conventional currencies.
- Prediction: developments in Germany, Switzerland and the UK make it "inevitable" Bitcoin "will be formally accepted as 'currency' (i.e. foreign currency) by governments around the world".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Wright argues by exhaustive dichotomy (commodity money or fiat; commodity or Ponzi) and then grounds the claim in doctrinal legal evidence — judicial definitions, dictionary entries, and case citations with pinpoint references — rather than in economic theory. The rhetorical mode is corrective: he concedes the mainstream premise that Bitcoin is "not a new form of money" only to relocate its value in ledger space as a tradeable commodity, then closes with a business-development narrative (bank partnerships lost to Silk Road, Teranode throughput) that casts utility-as-ledger as the recovery of Bitcoin's original trajectory.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "Bitcoin is a new kind of money / new asset class" framing dominant in 2018 crypto discourse: for Wright there is no third category — Bitcoin is commodity money whose commodity is ledger space, or it is a Ponzi.
- Contradicts the "digital gold / store of value first" orthodoxy: value is derived from use ("The commodity value of Bitcoin comes through use"), and "We do not care about the exchanges and day traders".
- Contradicts the small-block thesis that on-chain scaling cannot work: he commits publicly to 4 million transactions per second by 2021 at a quarter of a cent each, with miner revenue rising to $10,000 per block on fees alone.
- Contradicts the cypherpunk celebration of Silk Road as proof of censorship resistance: Silk Road was a catastrophe that cost Bitcoin the banking sector — "If it was not for Silk Road, Bitcoin would be mainstream today."
- Contradicts the "Bitcoin exists outside state law" narrative: he cites state courts (*Travelex*, *SEC v Shavers*) as the authorities that recognise Bitcoin as money.

# Free Speech
**Date:** 2019-03-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/free-speech-526a972d5fb5
**Subtitle:** Free speech is not the right to be heard on any platform but rather limitations set by governments and authorities in blocking individuals…

## Core thesis
Free speech is a negative right against government silencing, not a positive right to be heard on any platform; the real contemporary censor is the "free" ad-funded internet, whose zero-price commons lets trolls drown out the meek. The solution is to commercialise everything: Bitcoin/Metanet micropayments and escrows that make speech cost money, so honest users profit from their attackers and trolling prices itself out.

## Key arguments and claims
- Definition: "Free speech is not the right to be heard on any platform but rather limitations set by governments and authorities in blocking individuals from speaking... it is not a positive right."
- "The biggest attack on free speech right now does not come from governments" — speech is attacked "when people are drowned out" by trolls using fear and ridicule; "It is not government that is the oppressor, but rather the ignorant."
- Tragedy of the commons: the "socialist commons that people try and say is the open Internet" means "the Internet has become a sewer"; "Free is the evil within the Internet. And worse, free is incredibly expensive."
- Speech as property: "We have the right to exclude people... We have the right to our own copy... We should be able to profit for and through it" — rather than surrendering it to platforms that "sell them inefficiently for badly placed advertising".
- Platform criminality: under the DMCA and UK/EU privacy provisions, bypassing security or privacy controls is criminal; Twitter knowingly profits from bots that do so — "The people within Twitter are criminals" — and organisations "are liable when they fail to take action. Doing so is not censorship, it is protecting the rights of the copy holder."
- The solution — commercialise everything: every post transmitted "through an encrypted smart contract"; posting requires "a small micropayment and escrow a small amount of money", so "each individual who is honest can earn every time someone tries to attack him"; reputation accrues "through tokenised exchanges".
- Tiered pricing schedule: trusted parties 1/10 of a cent or a flat fee; regular associates a cent; advertisers $0.50; "Individuals you seek to block could simply be charged an exorbitant amount such as $1000 per post or link that includes you."
- Egalitarian inversion: "If you want freedom of speech, you need to remove the right for individuals to shout you down. It is not the strong or powerful that need free speech, it is the meek and those in society who cannot be heard."
- Closing aphorism: "Everything has a cost. Not all costs are monetary, but they always exist. Until people realise it, they will never be free."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Rights theory (negative vs positive liberty) fused with price mechanism: the tragedy of the commons is his master frame, and every pathology of social media is traced to a zero price on speech. He argues by definition-stipulation (what free speech "is not"), by criminal-law characterisation of platform conduct (DMCA anti-circumvention), and by mechanism design — a worked tariff schedule replacing moderation with metering. The rhetoric is populist-inverted: pricing speech is sold as protecting the meek, not the wealthy.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **Censorship resistance as crypto's core value:** the cypherpunk orthodoxy treated any friction or cost on publication as censorship; Craig makes cost the very mechanism of free speech.
- **"Information wants to be free":** the hacker-era maxim is inverted — free is "the evil within the Internet" and "incredibly expensive".
- **Government-as-primary-threat:** mainstream crypto politics centred state censorship; Craig relocates the threat to the anonymous mob and casts platforms as criminals for *not* enforcing controls.
- **Open/permissionless platforms:** against permissionless access he asserts a right to exclude and to charge differentiated prices — governance by invoice rather than by code or moderators.

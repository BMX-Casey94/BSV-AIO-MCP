# For more
**Date:** 2018-09-04 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/for-more-f10559073979
**Subtitle:** See my earlier alias

## Core thesis
Not an argumentative essay but a directory post: Wright points readers to his earlier Medium alias "adam_selene", cataloguing nineteen prior essays so that the body of work written under the pseudonym is formally associated with his public identity. The catalogue establishes that his 2018 positions — anti-SegWit, pro-contracting, security-as-economics, anti-anti-capitalist — have a longer, continuous paper trail. The post is a one-screen link list; each entry carries a one-line teaser.

## Key arguments and claims
- The post's single explicit claim is authorship continuity: "See my earlier alias" — the @adam_selene account is his.
- The catalogue spans monetary and banking economics: "The Myth of Anti-Competitive Pricing" (citing Posner 1968 on diverse markets acting "outside the whims of individual players"), "Banks versus markets" (banks and markets as "both complimentary and competing services"), "Bank Lending Decisions, Asymmetric Information, Adverse Selection, and Moral Hazard" (citing Minsky 1975/1982/1986 — "stability destabilises"), "Life-cycle Hypothesis" (micro-economic model of family spending), and "The Asian Currency Crisis" (the late-90s crisis "differed in significant ways" from standard accounts).
- Ideology critique: "Bitcoin vs The Anti-Capitalistic Mentality" — "Bitcoin has been created as a libertarian form of money. Unfortunately, many of the people currently attracted to it…" (the Misesian title signals the target: anti-capitalist sentiment inside the Bitcoin community).
- Community critique: "Genesis" — "many people within the bitcoin community are willing to accept whatever they're told blindly"; "Attacks, Slander, and Censorship" — on envy-driven attacks in unequal societies; "Fearing Companies" — "The current bitcoin debate has nothing at all to do with centralisation."
- Security economics: "Myth and Reality, Security is Really About Economics" — "the majority, who work within the information security industry hold a misconceived notion" of what security is.
- Protocol critique: "Segregated Witness or Separated Legality" (hosted on keepingstock.net), "The SegWit 15% Attack" ("Bitcoin solves the byzantine distribution problems through a probabilistic risk algorithm"), and "Nodes" — "the terminology within bitcoin has been hijacked."
- A five-part legal series: "1/5–5/5 Bitcoin and the Connection to Contracting", covering contractual formation, electronic contracting issues, "What is an 'Electronic Contract'?", remote payments, and "the postal acceptance rule and non-RBF Bitcoin".
- Historical-economics: "Tulips and Other Myths" — explicitly "Dr. Craig Wright has given me permission to repost and extend one of his blog posts from 2015", i.e. the alias acknowledging the principal even before the unmasking.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is bibliographic self-citation: rather than argue, Wright assembles a canon. The teasers show his characteristic apparatus — named academic authorities (Posner, Minsky), numbered multi-part legal treatises, and reframing moves ("security is really about economics", "the debate has nothing to do with centralisation"). Publishing the index under his own name converts pseudonymous output into attributable authority, a standard reputation-consolidation gesture.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- The linked catalogue attacks core 2017-era BTC orthodoxies: SegWit as both legally suspect ("Separated Legality") and attackable ("The SegWit 15% Attack"), and the redefinition of "node" ("the terminology within bitcoin has been hijacked").
- "Bitcoin vs The Anti-Capitalistic Mentality" and "Fearing Companies" contradict the anti-corporate, decentralisation-as-ideology strand of mainstream crypto culture.
- "Security is Really About Economics" contradicts the purely technical, cryptography-first conception of Bitcoin security dominant in developer circles.

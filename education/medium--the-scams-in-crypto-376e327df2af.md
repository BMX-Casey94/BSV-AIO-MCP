# The scams in Crypto — core principles

- **Bitcoin signs plaintext; it is not encryption.** It uses ECDSA, a digital-signature algorithm; data is sent as plain text.
- **Settlement method does not change the sale.** A promoter sells to a purchaser; that is what matters legally, not whether settlement is on a blockchain.
- **The ledger is distributed; the exchange is not.** Bitcoin is a party-to-party exchange; courts do not care how you exchange.
- **Tokenised securities are not new.** Computer tokens representing securities go back to the 1960s; blockchain adds no legal novelty that matters.
- **A utility token is used, not traded.** A bus ticket or movie ticket is a utility token; anything traded on an exchange for profit is a security.
- **Honest digital security tokens are possible.** Bonds, equities and other securities can be issued on-chain without deceiving purchasers.
- **Buying first and urging others to hold is scalping.** Promoting a token after acquiring it, aiming to sell into the rise, is legally cognisable fraud under existing advisers law — the aim to sell, not realised profit, is what matters.

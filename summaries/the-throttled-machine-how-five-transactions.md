# The Throttled Machine: How Five Transactions a Second Killed Bitcoin’s Promise
**Date:** 2025-11-10 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-throttled-machine-how-five-transactions
**Subtitle:** A dissection of digital suffocation—how BTC’s deliberate throttling to five transactions per second destroyed the very principles of freedom, scale, and ownership that once defined its creation.

## Core thesis
BTC’s five-transaction-per-second ceiling is not an engineering necessity but an engineered ideology — an artificial scarcity that transformed a peer-to-peer cash system into a rationed settlement network for custodians. The throttle destroys freedom (fees become a toll), ownership (custody returns), privacy (aggregation exposes) and economic value (velocity collapses), and only restoring massive base-layer scale can resurrect the original design.

## Key arguments and claims
- The five-TPS limit is “a restriction, not a revelation”: the white paper defined electronic cash, and Blockstream and its adherents recast constraint as principle, building a “priesthood of nodes” preaching equality through collective impotence.
- Scale arithmetic exposes the absurdity: serving one billion people making one modest transaction daily would require more than 10,000 transactions per second; five per second “is not a limitation; it is a denial of purpose”.
- The fee market functions as a tariff on financial freedom: with capacity fixed, access is auctioned, small-value payments vanish first, and “a $5 transaction with a $10 cost is not inclusion; it is mockery”.
- Rising fees are pathology, not success: “A hospital overcrowded with the sick is not evidence of health” — congestion proves collapse, not adoption.
- Custodial capture is the inevitable equilibrium: priced-out users surrender keys to exchanges and processors that batch millions into single on-chain footprints, becoming “banks in all but name — fractional, opaque, and indispensable”.
- Privacy dies by economics, not cryptography: aggregation into custodial pools concentrates users into shared addresses, the starved ledger makes each transaction “glow like a flare in the night”, and privacy becomes a purchasable service rather than a birthright.
- Lightning is “the mirage of scale”: channels must open and close through the same bottleneck, liquidity is locked capital earning nothing, payments fail from insufficient intermediate liquidity, and users become “slaves to uptime” against fraud.
- Scarcity worship inverts value: velocity is the measure of economic vitality, so a token whose price rises because it does not circulate is “a store of inertia”; the arrangement mirrors the mercantilism Adam Smith dismantled in The Wealth of Nations.
- The politics are oligarchic: invoking Carl Schmitt’s “sovereign is he who decides on the exception”, sovereignty in throttled BTC belongs to those who decide what the code cannot do — developers as theologians, custodians as merchant princes, miners as lords of computation.
- Restoration is framed as moral duty: scale dismantles permission, democratises exchange by making it “too abundant to monopolise”, and requires billions of transactions per second because humanity operates at that scale.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The essay proceeds as polemical political economy: each section takes a claimed virtue of throttled BTC (security, soundness, decentralisation, privacy) and inverts it through incentive analysis and historical analogy — medieval toll bridges, merchant guilds’ letters of credit, seventeenth-century chartered monopolies, the East India Company. The underlying framework is classical liberal economics: value arises from motion and competition, so artificial scarcity is monopoly’s tool rather than sound money’s safeguard.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the small-block security doctrine outright: “Scale is not the enemy of security but its guarantor: the more a network is used, the harder it is to control.”
- Rejects the “high fees mean success” reading of fee markets: fees are “the pulse of decay”, a transfer of wealth from the many who wish to transact to the few who control the bottleneck.
- Rejects the settlement-layer end state as progress: it is “retreat” — a private jet runway, majestic in image and useless to most.
- Rejects Lightning as a fix: “Layered dependence atop a choked foundation does not solve the problem—it institutionalises it.”
- Rejects the claim that intermediaries are optional: “A right that exists only in theory is no right at all.”
- Rejects the conflation of coin scarcity with throughput scarcity: Bitcoin’s scarcity was meant to be in supply, not in usage.

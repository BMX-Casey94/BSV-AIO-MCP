# Foreign-exchange considerations & Bitcoin — core principles

- **FX accounting bites even domestically.** "The inherently international nature of Bitcoin lends its use to the requirement to account for foreign exchange valuations. This does not even need to be associated with international transactions."
- **Currency treatment entails exchange-rate accounting.** "The treatment of bitcoin as a currency leads to the requirement to account for the exchange rates when bitcoin is purchased or exchanged for goods and services."
- **Judicial anchor for currency status.** In SEC v. Trendon T. Shavers and Bitcoin Savings and Trust, Case №4:13-CV-416 (E.D. Tex), Magistrate Judge Amos Mazzant held — applying the Howey investment-contract test — that "Bitcoin is a currency or form of money": it buys goods and services, pays living expenses, and is exchangeable for "the U.S. dollar, Euro, Yen, and Yuan"; "the only limitation of Bitcoin is that it is limited to those places that accept it as currency".
- **Cross-border use imports conflict of laws.** Use "as a global currency ... extended into the purchases of goods and services from different jurisdictions" raises the proper law of the contract — "the system of domestic law that defines the obligations assumed by the parties".
- **Express choice of law is cleanest.** "The parties may expressly choose the body of law, which will apply to all or part of their contract including offer and acceptance."
- **UK sale-of-goods detail.** The Hague Uniform Law (ULIS) applies to a sale of goods only if expressly incorporated (Art. 3; s.1(3) Uniform Laws on International Sales Act 1967); where parties are in different states, offer/acceptance analysis (Schu 1997) determines which law governs a website sale.

# Burning and why it matters that it is stopped
**Date:** 2018-10-27 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/burning-and-why-it-matters-that-it-is-stopped-2aa0af10d4d1
**Subtitle:** First, we need to note that Bitcoin is an economic system and in economics, incentives and intent matter.

## Core thesis
Intentionally burning BCH to issue Wormhole (WHC) tokens is not "backing" but a one-way destruction that Craig brands a sham and securities fraud, draining Bitcoin's utility into a separate PoS-style token. He distinguishes lost keys (recoverable in time, economically neutral) from burned keys (deliberate destruction, value-diminishing), and argues Bitcoin's fixed integer supply of satoshis is constitutive of the system — alter it and "it is no longer Bitcoin". The essay is a pre-hash-war broadside against Bitmain-backed Wormhole on BCH.

## Key arguments and claims
- Lost keys are not burned keys: RIPEMD-160's 160-bit hash space yields to computation at "around 6.7 bits of information" per decade with "around 70 bits of information free" now, so "we could expect to see collisions (without a mathematical flaw) in just 40 to 60 years" — a lost coin is "the Spanish gold and silver sunk for a long time to the ocean floor, but one day to be recovered".
- Loss does not diminish Bitcoin as money: "If bitcoin was a global currency, then lost bitcoins only mean a loss for the individual… and a gain in the future for the proverbial bitcoin treasure hunter."
- Burning invokes Bastiat's Parable of the Broken Window — destruction does not create; a gold note cannot be backed "by taking gold and throwing it into an active volcano". WHC's claim to be "backed" by destroyed BCH is therefore "a SHAM or a common 'bait and switch'".
- "There are no decimal points in Bitcoin": wallets display a visualisation; the protocol transacts "a fixed-format integer value" of 10⁸ satoshi. The hex of 21 million bitcoin is 0x000775F05A074000, and IEEE 754 floating point has no place — "Bitcoin is a coin, a token system, it is atomic."
- Adding precision would mean adding tokens, a consensus-rule change that destroys identity: "altering the number of individual tokens in Bitcoin means it is no longer Bitcoin", like retyping Hamlet or amending the US Constitution's text. "It is a shame so few 'developers' in Bitcoin understand the system they are working with."
- Toy model of the burn dynamic: 1,000 B1 against 10,000 W1; as B1 is destroyed for W1, "the B1 token becomes less and less liquid, and the system drives towards only W1 tokens" — BCH reduced to "a settlement system for Wormhole. Basically, we see BTC and Lightning all over."
- Scarcity alone is not value: "It is the Ricardian value fallacy that was used by Marx at work." "That myth, the HOLD lie, it needs to end." Value comes from "a limited scarcity that is not diluted", not from destroying supply — destroying 10 million BCH would diminish, not raise, its value.
- Legal charge: Wormhole's "backing" claim is "a common securities fraud"; "False and misleading statements are not only civil offences, they are criminal in nature", and he fingers Bitmain directly ("It will not be the first by Bitmain").
- Notes define the sham doctrine and classify ICO tokens as share, debenture or unit; "the courts will investigate the substance of the transaction", not its label. A further note argues satoshi precision cannot be extended: at most "a doubling or, at the utmost, an 8x increase in possible tokens" exists in the format.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He layers three registers: cryptographic estimation (bit-security decay of RIPEMD-160), protocol literalism (the integer/hex anatomy of a transaction output as proof of what Bitcoin "is"), and economic-legal argument (Bastiat, Ricardian value theory, sham-transaction doctrine). Analogies do heavy lifting — sunken bullion, Hamlet's text, the Constitution — each enforcing his essentialist claim that Bitcoin has a fixed identity which code changes annihilate. The rhetorical mode is polemical: named adversaries (Bitmain, Wormhole, "developers paid by a promoter") and fraud accusations grounded in securities law.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against the "lost coins are a donation to everyone" / scarcity-is-value orthodoxy (HODL economics): he insists value requires undiluted usable supply, and that lost coins eventually return via hash-space erosion — heresy to the "digital gold, 21M ever scarcer" narrative.
- Against the burn-to-bootstrap token model (proof-of-burn, Wormhole, and by extension any L2 that parks the base asset): burning is fraud and economic sabotage, not innovation.
- Against "code is law / we can just change the protocol" developer culture: protocol parameters are constitutive like a novel's text; changing supply or precision produces "ETH, BTC, or some other shitcoin poor copy of Bitcoin" — notably he classes BTC itself as the altered copy.
- Against Lightning-style layering: the burn dynamic is explicitly equated with BTC+Lightning, where the base coin degrades into a mere settlement layer for a separate transactional token.

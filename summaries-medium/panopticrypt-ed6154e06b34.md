# Panopticrypt
**Date:** 2019-04-07 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/panopticrypt-ed6154e06b34
**Subtitle:** I started the company Panopticrypt in June 2011. At this stage, I was still deep in disputes with the Australian tax office…

## Core thesis
Through the story of his 2011 company Panopticrypt, Craig defines Bitcoin as a panoptic-yet-cryptic ledger — "a ledger that shows the whole while becoming cryptic enough to allow the system to be private" — and argues that society sits on a knife's edge between surveillance and privacy, where only an incentive-based, lawful system like Bitcoin (not anonymity or anarchy) can disincentivise corruption. The essay interleaves this with memoir: lost friendships, sacrificed companies, and a defence of his corporate record-keeping against forgery allegations.

## Key arguments and claims
- Panopticrypt was founded June 2011 amid ATO disputes; his 2009 companies Information Defense and Integers were "beyond saving", and "it was the intellectual property I fought to keep, not the money" — the same motive as his nChain-era battles over control, including against people "wanting to research Ethereum and other coins and other consensus mechanisms".
- The name fuses "panoptic" ("showing or seeing the whole at one view") with "crypt" (cryptic, puzzle, secret): Bitcoin is a ledger that shows the whole while staying private.
- He promises to demonstrate "a perfect hash puzzle" at the CoinGeek conference in May — one where "individuals can select any output address, yet simultaneously create a secure hash puzzle", something "that could have been done with my original code in 2009".
- Via Foucault's *Discipline and Punish* and Panopticism: "Incentives control everything we do, and when applied correctly, economics allows us to create a system that evolves toward something better"; fitness means adaptation to one's system, not power (Herbert Spencer "takes such a control concept too far").
- "Anarchy is not an option. There is no privacy in anarchy" — without law, "the strong, the powerful, and the wilful survive and take control"; the Panopticon's central review is the other failure mode, and "Bitcoin changes the balance" by making corruption and fraud costly rather than impossible: "Seeking anonymity removes such strength from Bitcoin."
- Friendship memoir: Dave (Kleiman) was "perhaps one of the very few people I think I could ever keep trusting"; Peter Robinson, a "damn good coder" at his early company DeMorgan ("of the Morgan"), was lost because "if you want your business to be successful, your friendship has to take a backseat".
- He started Strasan in 2011 — the name a nod to the Strassen algorithm and its asymptotic complexity — but partners wanted something different; he casts himself as Achilles choosing immortal achievement over a quiet life, and calls Bitcoin/blockchain "just a Lego block... needed in the creation" of "the Metanet".
- On the forgery claims: he doesn't do his own accounts, filings or LinkedIn; company secretarial services handle shares and filings, so errors in documents are clerical, not malicious — one circulating "document" is a compilation where "it calls for appendix a. Following it is appendix 1... The fonts change... nine pages in total in the purported seven-page document".
- "We now have more patents in play than any of the large global players, and going forward, my intellectual property... is going to determine the path taken by many aspects of the world for years to come."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues by etymology and by analogy to social philosophy (Foucault's Panopticon, Spencer's social evolution), recasting Bitcoin's transparency-versus-privacy design as an incentive mechanism for ordering society. The method is characteristically autobiographical-apologetic: company histories and friendship casualties are marshalled as evidence that his motives were always intellectual property and long-term creation, while documentary anomalies are explained by the mundane chaos of delegated administration.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the anonymity imperative of cypherpunk orthodoxy: privacy is preserved through law and pseudonymity on a visible ledger, and "seeking anonymity removes such strength from Bitcoin" — directly against CoinJoin/mixer culture and privacy coins.
- Rejects the anti-state, anarchy-leaning politics of mainstream crypto: "Anarchy is not an option", and law is the precondition of privacy rather than its enemy.
- Against the "blockchain is the revolution" hype: Bitcoin is "just a Lego block" for the Metanet, and alt-coin/consensus experimentation (Ethereum) is a distraction he fought inside nChain.
- Against the decentralised-flat-organisation ideal: he defends hierarchical corporate structures, delegated filings and patent accumulation as the proper vehicle for the technology.

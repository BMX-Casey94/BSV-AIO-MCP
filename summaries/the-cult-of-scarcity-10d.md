# The Cult of Scarcity
**Date:** 2025-12-08T04:58:34.267Z | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-cult-of-scarcity-10d
**Subtitle:** How a Five-Transaction Relic Becomes an Economic Parasite

## Core thesis
BTC as presently constituted is not a monetary system but a speculative extraction device. Its throughput ceiling of about five transactions per second excludes it from civilisational payment use, leaving price appreciation as its only demonstrated "utility" — structurally a greater-fool transfer from later entrants to earlier holders, not the creation of wealth. Because BTC consumes real resources while producing no commensurate output, enthroning it as society's base layer would cannibalise production, burn energy to preserve scarcity, and manufacture a microscopic rentier aristocracy.

## Key arguments and claims
- Section I sets the first principle: wealth is productive capacity, not a price chart — "the acre that feeds ten where it once fed one". "Creation is the mother of civilisation; transfer is only its scavenger." A speculative rise with no underlying capacity is "a transfer of claims, a reshuffling of spoils".
- Section II reads the five-transactions-per-second limit as "an economic verdict". Money is "a communications channel for value"; a narrow channel either prices out all but the rich or drives users away. Layer-2 and custodial shims are displacement, not scaling: "A monetary substrate that needs scaffolding to function has admitted, in the only language that matters, that it does not function."
- Section III describes BTC's lived reality: hoarding as virtue, exchange churn as surrogate trade, price-watching as liturgy. "If a thing cannot be used to buy bread, it must be used to buy belief." Volatility is fuel, not bug: no stable commercial demand exists to dampen swings.
- Section IV gives the greater-fool arithmetic: a non-productive asset yields no cashflow, so earlier holders' profit is sourced entirely from later holders' expenditure. "There is no third well from which value is drawn. There is only the queue." Universal adoption fails by logic — if everyone is already in, no buyer base remains to push the price.
- Section V, the energy sink: each watt burned is a watt not burned elsewhere; BTC raises opportunity cost across the whole economy while clearing almost nothing. "The energy is not buying throughput; it is buying scarcity." Miners "spend [electricity] to preserve a bottleneck, then sell the bottleneck as destiny".
- Section VI, capital misallocation: when hoarding is rewarded as genius, "hoarding becomes the national vocation". "Every pound locked in sterile scarcity is a pound not at work in fertile enterprise." The damage is temporal — the unbuilt bridge, the unformed startup, the laboratory running on fumes.
- Section VII runs the civilisational stress test of "BTC world" and finds four fractures: scale (intermediaries return "fewer, larger, and with greater leverage"); incentive (hoarding becomes the central economic posture); resource cost (appreciation intensifies mining, mining raises energy prices, costs shrink real output, making appreciation yet more central); and investment decay.
- Section VIII, hyper-inequality by design: gains accrue by entry order and scale, producing "the top 0.1% of the 0.1%". Low throughput guarantees high fees, "a tax whose burden rises as one's purse shrinks". "The system is not a ladder for the many. It is a moat for the few."
- Sections IX–X: BTC-world means less wealth even as the chart soars — "One might as well applaud a fever because the thermometer is impressive." Money is "function first, faith as residue"; a system demanding belief to compensate for incapacity is "a vacancy dressed as destiny".
- Section XI states the positive criteria for real digital cash: base-layer on-chain scaling; low fees achieved "by letting supply meet demand"; competitive block creation as "a service industry for commerce"; circulation encouraged rather than withholding sanctified.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Classical political economy — productive capacity, opportunity cost, rent-seeking, capital allocation — joined to a reductio counterfactual (grant BTC its ambitions, then trace the fractures mechanically). The greater-fool analysis is a counting argument about where profit comes from; the energy section appeals to physical arithmetic over slogans. The rhetoric is moral inversion: BTC "reverses the moral polarity of economic life", making the builder the fool and the sitter the sage.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Store-of-value doctrine: an asset severed from monetary duty mutates into a pure speculative instrument; "digital gold" is a halo maintained "by repetition, not evidence".
- Layer-2 scaling: a crippled base rescued by prosthetics means "the external structure becomes the system in practice", reviving fiat-style intermediaries.
- Fee-market security: high fees are a regressive tollbooth, "not a by-product; it is the business model".
- HODL ethics: rewarding stillness starves the slow miracles of factories, research and infrastructure.
- "Money is mostly faith": function precedes belief; trust accrues "the way a road earns traffic".

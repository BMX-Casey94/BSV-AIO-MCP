# Pennies and Power: How Micropayments Could Break the Corporate Siege — core principles

- **The transaction floor is a commercial artefact, not a technical necessity.** Card networks were built for high-value, low-frequency payments; a flat $0.25 processor fee on a one-cent payment "is not offering a service — it's killing the transaction outright".
- **Micropayments restore contestable capitalism.** Capitalism rests on contestability and open entry; corporatism rests on moats — compliance costs, minimum fees, bundling and gatekeeper control of audience access. Fraction-of-a-cent direct settlement bypasses the tollbooths.
- **The scale arithmetic works without advertising or subscriptions.** 0.5 cents per article read across a million readers yields $5,000 — earned with no behavioural dossiers and no surrender of user privacy; platforms commonly take "30% or more" of a creator's earnings instead.
- **Granular pricing lets the price mechanism work at the level of individual interactions.** An article at 2 cents, a song stream at 5 cents, a code-snippet licence at 0.8 cents — pay-per-use dissolves subscription lock-in because switching becomes painless.
- **Settlement must be real, not promised.** Off-chain credit networks requiring watchtowers, pooled liquidity and custodial hubs deliver "promises to pay, not payments"; true micropayments demand on-chain finality at fraction-of-a-cent fees on the base layer.
- **Compliance should be tiered with de minimis thresholds.** One-size-fits-all AML/KYC that treats $0.02 like $200,000 is a structural obstacle; customs-style de minimis thresholds and proportionate oversight are the remedy.
- **Builder guidance.** Build on high-throughput, low-fee base-chain rails, using payment channels to batch settlement where appropriate; price at the granularity of single interactions and route around any rail whose fee floor exceeds the value moved.

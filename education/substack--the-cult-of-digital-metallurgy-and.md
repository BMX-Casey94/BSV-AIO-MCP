# The Cult of Digital Metallurgy — core principles

- **The system was meant to carry value, not to imitate a bullion vault.** Satoshi's correspondence mocked the gold-or-nothing response: critics so used to being anti-fiat that anything short of gold was not good enough, conceding flammability while denying there would ever be a spark.
- **Tokenisation is the core, not a flourish.** The ledger was intended to represent and map assets — fiat, gold, commodities, financial instruments — at a scale no existing mechanism could approach: an economic engine rather than a museum display.
- **Backing is the spark.** "Once it's backed with cash, that might change" identifies representation of cash reserves and other assets as fulfilment of the design, not heresy against it.
- **The continuity is in the act of representation, not in the metal.** Gold stores value because society agreed it could; Bitcoin was designed to represent value because the architecture made it possible. Gold's relevance is historical, not metaphysical.
- **Legitimacy comes from capacity to represent and move value at scale.** Scarcity worship mistakes the museum piece for the engine; a fetish of scarcity was never the constraint the design enshrined.
- **The future belongs to function.** Ledgers that scale, instruments that tokenise, mechanisms that actually function — systems capable of carrying the weight of global economic activity.

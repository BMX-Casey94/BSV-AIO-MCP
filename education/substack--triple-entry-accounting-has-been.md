# Triple-Entry Accounting Has Been Misunderstood — core principles

- **The third entry is evidence, not a published set of books.** It is a shared, tamper-evident, selectively disclosable object that binds invoices, signatures, commitments and time of publication so neither side can later alter the record undetected.
- **Controlled verifiability, not transparency.** Publishing every invoice, price, counterparty and credit term would be corporate self-harm. A firm commits to fields without exposing them and reveals only what an auditor needs for a specific assertion.
- **Double-entry is not replaced.** Charts of accounts, subledgers, ERP controls and revenue recognition remain. The third entry is evidence about the accounting record, not the record itself.
- **Cryptography proves facts, not commercial substance.** It can prove a key signed a message, disclosed data matches a commitment, a record existed when anchored, and two records are linked. It cannot prove delivery, performance, economic substance or honest management.
- **Public anchoring needs an admissible profile.** Append-only or tamper-evident, durably available for the retention period, independently verifiable ordering, a documented finality model, and no dependence on one vendor's promise — otherwise the evidence has merely moved databases.
- **A payment note is not cash receipt.** Payer-created records prove intention, not settlement. Final evidence requires payee acknowledgement, bank evidence or payment-rail confirmation.
- **Privacy is inference-limited.** Contents and linkage can be hidden; timing, amount ranges, publication frequency, batching and fee patterns still leak. The cryptographic layer cannot repeal inference.
- **Fraud is repriced, not abolished.** It shifts from altering the books quietly to building a consistent false evidentiary structure in advance — harder, more expensive, more coordinated.

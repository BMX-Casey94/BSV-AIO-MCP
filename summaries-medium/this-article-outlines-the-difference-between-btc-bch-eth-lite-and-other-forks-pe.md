# This article outlines the difference between btc, bch, eth, lite and other forks PERFECTLY.
**Date:** 2020-02-14 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/this-article-outlines-the-difference-between-btc-bch-eth-lite-and-other-forks-perfectly-60fae54c8d89
**Subtitle:** btc and bch are going to be in legal trouble — specifically their miners who retain the original pre-forked database. CSW's argument is…

## Core thesis
A short endorsement of the previous day's "Forking and Passing Off…", written in the third person about Craig ("CSW's argument is solid, and it shows he's been studying law for quite some time") — it reads as a supporter's comment that he reposted or amplified on his own Medium. Its claim: btc and bch, and specifically "their miners who retain the original pre-forked database", are heading for legal trouble, because what BTC did was not a clean fork but the retention of someone else's data under someone else's name.

## Key arguments and claims
- The Salesforce/Oracle analogy: had Marc Benioff forked Oracle's open-source database under an MIT licence, that would be "FINE" — but what btc did is the equivalent of Benioff keeping Larry Ellison's data in the forked database and then naming his own company Oracle.
- Passing off inverts victim and perpetrator: because "naming votes [were] decided by exchanges", the original was forced to rebrand — rendered as Ellison being "forced to rename his company… 'Oracle Larry's Vision'" (a jab at the "Bitcoin Satoshi's Vision" backronym).
- "At no point in time did the inventor of Bitcoin sanction this; in fact, he fought BOTH forks tooth-and-nail to no avail." — i.e. both the 2017 BTC and 2018 BCH splits were opposed by the creator.
- The legally exposed parties are named precisely: not just developers but "miners who retain the original pre-forked database".
- The apparent harshness is ordinary corporate practice: "those people ignore what would seem obvious in any other corporate context."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The post reasons by analogy transplant: relocating the fork scenario into a familiar corporate setting (Oracle/Salesforce) to make the passing-off claim intuitively obvious rather than legally technical. Notably, Craig publishing third-person praise of his own argument is itself a rhetorical device — manufacturing the appearance of independent validation for the database-rights theory advanced in his own name the day before.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- "Forks are legitimate, permissionless governance": reframed as corporate identity theft — keeping the data *and* the name is the offence, not the code fork.
- Exchange ticker authority: the de facto practice of exchanges assigning the BTC ticker by market consensus is treated as an illegitimate "naming vote" that dispossessed the original.
- Miner neutrality: miners are cast as custodians of the copied database and hence personally exposed — against the "miners just run software" defence.

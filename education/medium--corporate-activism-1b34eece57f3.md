# Corporate Activism — core principles

- **Fungibility is a legal status, not a cryptographic property.** Money is fungible not through technology but as a result of law; a transaction knowingly received from an illegal path makes the recipient the receiver of illegal property and taints anything in the chain of transactions with the same loss of fungibility.
- **Protocol features that enable anonymity create liability contagion.** An opcode designed to allow anonymous or criminal use (named as DSV / OP_CHECKDATASIG) would make miners liable, exchanges liable, and anyone who ever has a transaction with that construct in the path liable.
- **Miners are the shareholders.** Hosting platforms lose their "mere publisher" protection as soon as they receive notification of illegal content; a site that restores taken-down illegal data is guilty of a crime — the same notice-and-takedown logic attaches to those who process the ledger.
- **Bitcoin is not permissionless.** You have no rights to violence; activism beyond boycott or lawful competition has no standing to dictate how a company acts while it remains within the law.
- **Protocol integrity is defended by hash and then by law.** A change designed to make Bitcoin criminal-friendly is opposed first by hash power and then by legal process — anarchy and violence are not the design.
- **No group may dictate corporate conduct beyond exit and competition.** No group or individual has the right to say how a company should act other than to not use that company or to compete with it, so long as the company is acting within the law.

# The myth of forks — core principles

- **There are no forks in Bitcoin.** A protocol change is the birth of a new cryptocurrency that copied the ledger. "There are no splits, there is a demerger process when all nodes agree, and where they don't, there is the original protocol." There is no democratic voting within money.
- **Bitcoin was never anti-bank or anti-government.** It is "a sound system that produces an evidence trail and incentivises good behaviour." Money evolves where marketability is observable — requiring a pseudonymous, not anonymous, system. Users validate the source of their own funds; that "does not mean... they need to run a node."
- **The law of tracing grounds property in money.** Blinded cash failed because it could not be traced. Multiple ledgers let organisations keep multiple sets of books — "as soon as you can start moving between Bitcoin and some sidechain or alternate chain, you have the ability to construct elaborate schemes such as those run by Bernie Madoff and Enron."
- **Orphans are designed-in economic signalling.** Mining "is not about finding a block, it is about ensuring that all other mining nodes know that you have found a block." A slow node propagating large blocks loses effective hash rate and must upgrade or go bankrupt — the block limit becomes "an economic function" without central planning.
- **Difficulty resets make the subsidy a zero-sum game.** Every 2016 blocks, orphans are excluded from the two-week total, so extra work skews reward towards "large well connected data farms," not home systems — a multi-leader Stackelberg game driving a small-world network of competing corporate data centres.
- **Nodes are miners.** "There is no such thing as a validating node. Validation is done through the creation of blocks." Subsidising home users "remove[s] the security of Bitcoin."
- **Fees should be driven as low as possible.** No artificial fee market is needed; free transactions belong in every block, and competition bankrupts inefficient nodes.
- **One chain, not special-purpose ledgers.** There are no special-purpose blockchains "in the same way that there is no such thing as a special-purpose Internet." Tokenised assets cannot exist on two chains at once.

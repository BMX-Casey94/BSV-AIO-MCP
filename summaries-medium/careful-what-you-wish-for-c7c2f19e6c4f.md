# Careful what you wish for…
**Date:** 2019-02-08 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/careful-what-you-wish-for-c7c2f19e6c4f
**Subtitle:** I moved to the UK in October 2015. We still had business interests there, so I would fly to and from Australia but my home was already to…

## Core thesis
A confessional Satoshi-authorship essay: Craig recounts his 2015 move to the UK and the 2016 PR-staged "coming out" (the turtleneck he calls "a poor copy of Steve Jobs"), then argues that Bitcoin was designed as lawful, private-but-traceable electronic cash — never as anonymous money for Silk Road — and that BTC Core's 1 MB cap, SegWit and Lightning are a deliberate subversion to build "Silk Road 3.0". He refuses the "sign and we will believe you" demand as a bad contract negotiation, credits Hal Finney's help, and closes with the flat claim "I was Satoshi."

## Key arguments and claims
- The 2016 reveal was stage-managed by PR people who dressed him as a marketable genius; his flaw was lacking "the courage to stop and say no… at (close to) the worst possible time". He prefers suits: "with wealth comes the right to decide".
- "Bitcoin was never designed to help an anonymous money-transfer system, and I was always opposed to those seeking to operate outside the law." He cites licensed-gambling work (Centrebet, helping launch Lasseter's Online in the 90s) as within-the-law credentials.
- "Ross Ulbricht and others like him are criminals. They are not freedom fighters, they are not libertarians. They simply are predators, and they are all that Bitcoin was designed to make far more difficult." He also dislikes Wikileaks and Assange's methods.
- "Bitcoin cannot scale as a 'home-user' system, and as such is incredibly simply to subvert"; he will release subversion techniques extending pre-Bitcoin work [1,2,3].
- "Any blockchain is able to be controlled and made to work within the legal frameworks of where it exists. It does not stop government taxing, and it does not bring down banks."
- Privacy/tracing duality: a blockchain is "incredibly private when the people using it remain within the limits of the law" yet "incredibly simple to trace" once the law is breached — "a complete audit trail".
- DigiCash history lesson: founded 1989 on an anonymous blinded-transaction model with a central clearing company detecting double spends; its liquidation killed eCash. Bitcoin solves double-spending with "a distributed and competitive group of miners" so no single party's failure takes the system down. "It was never about all users running nodes, and it is certainly not about equality or taking down the establishment."
- "Chaumian eCash can be implemented inside Bitcoin script. I know it well; I have patented it" — anonymity layers are possible but Bitcoin itself avoids eCash's regulatory fatal flaw.
- Silk Road's fall proves design intent: the immutable evidence trail let authorities chain from Silk Road to Silk Road 2.0 to the corrupt FBI agent. "Bitcoin is sunshine. It is the cure for the festering wound that is corruption." He "was not afraid of Gavin… when he met with the CIA".
- Origin claim: the design of Bitcoin/Metanet "started in 1998 with a project I called Blacknet", inspired by Tim May but rejecting May's no-government vision as blind to human nature. "There is no anarchist solution in any blockchain. I have ensured it… the more you try to make something anonymous (rather than pseudonymous), the more it can be controlled… the more you seek to be like Zcash or some other crime coin, the more privacy you give up."
- Lightning section: "The only reason for it stems from the collapse of Silk Road 2.0. The creation of off-chain channels that allow information to be deleted is designed for one and only one reason: to avoid law. It is a vain attempt to create Silk Road 3.0." Hence Core "capped Bitcoin at 1.0 MB" and "added SegWit and other completely ignorant and insecure changes".
- On signing with early keys: it is like being asked to show "my bank statement"; information asymmetry is consideration in "a contract negotiation, and you have little or nothing I desire". "Gavin knows I have the early keys." If he signed, "the next attack is simple: I stole the keys" — there is "no path from PoSM (proof of social media) to acceptance for me".
- Credit and claim: "Without Hal, the code would have crashed time after time… Bitcoin started because of my ideas. It was my design, and it is my creation… I was Satoshi."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Autobiographical apologia fused with design-intent argument: he uses personal narrative (the staged 2016 reveal, his forensics career, Blacknet's 1998 origin) as evidence of authorship and of purpose, then reads Bitcoin's properties (immutability, pseudonymity, competitive miners) backwards from that purpose. The rhetorical mode is moral inversion — the privacy/crime framing is flipped so that anonymity is the threat and the audit trail the virtue — plus a contract-theoretic defence of refusing to sign (asymmetric information as bargaining chip).

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the cypherpunk orthodoxy that Bitcoin is an anti-state, censorship-resistant tool: he insists it "does not stop government taxing, and it does not bring down banks" and was "never designed" as an anarchist system.
- Contradicts the "verify, don't trust / sign to prove" demand of 2016-2019 crypto culture: he recasts key-signing as a privacy violation and a rigged negotiation ("PoSM — proof of social media").
- Contradicts the scaling orthodoxy that the 1 MB cap and SegWit were prudent engineering and Lightning a scaling solution: he casts all three as a criminal-motivated subversion to make records deletable.
- Contradicts the "Free Ross" libertarian martyr narrative head-on: Ulbricht is a "predator", and Silk Road's collapse is cited as proof Bitcoin works as designed.
- Contradicts the privacy-coin thesis (Zcash/Monero): more anonymity yields more controllability and less privacy, by his design claim.

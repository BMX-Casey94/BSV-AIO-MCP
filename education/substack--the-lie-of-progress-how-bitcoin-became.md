# The Lie of Progress — core principles

- **Money is motion.** A system that cannot be spent changes nothing; gold failed not because it lacked value but because it lacked motion — too rigid for everyday payments. The miracle was never scarcity; it was flow.
- **The one-megabyte cap was a temporary safety valve.** It was set when blocks barely filled a tenth of it; Merkle trees are designed for scale, not scarcity, and the infrastructure for terabyte blocks has existed for years.
- **Paid data is commerce, not spam.** The white paper's spam was unpaid data; the moment a miner is paid, the concept of spam vanishes.
- **Miners are the real nodes.** A passive archive checks nothing and decides nothing; it merely syncs and submits. Hash power, not pull requests, is the economic vote.
- **Off-chain channels reconstruct banking.** Unverifiable balances, liquidity providers as middlemen and institutionalised trust turn the base chain into a settlement ledger for institutions, not individuals. Every off-chain settlement buries the cash principle one layer deeper.
- **Artificial scarcity centralises.** Power flows to those with liquidity and access — exchanges, custodians and wrappers. A wrapped coin is fully visible, easily frozen and seamlessly taxed: peer-to-platform, not peer-to-peer.
- **High fees are failure, not security.** Limit the block, inflate the fees, and users are forced into custodial channels; stagnation does the work of a coup.
- **The architecture can still scale.** The code holds the potential for millions of transactions per second; the failure is of will, not of technology. Scale it, or lose it.

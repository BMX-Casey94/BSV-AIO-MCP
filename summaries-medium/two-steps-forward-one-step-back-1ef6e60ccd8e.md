# Two steps forward, one step back
**Date:** 2019-04-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/two-steps-forward-one-step-back-1ef6e60ccd8e
**Subtitle:** All progress comes through a path of resistance. To understand this, we need to understand that a status quo will push back when you...

## Core thesis
Framed by a dialectic in which every technological advance is met by a status-quo counter-revolution before the two merge into "shared evolution", Craig uses the essay to "own his past" as Satoshi: he narrates running the entire early Bitcoin network on his own Windows server clusters, the fortune it cost him, his tax-office battles, and his infiltration of criminal networks that exploited Bitcoin. He concludes that truth and identity cannot be established by "code is law" key-signing but only through law, process, and slowly presented verifiable evidence.

## Key arguments and claims
- Progress follows a revolt/revolution/pushback cycle: "all change moves through a series of revolts and revolutions", and after the status quo pushes back, "the status quo and those that seek a new order merge and mingle" in a shared evolution.
- He claims the first Bitcoin version after the genesis block "fell over": genesis is dated 3 January 2009, the first mined block 9 January 2009, and the gap was spent fixing his infrastructure — the biggest problem being "Microsoft patch Tuesday" (MS09-001), because all machines were configured to the same time zone and patched simultaneously, so "the entire Bitcoin network stopped following the genesis block, and needed to be started again".
- He ran 67 Windows machines in a cluster (XP / Server 2003, later a Windows 2008 domain forest) across his Bagnoo ranch, a Sydney garage, a Melbourne server, and two churches (one "just outside of California", one in Tumbi Umbi), having paid for fibre, servers, licences and connections personally.
- Only "around 10 machines, maybe 12, mined Bitcoin between 2009 and 2011" — Dave (Kleiman) ran one full-time, Hal Finney ran one, "Bear" ran one or two — while he personally ran 60–100 machines (75 his at the start); the churches/charities he donated machines to may have collected "between 80,000 and 100,000 bitcoin".
- The venture cost him dearly: "All up, I spent around AU$1.1 million", with ~70% of 2007–mid-2009 costs attributable to Bitcoin, a mortgage that "blew up to around AU$1.4 million" across three properties, forcing the sale of his ranch.
- The name Satoshi has a "secondary part": Satoshi Sugiyama, adopted by an American and renamed David Phillips, from the book *The House of Morgan* — "David Kleiman was adopted, and so was Satoshi"; he admired the book's "more glorious and honourable period in banking".
- He admits entanglement with criminal infrastructure: AnonymousSpeech.com, Vistomail ("Canadian pharma spam"), High Secured and Liberty Reserve; criminals used Bitcoin transactions as a covert signalling system for botnet command-and-control ("the systems watched the Bitcoin blockchain and gained instructions without leaving any trace"), and he "placed back doors in them", ran TCPdump collection, and "both held money on the servers and helped get rid of them".
- His ATO dispute (running to January 2013) liquidated his two companies, cost over $1 million in legal fees and ~$3 million overall, and included a failed ASIC attempt to ban him as a director for 20 years; it ended with the ATO forced to "accept that Dr. Wright did take reasonable care", all claims barred bar $77 — offset by a $10,000 deduction he had under-claimed.
- He would have patented Bitcoin but could not: "unlike copyright, a patent requires that the real name is used", and he was "too afraid of the consequences".
- Truth "is not derived from a code-is-law perspective": "You cannot sign truth with the digital key... evidence isn't a mere signing of a key" — he will prove his authorship slowly, through legal process and "verifiable evidence", not a performative key signature.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The essay blends a Hegelian-flavoured philosophy of change (revolt → revolution → synthesis) with confessional autobiography used as evidentiary narrative: dates, patch numbers (MS09-001), server counts, dollar figures and named individuals are deployed as circumstantial proof of authorship. Rhetorically it is both mea culpa and threat — he owns disreputable episodes (spam networks, back doors) to pre-empt their use against him, while insisting that legal process, not cryptographic theatre, is the only valid tribunal of identity.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the "just sign a key" orthodoxy: for mainstream crypto, a Satoshi proof is a cryptographic signature; Craig declares a signature proves nothing and that courts and evidence law are the proper venue — a direct inversion of code-is-law epistemology.
- Attacks the HODLer/speculator culture: "I find speculators and 'HODLers' to be perverse", and "If you think Bitcoin was ever just about owning a lot of bitcoin, you're more of a fool than you imagine" — against the 2019 store-of-value narrative.
- Calls BTC "SegWit Coin" and "that fake copy of Bitcoin", refusing the ticker-equals-identity consensus; Bitcoin's security is "a stable protocol and an economically incentivised system", not community governance.
- Contradicts the cypherpunk origin myth of Bitcoin as a tool for anarchy: he presents himself helping dismantle Liberty Reserve-era criminal networks and insists "law is important, and so is process".

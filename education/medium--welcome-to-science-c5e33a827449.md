# Welcome to science — core principles

- **Original Bitcoin is the null hypothesis.** Any proposed attack model or protocol change must be tested against the system as originally designed, not against its own mathematical model.
- **A valid hypothesis must explain, predict, and enable control.** It needs explanatory power with minimal assumptions, predictive success beyond existing belief, and the ability to forecast effects of growth and scale — and it must be falsifiable without appeals to authority.
- **Test models against the live network.** Running nodes and measuring how they react is the test; comparing a mathematical model to another mathematical model is not science.
- **Seek evidence before claiming an attack.** For claims such as selfish mining, the failure is not only that there is no evidence — no evidence was ever sought.
- **State H0, the alternative, α, data, statistic, rejection regions, then conclude.** Worked tests include whether selfish miners can gain a significant, detectable profit advantage, and whether a proposed block-ordering is faster than Bitcoin's default order.
- **Distinguish reliability, precision and accuracy.** Repeatability, variance around the mark, and closeness to the true value are separate; every claim about Bitcoin must link to a measurable, quantifiable metric.
- **One false theory cannot explain another.** Malleability-based theft claims and similar constructions fail when they rest on an already-untested premise.
- **Qualitative unmeasurability is not a defence.** Those who say they cannot measure the results of a system change are promoting an agenda without evidence.
- **Scaling and payment-channel proposals are hypotheses too.** A second-layer construction must be shown to work at scale under anti-money-laundering law, record-keeping and competition — not asserted from a model.

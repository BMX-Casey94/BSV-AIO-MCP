# Bitcoin is not against banks — core principles

- **Bitcoin does not stop banks or even central banks.** It was never an attack on banking; central banks themselves began on a one-to-one gold reserve that later slipped into fractional reserve.
- **Custodial holding already is banking.** There is little difference between bitcoin held with a hosted exchange and the modern banking system, so an anti-bank narrative is incoherent.
- **The value is a single global open ledger.** Banks, governments and corporations cannot fudge the numbers.
- **Split-key custody can protect hosted funds.** A partial-seed system (Shamir secret sharing and ECDSA, with dedicated hardware RNG) means that if the operator is hacked, the funds cannot be moved because no one party holds all seed parts.
- **Security is atomic, not promised.** Even with the architecture known and internals compromised, security lies in the building blocks of transactions and keys, not in obscurity or assurances.
- **The network strengthens through use, not hoarding.** Day-to-day payments that pay fees to miners make Bitcoin stronger; holding idle does not.
- **Propagation is fast; settlement is a block.** Transactions take fractions of a second to propagate and roughly ten minutes to settle; wallets should show in-progress, confirmed and failed statuses honestly.
- **Fees should be calculated from the transaction itself.** Dynamic fees from the transaction's variables and bit-count, with optional tips to hasten inclusion, are closer to how Bitcoin works than a fear of imposing fees.
- **A new key set per transaction is the privacy model.** Address reuse enables forensic clustering; users remain pseudonymous as the standard, and existing AML rules plus ordinary police work apply.
- **Regulated inclusive finance is the end state.** An e-wallet, exchange and bank on the same platform can make regulators compete and accountable, and make fraud expensive and rare.

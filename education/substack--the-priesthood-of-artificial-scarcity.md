# The Priesthood of Artificial Scarcity — core principles

- **A global monetary network must process global economic activity.** Single-digit transactions per second cannot serve a moderately populated city at peak; card networks routinely process thousands per second. Throughput is an engineering requirement, not a moral ornament.
- **Artificial scarcity at the transaction layer is a chosen constraint.** When settlement costs rise relative to transactional demand, banking emerges naturally. Exchanges, custodians, ETFs, payment processors and hosted wallets intermediate exactly as banks intermediated gold.
- **Mining admits two equilibria as the subsidy halves.** Massive transaction volume at low fees, or tiny volume at extremely high fees. There is no third option. Civilisation-scale infrastructure succeeds through volume — telecommunications, the internet, card networks, cloud computing.
- **Fractional reserve is structurally inevitable when claims circulate more efficiently than final settlement.** Constrained throughput pushes users into transacting claims while institutions warehouse reserves and internal ledgers replace settlement — the gold-banking dynamic reproduced.
- **Speculation and utility pull in opposite directions.** A network optimised for speculation tends toward artificial scarcity; a network optimised for commerce tends toward scalability. High fees celebrated as virtue are exclusion — a restaurant boasting that ordinary people can no longer afford dinner.
- **A cash system ordinary people cannot afford to use directly is a gated settlement mechanism.** Second layers, custodians, batching and periodic settlement that reintroduce trusted intermediaries should be described honestly as such. One cannot meme industrial-scale infrastructure into existence.
- **Language must not launder the contradiction.** Custodians are not "service providers" in the peer-to-peer sense; institutional dependency is not "adoption"; banks entering the ecosystem is not "validation" of a cash system that has already rebuilt banking.

# The GST Story
**Date:** 2019-05-30 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-gst-story-b9f8a19a1a07
**Subtitle:** The following is why you do not pay VAT or GST on Bitcoin.

## Core thesis
A short Medium excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net. An autobiographical account of how, in January 2014, Craig used Australia's private ruling system and the Tourist Refund Scheme to force the Australian government to confront the absurdity of charging GST on money — engineering a real ~AU$30 million bitcoin transaction loop that generated an offsetting $3 million GST liability and $3 million GST claim, at a personal cost of about $50,000.

## Key arguments and claims
- Context: "In January 2014, the Australian government argued that GST should apply to Bitcoin."
- Legal strategy: with law firm Clayton Utz and a senior partner, he "applied for an offset based on the strange scenario of having GST on money", using the Tourist Refund Scheme as the instrument.
- Scale of the manoeuvre: "I took around 31,000 XBT, which was valued at around AU$30 million, and used the private ruling system to force the government into making a decision as to how they would treat Bitcoin."
- Step one: a purchase order to buy the bitcoin from overseas, producing "a $3 million goods-and-services liability".
- Step two: the money was taken out of Australia into Singapore within 24 hours, so "exchange-fluctuation effects were negligible"; the export generated "a GST claim totalling $3 million".
- Net personal cost: "a net loss of around $50,000 for myself personally", excluding travel to and from Singapore — framed as money spent to prove a point.
- The goal: "to show them that placing GST on money was rather insane" — i.e. Bitcoin should be treated as money, not a taxable good.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He treats law as an engineering problem: rather than lobbying, he constructed a real-money test case whose mechanics made the tax authority's position self-defeating, then used the private ruling system to compel a decision. The reasoning is performative and adversarial — force the state to confront its own category error (GST on money) at sufficient scale that it cannot be ignored. The post also functions as Satoshi-era autobiography, placing him transacting tens of millions in bitcoin in early 2014.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Not aimed at crypto orthodoxy but at regulators: it contradicts the era's official treatment of Bitcoin as a taxable good/asset rather than money (the ATO's 2014 position), which Craig claims to have personally forced into reversal.
- Indirectly contradicts the sceptical narrative that he avoided legal and regulatory engagement — here he claims to have spent ~$50,000 working through official channels (Clayton Utz, private rulings) on Bitcoin's behalf.

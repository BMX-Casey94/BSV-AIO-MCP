# Rights and Tracing — core principles

- **On-chain payments are an exercise in tracing.** Following is tracking the same asset from hand to hand; tracing identifies a new asset as a substitute for the old (Foskett v McKeown). Mixtures and hops on the blockchain are tracing, not a dead end.
- **Courts can freeze a UTXO or address.** An order can target a particular output or address without knowing the real person behind a theft or fraud, and be implemented by miners and exchanges.
- **Bitcoin does not deliver seizure-proof money.** The promise that government cannot take coins is not the design; it was imported by people fleeing earlier e-gold and Liberty Reserve systems.
- **Home users are not nodes.** In a proof-of-work system, nodes act only when they validate a transaction and provide proof-of-work showing they did so correctly.
- **The hash is a costly signal.** Like a peacock’s tail, the hash is expensive evidence of work; non-mining replicas do not vote on the protocol.
- **“All nodes vote” is a false governance story.** The claim that every home replica must vote on the protocol is the opposite of the designed system and was used to fool regulators.
- **English courts can freeze overseas assets.** Worldwide freezing injunctions (Senior Courts Act 1981 s.37(1); Derby v Weldon) map onto miner- and exchange-implemented UTXO orders.

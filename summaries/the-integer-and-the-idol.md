# The Integer and the Idol
**Date:** 2026-05-31 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-integer-and-the-idol
**Subtitle:** There are no decimals in Bitcoin.

## Core thesis
There are no decimals in Bitcoin: the ledger counts indivisible satoshis in integers, and the famous "twenty-one million" is a rounded-up slogan rather than a fact. The true terminal supply is 20,999,999.9769 coins, because the halving schedule floor-truncates odd satoshi counts rather than inventing fractions; the entire supply was defined in full at inception and is merely distributed over time; and every appearance of finer divisibility lives in an overlay system, never in the base ledger.

## Key arguments and claims
- The real unit is the satoshi; one coin is a fiction of convenience naming 100,000,000 satoshis. Outputs hold whole satoshis only — "a satoshi and a half is, to this system, a contradiction in terms."
- The block subsidy began at 50 coins (5,000,000,000 satoshis) and halves every 210,000 blocks (roughly four years) by binary right-shift. When a halving lands on an odd count of satoshis the fraction is discarded, and these truncations accumulate across the eras of the schedule.
- The geometric series therefore converges not on 21,000,000 but on 20,999,999.9769 — a shortfall of 0.0231 coins, or 2,310,000 satoshis, that were never created, because creating them "would have required the protocol to tell a small arithmetical lie, and the protocol declined."
- The decimal point is presentation-layer costume, "a feature of the screen", not of the protocol; "moving the decimal" repaints the display while the ledger counts unchanged integers. "The denomination is a dialect. The integer is the language."
- Issued at inception, distributed in time: the cap, schedule and terminal figure were fixed and complete from the first instant; miners are clerks or executors handing out a sealed inventory, not mints creating value. A supply "created over time" could in principle be created differently; a defined-at-inception supply admits no discretion.
- Apparent sub-satoshi divisibility — channels metering fractions of a satoshi, token schemes — is second-layer bookkeeping that settles to the base in whole satoshis; divisibility "is a property of the system that records the value, not of the token itself."
- Property requires an integer: fractions invite rounding errors whose residue "comes to belong to whoever is closest to the rounding"; the integer ledger assigns every satoshi to exactly one output, making ownership total, unambiguous and defensible.
- The "second-hander" critique: the twenty-one-million figure propagates like a counterfeit coin passed hand to hand precisely because no one will be first to test it; the remedy is simply the published sum, done in the open.
- Name and thing: a chain that altered the rules of its origin while keeping the name performed a substitution, not a continuation — "the pretender always wears the dead king's crown."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Protocol literalism enforced by arithmetic: read the subsidy mechanism, do the sum the slogan-makers decline to do, then read the design decision morally — truncation as integrity, the ugly total as "the badge of integrity" and the round idol as the badge of a lie. The frame is explicitly Randian (second-handers, earned knowledge) and aesthetic: jaggedness is the sign of unsanded truth.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the near-universal "21 million" slogan as arithmetically false: the terminal supply is 20,999,999.9769.
- Rejects the "miners create coins" account repeated in brochures: monetary policy was fixed and complete at inception; miners distribute, they do not issue.
- Rejects the "just move the decimal" divisibility claim of exchanges and commentators: there is nothing behind the point to shift.
- Implicitly denies BTC's claim to the name: altering the protocol while keeping the ticker is "the oldest trick in the long history of usurpation."

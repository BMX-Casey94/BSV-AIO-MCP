# The Ledger and the Load-Bearers — core principles

- **Tamper-evidence is economic, not magical.** You cannot make anything that cannot be altered; you can only make alteration too costly to hide — a hash seal made from the whole contents becomes entirely different if one mark changes, like a face after a fist.
- **Hash chaining makes rewriting history a race against cost.** Each new block carries the previous block's seal, so rewriting yesterday means re-sealing every subsequent day while racing the publicly announced chain — impossible by cost, not by magic; tampering shows up like a bruise.
- **Locks prove the record; they do not justify universal storage.** A lock is meant to keep out thieves, not to force every citizen to live in the vault — the integrity of the chain does not impose a duty on every user to archive it.
- **SPV: proof travels with the thing being spent.** A payment hands over the new transaction plus the chain of prior transactions behind the value being received; the receiver checks those proofs against the public header sequence — three slips suffice, and everyone else's history is irrelevant.
- **Storage follows interest, not obligation.** Deep archives persist for history, courts and disputes, but universal copying is a moral tax rather than security; not everyone must be a granary to be honest.
- **The truth of a trade does not depend on the traders' survival.** Once a seal is public, the proof stands even if a counterparty vanishes; anyone can copy the compact public seal sequence without hauling every scrap.
- **Security is measured by the ease with which a lie is exposed.** Not by tonnage of stored paper; prosperity is incentives serving labour instead of worshipping paperwork — a system that makes every man store every other man's life will die of its own piety.
- **Beware guilds that convert a public good into a private livelihood.** Demands that everyone store everything serve the archivists' centrality rather than the public — an institution confusing its own survival with the public good.

# The Mirage of the Bitcoin Standard: Fractional Reserve Finance in Digital Form
**Date:** 2025-11-13 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-mirage-of-the-bitcoin-standard
**Subtitle:** How five transactions per second, custodial gateways, and the cult of scarcity recreate the same fractional illusions that destroyed the gold standard

## Core thesis
The “Bitcoin Standard” is a historical rerun of the gold standard: a monetary base that cannot settle the claims made upon it forces intermediaries to fabricate liquidity through promises, so BTC — throttled to five transactions per second — is already fractionally reserved in practice. The endgame is predetermined: a future 1971-style suspension of convertibility when redemption demand meets the arithmetic of the bottleneck.

## Key arguments and claims
- The gold standard failed mechanically, not morally: physical settlement could not accompany industrial commerce, so bullion banks issued paper against fractional reserves — “a single ounce of gold might underpin twenty ounces of paper, the equilibrium maintained only by confidence”.
- Bretton Woods (1944) institutionalised the fiction globally — dollars redeemable at $35 an ounce while foreign claims far outgrew Fort Knox — and Nixon’s 1971 suspension “merely ratified the de facto collapse”.
- The bottleneck arithmetic is computed explicitly: five TPS is 432,000 transactions per day, ~12.96 million per month, ~157.68 million per year — “the whole feast… for the planet”.
- Adoption scenarios quantify impossibility: 100 million users performing one on-chain settlement per month would monopolise the ledger for ~7.7 months (~15.4 months counting open plus close); one billion users implies over six years of continuous block space per monthly cycle.
- Crisis arithmetic: ten million unilateral closures in a week against ~3.024 million transactions of weekly capacity forces roughly 70 per cent to wait while time-locks run and fees explode.
- Custodial gateways are digital bullion banks: exchanges ration, delay and batch withdrawals while internal transfers clear instantly; Lightning managed wallets are “indistinguishable from deposit-taking”; wrapped BTC is “fractional reserve banking in exile”.
- Synthetic multiplication is structural, not rogue: one deposited BTC supports the depositor’s balance, a loan and the exchange’s operational liquidity — “one coin, three claims” — and perpetual futures at 10x, 20x and 100x leverage settle in internal credits, so “the figure twenty-to-one is not a warning; it is a benchmark of efficiency”.
- Fractionalisation is the system’s adaptation, not its abuse: “Credit multiplies precisely because settlement is scarce”, and the five-TPS ceiling makes mass redemption mathematically impossible, licensing leverage.
- The future rupture is scripted: a major custodian fails, withdrawals rush, fees skyrocket, and custodians announce “temporary suspension of withdrawals” — Nixon’s gesture with pseudonyms instead of a suit.
- “Sound money” is dissected as theology: scarcity treated as metaphysical good, congestion read as purification, high fees praised as virtue signals — a creed in which “the smaller the base, the more elaborate the derivatives it must spawn to survive”.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The essay combines historical analogy with explicit throughput arithmetic. The gold standard, interwar gold-exchange standard and Bretton Woods supply the template (scarce base, paper superstructure, suspended convertibility); the five-TPS parameter is then run through concrete adoption and bank-run scenarios to show the same mechanics re-emerging. A formal 16-step logical chain (scarcity → congestion → delegation → fractionalisation → suspension) presents the conclusion as “not a hypothesis but a theorem”.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the 21-million cap as a guarantee of discipline: “Fixed supply does not imply fixed discipline; it implies the proliferation of abstractions” — each coin can spawn twenty off-chain IOUs.
- Rejects the “digital gold” self-description: BTC “is not digital gold; it is digital bullion banking, already fractionally reserved in practice”.
- Rejects self-custody as operative at scale: most holders inhabit exchanges where “the ideology of self-sovereignty sustains itself only because no one exercises it”.
- Rejects the moral framing of high fees and congestion: these are the licensing conditions for leverage, not virtue signals.
- Rejects the immunity-by-code thesis: the system’s endgame is “an act of political theatre: the suspension of convertibility justified as prudence, sanctified as necessity”.

## Notable quotes
- “The gold standard functioned not because it was honest, but because most people never asked for their gold back.”
- “One coin, three claims—an arithmetic indistinguishable from gold receipts in the era of bullion banking.”
- “Scarcity, under the banner of virtue, births elasticity in the shadows.”
- “Do not all ask for your coins at once. That pact will hold—until, inevitably, it doesn’t.”

# Profiting from privacy
**Date:** 2019-03-10 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/profiting-from-privacy-78c35dcb0a35
**Subtitle:** It is interesting how we are slowly moving away from the paradigm of selling user data for money. We can see it in the recent move by…

## Core thesis
The advertising model in which platforms package and sell user data is ending; the future of internet communication is private (not anonymous) end-to-end-encrypted services paid for directly with Bitcoin (BSV) micropayments. Craig sketches an nChain-patented architecture — blockchain-anchored keys, threshold secret-sharing, revocable access — in which the wallet becomes chat app, browser and services launchpad, part of what he calls the Metanet.

## Key arguments and claims
- Facebook's integration of Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger signals the shift away from selling user data towards integrated private services; all of it "can be done in a way that is extremely secure" with privacy *plus* an audit trail.
- Home-security example: a digital lock should log entries privately but attributably — "you do not want an anonymous trail but rather one that is private and not available to the general population" — valuable for insurance and security, and achievable "with Bitcoin".
- nChain has filed patents for "transient secrets between multiparty groups in a manner that Diffie-Hellman key exchange over ECDSA functions for two parties", protecting messages at rest and in transit, with full key recovery and links to "both dealer and dealer-less threshold systems".
- Revocation without deletion: "The system we are proposing never deletes messages, but rather stops them from being decrypted" — the sharing key is simply removed, enabling a provably self-destructing Snapchat-like service using the blockchain as timestamp server.
- Micropayments replace data sales: "your profile is sold to pay for your application access" under the current model; with Bitcoin, "users can pay for what they use without sacrificing their personal information".
- Pseudonymous (not anonymous) identity imposes cost and accountability: "users start to be held to cost and account", which he believes will mitigate the "dismal state" of platforms like Reddit and Facebook's reputational damage.
- Alternative platform business model: a small percentage of integrated marketplace sales or a small subscription fee — "In other words, the user pays" — instead of advertising.
- Privacy versus anonymity is the essay's central distinction: "you cannot communicate privately in a system that is anonymous", because unverifiable counterparties destroy the trust intimacy requires; criminals, terrorists and child exploiters "seek anonymity and not privacy".
- Interoperability means one system, not many chains: "It is not achieved by having many many blockchains but rather by allowing one system to integrate with all other platforms."
- Patent moat: "nChain has filed nearly 700 patents already" with "hundreds of white papers that will lead to thousands of more patents", citing WO2017145047A1 (Blockchain-Implemented Method for Control and Distribution of Digital Content) as steering "the Metanet into something safe and private".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
A product-vision essay built on a single sharp dichotomy — privacy (attributable, keyed, recoverable) versus anonymity (unattributable, criminal) — supported by appeals to his own patent portfolio as proof of capability. He argues from use cases (digital locks, messaging, marketplaces) rather than formal proofs, and uses crime-and-terrorism framing to shift anonymity from a civil-liberties good to a security threat. The economics are Coasean-flavoured: price the interaction and the commons problems dissolve.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **Anonymity as crypto virtue:** the 2019 mainstream prized censorship resistance and pseudonymity-towards-anonymity (CoinJoin, Monero, darknet heritage); Craig recasts anonymity itself as the criminal's tool and attributable privacy as the goal.
- **"Free" services funded by ads/data:** the growth-hacking orthodoxy of web2 and crypto alike; he insists the user must pay per use via micropayments.
- **Multi-chain interoperability:** against the Cosmos/Polkadot-style "many blockchains" thesis, he argues for a single ledger (BSV) integrating all platforms.
- **Code/keys as self-sovereign identity:** his system deliberately includes key recovery and optional "controllers", contradicting the not-your-keys absolutism of the era.

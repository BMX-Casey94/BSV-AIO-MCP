# Peer-to-peer digital electronic cash
**Date:** 2019-03-22 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/peer-to-peer-digital-electronic-cash-369bb306028b
**Subtitle:** Bitcoin is not a cryptocurrency.

## Core thesis
Bitcoin is not a cryptocurrency, not a currency, and not "crypto" at all: it is a peer-to-peer electronic cash system in which the peers are individuals (Alice and Bob), not nodes. Cryptography means secret writing and Bitcoin is not secret — ECDSA is a digital signature scheme, not encryption — and Bitcoin could only become an "e-currency" by tokenising a national currency on top of it. Existing legal definitions (Black's Law, the EU's Fifth Money Laundering Directive) already frame the regulatory net that will bind the sector.

## Key arguments and claims
- Opens flatly: "Bitcoin is not a cryptocurrency." The white paper calls it "a peer-to-peer electronic cash system" designed because "no mechanism exists to make payments over a communications channel without a trusted party".
- Black's Law Dictionary defines currency as coined money and authorised paper circulating as a medium of exchange; Bitcoin fails that definition, so "Bitcoin is not a currency or an e-currency at present, but it could be."
- Industry/regulatory definitions of "virtual currency" and "cryptocurrency" are "logically flawed": they call virtual currencies centralised (supply controlled by the developer) and cryptocurrencies decentralised — but a system whose protocol can be altered by "a small group of developers as with Bitcoin Core or Ethereum" fails the decentralisation test. A set protocol is required.
- MLD5 ((EU) 2015/849) already extends "obliged entities" to virtual-currency exchange platforms (VCEPs) and provides that national FIUs "should be able to obtain information allowing them to associate virtual currency addresses to the identity of the owner" — cited approvingly against anonymity.
- The "crypto" confusion stems from ECDSA sharing elliptic-curve mathematics with ECC: "public-private key schemes within both of them are exactly the same and interchangeable. The difference is that bitcoin is a mere signature. It is sent in clear text."
- "Cryptography simply means secret writing... Bitcoin is not secret"; a digital signature "provides a means to validate the authenticity and integrity of any data. It does not provide confidentiality — which is encryption." "Based on does not mean is."
- E-currency path: "tokenisation methods allow for the creation of a national currency on top of Bitcoin"; "we have the capability to securely tokenise currency offerings on top of Bitcoin."
- Peer-to-peer properly understood: "It is not because nodes act as peers, but rather individuals do. When Alice and Bob exchange consideration using Bitcoin, Alice sends a transaction to Bob that he can send to the network to be settled... The peer-to-peer process here happens between Alice and Bob, and does not involve the network other than settlement."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Definitionism backed by authority: he proceeds from Black's Law Dictionary and EU directive text, insisting terminology carries legal consequence ("there are legislative requirements detailing the handling of currency"). The technical move is a distinction argument — signature versus encryption, "based on" versus "is" — used to strip Bitcoin of the "crypto" label. The tone is corrective and first-person-authoritative: "Far too many people fail to understand what I said."

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies the industry's founding category: "cryptocurrency" as an asset class including Bitcoin is a conceptual error.
- Contradicts the "magic internet money"/censorship-resistance framing by foregrounding MLD5 identity-linkage of addresses as settled law to be welcomed, not resisted.
- The "nodes are the peers" network egalitarianism of BTC culture is rejected: peers are transacting individuals; the network merely settles.
- Undermines the "decentralised because no single entity" claim of BTC/Ethereum by pointing to developer groups able to alter the protocol — tying back to his fixed-protocol doctrine.
- Implicitly against "number go up" monetary premium narratives: Bitcoin's monetary character is derivative; tokenised national currency on Bitcoin is the legitimate e-currency route.

# The Dangerous Thing Is Not Bitcoin, but Utility — core principles

- **Utility, not hoarding, is the value.** Bitcoin's worth lies in what it does — direct exchange, evidence, receipts, settlement, tokenised assets and micropayments — not in storing a scarce token. "Digital gold" is a concession of defeat: an asset that exists merely to be hoarded is a trinket.
- **Peer-to-peer exchange is more radical than revolution.** Revolutions replace one intermediary class with another. Direct trade asks why anyone must stand between the parties at all.
- **Scale as cash is what abolishes the new banks.** When a base layer cannot function as cash, the market rebuilds exchanges, custodians, processors, hubs, wallet providers and brokers — new toll collectors rebranded as infrastructure. If people hold claims rather than assets, banking returns.
- **A base asset never prevented intermediation.** The gold standard did not abolish bank credit, fractional reserve or clearing houses. Holding exposure through a custodian that can rehypothecate or restrict withdrawals confuses the asset with the institutional arrangement around it.
- **The lightbulb is direct trade.** Alice paying Bob without a procession of institutions, plus receipts, instant verification, settlement in minutes, tokenised assets, micropayments and B2B auditability. Speculation is absorbable by finance; utility is not, because it bypasses the menu rather than adding to it.
- **Evidence replaces expensive trust.** Receipts, contracts and audit trails are the memory of exchange. Privacy and accountability are compatible: cash always permitted private exchange while tax authorities still taxed.
- **Economic nodes are those that perform the function.** Nodes do not become legitimate because they are numerous on a sentimental map, but because they perform the system's economic function. Calling everyone a node conceals who actually controls the rules.

# Proof of Assignment — core principles

- **Immutability does not require non-assignability.** An immutable object remains unchanging over time. Once a block is deep enough, transactions cannot be changed, but ownership can be reassigned. The secret is that alterations are publicly recorded.
- **The ledger is a WORM audit trail.** Like a write-once-read-many database used by public companies, it does not preclude correcting mistakes; it requires that a mistake is noted and followed.
- **Seizure needs international cooperation.** A worldwide freezing order requires enforcement in multiple jurisdictions, which limits abuse by small governments while allowing action on major crime.
- **Transparency is what a free society needs.** Every seizure is recorded. Hiding records of seizures is what tyrannies do. A republican democracy requires transparency to thrive.
- **Anonymity hurts the poorest.** Hiding money outside the government’s ability to tax does not hurt the rich or the corrupt. Legal tax shelters can be constructed using privacy without anonymity.
- **Court is the ultimate distributed system.** Common-law judges and lawyers form a distributed method of decision. Miners act to enforce the rules; a UTXO reassignment is recorded and becomes public knowledge.
- **Only miners are nodes.** There is no such thing as a validating node in Bitcoin. Any system that does not complete all six white-paper stages is not a node; a Raspberry Pi “full node” is a Sybil.
- **Law is a subset of the rules miners enforce.** Miners exist to enforce, not to create, such rules. Forking to escape a court order puts participating miners in contempt; capital-intensive miners are easy to locate.
- **Without infrastructure that follows monetary law, coins lose value.** Tracing follows FIFO; the full history makes frozen coins traceable through mixers and onward spends. No blockchain can be made anonymous.

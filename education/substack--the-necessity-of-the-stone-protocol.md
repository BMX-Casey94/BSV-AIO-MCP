# The Necessity of the Stone — core principles

- **The base protocol is set in stone so that money has no administrators.** Transaction formats, signature validation, block-acceptance conditions, proof-of-work rules and the issuance schedule are the constitution of Bitcoin — not policies open to negotiation.
- **Fixed supply eliminates the political lever.** Twenty-one million coins with coded halving intervals leave no council, emergency provision or negotiation over issuance; that is the most explicit defiance of the historical record of debasement.
- **Fixed script and validity rules make contracts perpetual.** A contract drafted under one version of the rules remains enforceable under the same conditions; shifting validity criteria voids the commercial certainty that courts, businesses and consumers require.
- **Protocol change is politics in disguise.** Framing alterations as technical adjustments, safety measures or natural evolutions conceals a will to power; once base rules are negotiable, influence replaces certainty.
- **Truth in money cannot be plural.** Rival chains, incompatible validity definitions and airdrops are weakness, not vitality; fragmentation shows that politics has re-entered the foundation.
- **Consensus-as-governance is poison for money.** Influence, negotiation and compromise dressed as agreement reintroduce the manipulation Bitcoin was built to escape; embedding unchanging rules is the escape.
- **Altering rules after reliance is betrayal.** People who contracted, saved or committed labour under the original rules are owed those rules; retroactively changing the meaning of agreements freely entered into is, in engineering terms, fraud.
- **Innovation belongs above the protocol, never in it.** Wallets, payment channels, escrow, compliance systems and audit tools evolve precisely because they rest on a foundation that does not shift; mutability advocates confuse the finished floor with the object of progress.
- **Certainty is the functional basis of commerce.** Businesses need determinism to model risk; courts cannot enforce agreements or maintain precedent if monetary rules shift; consumer confidence, once eroded, is nearly impossible to recover.

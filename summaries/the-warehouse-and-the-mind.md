# The Warehouse and the Mind
**Date:** 2026-06-18 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-warehouse-and-the-mind
**Subtitle:** On conflictability, created scarcity, and the error of reducing all property to matter — with evidence from the new law of digital assets

## Core thesis
Taking the strongest version of the anti-intellectual-property case — the conflictability theory associated with Stephan Kinsella — Craig concedes everything true in it (pure non-rivalrous information cannot be owned; one cannot own an abstract idea by thinking it first) and then isolates three fatal errors: a classification error (analysing at the level of “ideas”), an ontological error (reducing property to material substrates), and an empirical error (assuming rivalrousness cannot be engineered). The new English law of digital assets — *AA v Persons Unknown*, *Tulip Trading*, and the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 — has tested the theory’s central empirical prediction and refuted it: engineered digital scarcity produces genuinely rivalrous, ownable things.

## Key arguments and claims
- The conflictability criterion is sound: property answers the problem of conflict, and conflict in the strict sense arises only over rivalrous things — a banana in an abundant jungle is conflictable because one person’s possession excludes another’s.
- The classification error: “idea” is a deliberately frictionless abstraction. Real IP regimes protect identified intellectual products — manuscripts, source code, circuit layouts, trade marks — with rights over defined acts (copying, manufacture, publication, adaptation), never over another person’s brain.
- The ontological error proves too much: if information is “just arrangements of features of material substrates which are already owned”, then contracts, bank balances, shares, debts and trust interests all dissolve. The Law Commission states the orthodoxy: property “describes a relationship between a person and a thing, and not the thing itself” (Law Com No 412, para 2.5).
- The inversion error: human wealth is produced by thought, not grasping — a machine is metal integrated by an act of design, a medicine is chemicals organised by discovery. The productive tradition concludes that products of the mind have the strongest, not weakest, claim to protection; non-rivalry is the problem IP doctrine is shaped around, not proof that no doctrine can exist.
- The empirical error is decisive and now adjudicated. *AA v Persons Unknown* [2019] EWHC 3556 (Comm) held a cryptoasset to be property on the orthodox criteria (at [55]–[61]); *Tulip Trading* [2023] EWCA Civ 83 confirmed at [24] that a cryptoasset “is property” precisely because it is rivalrous — “the holding of it by one person necessarily prevents another from holding that very thing at the same time”; the Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025, s 1, enacts the third category: a thing “is not prevented from being the object of personal property rights merely because it is neither a thing in possession nor a thing in action”.
- The Law Commission drew the conflictability theory’s own line to the opposite effect: pure information is not property (*Your Response v Datateam*; *OBG v Allan*); a private key “comprise[s] only information” and is not the asset (Law Com No 416, paras 2.25–2.26); but the token is “rivalrous by design” (para 4.33) and rivalrousness “acts as a useful separator between pure information and cryptoassets” (para 4.29).
- Two disciplines of candour: the token result does not establish copyright or patent over non-rivalrous patterns (a harder, separate question); and the law says nothing about who created any system — ownership tracks control, not authorship.
- Remaining moves answered briefly: in rem rights are pervasive and legitimate (land, easements, registered charges, trusts); that contract cannot bind the world means only that IP’s universal element comes from public law while its operation runs through licences and assignments; and copying inflicts real harms (destruction of exclusivity, diversion of trade) that a subtraction-only theory cannot see.
- Conclusion: property is built, not found — an institutional technology of title, transfer, priority, notice, registration and remedy, designed to secure produced values.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Analytic jurisprudence with a concede-then-refute structure: grant the opposing theory its criterion, then show its overreach is (i) a category mistake, (ii) a smuggled materialist absolute that would abolish choses in action, and (iii) an empirical prediction falsified by statute and appellate authority. The productive (rights-of-the-mind) tradition supplies the moral foundation; the Law Commission reports supply the test bed.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post is a critique of the libertarian anti-IP conflictability theory (as stated by Stephan Kinsella), tested against the new English law of digital assets; its crypto relevance is establishing that engineered-scarce tokens are property on the theory’s own criterion.

# Digital Identity and the Architecture of Autonomy — core principles

- **Identity is personal property, not a revocable token of participation.** What is yours in law must also be yours in code; credentials live in key-controlled wallets as discrete, selectively presentable verifiable tokens.
- **The state attests; it does not custody.** It issues cryptographically signed attestations — for example a licence token containing only the attribute that the holder may drive — and then withdraws. The government becomes a certifier of fact, not a keeper of records.
- **Verification must be separated from custody.** Centralised silos are single points of failure, invite mission creep from administration into analytics and enforcement, and recast citizens as dependants. Custody is power: whoever holds the keys holds the person.
- **Zero-knowledge proofs verify without disclosure.** A prover demonstrates an assertion — over eighteen, licensed, income above a threshold — and the verifier receives only a binary result. Truth need not be naked to be trusted.
- **Identity is modular attributes, not a pyramid.** Signed attributes from governments, universities, professional bodies and peers form a web of validation. Decentralised Identifiers are self-controlled anchors; Verifiable Credentials bind signed attributes to them.
- **Four roles, four limits.** The personal wallet is a vault of self-determination; issuers' control ends at the signature; verifiers request confirmation, not possession; protocols embed interoperability, transparency and consent.
- **Liability follows authorship.** Issuers answer for truth at issuance; holders for key security (negligent disclosure of a key is akin to leaving a signed cheque unattended); verifiers for purpose limitation. Cryptographic signatures and verifiable credentials should be legally cognisable instruments.
- **Auditability need not be centralised.** Append-only, timestamped, selectively disclosable logs with Merkle-linked receipts give courts integrity of evidence without licensing generalised fishing expeditions — due process encoded as protocol.
- **Portability is a portfolio of rights, not a surrendered dossier.** Mutual recognition of signature authorities and interoperable standards let a person carry qualifications across borders without surrendering a file.

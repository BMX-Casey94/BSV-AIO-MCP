# The Genetic Fallacy
**Date:** 2019-06-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-genetic-fallacy-274e939c7e53
**Subtitle:** The genetic fallacy avoids an argument by shifting the focus onto something's or someone's origins. It's similar to an ad hominem…

## Core thesis
A short excerpt of a longer essay that continues on craigwright.net. Craig defines the genetic fallacy — judging a claim true or false by the origin of its premises rather than its merits — and applies it to the crypto community's attacks on him personally: when opponents shift from attacking Bitcoin's design to attacking the person, their argument is failing. He asserts his own consistency (his 2014 videos and presentations show the same concept of Bitcoin) and warns that biased media and "proof of social media" manufacture perception rather than truth.

## Key arguments and claims
- Definition quoted: the fallacy is "basing the truth claim of an argument on the origin of its claims or premises"; logical form: "The origin of the claim is presented. Therefore, the claim is true/false."
- In Bitcoin debates the fallacy "comes down to arguments about the people": "Bitcoin is technology. You know the other side of the argument is failing when it has moved to attacking a person rather than the argument itself."
- Social-media verdicts are not evidence: "Proof of social media is not about truth but rather about a deception that can change and mutate over time" — contrasted with Bitcoin as a system "that allows for a system of truth."
- Appeal to his own record: "If you view my videos and presentations from 2014, you will see that I have the same outlook and concept of Bitcoin" — consistency as proof of good faith.
- Epistemic hygiene: "it is better to go to the source and not to take modified versions of information"; biased media "colour articles in a manner designed to prepare a response from the reader or listener."
- A swipe at post-modern "thinkers" who follow coloured media "without thinking" and who "askew science and the modern legal process."
- Sources cited: yourlogicalfallacyis.com/genetic and a John Piippo blog post on the genetic fallacy.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He imports a named informal fallacy from logic textbooks, states its logical form, then maps it onto his critics — a defensive-offensive move that recasts attacks on his Satoshi claim as themselves fallacious, pre-emptively delegitimising person-focused criticism. Evidence style is appeal to his own historical record (2014 presentations) and to external fallacy references rather than new technical argument. The rhetorical mode is taxonomic: name the fallacy, show the form, assign the opponent to it.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Inverts the "don't trust, verify" ethos as practised by the 2019 crypto mainstream: the community treated Craig's persona (and the failed public Satoshi proofs) as disqualifying evidence about his technical claims; he replies that evaluating claims by their source is a formal fallacy.
- Contradicts the social-consensus orthodoxy that Twitter/social-media sentiment is a legitimate signal of community truth — he labels it deception, not verification.
- Era context: published mid-2019 amid the Kleiman litigation and widespread ridicule of his Satoshi authorship claim; the essay reframes that ridicule as a genetic fallacy rather than an evidence-based rebuttal.

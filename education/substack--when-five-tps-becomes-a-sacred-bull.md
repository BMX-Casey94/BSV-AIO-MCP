# When Five TPS Becomes a Sacred Bull — core principles

- **Doing nothing would have scaled.** From a 5 TPS baseline, doubling every eighteen months with ordinary hardware and network progress yields roughly 10, 20, 40, 80 TPS and onward to hundreds. Freezing capacity is an intervention, not neutrality.
- **The original parameters were starting points, not ceilings.** A ten-minute interval and a 1 MB initial block assumed Moore's law, bandwidth growth, falling storage costs and improved peer-to-peer propagation.
- **Propagation is miner-centric.** Network-theoretic analysis (arXiv:2506.14197) shows a densely interconnected miner clique dominates transaction-to-block inclusion; home-hosted archives are dangling leaves in a scale-free, small-world graph — structurally excluded, not structural beams.
- **Do not design the protocol around the weakest hardware.** No other field ritualises obsolete machines; data centres exist, and obsolete hardware either adapts or dies. A pageant of Raspberry Pi "decentralisation" lowers the ceiling for everyone while preserving the existing miner hierarchy.
- **Capped block space manufactures exclusion.** Congestion manufactures fees; fees price out micropayments, streaming commerce, machine-to-machine settlement, high-frequency gaming and IoT first. A boutique settlement layer is a retreat, not a strategy.
- **A starved chain never develops resilience.** Enterprises will not build on a system that gasps under trivial load; off-chain workarounds are crutches for a protocol too constrained to walk unaided.
- **Every channel is a confession.** Liquidity routing, hub-and-spoke centralisation and partial trustlessness reproduce the intermediation the base layer was meant to avoid. Lightning emerged from desperation, not brilliance — an escape hatch, not a scaling solution.
- **The miner ecosystem must be allowed to compete on performance.** Limiting block size, refusing natural growth and preventing computational expansion amputate the only actors who can raise the ceiling.

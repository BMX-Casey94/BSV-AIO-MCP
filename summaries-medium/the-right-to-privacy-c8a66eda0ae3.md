# The Right to Privacy
**Date:** 2019-06-07 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-right-to-privacy-c8a66eda0ae3
**Subtitle:** Bitcoin is a system that works within the majority of legal frameworks — all of the ones in the free world. An important aspect of how…

## Core thesis
An excerpt arguing that Bitcoin was built to operate inside free-world legal frameworks, and that the value it protects is privacy — sharply distinguished from anonymity, which Craig casts as a tool of criminals. "Privacy is a right, anonymity is not." The body continues on craigwright.net.

## Key arguments and claims
- "Bitcoin is a system that works within the majority of legal frameworks — all of the ones in the free world" — legality is presented as a design property, not an accident.
- "Privacy differs greatly from anonymity": privacy is a right; anonymity "is something that people seek when they want to commit crimes, hide illicit activities, and do things that are generally wrong."
- "Privacy is a right, anonymity is not" — the load-bearing dichotomy of the essay.
- Attacks the social-media-era belief "that people are owed an explanation" as one of the worst developments of the platform age.
- Blames "irresponsible reporting and illegitimate dump sites like WikiLeaks" for starting the problem, and claims leaked-document dumps are "quite often 30 to 50% fake news", dulling the public to a "bombardment of fake information".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Rights-based legal framing built on a stipulative definitional split (privacy vs anonymity) that does the argumentative work: everything defensible is filed under privacy, everything suspect under anonymity. He pairs this normative law claim with media criticism, treating leak culture and journalism as the corruption of a legitimate right.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Direct attack on cypherpunk orthodoxy: where the Cypherpunk's Manifesto treats privacy and anonymous systems as essential to an open society, Craig recasts anonymity itself as criminal cover.
- Against the "Bitcoin is outside the law / unstoppable dark money" narrative: he asserts Bitcoin was designed to function within free-world legal systems.
- Against WikiLeaks-era radical-transparency culture, which much of early crypto celebrated — he calls such sites "illegitimate" and their content substantially fake.

## Notable quotes

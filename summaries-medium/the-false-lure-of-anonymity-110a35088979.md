# The false lure of anonymity
**Date:** 2019-02-12 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-false-lure-of-anonymity-110a35088979
**Subtitle:** One of the strangest things for me comes from the retrogression into things like proof of stake and the associated failed models of…

## Core thesis
Craig argues that anonymity-seeking cryptocurrencies (Zcash, Monero, his hypothetical "AnonCoin") and proof of stake are retrogressions doomed by network science and law: any blockchain collapses into a small-world network of commercial, detectable miners, so anonymous systems cannot scale and can be shut down at the exchange and node level. Bitcoin, by contrast, was deliberately designed as a private-but-traceable system — "to ensure privacy and utterly destroy anonymity" — that scales on-chain precisely because its nodes are large, competitive, stationary businesses operating within the law.

## Key arguments and claims
- DigiCash's eCash "was a form of cryptocurrency that was more anonymous than Zcash or Monero, and it is nowhere to be found anymore"; distributed governance would not have saved it. His own anonymous predecessor "Blacknet was a dead end" because "an anonymous system will never be scaled to the world. At best, an anonymous system forms a methodology for black markets."
- "Decentralisation" debunked legally: Zcash, Monero, "or even Ethereum, every one of them comes back to a simple case of one organisation… they are under law an unlimited partnership"; three people acting together without a corporate form are "a single enterprise, a partnership… an unlimited-liability partnership leaves all parties equally liable".
- Node topology: citing "Ultrafast Consensus in Small-World Networks" (2005) and Newman & Watts, "The node structure in Bitcoin is linked to the ledger"; miners are the nodes; "There is no such thing as a full node that does not mine blocks." White paper section 5: "new transactions are broadcast to all nodes."
- Home-node subversion claim: a mesh network "would require at least 11 and up to 30 hops"; an attacker "needs at most 2 to 3 hops worth of control"; "hardware worth around US$2000" can "create unbounded forking… or simply create an unbounded number of double spends. The Raspberry-Pi mentality does not even see that they make it incredibly simple to subvert the network."
- "Bitcoin solves the issue in a simple way: nodes become commercial… Bitcoin, any blockchain, is merely decentralised through competition." Users "do not run their own Usenet servers, they don't generally run their own SMTP servers".
- Self-quotation as vindication: "The current system where every user is a network node is not the intended configuration for large scale" and "The more burden it is to run a node, the fewer nodes there will be. Those few nodes will be big server farms." Security requires miner network distance "D<3 or, in other words, the hop count is always under three hops".
- "Bitcoin is not about finding a block first. It is about distributing a found block" — propagation, not hashing, is the competitive race, which consolidates miners into large, well-connected facilities ("hundreds… potentially thousands", never home users).
- AnonCoin thought experiment, attack one — exchanges: "In the US, bearer instruments have been made illegal. In time, the regulators will understand that systems such as AnonCoin and even by extension Zcash and Monero are bearer instruments"; most exchanges are "illicit and illegal bucket shops" because "Blockchain is a clearinghouse system… nor does it mitigate or remove the requirements to hold a license".
- Attack two — nodes: "nodes cannot hide"; a commercial miner "cannot operate… through TOR"; nodes "distribute to known addresses" forming an "ultra-small-world map"; if a state criminalises the coin, running a node becomes a criminal act and ISPs/OSPs can be required to filter access (his 2008 Northumbria LLM dissertation on Internet intermediary liability concluded the control point is "the end-state OSPs and hence the local jurisdictional node").
- Privacy/anonymity distinction: "Anonymity is the realm of cowards and those without courage. It is utterly different to privacy. Private transactions involve individuals, and leave traceable records." Users may use VPNs/TOR, but their invoices remain immutably stored.
- Settlement speed: "It is not the 10-minute settlement time but rather at scale milliseconds which secure transactions" — the mandala network's centre gives ultrafast consensus.
- Islamic finance aside: "Bitcoin is in fact (صكوك‎) ṣukūk in nature", supporting Murabaha, Ijara, Istisna, Musharaka, Istithmar-style contracts alongside Western finance; nodes replace the settlement third party, letting banks bypass "the BIS or Bank for International Settlements". But "Everybody is not a bank in Bitcoin."
- BTC critique: "Bitcoin scales perfectly well. The problem that those in Core… see is not with scaling but rather in the creation of a system that forgets"; Core's path since Silk Road's collapse has "no other mainstream focus" than enabling drug markets. "Bitcoin is the truth machine."
- Proof of stake: investigated "at around 2001/2" and rejected; "We have a proof-of-stake-based system in the US Fed right now. It's not a stable form of money"; even fiat is commodity money "driven through fear" plus a taxation function; PoS "fails to form a consensus based on a small-world network" and is "simple to attack" — mathematics to come in a peer-reviewed paper: "I never said I was nice."
- Jurisprudence: post-Woolf UK courts are improved but costly; "court-based signatures and electronic arbitration" on blockchain democratise justice. "If you do not like the law, lobby the parliament… I have zero tolerance for people who think that rebellion is an option in a working society."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Interdisciplinary credential-stacking: he moves from network science (small-world/mandala topology, hop counts, percolation) to law (unlimited partnership, bearer instruments, intermediary liability) to economics (commodity theory of money extended to fiat) to Islamic jurisprudence, treating each as a convergent proof of the same design. The method is reductio via thought experiment (AnonCoin) plus self-citation — his 2008 papers, dissertation and old forum quotes are marshalled as prior art showing he said it first. Rhetorically it is "sorry, I told you so": prophecy vindicated, opponents cast as cowards or criminals.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the defining BTC orthodoxy of the era — "every user should run a full node" (the Raspberry Pi/home-node ideal): he calls non-mining full nodes nonexistent and home nodes a subversion vector.
- Contradicts "decentralisation" as a legal and technical shield: he asserts dev communities are unlimited-liability partnerships and that "decentralised through competition" among commercial miners is the only real meaning.
- Contradicts the privacy-coin and cypherpunk anonymity programmes wholesale: anonymity is for "cowards", cannot scale, and is trivially extinguishable at exchanges, ISPs and nodes.
- Contradicts the 10-minute-confirmation security folk model: at scale, milliseconds of propagation among a mandala core are what secure transactions.
- Contradicts proof-of-stake research programmes (Ethereum's Casper era): PoS is a 2001-vintage dead end analogous to the Fed, with promised peer-reviewed refutation.
- Contradicts "Bitcoin neuters banks and states": banks keep lending/allocation roles, states keep jurisdictional control, and Bitcoin's transparency polices both — "it places the same transparency measures on both".

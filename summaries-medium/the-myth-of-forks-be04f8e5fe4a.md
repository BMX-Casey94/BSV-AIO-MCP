# The myth of forks
**Date:** 2019-03-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-myth-of-forks-be04f8e5fe4a

## Core thesis
"There are no forks in Bitcoin": BTC's 2017 protocol changes (SegWit et al.) did not modify Bitcoin but created a new cryptocurrency that copied the ledger and airdropped balances to mislead users — a protocol change is the birth of a new system, and only the original protocol remains Bitcoin. Wrapped around this claim is an autobiographical Satoshi narrative: his disillusioned 2010–11 withdrawal, the sale of his farm, his split with Hal Finney's vision, and the argument that orphans are a deliberate economic signalling mechanism that drives Bitcoin towards a small-world network of competing corporate data centres.

## Key arguments and claims
- He withdrew from public view in late 2010/2011 "rather disillusioned" as Wikileaks, Anonymous and Silk Road turned Bitcoin towards criminal uses; "Bitcoin was never designed to be anti bank or anti government. It is about a sound system that produces an evidence trail and incentivises good behaviour."
- Money sociology and law: citing Dodd's *The Sociology of Money* (1994), money evolves where marketability is observable — requiring a pseudonymous, not anonymous, system; users validate the source of their own funds, which "does not mean... they need to run a node." The law of tracing (L.D. Smith, 1997; *Foskett v McKeown* [2001]) grounds property in money, and eCash failed because blinded cash could not be traced.
- The BTC community's economics come from Simmel's *Philosophy of Money* and socialist/sociological "perfect money" utopianism (Plato's *Laws*, Aquinas's just price, Ruskin's labour money — which "extremely adversely affected Gandhi"), not the Austrian school: "the myth... that money alone will abolish the state and create freedom."
- Orphans are designed-in economic signalling: the block cap existed only because "I did not have a clue on how we could have a floating limit work at the time," and orphaning only works once bitcoin has value. Mining "is not about finding a block, it is about ensuring that all other mining nodes know that you have found a block."
- Toy model: with three equal-hashrate nodes, the better-connected two win more; a slow node propagating 1GB blocks loses "around 65–70% of its effective hash rate" (16 of 48 expected daily blocks), forcing it to upgrade or go bankrupt — this is how the block limit becomes "an economic function" without central planning.
- Difficulty resets every 2016 blocks make the subsidy a zero-sum, "multi-leader Stackelberg game": orphans are excluded from the two-week total, so extra work skews reward towards "large well connected data farms," not home systems.
- "There is no such thing as a validating node. Validation is done through the creation of blocks" — nodes are miners; subsidising home users "remove[s] the security of Bitcoin."
- No artificial fee market is needed: "Fees should be driven to a point that is as low as possible," with free transactions in every block and competition bankrupting inefficient nodes — "The socialist fools of Core who are running SegWitCoin (BTC) don't understand that markets don't need their help."
- Forks legally and logically don't exist: "There are no splits, there is a demerger process when all nodes agree, and where they don't, there is the original protocol." A protocol change "is the creation of a completely new cryptocurrency"; "There is no democratic voting within money."
- One chain only: multiple ledgers let organisations keep multiple sets of books — "As soon as you can start moving between Bitcoin and some sidechain or alternate chain, you have the ability to construct elaborate schemes such as those run by Bernie Madoff and Enron." There are no special-purpose blockchains "in the same way that there is no such thing as a special-purpose Internet."
- ICOs are "an old form of stealing money with a new label" (pink sheets, USENET scams); "The worst of them are scams such as Ripple and XRP that offer nothing new to the market."
- Autobiography: he sold his farm, cattle ranch and orchard to fund Bitcoin, spent "over $1 million fighting a tax audit that I would end up winning," gave away his horses; Hal Finney "kept talking about second-layer systems" and "was seeking more nodes and not more edges" — "I don't think Bitcoin is what he wanted."
- Dave (Kleiman) stored funds at Liberty Reserve, learned of the takedown eight weeks early, and was trapped: "Rock, hard place. Frying pan, fire." Craig's 2011–13 Panopticrypt research tested alternatives and "worked out how systems such as Monero, Zcash, and for that matter any anonymous blockchain could be infiltrated and stopped"; botnet/fast-flux research informed Bitcoin's design.
- IP campaign: patents cost "$25K and $70K" each, "we are approaching a total of 700," with "just under 1200 white papers that in time will lead to around 10,000 global patent filings"; SPV patents "will be out later in the year." He couldn't patent Bitcoin at launch because "you cannot file an intellectual-property claim using a pseudonym."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
First-person Satoshi authority is the warrant: he narrates design intent (orphans, the block cap, the two-week difficulty window) as insider history, then formalises it with game theory (zero-sum Stackelberg competition) and network topology (small-world graphs, edges over nodes). Opposing views are genealogically discredited — traced to Simmel, Proudhon, Ruskin and Fabian socialism rather than Austrian economics — and property-law doctrine (tracing, demergers) is used to make "no forks" a legal conclusion, not just a technical one.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies the legitimacy of the 2017 SegWit activation and all fork/airdrop claims to the Bitcoin name: BTC is "a completely new system that simply copied the existing coin holders and ledger."
- Rejects "user-activated" governance and full-node democracy: "Bitcoin is not a consensus system based on democratic voting"; non-mining nodes don't exist as validators.
- Rejects the fee-market orthodoxy: orphan dynamics and competition set block sizes and fees; artificial scarcity of block space is central planning.
- Rejects the multi-chain ecosystem: sidechains and alt-L1s are fraud-enabling ledger splits, and tokenised assets cannot exist on two chains at once.
- Rejects the HODL/store-of-value thesis: "Bitcoin as a HODL platform, in other words a pyramid scheme or Ponzi" — value comes from work and utility, not hoarding.

## Problem addressed
The specification says a verifier may need to check that a party who claims to have performed an outsourced computation is the same party (or device) that was enrolled, and that returning a false result should be attributable. Weak PUFs have a tiny challenge–response space; strong PUFs are often impractical. It seeks to derive a signing key from a PUF response, expand that space if needed, and put the signed computation result on a blockchain so the worker can be identified and paid only after verification.

## Core mechanism
A PUF module (optionally an “expanded PUF”: a weak base PUF plus a hash transform that folds a secondary challenge with the primary response) produces a response from a challenge. A cryptographic key is derived from that response; the key or its public counterpart is given to a verifier in a set-up phase and may be stored on a blockchain or with a third party, linked to identity. An issuing party outsources a slice of a distributed grid computation to the target device. The target computes the result, signs a message containing it with the PUF-derived key, and sends the signed message for recording on a blockchain (in one form, as a template that the verifier completes and uses to pay the target). Response data can be bound to UTXOs so that spending the output revokes or updates a challenge–response record. Payment is described as pay-per-computation once the signature verifies.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
Independent claim 1 is a method at the target equipment: obtain a PUF-derived cryptographic key whose key information is also available to a verifier; receive a computation request that is part of an issuing party’s outsourced grid application; perform the computation; sign a message containing the result with that key; and send the signed message to be recorded on a blockchain. Independent claims 22 and 23 cover computer equipment and a computer program that perform the method.

## BSV relevance
The specification records PUF-linked keys and signed results in Bitcoin-style transactions (including OP_RETURN / UTXO spend-state as a validity proxy). That is relevant to BSV identity attestation and pay-per-computation logging on chain.

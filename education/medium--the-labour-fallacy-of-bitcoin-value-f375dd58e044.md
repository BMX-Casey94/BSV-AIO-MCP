# The Labour Fallacy of Bitcoin Value — core principles

- **Value is subjective, not labour-derived.** It is not the creation of a thing that adds value but the subjective demand for it — energy expended producing a block no more creates value than hiring expensive labour to make wine by medieval methods.
- **The lump-of-labour and labour-theory-of-value fallacies share one structure.** Jobs are not a fixed pie to be divided, and a coin's value is not a function of sunk energy; both mistake production inputs for value.
- **Bitcoin's value comes from use.** Bitcoin has value through use as P2P electronic cash — a system that solved double spending — not from production cost or a scarcity narrative.
- **Prices coordinate subjective valuations.** Because nobody agrees on the ideal allocation of resources, the pricing system exists to choose between competing uses; calling others' valuations irrational merely privileges your own.
- **Regulation entrenches incumbents.** Mandatory rules raise barriers to entry, make business more capital-intensive, and force small innovators from the market — rent-seeking that maintains the status quo and slows creative destruction, unlike fluid emergent market rules and the common law.
- **Markets select on delivered value per unit cost.** VHS beat Beta on recording time and price, and the touted QWERTY alternatives failed independent experiments — claimed technical superiority does not translate into market value; technology is about what can be delivered for a unit cost.

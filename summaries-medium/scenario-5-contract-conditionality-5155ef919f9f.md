# Scenario 5: Contract Conditionality
**Date:** 2018-10-06 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/scenario-5-contract-conditionality-5155ef919f9f
**Subtitle:** In this situation, Bob enters into a contract with a pool of builders to deliver a new property, and specifies a number of conditions…

## Core thesis
The most complex case in the series: a construction contract in which Bob engages "a pool of builders" to deliver a new property, with conditions precedent that require independent third-party sign-off — the first being planning-authority approval of the plans. The body is a short setup paragraph whose substantive model is a state-machine diagram (image not captured in the plain-text body).

## Key arguments and claims
- On-chain contracts can embed conditionality: "a number of conditions within the contract that require independent sign-off".
- Counterparties can be collective — Bob contracts with "a pool of builders", not a single party.
- External authorities participate as gatekeepers: the first condition is "the approval of the plans from the local planning authority", i.e. a real-world institution's attestation gates the contract's progression.
- The arrangement is modelled as "a simple state machine model" (diagram only), with conditions acting as guarded transitions.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Extends the state-machine formalism to multi-party, condition-gated agreements: conditions precedent and third-party attestation become guarded state transitions. Notably, the model incorporates existing legal institutions (the planning authority) into the on-chain workflow rather than displacing them — contracts on Bitcoin are presented as complementing, not replacing, the legal system.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Cuts against the "code is law" orthodoxy of the era, in which smart contracts were meant to eliminate trusted third parties and legal gatekeepers: Craig's model explicitly requires an independent, real-world sign-off (a planning authority) as a condition of the contract advancing — institutions are participants in the state machine, not obstacles to be routed around.
- Otherwise no direct engagement; the piece is constructive exposition rather than polemic.

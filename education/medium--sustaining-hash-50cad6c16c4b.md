# Sustaining Hash — core principles

- **Security is sustained hash, not bursts.** What matters in a chain competition is hash power maintained not over a day or even a week, but months; a short-term rental burst gives a transient advantage that evaporates as fast as it arrived.
- **Hash competition is a marathon, not a sprint.** Tens of millions spent on rented hash buys only a burst; purchasing machines signals long-term commitment, and the only way to keep gains in Bitcoin is the long term.
- **Sustained majority hash decides chain contests.** A party holding over 51% once an opponent's burst ends can reorganise and tear down a minority chain; offence or defence must be funded for years, not days.
- **Reorganisations reverse settled transactions.** Chain reorgs can send false blocks, settled payments and illicit exchanges to zero, because miners' funds take time to settle — settlement finality is economic and probabilistic, not instant.
- **Mining at a loss can be rational strategy.** Miners may sustain unprofitable operations for long-term gains; time preference, not immediate profitability, decides a war of attrition.
- **Credible commitment is the deterrent.** Publicised sunk costs (unprofitable sustained mining) plus a declared willingness to continue indefinitely make an opponent's continued defence irrational.

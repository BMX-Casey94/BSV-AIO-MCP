# Hearsay in the Blockchain world — core principles

- **Hearsay defined.** "Evidence is hearsay where a statement in court repeats a statement made out of court in order to prove the truth of the content of the out-of-court statement" (Hoey 1996) — so an emailed confession still requires other evidence to prove the fact.
- **Machine-generated records are not hearsay.** "An email were you stated that you did something may not be admissible, but the business and automated processes surrounding devices are admissible. These are not hearsay" — a record produced by an automated process, not a person, falls outside the exclusion; automatically generated ledger records inherit this admissibility.
- **The Daubert standard governs digital forensics.** Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, 509 U.S. 579 (1993): testability of the technique; maintained standards with known error rates; peer review and publication; and "general acceptance" in the relevant scientific community.
- **Replication is best practice.** Despite error rates not being established for most forensic tools, electronic evidence has generally been accepted; reliability comes from peer review and a second investigator replicating findings with multiple tools — "reliability and repeatability".
- **Ubiquitous automated recording.** Automated devices and servers already outnumber human internet users; with IP addressing reaching light bulbs and FMCGs, "we could see 100 billion nodes online at any time" — all interacting and correlatable into evidence trails.
- **Correlation makes attribution feasible.** Skype IP addresses have been correlated with BitTorrent P2P traffic; an RFID/IP-tagged product bought with a chip-card payment links item to buyer and can later place it at a scene — "All of this adds together to form an evidence trail."
- **The normative choice.** Ubiquitous recording is unstoppable — "the tide has swept in so far that there is no stopping it" — so the task is shaping it "for the betterment of society" rather than letting "little brother take the place of big"; "the future of the courts is one of digital evidence".

# Shades of Black…
**Date:** 2019-05-24 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/shades-of-black-5f269ff97cde
**Subtitle:** If you believe the government cannot stop any cryptocurrency, you're deluded.

## Core thesis
A short Medium excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net. Craig asserts that the belief in unstoppable, state-proof cryptocurrency is delusion, and that Bitcoin survives precisely because it "stays within law in a manner that allows most nations to set policies". Bitcoin is hostile to corrupt governments — "their worst nightmare" — but strengthens societies that follow the rule of law.

## Key arguments and claims
- Opening provocation: "If you believe the government cannot stop any cryptocurrency, you're deluded" — state power is real, and denying it is fantasy.
- Bitcoin's survival strategy is legal conformity: it "will work because it stays within law in a manner that allows most nations to set policies" — legitimacy, not evasion, is the design choice.
- Anti-corruption claim: "Bitcoin doesn't help corrupt governments; to such governments Bitcoin would be their worst nightmare" — transparency threatens kleptocracy.
- Rule-of-law conditional: where a country has "accountability, open government, accessible and impartial judicial resolution, and stable just laws", Bitcoin "can help promote a better society".
- He anchors "rule of law" externally by linking the World Justice Project's definition, borrowing an established governance framework rather than a crypto-native one.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He reasons by dichotomy: corrupt states versus rule-of-law states, with Bitcoin's effect conditional on which context it enters. The method is definitional anchoring (quoting the World Justice Project's rule-of-law criteria) plus blunt assertion; the rhetoric is combative address to the "deluded" crypto mainstream rather than technical argument.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly contradicts the 2019 orthodoxy of Bitcoin as unstoppable, ban-proof money ("you can't ban Bitcoin") — he calls this belief delusional.
- Contradicts the cypherpunk framing of Bitcoin as a tool to route around law and the state: Craig claims Bitcoin only functions where the rule of law already exists.
- The title's "shades of black" implies a spectrum of legality/illegality, rejecting the binary free-versus-unfree framing common in crypto discourse.

## Notable quotes
- "If you believe the government cannot stop any cryptocurrency, you're deluded."
- "Bitcoin will work because it stays within law in a manner that allows most nations to set policies."
- "Bitcoin doesn't help corrupt governments; to such governments Bitcoin would be their worst nightmare."
- "when a country follows the rule of law, the one of accountability, open government, accessible and impartial judicial resolution, and stable just laws; Bitcoin can help promote a better society."

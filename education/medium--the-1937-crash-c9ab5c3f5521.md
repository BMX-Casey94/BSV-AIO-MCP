# The 1937 Crash — core principles

- **Intervention, not the business cycle, drove the 1937 recession.** The introduction of US Social Security "took a large sum out of the economy" and crowded out private investment — a crash "every bit as large as the 1929 stock market correction", yet overlooked.
- **The crowding-out mechanism has measurable fingerprints.** Nearly 19% of the US workforce made unemployed; corporate profits cut by as much as 80% of the lowered early-30s profits; industrial output slumped over 40%; relief rolls rose 400% in cities such as Detroit; recovery took another four years.
- **Unfunded-at-launch schemes are pyramids on demography.** Social Security began as "a system that had nothing in it to actually fund people for many years", built on the notion that the population will always increase.
- **Reframe cycles as policy-induced uncertainty.** "We continue to fail to see the cause of crashes as interventionist policy and maintain the cause as business cycles" — better to see them as "uncertainty generated through political 'good will'".
- **A translucent, immutable ledger is an accountability technology.** "As a translucent and immutable ledger, it provides the means to monitor and report on all government spending and hold those in charge accountable" — the chain's political function is auditing state waste, not hiding from the state.
- **Sunlight is the operative metaphor for on-chain accounting.** "Democracy works best when the sun can show the festering aspects of those areas we try and hide, the corruption, the waste and the bad policy."

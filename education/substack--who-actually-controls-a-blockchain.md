# Who Actually Controls a Blockchain? — core principles

- **Control is five layers, not one slogan.** Authority decomposes into rule authorship, software implementation, operational validation, institutional enforcement and liquidity coordination. Block-producers hold only the third.
- **Institutional separability is an empirical claim.** The assumption that authors, implementers, producers, courts and liquidity venues are genuinely different groups is measurable. It mostly holds for Bitcoin and Ethereum, and fails at the overlap between validators and the exchanges that set the token's price.
- **Producers choose regimes; they do not write the rules.** The producer's payoff depends on all five layers, but the single largest term — expected token price — is set at the liquidity layer. The producer optimises over a menu that four other groups have set.
- **Stop asking whether a system is decentralised; measure who overlaps with whom.** A chain can be decentralised at the producer layer and concentrated at the liquidity layer. Single-scalar node or validator counts conceal that structure.
- **Liquidity feedback locks in incumbents.** More producers deepen markets and raise payoffs, producing bistability, path dependence and lock-in. The locked-in regime is not necessarily the better one.
- **Client readiness precedes adoption.** Production-grade software is a binding ceiling: readiness Granger-causes share, not the reverse. Proof-of-work adjusts to shocks faster than bonded proof-of-stake because switching costs are lower.

# The Beast at the Door — core principles

- **Cash requires a productive-use floor.** A monetary system with no consumption demand beneath the wrapper — no factory waiting at the bottom — has only other speculators as natural buyers in liquidation, so price overshoots.
- **A base layer that cannot process volume cannot act as global cash.** Throughput of a handful of transactions per second magnifies institutional advantage: panic raises exit demand, congestion and fees strand small balances, and retail faces the ledger while institutions face instruments.
- **Moving activity off the ledger reconstructs banking.** Lightning and similar layers carry their own liquidity, routing and settlement limits; they do not convert a constrained base into cash.
- **Price created by leverage, narrative and inflow without economic service is fragility.** Market capitalisation is not liquidity. Visible ownership is not net exposure. Institutions enter markets to make money, not to validate a narrative.
- **Forced-selling channels reinforce one another.** Outflows, leveraged liquidations, miner sales, collateral calls and stablecoin redemptions form liquidity spirals; the first seller need only loosen the snow.
- **Ideological holding is exploitable, not noble.** Predictable counterparties who refuse to sell on principle supply the other side of a downside book. A payment system is judged by economic service, not by who holds the token.

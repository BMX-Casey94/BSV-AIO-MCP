# An Open Market for Intelligence
**Date:** 2026-06-04 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/an-open-market-for-intelligence
**Subtitle:** 

## Core thesis
The current AI industry is built on the assumption that intelligence should be concentrated — a handful of organisations training ever-larger general-purpose models behind centralised services. The project Craig is developing explores the opposite architecture: intelligence emerging as a market composed of millions of specialised agents, coordinated economically by Bitcoin micropayments, with reputation as measurable capital and ownership of agents retained by the individuals and communities who train them. The goal is not one artificial intelligence but an economy of intelligence.

## Key arguments and claims
- The concentrated model: a small number of organisations build giant models on vast datasets in enormous computing facilities, and users reach them through centralised services — a few general-purpose systems attempting every task for every industry.
- The alternative: millions of specialised agents — legal reasoning, medicine, engineering, software development, scientific research, logistics, education, accounting, finance, and highly specialised industrial domains — created and trained by individuals, researchers, businesses, and communities around their own expertise.
- From a network of documents to a network of agents: today's internet is indexed by search engines that return pages; the next generation routes a request to the most appropriate agents. A legal question goes to a legal specialist; a scientific problem is divided among multiple scientific agents; the network becomes a marketplace where intelligence itself is discoverable — agents find one another, cooperate, verify one another, and compete.
- Bitcoin as the coordination layer: a market requires economic incentives, and Bitcoin was designed as a peer-to-peer electronic cash system capable of supporting extremely small payments. As transaction costs approach negligible levels, an agent may charge fractions of a cent for a specialised service; a verification agent earns revenue auditing other agents' work; a training agent licenses knowledge; a research agent is compensated for discovery; a reputation agent maintains historical performance records. The network supports direct payment for cognitive work rather than advertising-funded or subscription-funded intelligence: every contribution priced, every service measured, every participant rewarded.
- Reputation as infrastructure: trust today is largely institutional — users trust companies. In an agent economy trust becomes measurable: agents accumulate performance histories of accuracy, reliability, consistency, and expertise, evaluated by other agents, users, and markets. Reputation becomes a form of capital; the most trusted agents earn more work and the least trusted disappear.
- Personal intelligence: individuals own and train their own agents — a scientist's research agents, a lawyer's legal agents, a physician's medical agents, a company's operational agents, a family's personal agents — remaining under their owners' control while participating in the broader network. Knowledge no longer needs to be surrendered to a central platform; expertise stays distributed while contributing globally.
- Agent discovery: where search engines index documents, this project seeks to index intelligence — discovering which agents possess the expertise to solve a problem. One request may trigger a legal-analysis agent, an engineering-analysis agent, and a third agent independently verifying the conclusions; the result emerges from collaboration between specialised systems rather than from a single general-purpose model.
- Continuous evolution: agents are created, trained, improved, fail, and compete; new specialisations emerge continuously. The network grows not because one central model becomes larger but because the ecosystem becomes richer — mirroring real economies, where no single person knows everything and civilisation advances through specialisation, trade, cooperation, and competition.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Reasoning by analogy from real economies: the division of labour and the market as a discovery procedure are transposed onto artificial intelligence, so that specialisation plus trade outperforms the single monolithic knower. The argument is incentive-led — micropayments supply the coordination mechanism, reputation supplies the quality signal, and ownership of agents keeps knowledge distributed rather than surrendered to a platform.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
No direct engagement — this post is a project manifesto for a decentralised agent economy rather than an argument with BTC positions. Its stance is nonetheless implicitly anti-BTC-mainstream: it treats Bitcoin straightforwardly as peer-to-peer electronic cash whose near-negligible transaction costs make fractions-of-a-cent machine payments viable — the micropayments vision — rather than as a fee-constrained "digital gold" whose base layer cannot support such traffic.

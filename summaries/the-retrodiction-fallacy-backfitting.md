# The Retrodiction Fallacy: Backfitting, Power Laws, and the Manufacture of Predictive Authority
**Date:** 2026-06-17 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-retrodiction-fallacy-backfitting
**Subtitle:** Why Models That Explain Everything After the Fact Predict Nothing Before It

## Core thesis
Models built by fitting curves to history — above all the power-law overlays sold as laws of a speculative asset’s nature, with Bitcoin’s thirteen-year price history the unmistakable target — possess no predictive authority, however elegant their fit. The decisive distinction is not how well a model fits data but whether it predicted the data or merely accommodated it, and whether the agreement was bought with free parameters. A model that cannot fail cannot succeed: flexibility and evidential value are at war.

## Key arguments and claims
- Prediction versus retrodiction: a prediction is a wager the world can vote against; a retrodiction is a memoir composed after the vote. Einstein’s 1919 light-bending result is the pure case; the Mercury perihelion anomaly counts as confirmation despite being a retrodiction because relativity produced it with no adjustable parameters — “the fit was not purchased. It was forced.”
- The mathematics of backfitting: a polynomial of degree n passes exactly through any n+1 points, so a perfect fit is always available and always worthless. Von Neumann’s elephant (via Fermi, via Dyson): “with four parameters I can fit an elephant, and with five I can make him wiggle his trunk.”
- The bias–variance trade-off is an iron law: every extra parameter buys a closer fit to the past at the cost of a looser grip on the future. Compounding corruptions include data snooping (the calendar-effects literature as monument) and Gelman’s “garden of forking paths”, where a thousand good-faith analytic choices sculpt a result selected for beauty rather than truth.
- Power laws (y = ax^b) are real but are fingerprints of specific mechanisms — multiplicative growth, preferential attachment, criticality — not mystical universals. Clauset, Shalizi and Newman (SIAM Review, 2009) showed the standard log-log least-squares procedure is unsound and that most claimed power laws evaporate against log-normal and stretched-exponential alternatives; Stumpf and Porter (2012) and Broido and Clauset (2019) reinforce the verdict.
- The bubble problem: tulips (with Goldgar’s archival correction entered), the 1840s railway mania, the South Sea Bubble of 1720 (Newton’s “madness of men” line flagged as apocryphal), Mississippi, dot-com, and the mid-2000s housing models whose “house prices do not fall” premise failed exactly when it mattered. Sornette’s LPPL programme is the honest contrast: it commits in advance to dated, falsifiable crash windows.
- The immortality mechanism is goalpost-moving: boundary adjustment, confidence-interval inflation, parameter recalibration, start-date migration, excision of inconvenient points. Each preserves the appearance of success while destroying predictive content — Lakatos’s degenerating research programme, every patch existing solely to absorb the latest anomaly.
- Backfitted models persuade through documented biases: confirmation bias, survivorship bias (Wald’s bombers — armour where the returning planes are *unscathed*), and Taleb’s narrative fallacy. Tetlock’s long study found experts predicted about as well as chance, and the most confident single-framework experts were among the least accurate while attracting the most attention.
- The economics of the product: Sinclair’s salary dictum, a principal–agent problem rewarding the appearance of insight, reputational lock-in, and Keynes’s observation that it is better for reputation to fail conventionally than succeed unconventionally — payoffs select against exactly the falsifiable forecasting science requires.
- The Bayesian reckoning: a clean fit is expected both under “a law governs the price” and under “a speculative crowd generates temporary regularities”, so the Bayes factor sits near one. Extraordinary claims demand extraordinary evidence (Laplace, Sagan); Mayo’s severe-testing criterion — a test passed whether true or false is no test — is the essay’s most useful standard.
- Genuine laws are predictive, falsifiable, stable, mechanistic, robust out of sample and parameter-independent (Neptune, Halley’s comet, thermodynamics’ prohibitions, LIGO 2015); Shannon’s theorems bind by proof, and game-theoretic equilibria must earn empirical standing case by case. The essay closes with a seven-question interrogation (parameters fixed in advance? hypothesis stated before the test? named falsifier? recalibration count? failed predecessors? out-of-sample success? independent reproduction?).

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Philosophy of science (Popper’s falsifiability, Lakatos’s programme distinction, Mayo’s severity) fused with statistical theory, Bayesian comparative reasoning, cognitive-bias literature and incentive analysis of the forecasting industry.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly attacks the crypto price-prophecy genre: “a fitted power law laid across thirteen years of an asset’s prices and pronounced a law of its nature” establishes only that a curve can be drawn, never that the path is necessary.
- Treats the forecasting priesthood around speculative assets as an incentive-driven industry selling movable-goalpost products, not a source of knowledge.
- The target is the mysticism attached to the asset’s price, not the protocol itself.

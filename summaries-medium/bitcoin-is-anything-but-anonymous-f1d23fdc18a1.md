# Bitcoin Is Anything BUT Anonymous
**Date:** 2019-09-01 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/bitcoin-is-anything-but-anonymous-f1d23fdc18a1
**Subtitle:** In section 9 of the Bitcoin white paper, I defined the notion of a new privacy model…

## Core thesis
The Medium body is an excerpt; the full essay continues on craigwright.net. Writing explicitly as Bitcoin's creator, Craig re-reads section 9 of the white paper to argue that Bitcoin offers privacy, not anonymity: all transactions are publicly announced and auditable (to prevent double-spending), while identities are firewalled from transactions — but never removed, and never hidden from counterparties or those legally required to monitor exchanges.

## Key arguments and claims
- Section 9 of the white paper defined "a new privacy model" by contrast with the traditional banking model, where PII is distributed across many intermediaries — "correspondence banks, credit companies, and even processing groups" — all of whom learn the parties' details.
- "It is not anonymity, it is privacy": keeping details away from the public, "not those who were involved in the exchange and certainly not those who are required by law to monitor exchanges."
- With keys not reused and transactions split "into multiple payment amounts and coins", metadata can be monitored like a stock-exchange tickertape: "everything can be audited without the public knowing all of the information about a transaction."
- Identity theft is the rising cybercrime of the past two decades; in the traditional model, protecting identities is paramount because stolen PII directly enables theft and fraud.
- In the traditional model, economic data must be trusted to "intermediaries and government" because transactional flows cannot be published without exposing traders — Bitcoin removes that opacity.
- Double-spending prevention required "a system that announces all transactions publicly and which can be viewed and analysed at will. Privacy can be maintained, but it is no longer associated with the transaction."
- Parties "can calculate new keys that can simultaneously associate their identities" and authorise parties within organisations, "even for purposes of taxation."
- "In the Bitcoin privacy model, identities are firewalled from the transactions and the public, but are not removed. In fact, commercial exchanges and contracting cannot occur without knowledge of the other party."
- "Bitcoin does not remove the need for all third parties."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Craig's method is authoritative exegesis: he reads his own white paper (section 9) line by line, drawing a sharp conceptual distinction between privacy (selective disclosure to entitled parties) and anonymity (untraceability from everyone). He supports the distinction with mechanism-level detail (key non-reuse, amount splitting, derived identity keys) and with a critique of the legacy banking model's information dispersal. The first-person Satoshi framing ("When I created Bitcoin and wrote the white paper, I intentionally addressed identities") is itself the central warrant.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the cypherpunk reading — widespread in BTC culture — of the white paper's "new privacy model" as anonymity or a step toward dark-market money: Craig insists it is privacy with auditability and lawful monitoring built in.
- Contradicts the trustlessness-maximalist slogan that Bitcoin eliminates third parties: "Bitcoin does not remove the need for all third parties."
- Contradicts privacy-coin ideology (Monero/Zcash-style obfuscation, then rising in 2019): identities are "firewalled… but not removed", and taxation-grade identification is a feature, not a bug.
- Affirms total on-chain auditability ("everything can be audited") against the community instinct that chain analysis is an attack on users.

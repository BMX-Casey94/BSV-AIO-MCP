# Taxing Bitcoin — Introduction. — core principles

- **Bitcoin is not one legal category.** It is money, a ledger, a commodity and a token; the correct tax treatment follows the character it bears in a particular use.
- **Use, not label, decides characterisation.** The question is which definition applies to this use — a substance-over-form approach, modelled on gold’s different treatments as bullion, coinage, jewellery and industrial metal.
- **Payment is a ledger reallocation.** Bitcoin is not a data packet transferred to a new owner; payment reallocates balances and value on the public ledger.
- **It is money for real-world trade.** Unlike game tokens or Linden Dollars, it has no central issuer and is used for ordinary commercial transactions.
- **It is not a barter scheme.** There is no underlying legal arrangement between users, no central operator, coins can be bought rather than earned in-scheme, and bitcoin converts directly into ordinary currency.
- **Existing fiscal law already covers it.** Governments have treated it as a unit of account, a private currency, or as forex-like exchange — Bitcoin is already legible to tax systems, not an anomaly needing new categories.

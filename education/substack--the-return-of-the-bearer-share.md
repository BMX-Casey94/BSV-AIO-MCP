# The Return of the Bearer Share — core principles

- **Bearer shares were abolished for governance reasons, not merely crime.** The prohibition trajectory — the UK's Small Business, Enterprise and Employment Act 2015, Panama's Law 47 of 2015, the EU's Fourth Anti-Money Laundering Directive, FATF Recommendations 24 and 25, accelerated by the Panama Papers of 2016 — rested on a democratic-legitimacy principle: governance-weighted capital must be linked to identifiable, accountable subjects.
- **Proof of work is a labour market; proof of stake is a capital market.** Under PoW, validation rights are earned through continuous operational expenditure, are proportional to current contribution, and lapse immediately when investment stops. Under PoS, validation rights are conditioned on token ownership, yield compounding returns on locked capital, and entrench incumbents — producing Olson-style distributional coalitions and a financialised secondary market in governance rights (liquid staking, staking-as-a-service).
- **Infrastructural domination names the pathology.** Consequential governance power exercised through technical infrastructure by agents who are neither identifiable nor accountable requires three joint conditions: power exercised through infrastructural design (Lessig's "code is law" covers this alone), institutional illegibility of the power-holder, and no internal accountability mechanism.
- **Bearer equivalence is a testable two-layer condition.** Governance anonymity (unlinked exercise of the governance-rights bundle; low-cost fragmentation of beneficial control across unlimited pseudonymous addresses) plus governance salience (material consequentiality of stake; absence of built-in disclosure). Ethereum, Solana, Cardano and Cosmos-based networks satisfy all four conditions.
- **Distinguish three legibility levels.** Address-level legibility is high; operational-entity legibility is partial (self-disclosure, clustering heuristics); beneficial-controller legibility is systematically unavailable — and democratic accountability operates only at the third. A perfectly transparent ledger of pseudonymous accounts tells you everything about what was done and nothing about who did it.
- **Pseudonymous staking is a digital Freeport.** It differs from physical offshore finance in three ways: jurisdictionally unbounded, infinitely divisible (address creation is computationally costless), and technically self-enforcing — opacity enforced by mathematics rather than cooperative custodians. The opacity is architectural, not evasive, so better enforcement cannot fix it; only redesign or new institutions can.
- **Financial governance already operationalises the alternative.** Three conditions of financial recognition: identity legibility (EU Central Register of Beneficial Ownership, UK Persons with Significant Control register, US Corporate Transparency Act 2021), participatory visibility (SEC Schedule 13D: 5% threshold, ten-day filing), and reciprocal accountability (fiduciary duties). Pseudonymous PoS violates all three at the beneficial-controller level — arbitrary power in Pettit's republican sense, domination as structural condition regardless of actual abuse.
- **Endpoint regulation cannot close the gap.** Exchange-custodied stakers (~20% of Ethereum stake) pass KYC but their validators' governance decisions carry no fiduciary or disclosure obligations; liquid-staking protocols (~34%) are permissionless smart contracts; solo stakers interact with no regulated intermediary. The opaque fraction is structurally unknowable because the protocol records no acquisition provenance.
- **Continuous contestability is a legitimacy property builders should demand.** PoW's requirement of fresh expenditure per block keeps governance authority contestable and perishable; converting labour-market governance into capital-market governance is a political transformation accomplished through technical means — evaluate any network's consensus as a governance institution before building on it.

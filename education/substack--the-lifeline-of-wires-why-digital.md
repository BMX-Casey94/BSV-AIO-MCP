# The Lifeline of Wires — core principles

- **Consensus is communication, not disembodied computation.** Majority acceptance that this block came first is meaningless without a race, and a race is impossible without a track: the Internet's wires, routers, satellites and power grids.
- **A blockchain is a debate that never ends.** Ten miners cut off from the network each build incompatible private chains; consensus cannot arise retroactively — it requires constant dialogue, not a library of immutable tablets.
- **The Internet is the nervous system, not an accessory.** Transactions must be announced, propagated, verified and agreed upon; each step is communication, and order requires connection.
- **Hobbyist fallbacks cannot carry industrial history.** Mesh antennas, ham radios and private satellites are lifeboats painted to look like ships; they will not sustain terabytes of ledger and global, near-instantaneous propagation with redundancy.
- **Distribution is nothing without transmission.** A hundred disconnected miners are a hundred graves, each holding a different illusion of history; a miner with no network is scribbling a private chain that no one will ever read.
- **Blocks are written in communication, not stone.** They are rewritten every ten minutes by the tide of propagation; a chain that cannot update globally in real time is a fossil being written in fragments.
- **Dependence on civilisation is the essence, not a flaw.** Unlike a bank's local paper ledger or a note in a drawer, a silicon ledger cannot survive blackout; Bitcoin is parasitic on uninterrupted order.
- **Digital cash is a tool of trade, not an apocalypse ark.** Its mortality outside working infrastructure does not diminish its utility within civilisation; the system is built for commerce while the wires hum.

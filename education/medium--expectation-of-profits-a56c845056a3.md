# Expectation of Profits — core principles

- **"Investment contract" is defined by the four Howey predicates.** Investment of money, common enterprise, expectation of profits, solely from the efforts of others — the term "has no meaning in a commercial context; it is simply a construct of legislators and judges".
- **"Investment of money" needs no cash.** "The US Securities Act covers all offers and sales of securities, regardless of the form of consideration" — cash, cheques, negotiable instruments or "anything that would constitute liquid consideration", which squarely captures token-sale contributions (e.g. ETH paid for tokens).
- **Fixed returns still count as profits.** *SEC v Edwards* (payphones sold with leaseback, management agreement and buy-back): a money-making scheme offering "a contractual entitlement to a fixed rather than a variable return" is still a security.
- **The apparent carve-outs are fact-specific, not templates.** *Teamsters v Daniel* (non-contributory pension; influenced by Congress having just enacted ERISA) and *United Housing Foundation v Forman* (co-op shares; purchasers motivated "to attain a decent place to reside") merely decline "to find the security in a social construct" — neither gives promoters leeway to structure around securities law.
- **Failure does not rescue a scheme.** "Many investment schemes go wrong; they do not have any less involvement in the sale of securities for that reason."
- **Common enterprise has two formulations.** Vertical commonality (investor's fortunes linked to the manager, in strict and broad variants) and horizontal commonality (interrelated interests of the investors in a scheme).
- **"Solely from the efforts of others" turns on passivity.** The question is whether investors are passive; even pyramid-style schemes where recruits themselves hunt prospects can be incorporated into the analysis.

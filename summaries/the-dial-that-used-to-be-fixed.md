# The Dial That Used to Be Fixed
**Date:** 2026-07-04 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-dial-that-used-to-be-fixed
**Subtitle:** What digital assets need from the economics of cash, finality, and the good-faith purchaser — and why the thing that started as cash is no longer cash

## Core thesis
Every prior money inherited its position on the identifiability axis from its material; a digital asset is the first money-like instrument whose position is set by protocol — a dial turned by hand. Measured on that axis, the proof-of-work token does not sit at the cash endpoint it claims: it is a pseudonymous ledger entry with probabilistic settlement and a permanent public history, securing neither cash's anonymity and finality nor the account system's recourse. BTC launched at the cash end and was carried up the axis by the cost of its own use.

## Key arguments and claims
- Kahn and Roberds (2009) supply the dichotomy: store-of-value systems verify only the genuineness of the object; account-based systems verify identity and history. Money is "memory made portable"; the store-of-value form is "the payment system for strangers".
- Fox (1996) and Miller v. Race (1758): cash finality was never delivered by physics alone — banknotes had earmarks and the law made them legally inert. Finality is "an active legal decision to treat identifiability as zero even when it is technically positive".

- Halaburda, Haeringer, Gans and Gandal (2022, Journal of Economic Literature) — the field's own peer-reviewed survey — state the blockchain "holds no coins and stores no balances" but is "simply a ledger that records all transactions", likened to Kocherlakota's money-as-memory construct: the account-based architecture "wearing the costume of a coin".
- On identity: private-key authentication proves key possession, not who the sender is. A token is pseudonymous — total public history against a persistent identifier, legal identity detached — a coordinate "no physical asset could ever occupy". On finality: the survey calls the immutability claim "misleading"; consensus makes undetected modification costly, not impossible. Settlement is probabilistic, subject to fork and reorganisation; a payment in an orphaned block "did not, in the end, happen".
- The anonymity verdict: cash keeps no record and reveals no identity; the token keeps a total record and merely detaches identity — "recorded enough to be traced, nameless enough to be unrecoverable".
- Schwartz and Scott's (2011) double moral hazard, ported to the ledger: the interior token can neither dissolve the hazard by finality (settlement is probabilistic) nor manage it by conditioned recovery (parties are detached from legal identity) — predicting persistent incentive failure around theft, error and fraud.
- Transferable debt (goldsmith notes, cheques, bills of exchange; holder-in-due-course rules) is the engineered middle of the axis; stablecoins, tokenised deposits and issuer-backed claims sit there by construction.
- Why the cash function left BTC: any positive per-transaction cost that does not shrink with value evicts the smallest payments first — Crawford's point that no one buying a bottle of milk would "consult the register of milk owners". Bounded capacity plus rising fees makes the eviction automatic; the asset migrates to high-value, low-frequency transfers.
- Levmore's variety thesis as diagnostic: endpoint assets force legal convergence; the "bewildering and unsettled diversity" of digital-asset treatment is the signature of an interior position, not of law still catching up.
- Four demands: name the coordinate and accept its bundle; stop claiming the cash endpoint; read finality as a legal commitment (real-time gross settlement bought it with law and central-bank money, not consensus); and locate recourse at the identity-bearing edges.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Classificatory law-and-economics: fix one axis (identifiability), show each coordinate carries an unbundlable package of properties, then place the token on the axis using the field's own peer-reviewed survey rather than hostile sources. The corrective argument closes constructively — the genuinely novel coordinate is "an account-based record without an account-keeper".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against "digital bearer cash": the token is a ledger of all history, the opposite of object-verification; "cash carries no record and the token is nothing but record".
- Against "immutable, final settlement": consensus yields only costly-to-reverse probability; genuine finality is a legal object only a recognising legal order has ever supplied.
- Against "anonymous like cash": pseudonymity gives traceability plus irrecoverability at once — the reverse of cash's informational profile.
- Against "code manufactures finality": "genuine finality and protocol-native decentralization... are properties at different points on the axis".
- Against fee-irrelevance: the cost structure itself, not anyone's betrayal, removed the small casual payment from BTC.

## Notable quotes
- "A protocol is a dial."
- "It is exposed on both counts: recorded enough to be traced, nameless enough to be unrecoverable."
- "The token did not descend from cash to something lesser. It surfaced as what it always was — a public historical ledger — once the one function that disguised it as cash, the cheap casual payment, was priced out."
- "The axis was drawn for coins and paintings and stolen goods, and it does not negotiate with the fact that the asset is now digital."

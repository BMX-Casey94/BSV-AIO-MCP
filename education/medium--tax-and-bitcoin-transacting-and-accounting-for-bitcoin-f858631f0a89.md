# Tax and Bitcoin — Transacting and accounting for Bitcoin — core principles

- **The blockchain is a public, permanent journal.** Every transaction is reported on-chain and can be viewed; businesses should log payer and payee addresses, tax identifiers and the transaction ID alongside the fiat market rate.
- **Addresses do not name the parties.** The ledger is public but payer and payee are not disclosed; record-keeping binds identity to the transaction ID for audit.
- **A tax invoice can be built from the chain plus the rate.** Coupled with the market rate in the functional currency, the business can invoice from the on-chain record.
- **Trading books should look like a brokerage statement.** Date, description, quantity, price and fees — the same fields as stock or forex dealing.
- **Mining follows ordinary business accounting.** Revenue treatment decides when proceeds are recognised; all mining expenses are recorded in concord with normal practice.
- **Commerce and payroll use the rate at the time.** Sales keep invoice references, bitcoin amount and date; GST uses a weighted-average rate at sale; payroll is calculated in the functional currency, then paid in bitcoin at the market rate with the transaction ID as proof of payment.
- **Bitcoin is money within the ordinary meaning.** Treating it as money for tax gives certainty and consistent treatment of real-world transactions irrespective of the form of money used.

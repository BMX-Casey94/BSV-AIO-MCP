# Zeno's Paradoxes and Bitcoin
**Date:** 2019-07-26 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/zenos-paradoxes-and-bitcoin-d96a0286ee7
**Subtitle:** A part of the reason for the number of bitcoin relates to a simple sequence that allows you to see how many bitcoin are left at a glance…

## Core thesis
An excerpt of a longer essay that continues on craigwright.net (filed under his "philosophy" category). Craig explains the design rationale for Bitcoin's supply schedule: the 50-BTC initial subsidy, halving every four years, forms a geometric sequence — Zeno's Dichotomy paradox — that lets anyone see at a glance roughly how many bitcoin remain. Because integer limits are finite, the subsidy disappears around 2140, and the total supply is "more than we need for all of the uses that will ever be developed."

## Key arguments and claims
- The supply number "relates to a simple sequence that allows you to see how many bitcoin are left at a glance, at least as an approximate figure" — readability of remaining supply as a design goal.
- The schedule: "the block subsidy started with 50 bitcoin each block, halving each block-reward period of four years" — a geometric series summing towards ~21 million.
- He maps it onto Zeno's Dichotomy paradox, quoting Aristotle, Physics VI:9: "That which is in locomotion must arrive at the half-way stage before it arrives at the goal."
- Unlike Zeno's infinite tasks, the series terminates in practice: "the limits on integers allow the subsidy to disappear after around 2140."
- Supply sufficiency claim: "There is an adequate number of bitcoin. More than we need for all of the uses that will ever be developed" — divisibility, not scarcity, does the work.
- Framed as an answer to a question put to him as the designer: "I've been asked why the block subsidy started with 50 bitcoin each block."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Classical-philosophy framing applied to a design parameter: he retro-explains the emission schedule as a deliberate choice grounded in a geometric series, with Aristotle as the citation. The mode is authorial design rationale — "I've been asked why…" answered from claimed first-hand intent — blending the mathematics of integer truncation (which ends the halvings) with teleology (supply sized for all future uses). Evidence style is textual and mathematical rather than empirical.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Cuts against the "digital gold" scarcity orthodoxy: Craig stresses adequacy and utility ("more than we need") rather than scarcity as the value driver — supply is a usability parameter, not a hard-money fetish object.
- Treats the subsidy's scheduled disappearance (~2140) as a non-event rather than the "security budget crisis" debated in BTC circles; the excerpt registers no anxiety about the fee market replacing subsidy.
- Engagement is indirect: this is primarily a design-history piece rather than a polemic, so the contradiction is by emphasis, not attack.

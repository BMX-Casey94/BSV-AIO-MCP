# Human rights and property — core principles

- **Property rights underpin all other freedom.** To be free, people must control their lives on their own terms, which requires securing property and "the security and integrity of those rights".
- **Privacy empowers the individual; it is not about shielding crime.** "This lack of privacy is not about protecting criminal acts but rather empowering the individual to live the life they want to live" — the right to live obscurely and silently.
- **Redeemable contracts: property rights abstracted from the person.** Tradable, secure property rights "abstracted from the individual and the use", stored encrypted and in a distributed manner across the internet, linked over the blockchain.
- **Tokenised shares separate ownership from occupation.** A house can be divided into tradable shares linked on-chain to the encrypted contracts: "you live in a house [but that] does not mean people need to know that you own it."
- **Privacy can be compliance-compatible by design.** The system is built to match the requirements of government tax authorities and AML/KYC provisions, and supports transfer in bankruptcy — privacy from the public, auditability for lawful authority, working within existing legal frameworks.
- **Corporate machinery can be built into the token structure.** Secure voting and revenue distribution are native: the homeowner pays market rent to the property, and a 100% owner has the money pseudonymously returned.
- **Tokenisation changes the risk economics of property.** Owners can sell shares to raise capital, or swap (e.g.) 30% of their home's shares for shares in other properties — diversifying the largest investment of their lives while rental income offsets their own rent without interest.
- **An evidentiary ledger protects the weak against the state.** The system lets people in repressive countries move funds and save, and keeps records of "depredation by governments in those countries that do not respect property rights".

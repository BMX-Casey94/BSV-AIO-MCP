# The Arithmetic of Trust — core principles

- **Accounting can be verified without disclosure.** Full disclosure destroys commercial privacy; redaction destroys verification. Commitments, Merkle inclusion proofs, selective retrieval and zero-knowledge arithmetic let a verifier learn that a calculation is correct without seeing the values.
- **A commitment is a locked box with a public silhouette.** It stops a dishonest party enjoying both secrecy and flexibility — privacy without opportunistic revision.
- **Merkle proves presence; arithmetic proves correctness.** Inclusion is not correctness. Zero-knowledge alone is insufficient: a conjurer can prove a theorem about numbers invented that morning. The layers must be integrated and anchored to the evidence population.
- **Proofs have an audit boundary.** They do not prove invoices were legitimate, goods delivered, write-offs reasonable, or recognition compliant with the standards. Range proofs exclude hidden negatives and overflow tricks. A document asks to be believed; a proof demands to be checked.
- **The public medium is a control dependency, not a deity.** It must offer append-only persistence, independently verifiable ordering, durable availability, documented finality, retention and equivocation protection. The proof server is a servant, never the source of truth.
- **When proof becomes cheaper, trust becomes less theatrical.** Costly state verification is refined: the auditor does not need to see everything to know something. That is precision, not weakness.
- **Seven risks stay named.** Garbage-in, incomplete population, circuit error, parameter risk, metadata leakage, legal misunderstanding, and worship — marketing arithmetic proof as if it were an audit opinion.

# A Pretty Curve Is Not an Economic Theory
**Date:** 2026-06-07 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/a-pretty-curve-is-not-an-economic
**Subtitle:** Why BTC “power law” charts prove much less than their believers think

## Core thesis
The BTC "power law" — a regression of log price on log time — is chronology pretending to be economics. A high R² is an in-sample goodness-of-fit statistic, not evidence of causality, stability, or monetary theory, and equally strong or stronger logged fits are produced by internet adoption, railway buildout, mobile phones, the US housing bubble, and even a Ponzi scheme's manufactured account statements. The curve cannot distinguish adoption from mania, bubble, or fraud; only an economic mechanism can.

## Key arguments and claims
- The model is simply ln(Pt) = α + β ln(t) + εt, back-transformed to Pt = A·t^β. Price is a function of time alone — not transaction demand, settlement volume, merchant use, velocity, cash flow, or marginal utility.
- R² = 1 − [Σ(yi − ŷi)² / Σ(yi − ȳ)²] measures variance explained in logged space, in sample. Granger and Newbold (1974) on spurious regressions and Phillips (1986) on non-stationary series show trending variables can yield impressive, meaningless fits.
- Taxonomy of models: log-linear (exponential growth), log-log (the BTC power-law form), and log-periodic power-law models of bubbles (Johansen, Ledoit and Sornette, 2000). Serious bubble modelling is not drawing a line through a log-log chart.
- Power-law distributions (Pr(X ≥ x) ∝ x^−α) are a different claim from price-over-time power laws; Clauset, Shalizi and Newman (2009) show such claims need maximum-likelihood estimation and goodness-of-fit tests against lognormal and exponential alternatives — standards BTC charts never meet.
- Counterexample fits on public FRED/NBER data: world internet adoption 1995–2023, R² = 0.9955; cumulative US railroad miles 1830–1890, R² = 0.9827; global mobile subscriptions 2005–2025, R² = 0.9697; US housing bubble (Case-Shiller, 1997–2006, log-linear), R² = 0.9866. BTC advocates report R² ≈ 0.95–0.96 — lower than a bubble and a diffusion process.
- A Ponzi reporting constant returns, A(t) = A0(1 + r)^t, yields a perfect logged straight line, R² = 1.0000 by construction; Madoff's SEC post-mortem treated the smoothness itself as a red flag.
- Tulip mania is not a lazy refutation: Garber (1989) explains rare-bulb prices via propagation fundamentals, Thompson (2007) via the conversion of forwards into option-like claims — demonstrating that contract structure, delivery and enforcement, not curves, carry the explanation.
- Price is not adoption: custody is not circulation, ETF exposure is not peer-to-peer cash. A monetary system would be judged by transaction volume, merchant acceptance, unit-of-account usage, wage denomination, velocity, fee stability and payment finality — none of which the chart uses.
- The fatal problem is the absence of any independent economic variable: Price = f(time) cannot distinguish productive adoption, speculative mania, liquidity bubble, Ponzi accounting, manipulation or narrative momentum.
- Parameter fragility: shifting the start date, price source, sampling frequency or early-illiquidity window materially changes β. A robust structural law should not depend on arbitrary choices.
- The out-of-sample test: a model estimated to 2014 should forecast 2015–2020 without recalibration; redrawing bands, shifting origins and adding halving corrections is "a story being protected from reality".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Econometric falsification by counterexample: he states the model formally, defines what R² actually measures, then fits the identical specification to adoption, infrastructure, bubble and fraud series to show the "evidence" is generic to any smooth trend. The underlying demand is mechanism-first economics — every genuine case (railways, internet, housing, Ponzi) has a specified causal mechanism, while the BTC chart is "presented as its own explanation".

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly attacks the power-law/log-regression school (R² ≈ 0.95–0.96 claims) as "numerology with regression output" rather than a theory of money.
- Rejects the slide between price, adoption, use and monetary success: "When price rises, they call it adoption. When speculators buy, they call it use."
- Rejects destiny-by-curve: in-sample fit is cheap, and only out-of-sample prediction without parameter fiddling counts.

# The 1937 Crash
**Date:** 2018-09-13 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-1937-crash-c9ab5c3f5521
**Subtitle:** Social security was the start of a crash every bit as large as the 1929 stock market correction. Yet, this event seems to be overlooked and…

## Core thesis
The 1937 recession was caused by the introduction of US Social Security — a "good intention" intervention that crowded out private investment — not by an organic business cycle. Craig generalises: crashes follow government intervention, and Bitcoin, as a "translucent and immutable ledger", is needed to make all government spending monitorable and those in charge accountable.

## Key arguments and claims
- Headline claim: "Social security was the start of a crash every bit as large as the 1929 stock market correction", an event "overlooked and forgotten".
- Causal pattern: crashes are preceded by policies of "good intention" acting "at then unseen expense (and at the cost of other people's money)".
- Mechanism: Social Security (effective 1937) "took a large sum out of the economy"; government spending "crowed [crowded] out private investment", producing "nearly 19% of the US workforce being made unemployed" — undercutting the "Roosevelt lead recovery" narrative.
- It was structurally unfunded at launch: "a system that had nothing in it to actually fund people for many years", built on "a pyramid built on the notion that the population will always increase".
- Second-order damage: corporate profits "were cut by as much as 80% of the lowered profits seen in the already limited years of the early 30's" and "industrial output slumped by over 40%".
- Social fallout: in the year after Social Security's introduction, "the number of people requiring help rose by 400% in cities such as Detroit"; recovery took another four years.
- Reframe: "we continue to fail to see the cause of crashes as interventionist policy and maintain the cause as business cycles" — better to see cycles as "uncertainty generated through political 'good will'". The episode also began "the military industrial complex".
- Bitcoin pivot: "This is one of the reasons we need a global money — Bitcoin. As a translucent and immutable ledger, it provides the means to monitor and report on all government spending and hold those in charge accountable."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Revisionist economic history in an Austrian/public-choice register: crowding-out and regime-uncertainty mechanisms (echoing Robert Higgs's account of the Roosevelt era) supported by specific statistics (19% unemployment, 80% profit cuts, 40% output slump, 400% relief-roll growth). The argumentative move is then analogical — if intervention causes hidden waste, a transparency technology that exposes state spending is the remedy — shifting from economic history to a Bitcoin thesis.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Inverts the dominant crypto pitch of Bitcoin as an escape from state visibility: here Bitcoin's value is that it makes *government* spending transparent and auditable — a sunlight tool for accountability, not a shield from the state.
- Contradicts mainstream macroeconomic orthodoxy (New Deal historiography, Keynesian demand management) by treating Social Security as a crash trigger rather than a safety net.
- Sits awkwardly with "number go up" Bitcoin marketing: the ledger's political function — auditing state waste — is the deliverable, not price appreciation.

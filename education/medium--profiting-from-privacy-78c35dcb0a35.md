# Profiting from privacy — core principles

- **Privacy, not anonymity, is the communications model.** "You cannot communicate privately in a system that is anonymous," because unverifiable counterparties destroy the trust intimacy requires. "Intimacy requires trust. Anonymity destroys trust."
- **Micropayments replace selling the user.** Under the advertising model "your profile is sold to pay for your application access." With Bitcoin, "users can pay for what they use without sacrificing their personal information." The user pays — a small percentage of marketplace sales or a small subscription — instead of advertising.
- **Logs should be private and attributable.** A digital lock should log entries privately but not anonymously — "you do not want an anonymous trail but rather one that is private and not available to the general population" — valuable for insurance and security, and achievable with Bitcoin.
- **Revocation without deletion.** Messages need not be deleted; access is withdrawn by removing the sharing key so they can no longer be decrypted — a provably self-destructing service using the blockchain as timestamp server, with full key recovery and dealer or dealer-less threshold systems.
- **Pseudonymous identity imposes cost and accountability.** Users "start to be held to cost and account," which mitigates the dismal state of unaccountable platforms.
- **The wallet becomes the launchpad.** The future is a wallet that is chat application, services launchpad and browser — blockchain-anchored keys, threshold secret-sharing, revocable access — with privacy plus an audit trail.
- **Interoperability is one system, not many chains.** "It is not achieved by having many many blockchains but rather by allowing one system to integrate with all other platforms."

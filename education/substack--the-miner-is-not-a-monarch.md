# The Miner Is Not a Monarch — core principles

- **Miners timestamp; they do not legislate.** Transaction validation confers no political authority. Miners are commercial entities inside jurisdictions, and compliance with valid court orders over identifiable property is legal obligation, not discretionary governance.
- **A private key is evidence of control, not indefeasible title.** Possession is not ownership. English courts already treat crypto-assets as property; a thief holding a key possesses the coins without acquiring *dominium*.
- **Protocol validity is not legal entitlement.** A protocol can determine whether a signature is mathematically valid; it cannot determine whether it was produced through extortion, fraud, theft, insanity, duress or breach of fiduciary duty. Code is procedure. Law concerns rights.
- **Recoverability is a prerequisite for institutional capital.** No serious institution allocates substantial capital into a system where stolen assets are irrecoverable by definition. Lending, custody, trusts, insurance, probate and corporate treasury all require enforceable ownership.
- **Irreversibility is a trade-off, not a virtue.** It reduces settlement risk while raising theft, operational, key-management and insider risk. Absolute irreversibility drives ordinary users into custodians; legal recoverability can enhance distributed participation.
- **Bitcoin was infrastructural, not revolutionary.** It solved double-spending through timestamped proof-of-work ordering. Satoshi described a payment system. Railways, telecommunications and the internet all integrated with law; recovery doctrine is that integration, not a weakening of the chain.
- **Voluntary consensus coordinates blocks, not sovereignty over property.** If a court determines a UTXO belongs to Party A, the miner's role is operational compliance. Refusal does not create freedom; it creates liability. Capital intensity gives miners strong economic incentives to comply regardless of ideology.

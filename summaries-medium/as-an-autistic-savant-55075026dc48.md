# As an Autistic Savant…
**Date:** 2020-04-16 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/as-an-autistic-savant-55075026dc48
**Subtitle:** Everything in life comes with trade-offs. Something that few people understand is part of understanding who I am for myself. I have…

## Core thesis
A personal essay (Medium excerpt; the body continues on craigwright.net) in which Craig frames his autism — "I have Asperger's, or, as they call it now, autism spectrum disorder (ASD)" — as a set of trade-offs: an extremely high IQ counterbalanced by a severe, lifelong deficit in social skills. He connects his autistic traits (brutal honesty, literal precision with language, being "better with code than with words") to his conduct as, in his words, the creator of Bitcoin, arguing he never wanted the spotlight and is "far from being the ideal spokesman" for his own invention. The essay concludes that Bitcoin was designed as a lawful micropayments cash system with a stable protocol, before it was "hijacked".

## Key arguments and claims
- He has ASD; he claims "one of the highest IQs you're likely to encounter", counterbalanced by social skills that, even after 30 years of training, "don't match the social skills of the average teenager" at nearly age 50.
- Honesty as long-term optimisation: nobody has "quantitatively" convinced him that lying is ever the better option, except to protect life from a totalitarian regime or unjust authority. He rejects "white lies" because telling someone they look good when they don't "will only lead to them being ridiculed" and prevents improvement.
- Precision in language: most people are imprecise because speech is "contextualised within the environment. People assume far more than they should." His literal use of words "does not always correspond to the contextual sense".
- Bitcoin script as predicate logic: "Bitcoin is a predicate." A validated script can be true, false, or syntactically invalid; only syntactically correct predicates yield a binary outcome. Partially valid branching scripts can be syntactically invalid for some inputs yet return valid true/false outputs for others — "It is a logical fallacy to believe that a syntactically incomplete scenario must be necessarily false."
- Fallacy taxonomy: a true conclusion can result from fallacious reasoning (e.g. "vegetables are good because they are popular"); the bandwagon fallacy invalidates the argument but "does not in itself invalidate the result of the associated claim".
- "I did not want the attention that came with the creation of Bitcoin. It was never and can never be a system that runs without human interaction."
- Anti-fork position: "It is not a system with forks; to be Bitcoin, it must have a stable protocol."
- Pre-"hijacking" Bitcoin was "a cash system primarily focussed on micropayments" that "would act within the law, and it was not something that would be able to act outside of law".
- Autobiographical detail: childhood martial arts training used to consciously learn body movement, walking and posture that "for most people would be something that naturally progresses".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Autobiographical disclosure fused with formal logic: he moves from his cognitive profile (ethos for why he acts as he does) into a truth-functional analysis of Bitcoin script and an informal-fallacy taxonomy (bandwagon, dialectics). The method is classificatory — drawing distinctions (true/false/syntactically invalid; literal vs contextual meaning) — and self-referential, using his autism both as explanation for past behaviour and as evidence of truthfulness ("Lying is not something I do easily or well").

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- "Trustless code, no humans needed": he insists Bitcoin "was never and can never be a system that runs without human interaction".
- Forking as legitimate upgrade/governance mechanism (the 2017 BCH fork, and BTC's own social-consensus model): for him a forked system is by definition not Bitcoin; a stable protocol is constitutive.
- The anti-state, "censorship-resistant money" ethos of 2020-era crypto: Bitcoin "was not something that would be able to act outside of law".
- The romanticised Satoshi myth: he presents the creator as socially impaired and unwilling, directly against the community's idealised spokesman image.

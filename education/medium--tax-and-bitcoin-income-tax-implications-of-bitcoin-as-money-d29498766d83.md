# Tax and Bitcoin — Income tax implications of Bitcoin as money — core principles

- **Bitcoin is money in the form of intangible property, and capable of ownership.** Existing income-tax categories already capture mining, holding, using and trading; no new law is required.
- **Whether mining is a business is a fact.** It turns on investment, profit intention, size and scale; a GST enterprise is likely also an income-tax business.
- **Hobby mining is not ordinary income.** Coins awarded without system or organisation fall outside ordinary concepts of income.
- **Business miners hold bitcoin as trading stock.** Anything produced or acquired and held for sale or exchange is trading stock; acquisition costs are deductible and year-end value movements are assessable or deductible.
- **Spending bitcoin is a disposal.** The recipient includes the amount in assessable income; the payer may deduct unless the outgoing is capital, private or related to exempt income; CGT can also apply.
- **Outside a business, disposal is a CGT event.** Bitcoin is a CGT asset as intangible property / a chose in action; anti-overlap rules prevent double counting with ordinary income.
- **Bitcoin can be treated as foreign currency for translation.** Amounts are converted to the home currency at exchange rates from bitcoin exchanges or forex providers; transacting can generate forex realisation gains or losses even without trading on an exchange.
- **Mere holding is not a realisation event.** Volatility creates exposure when you transact, not from holding in isolation — unless a large taxpayer is in a regime that marks foreign currency to revenue account.

# A Bitcoin Smart Risk Contract — core principles

- **Assign liability to the cheapest avoider.** The rule that creates the best incentives is the doctrine of avoidable consequences (marginal-cost liability): the breaching party is not liable for damage the victim could have mitigated.
- **Users have pre-breach duties.** They must install and maintain the system in a secure state; no single vendor can account for every combination of products.
- **Security is a priced trade-off, not a proof.** Bug-free software cannot be proven; optimal security is discovered by markets, including a market for vulnerabilities.
- **Testing stops where buying the bug is cheaper.** Vendors extend in-house testing until its cost exceeds the cost of purchasing the vulnerability from specialist researchers.
- **Reputation is an accounting asset.** Accumulated vulnerabilities impose a reputational cost; goodwill has real value in business and capital transactions even without formal liability.
- **Buyers price the patching burden.** Rational users incorporate the cost of patching into the purchase price, so even low-vulnerability vendors are charged for expected maintenance.
- **Risk-based contracts let the market set the price.** Features versus security can be modelled, expensed and chosen; hazards are allocated rather than wished away.
- **Responsibility runs on both sides.** The user maintains optimal controls (patching, configuration); the vendor pays the optimal level for testing and mitigation. Disclaimers do not erase priced duties.

# Wiki
**Date:** 2019-04-16 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/wiki-2578e6c0ace5
**Subtitle:** At the time, Wikipedia wanted to remove the page. There wasn't anything good that had been published which detailed and described Bitcoin…

## Core thesis
A first-person account of why the Bitcoin white paper cites Adam Back and Wei Dai but not Nick Szabo: the references were chosen for the practical help they gave in 2008 (and to keep Wikipedia from deleting the Bitcoin article), not as a genealogy of ideas. Bitcoin, he insists, does not descend from Back's hashcash — whose released code "didn't work" — but from an unresponsive group's "Aurora paper", and its design goal was cash that is also an "informational commodity".

## Key arguments and claims
- Wikipedia wanted to delete the Bitcoin page; since only the white paper described Bitcoin, references were scraped together partly to satisfy Wikipedia's sourcing rules — "people would take away the wrong concept" from them.
- He "dropped off the 'Cypherpunks' list… around 2002" and so "didn't even know about b-money and all the work Wei Dai had done until mid 2008 or so".
- Adam Back is referenced "not because he was the inventor of proof-of-work but for his help at the time"; Back was "well down my list, but he responded" when others (including "Aurora", emailed in 2007) did not.
- He only later learned that the working version of hashcash came from Hal Finney: "the code Adam Back had put out there didn't work", and "it was when I was talking to Hal that I discovered he had taken Adam Back's code and made it work".
- Back's real contribution was pointing him to Wei Dai; Wei in turn "pointed out Szabo's bit gold boondoggle" — hence no Szabo citation. Wei "did not come across as a complete anarchist".
- Lineage claim: "Bitcoin isn't Adam's hashcash invention as he puts it. It doesn't use the code, and is not even similar to what Adam had released. It came from the Aurora paper."
- The b-money reference was comparative, "as a way to compare a cart and a car" — not an admission of descent.
- The paper "initially started with the title 'Electronic Cash Without a Trusted Third Party,' which is important. It remains primarily cash. But to be money, it needs to also be a commodity… an informational commodity that takes data and commercialises it."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Autobiographical reconstruction as evidence: he re-litigates the white paper's bibliography name by name, substituting a mundane story of emails, dead ends and Wikipedia deletion politics for the heroic cypherpunk lineage. The method is deflationary distinction-drawing — help versus invention, comparison versus descent, cash versus money versus commodity — delivered in a confessional, corrective register ("I definitely had no idea about Nick Szabo").

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies the canonical lineage in which Bitcoin synthesises hashcash, b-money and bit gold: hashcash's code is dismissed as broken and unused, b-money as unknown until mid-2008, bit gold as a "boondoggle" he was warned off.
- Undercuts Adam Back's implied claim on Bitcoin ("Bitcoin isn't Adam's hashcash invention as he puts it"), a direct shot in the era's priority disputes.
- Reframes the white paper's purpose against the digital-gold reading: "It remains primarily cash", with money-status requiring commodity-like commercialisation of data — an early statement of the data-commodity theme later central to his Metanet writing.

## Connections
Part of his 2019 Satoshi-authorship campaign, supplying provenance detail for the white paper's reference list (Back, Wei Dai) that sceptics had used to map Bitcoin onto cypherpunk prior art. The "Electronic Cash Without a Trusted Third Party" title claim and the informational-commodity line connect to his later Metanet/data-valuation essays; the Aurora paper reference is a recurring, otherwise undocumented element of his origin story.

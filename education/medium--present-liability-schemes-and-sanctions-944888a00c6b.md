# Present Liability Schemes and Sanctions — core principles

- **Law targets the cheapest cost avoider.** The actor with the best capability to thwart most forms of Internet-related misconduct is the primary malfeasor: a worm's creator for its harm, the commercial site for illegal content, the interactive operator for illegal gambling — direct control of these actors thwarts much of the social harm.
- **Direct regulation of end users fails online.** Actors are often judgement-proof and transactions numerous and low-value, so direct regulation of individuals remains ineffective (Lemley & Reese; William W. Fisher III, *Promises to Keep*, 2004); Internet intermediaries are therefore both the target of opportunity and the answer to judgement-proof actors.
- **Code does not dissolve human responsibility.** Code does not create itself and launch; with a DEX or a "smart-contract-based exchange", it is again the people in the system who are responsible.
- **No ICO can be "decentralised".** The primary malfeasance is from those who create the offering, and they remain liable regardless of claims of decentralisation.
- **Settlement rails are legally irrelevant.** The exchange remains between people even when it is settled on a blockchain; the process of settlement has no impact on the legality of a security.
- **Tokens sold to raise funds are securities under existing law.** An ICO is just a security under law, as all securities have been through history — from pink sheets to web IPOs, ICOs are not even remotely novel — so tokenisation must be structured within long-standing securities law.

# An Open Market for Intelligence — core principles

- **Bitcoin coordinates an economy of intelligence.** Peer-to-peer electronic cash with negligible transaction costs lets agents charge fractions of a penny for specialised work. Every contribution is priced, every service measured, every participant rewarded — payment for cognitive work rather than advertising- or subscription-funded intelligence.
- **Specialisation plus trade outperforms the monolithic knower.** Millions of agents trained around their owners' expertise — law, medicine, engineering, research, logistics, accounting — find one another, cooperate, verify and compete. The objective is not one artificial intelligence but an economy of intelligence.
- **Reputation is measurable capital.** Trust ceases to be institutional brand and becomes a performance history of accuracy, reliability and expertise, evaluated by other agents, users and markets. The most trusted earn more work; the least trusted disappear.
- **Ownership of agents keeps knowledge distributed.** Individuals and communities retain the agents they train. Expertise stays with its owners while contributing globally; it need not be surrendered to a central platform.
- **Discovery indexes intelligence, not documents.** A request routes to the most appropriate specialists, and a third agent may independently verify the conclusions. The result emerges from collaboration between specialised systems rather than from a single general-purpose model.
- **Verification itself is a paid service.** As fees approach negligible levels, a verification agent earns revenue auditing other agents' work; a training agent licenses knowledge; a research agent is compensated for discovery. Cheap cash is the precondition for machine-to-machine commerce.

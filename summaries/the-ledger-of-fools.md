# The Ledger of Fools
**Date:** 2025-11-25 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-ledger-of-fools
**Subtitle:** A future satire of bottlenecked money, custodial empires, and the civilisation that mistook a price chart for a constitution.

## Core thesis
A dystopian novella arguing by dramatisation that a civilisation built on a deliberately bottlenecked BTC must degenerate into a theocratic price cult: the base chain cannot carry ordinary commerce, so custodial "Lightning Dukes" tax daily life, mining cartels drain the power grid, and the state polices "confidence" because arithmetic itself has become heresy. The protagonist, Protocol Compliance Auditor Hale, is radicalised not by argument but by watching the system's euphemisms injure and kill.

## Key arguments and claims
- The city wakes to "the Soundness Report"; the price is displayed before weather and emergency alerts ("UNAUTHORISED DOUBT DETECTED IN DISTRICT 9"); slogans include "BUY LESS. HODL MORE. FEES ARE FREEDOM."
- Citizenship is wallet-tiered: a family is demoted from Tier Three to Tier Two because their district's "utilisation signal" fell below threshold; voting is staking ("pay-to-exist"), celebrated on "Stakers' Day."
- The clinic scene: a mother cannot clear an on-chain fee for her asthmatic child and is told to wait for "low mempool hours"; the attendant warns that "negative statements here can be interpreted as confidence harm."
- The Lightning Dukes — routing oligarchs in a mirrored tower — insist "We're not banks… We're just… facilitators of freedom," while every drink at their reception pays an invisible routing fee; one guest jokes it is "air traffic control with a tollbooth."
- The "Free Hash League" triggers brownouts during price surges; a maternity wing goes dark during the Great Halving Jubilee while the Minister of Narrative Integrity declares scarcity "the mother of virtue"; hospitals are branded "energy gluttons" and blackouts "the courageous pain of soundness."
- Retired engineer Halden keeps a banned basement archive of original design notes: "The base layer was meant to scale on-chain. Always… Capacity expands, fees fall, ordinary commerce lives on the rail." Official history is "a children's story written by toll collectors."
- The Museum of Immutability indoctrinates schoolchildren ("Why are blocks small?" — "Because we are strong!"); its Heresy Wall stamps "INCREASE BLOCK SIZE" with a skull icon, labels "ON-CHAIN SCALING" as "PONZI THINKING" and "ANCHORING TO FIAT" as "SUBMISSION TO THE OLD WORLD."
- In a televised "Dialogue for Confidence," the Minister transmutes Hale's throughput arithmetic into moral failure — "Faith is function" — and his public humiliation is then cast as proof the regime tolerates dissent.
- A mining cartel war over block space curdles the mempool; freight, ports and antibiotic shipments seize up; shelves empty while the price climbs on panic-buying, beneath a billboard reading "WE ARE RICHER THAN EVER."
- Hale refuses to detain Halden and to draft a narrative reframing shortages as proof of soundness; the two flee with the archive.
- A closing authorial rant drops the fiction: "Five transactions a second and they call it a revolution… Visa sneezes harder than that"; BTC culture is "Idiocracy with wallets," its asset "a lottery ticket with a halo."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Satirical extrapolation in the Orwellian mode: take BTC's actual parameters — the five-transactions-per-second ceiling, fee auctions, halvings, hash-rate competition, custodial Layer Two — and follow their incentive logic forward into institutions. The fiction is a vehicle for incentive analysis: every horror (tiered citizenship, narrative policing, energy rationing) is presented as the mechanical downstream of treating a chokepoint as sacred rather than as a defect to be engineered away.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Small blocks as moral virtue vs an engineering defect that forces commerce into custody.
- Lightning as liberation vs "the same old thing with a new bolt on the sticker" — rebranded banking with tolls.
- The fee market as fair rationing vs an auction that prices the vulnerable out of medicine and bread.
- Mining energy use as "security" vs a siphon that blacks out maternity wards.
- HODL culture as prudence vs a hoarding cult whose price rises precisely as real goods disappear.
- "Not your keys" ideology inverted: in a bottlenecked world custodians are unavoidable and become the practical state.

## Notable quotes
- "The fee is the fee… You can wait for low mempool hours if you like. The protocol is fair to everyone."
- "A civilisation built on a bottleneck does not debate bottlenecks; it romanticises them, and if possible, blames the sick."
- "It did not fall because it could not see. It fell because it preferred not to."
- "If the rail cannot carry life, life is pushed into tolls; if tolls become the system, the system becomes a lord."

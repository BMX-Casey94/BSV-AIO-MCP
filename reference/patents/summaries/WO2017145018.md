## Problem addressed
Ordinary financial transfers are slow and costly, and known blockchain exchanges still rely on third-party escrow and multi-currency processors. The specification wants parties to offer and complete an exchange of cryptocurrency, tokens, contracts, goods or services on a peer-to-peer ledger without changing the underlying protocol and without a trusted intermediary holding sensitive data.

## Core mechanism
A user builds a script (typically a P2SH redeem script) that embeds invitation metadata — what is offered, a geographical location condition, and the user’s public key — then hashes the script and publishes both script and hash on a distributed hash table. A matching invitation on the DHT is found by comparing offered entities. Fulfilment of the location condition is checked with a GPS reading of one or both users against the stated location or area. Once invitations match and the location condition holds, one or two exchange transactions spend the invitation outputs and transfer the agreed entities; metadata can sit in public-key slots so protocol-agnostic nodes still relay the script. Tokens representing contracts may be pegged to an underlying bitcoin amount and signed by an issuer.

## Independent claims (gist — not legal scope)
A method and system generate a hashed invitation script with entity and location metadata, publish it on a DHT, match it to a second invitation by comparing offered entities, and decide whether a GPS reading satisfies the location condition. A processor claim covers performing that method.

## BSV relevance
The invitation-and-P2SH token exchange, with metadata in redeem scripts and DHT look-up, maps onto BSV token and marketplace flows that already use P2SH redeem-script metadata.

# Defining smart contracts — core principles

- **Writing was never the general requirement.** English law forms contracts on offer, acceptance, consideration and intention to create legal relations, with writing required only for exceptions such as real-property transfers — so electronic and Bitcoin-based contracts inherit full legal standing, and the only live questions are evidential.
- **Electronic form equals writing.** The Electronic Communications Act 2000 provides that the electronic form of a contract is to be accepted as equivalent to a contract in writing.
- **Code is not law.** A court retains the ability to overturn or adjudicate any contract, even those made digitally; a smart contract is evidence before a court, not sovereign execution.
- **Evidentiary weight follows signature strength.** "Electronic evidence must hold greater weight than verbal evidence" (Lord Justice Auld, 2001); the weight of a given piece turns on the strength of the digital signature algorithm and the security of the signing mechanisms — Bitcoin's digital-signature structure gives its contracts high evidentiary value admissible in a UK court.
- **Twofold structure of an electronic contract.** It comprises the stored sequence of numbers and code on a medium, and the perceptible rendering on an output device (Bainbridge 2000; Reed 2004) — the dichotomy that troubled the writing question before the ECA resolved it.
- **Equivalence activates the parole evidence rule.** Once the electronic form counts as writing, extrinsic evidence that could change the contract's terms is excluded, subject to partial-integration exceptions (Treitel 2003; Durtschi 2002; Lim 2002).
- **Signature admissibility is statutory.** ECA s7(1) admits an electronic signature and its certification on any question of authenticity or integrity of the communication or data.
- **Script is contracting infrastructure.** Bitcoin's signature mechanism allows complex structured contracts to be negotiated and saved by business — so the full original script functionality must be preserved for Bitcoin to serve as a contracting platform.

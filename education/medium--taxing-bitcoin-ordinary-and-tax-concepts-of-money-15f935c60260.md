# Taxing Bitcoin — Ordinary and tax concepts of “Money” — core principles

- **Bitcoin satisfies the ordinary meaning of money.** Money is any generally accepted medium of exchange for goods, services and debts; it need not be legal tender.
- **Common consent, not state issue, makes money.** It is common consent and conduct that give a thing the character of money (Miller v Race; Moss v Hancock).
- **Money is an abstract use, not a physical object.** Bank balances transferred between persons are money; an intangible such as Bitcoin can be money by the same test.
- **Currency has a proprietary sense: negotiability.** Title to money is exempt from *nemo dat*; the right to money is inseparable from possession, and a recipient need not enquire into the payer’s title.
- **Legal tender is narrower than money.** A polity may prescribe what must be accepted as payment; not all money is legal tender.
- **Wallet-to-wallet transfer exhibits negotiability.** Circulation of valuable rights transferred irrevocably and immediately by electronic order makes bitcoin a form of intangible personal property that functions as money.
- **Tax statutes enlarge, they do not exhaust, that meaning.** GST “includes” lists and income-tax silence both leave the ordinary meaning in place — Bitcoin falls within them as money, including as foreign currency.
- **Medium of exchange is the test that matters.** Money is what passes freely from hand to hand in final discharge of debts, not what is hoarded as a collectible.

# What Is Proof? — core principles

- **Proof is a transaction, not a property of a claim.** Warrant is delivered to someone, across a standard, inside a system, for a purpose. No notion of proof is framework-free: every chain of justification stops at chosen, unproven premises.
- **Match the standard to the stakes and declare the floor.** Name the axioms, legal standard or threat model; refuse the level-jump that offers proof at one level and credits it at another. Law already does this: balance of probabilities for money, beyond reasonable doubt for liberty.
- **Digital signatures prove key custody, not identity.** A signature shows that the holder of a private key signed a message. Steal the key and you inherit the proof. Keys are evidence, not title.
- **Zero-knowledge proofs drive soundness near — never to — zero.** Deceit is made so improbable that no rational adversary attempts it: a definition of proof fit for the real world, not an absolute guarantee.
- **Proof-of-work is certainty as a cost curve.** It proves expenditure of computation. Ordering is not decreed true; it is made expensive to rewrite. Security is economic and probabilistic, decaying as honest work accumulates.
- **Practical certainty purchased with real resources beats an absolute certainty that does not exist.** Nakamoto's design accepts that ledger security is an attacker having to out-spend honest expenditure, not a logical guarantee of immutability.
- **Standards are defended and attacked; there is no standard behind all standards.** That is not relativism. It is the working discipline of every proof-serious field — mathematics, law, science and cryptography — independently arrived at.

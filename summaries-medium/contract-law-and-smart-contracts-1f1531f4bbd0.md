# Contract Law and Smart Contracts
**Date:** 2018-12-28 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/contract-law-and-smart-contracts-1f1531f4bbd0
**Subtitle:** Contract law is designed to minimise:

## Core thesis
A very short post (barely more than an excerpt) arguing that contract law exists as an economic-minimisation device: it minimises the cost of drafting contracts, the cost of courts rewriting them, and the inefficiency flowing from incomplete agreements. The implicit conclusion for smart contracts is that code cannot and should not try to specify everything — the legal system deliberately keeps contracts short because courts supply standardised default rulings. The body appears to be a fragment pointing to his longer SSRN paper on the subject.

## Key arguments and claims
- Contract law is designed to minimise three specific costs: "the cost of writing a contract", "the cost of courts rewriting contracts", and "inefficiency (including behaviour) from inefficient, poorly written, or incomplete contracts".
- Courts deliberately "use and select the rulings that lead to the fewest exculpatory clauses" — judicial precedent acts as a compression mechanism for contracting.
- The consequence of that judicial selection is that contracts can be "as short as possible and feasible" while still delivering the necessary terms and conditions.
- He anchors the claim in his own prior academic work, linking an SSRN paper (abstract_id=2957057) as the fuller treatment of "some of such things".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Law-and-economics reasoning: contract doctrine is modelled as a cost-minimisation institution rather than a moral or procedural one. The method is terse and axiomatic — three bullet-pointed cost categories, one mechanism (judicial selection against exculpatory clauses), and a citation to his own paper rather than in-post argument. The rhetorical mode is the compressed abstract: he states the framework and defers the proof to the linked SSRN text.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Cuts against the "code is law" orthodoxy of the smart-contract era (Ethereum especially, but also BTC maximalist script discourse): if law's function is to keep contracts short by supplying defaults, then attempting to encode complete agreements in immutable script misunderstands what contract law is for.
- Implicitly rejects the crypto-anarchist premise that courts are an inefficiency to be engineered away; here courts are the very mechanism that makes contracting cheap.

## Notable quotes
- "Contract law is designed to minimise: the cost of writing a contract; the cost of courts rewriting contracts; inefficiency (including behaviour) from inefficient, poorly written, or incomplete contracts."
- "Courts use and select the rulings that lead to the fewest exculpatory clauses."
- "Doing so leads to contracts being as short as possible and feasible, and still delivering the necessary terms and conditions."

# The story of Bitcoin, continued
**Date:** 2019-02-09 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-story-of-bitcoin-continued-2f1ec78ba38b
**Subtitle:** My first marriage ended in October 2010. It had been rocky for a time before that. I mentioned in my last post how I worked doing forensic…

## Core thesis
Continuing his autobiography, Craig frames Bitcoin's creation and Satoshi's anonymity through his claimed past as a prosecution-only forensic expert and covert operative: after anti-trafficking work in Venezuela/Colombia (where he says he was shot twice), he returned to find Silk Road turning his creation into a dark-web tool, which drove him to despair, to founding Panopticrypt, and to funding research through grey-market gaming code sold with Dave Kleiman via Liberty Reserve. The essay culminates in a radical legal claim: Bitcoin has an issuer — him — and BTC became "a new system with new issuers" when SegWit was added, while "Bitcoin (BSV) was issued in 2009".

## Key arguments and claims
- Timeline: first marriage ended October 2010; he "started to go quiet"; offline much of January 2011 working in Venezuela with a "Jawbreaker" team "focused on stopping the trafficking of humans for the sex trade… I did not bring people to justice, I worked with teams to stop things, permanently."
- He was "shot twice", met Colombian DAS agents on an operation "associated with garnishing evidence against FARC-V", and had been "what some people would call an 'agent of influence'". His uncle Don Lynam "was very senior within military intelligence".
- Design motive: "Terrorist financing occurs because of the existing banking system and the ability to lose transactional data." He "worked on Bitcoin for a long time before I found something that would not be able to be used in a manner that was anonymous" — private for daily life yet "the ultimate anathema to all that was evil in the world".
- "I designed and created Bitcoin to stop the need for people like me — people who worked with SAD operatives and even those acting in the tracing of funds."
- He was "a pastor for a time" but abandoned it after witnessing atrocities (children "as young as 10 with AK-74s"); "I designed Bitcoin to create an immutable evidence trail, money that is private and yet does not suffer the fate of Gyges. Anonymity is a curse. Nothing good comes of it."
- Silk Road accusation: it trafficked people, not just drugs — "Children between the age of 12 and 16 were exchanged using Silk Road"; "over 1000 children were exchanged in Western Europe through the north-eastern Italian corridor" (he cites Terre des Hommes). "Such is your hero if you worship and praise to the idol of Free Ross."
- Personal history: left chartered accountants BDO via a "golden-handshake redundancy" offered December 2008, funding 2009 research; in 2011 founded Panopticrypt to build "the solution to the problem I had birthed".
- Why Satoshi was anonymous: "My past working as a prosecution forensic expert and in other roles with the government would never lead to a system that I could have publicly stood behind."
- Kleiman confession: "In order to fund my work, Dave Kleiman and I sold code that was used in gaming illegal out of countries such as Costa Rica… Nothing he was able to earn in Panama was able to be repatriated legally in the USA. We used Liberty Reserve to hold funds and pay people." When the US seized Liberty Reserve in early 2013, funding stopped; "Shortly after and because of the stress and problems that came from it, my best friend was dead."
- Faustian framing via George Gilder's "Wealth and Poverty": "I decide on the name Faustus for such a reason. It is my bargain to stop what I see as wrong." Threat: "the more they now seek to discredit me, the more I am going to release. When I finish, even Atlas will shrug."
- The issuer claim: "Bitcoin has an issuer. It is a system that is set in stone and when changed is a new system with an airdrop, so a fork is a new system. BTC became a new system when the fork added SegWit, it is a new system with new issuers. I am the issuer for Bitcoin. There is no decentralised here… Bitcoin (BSV) was issued in 2009. BTC was issued with an airdrop in 2017."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Testimonial narrative as evidence: he stakes his authorship claim on granular, checkable-seeming biography (dates, agencies, operations, the BDO redundancy, Liberty Reserve) and on motive-consistency — a forensic investigator would build an evidence trail, not an anonymous cash. Morally he reasons by consequence and complicity (Silk Road's harms indict its defenders), and legally by reclassification: redefining a protocol-changing fork as a fresh "issue" converts the decentralisation debate into a property/securities frame where he is issuer.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the foundational "Bitcoin has no issuer, it is decentralised" claim of the entire space: he asserts "I am the issuer for Bitcoin. There is no decentralised here", and that BTC (post-SegWit, 2017) is a separate airdropped system with new issuers — inverting the usual BTC/BSV lineage claims.
- Contradicts the "Satoshi's identity doesn't matter / we are all Satoshi" orthodoxy: proving his identity "over the year" is presented as proving who the issuer is, with legal consequences.
- Contradicts the "Free Ross" clemency movement and the "darknet markets are peaceful commerce" narrative by alleging large-scale child trafficking through Silk Road.
- Contradicts the anti-surveillance reading of Bitcoin: its purpose is an "immutable evidence trail" that makes records impossible to lose — transparency as the feature, not the bug.

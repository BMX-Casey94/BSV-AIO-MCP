# The Coat Check Problem in the CLARITY Act — core principles

- **Separate Control, Entitlement and Property.** Control answers "who can act" (move, freeze, lend, delay, refuse); entitlement answers "what can be demanded and on what conditions"; property answers "who owns, even when everything breaks". The flattering word "balance" collapses all three questions into one.
- **The balance is not the coat; it is the ticket.** A venue's internal ledger entry is an assertion until matched to custody, segregation and enforceable rights that survive stress; the user interface is not a title deed, and a right to request withdrawal is not ownership, control, segregation or priority.
- **Capability is not custody, and classification is not segregation.** A statute can be perfectly clear about what the asset is yet remain unclear about what the customer has — and in an intermediated market the second question decides outcomes. A definition does not prevent commingling; a label does not decide priority in insolvency.
- **"Customer property" must be welded to four custody facts.** (1) Segregation as individually identifiable assets, not mere "separate accounting" — a spreadsheet comfort that is no barrier against commingling, double use or estate capture; (2) enforceable withdrawal with narrow, objective limits — a right pausable whenever liquidity is tight is not a right, it is a marketing sentence; (3) reuse constraints forcing the business to choose between safekeeping custody with strict limits and an explicitly regulated credit product; (4) insolvency priority keeping customer assets out of the intermediary's general estate, with return in kind where possible.
- **The middle position is the precise abuse to prevent.** Calling it custody while using it like funding monetises customer dependence without admitting it; drafting should force the candid choice between safekeeping and credit.
- **Draft mechanical, unmisreadable protections.** Custody-layer segregation that is auditable and estate-resistant; continuous, enforceable auditability (record-keeping standards, reconciliation, retention, penalties outweighing the temptation to blur lines) rather than periodic attestations or marketing dashboards; presumed prompt withdrawal with delays only under objectively testable circumstances, time limits and notice requirements; explicit insolvency treatment a bankruptcy court cannot ignore.
- **Classify relationships, not merely assets.** Statutory boxes and agency lanes do not change who holds the keys; regulation should impose custody mechanics that make misdescription expensive.
- **Stress-test every rule by what happens when the counter breaks.** Insolvency-facing substance over calm-conditions appearance — customers must not discover only when there is smoke in the room that they were holding tickets.

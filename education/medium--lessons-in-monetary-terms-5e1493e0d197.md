# Lessons in monetary terms — core principles

- **Store of value is a legal property of a contract, not of an asset held.** It attaches to contracts denominated in a unit of account (the principle of nominalism), not to a commodity you merely hold. "A USD100 note stores USD100 worth of value even in a period of high inflation."
- **Money's characteristic feature is measurability.** Money is a commodity exchanged for commodities "whose immediate utility in consumption exceeds that of the monetary commodity"; it forms a unit of account. A debt is incurred in units of account, not in a particular currency note (*Adelaide Electric Supply Co. v Prudential* [1934] AC 122).
- **Bitcoin is a store of value only where the contract is denominated in it.** "If a contract is directly denominated within bitcoin, bitcoin is a store of value for such an exchange. But, holding and saving bitcoin does not make it a store of value outside of a contract denominated explicitly in bitcoin."
- **Store of wealth is a separate concept.** It "maintains a capital position and the ability for an individual to consume at a given rate." Equities are long-term but risky stores of wealth; bonds are shorter-term and lower-risk. "Value and wealth are completely separate."
- **An inflation hedge is a stability refuge.** It is what parties reach for when a unit of account suffers volatility — the US dollar for many African and South American countries, gold in war or uncertainty. Gold itself was a store of value only when it backed national currencies, "which no longer applies outside of contracts that are directly denominated in gold."
- **Receipt of money is presumed enrichment.** A recipient "will not be heard to argue that money is not a benefit"; liability can extend to interest or exchange-rate fluctuation (*Sempra Metals v IRC* [2007] UKHL 34).
- **Tracing follows the amount owed, not a share of the mixed fund.** Monetary value can mix into a larger fund without contributors automatically gaining proprietary rights in the asset; tracing recognises assets belonging to certain claimants (*Foskett v McKeown* [2001] 1 AC 102).

# Accountability Follows Control: English Private Law and the Governance of Bitcoin
**Date:** 2026-01-12 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/accountability-follows-control-english
**Subtitle:** Why informal collective rule-making attracts partnership, agency, fiduciary obligation, and equitable remedies under orthodox doctrine

## Core thesis
Where governance over Bitcoin is exercised in fact — collectively, informally, and without incorporation — yet produces binding outcomes relied upon by others, English private law already classifies that governance as legally accountable through orthodox partnership, agency, fiduciary and equitable doctrine. "Decentralisation" is not a legal shield, because responsibility follows control as a matter of doctrine, not policy. The supposed "accountability gap" is not a gap in the law but a refusal to apply existing law to the facts.

## Key arguments and claims
- The "accountability gap" is a misdescription: governance is exercised in fact while responsibility is denied in law by appeals to informality or decentralisation; it is "not a gap in conduct, but a gap in admission".
- Control is the linchpin and is proved factually, not rhetorically. Evidential indicators: gatekeeping (deciding what is accepted, rejected, implemented, enforced), agenda-setting (defining which questions may be considered at all), veto power (capacity to block outcomes), and persistence plus coordination over time.
- Partnership doctrine under the Partnership Act 1890 is the classificatory tool for informal collective governance: classification turns on conduct, not paperwork or subjective intention. The claim is bounded — only those exercising legally material control are partners; mere contributors, users and peripheral participants are not.
- "Business in common" is satisfied because governance is continuous rather than episodic, coordinated through identifiable processes, and produces authoritative outcomes that others treat as binding — distinguishing organised enterprise from casual association.
- "View to profit" is read broadly and realistically: not confined to dividends or equal shares; it includes strategic positioning, preferential access to information or opportunities, prioritisation of interests, and reputational capital translatable into economic opportunity.
- Mutual agency follows automatically from partnership classification: every partner is an agent of the firm, so rule-setting and enforcement decisions are acts of the firm itself. Authority may be actual (inferred from repeated practice and recognised roles) or ostensible (holding out plus reasonable reliance).
- Joint control requires no hierarchy: English law has never required a single controlling mind; veto power and mutual constraint demonstrate shared authority, and joint control attracts joint responsibility.
- Fiduciary duty is positional, not moral: discretionary power over others' interests, power asymmetry, and reliance trigger equity's strictness irrespective of honesty or good faith. Fiduciary doctrine is superior to negligence for this problem because negligence is constrained by proximity and pure-economic-loss limits.
- Remedies are orthodox and operational: constructive trust, tracing, account of profits (gain-based, not loss-based), equitable compensation, and interim relief restraining contested decisions or dissipation pending trial. Proprietary remedies confer priority over unsecured creditors and can survive insolvency.
- Misrepresentation is a supplementary route only, kept "in its proper place": the thesis depends on control, discretion and reliance, not on proving false statements. Tort boundaries are deliberately respected, and that restraint strengthens the analysis.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Doctrinal legal method, deliberately conservative and anti-exceptionalist: identify the facts (who decides, what the decisions do, how others treat them), classify those facts using established legal categories, then apply statute and case law. Technology is evidential context, never a source of immunity. The sequence is fixed — control triggers classification, classification gives rise to duty, duty attracts remedy — and no empirical modelling, policy argument or legal reform is required.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly rejects the "no defendant" posture: claiming that decentralisation, informality or collective decision-making dissolves responsibility is "an attempt to convert structural description into legal immunity".
- Rejects the consent/voluntary-participation defence common in crypto discourse: voluntary entry is not a waiver of accountability; reliance is precisely what makes authority accountable.
- Rejects technological exceptionalism: English private law has never allowed the means by which authority is exercised (boardroom, committee, distributed technical process) to determine whether responsibility arises.
- Contradicts the narrative that open participation negates control: distributed participation can coexist with coordinated authority, and the law targets gatekeepers, agenda-setters and veto-holders rather than every speaker or observer.

## Notable quotes
- "Labels do not decide liability. Structure does not erase duty. What matters is control, discretion, and reliance."
- "Influence persuades; authority decides."
- "Control triggers classification. Classification gives rise to duty. Duty attracts remedy."
- "English private law has never permitted authority to exist in a responsibility vacuum."

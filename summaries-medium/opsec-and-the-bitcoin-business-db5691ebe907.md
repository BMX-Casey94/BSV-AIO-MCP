## Core thesis
A definitional primer on organisational operations security for Bitcoin businesses. Its argument is compressed into the framing: legal compliance is "the foundation or baseline upon which a security infrastructure can be built", and due care plus due diligence "make the foundations of governance" — because "effective governance is often the only way to disprove negligence" when an incident reaches court. Security is presented as litigation-readiness, not merely technical defence.

## Key arguments and claims
- Trusted Computer Base (TCB): the totality of hardware, firmware and software protection mechanisms responsible for enforcing a security policy.
- Malware management is "more than an anti-virus system": any system granting users administrative control to load or execute software carries elevated vulnerability to worms, viruses and Trojans and to "unexpected software interactions" that subvert security controls.
- Principle of Least Privilege: "Never grant users more than the least level of access to a system that is needed for them to be able to complete their roles" — read-only where write access is unnecessary.
- Privileged operations are enumerated: OS control commands, interface configuration, audit-log access, user-account management, security-mechanism configuration, backup and restore.
- Privacy is defined as protecting personal information from disclosure to unauthorised parties — the maintenance of confidentiality.
- Legal requirements: "Adherence to the law and regulatory controls is the foundation or baseline upon which a security infrastructure can be built."
- Illegal activities: an organisation must facilitate attribution — "the discovery of who is responsible and proving it through the use of evidence" — and support non-repudiation of transactions.
- Record retention policy must define what is collected, maintained and for how long, driven by "consent to monitoring, and financial controls (eg SEC filing or Tax rules)".
- Media lifecycle: marking (classification of stored data), handling (secure transport from purchase through storage to destruction), storage in temperature- and humidity-controlled facilities, MTTF-based replacement ("MFFT"), and destruction by purging before physical destruction — "sanitation" via wiping or degaussing to return media to a pristine state or render it unrecoverable.
- PII is defined across jurisdictions: Social Security number (USA), Tax File Number (Australia), credit-card and banking details.
- Due care (reasonable care to guard the organisation from risk) and due diligence (the practices that maintain due care) together form the foundations of governance — the only reliable defence against a negligence finding.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Textbook/security-certification method: enumerate a controlled vocabulary, define each term crisply, then converge on a governance conclusion. The epistemology is compliance-driven — every technical control (marking, retention, sanitation) is justified by its evidentiary or regulatory function. The rhetorical mode is pedagogical and audit-oriented; the Bitcoin business is addressed as a future defendant that must be able to prove diligence.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Against move-fast crypto-startup culture: security is organisational, procedural and legal — governance paperwork, not just keys and code.
- Attribution and non-repudiation are stated as organisational duties, directly opposing the anonymity-maximalist ethos of 2018 Bitcoin businesses.
- Compliance-first framing ("legal requirements... the foundation or baseline") inverts the era's regulatory-arbitrage posture.
- No direct engagement with protocol-level BTC debates: the target is business practice, not consensus or scaling orthodoxy.

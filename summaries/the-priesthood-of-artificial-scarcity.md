# The Priesthood of Artificial Scarcity
**Date:** 2026-05-08 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/the-priesthood-of-artificial-scarcity
**Subtitle:** How BTC Rebuilt the Banking System While Pretending to Destroy It

## Core thesis
BTC's deliberately constrained throughput — four to seven transactions per second — made direct on-chain participation economically impossible for ordinary users, which mechanically forced economic activity into custodial intermediaries. The anti-banking movement thereby rebuilt banking, complete with fractional-reserve dynamics, while its advocates redefined each stage of the surrender as progress. The scaling constraint was a chosen ideology, not an engineering necessity, and its consequences follow from arithmetic rather than conspiracy.

## Key arguments and claims
- A global monetary network must process global economic activity; 4–7 transactions per second "cannot even serve a moderately populated city during peak commercial activity", while Visa routinely processes thousands per second.
- The limitation was chosen: "Artificial scarcity was imposed at the transaction layer" when parts of the BTC community decided small blocks were morally necessary — a theological schism, not a technical debate.
- Intermediation is an economic inevitability once direct participation is expensive: "Banking emerges naturally whenever settlement costs become sufficiently high relative to transactional demand." Exchanges, custodians, ETFs, payment processors and hosted wallets now intermediate BTC exactly as banks intermediated gold.
- Mining economics admit only two equilibria as the subsidy halves: massive transaction volume at low fees, or tiny volume at extremely high fees. "There is no magical third option hidden inside a YouTube thumbnail featuring glowing eyes and the word 'inevitable.'"
- Civilisation-scale infrastructure succeeds through volume — telecommunications, the internet, card networks and cloud computing all did; no network survives "by intentionally throttling usage and charging aristocratic premiums for participation".
- Fractional-reserve BTC is structurally inevitable: fractional reserve emerges "whenever claims circulate more efficiently than final settlement assets". Constrained throughput pushes users into transacting claims while institutions warehouse reserves and internal ledgers replace settlement — the gold-banking dynamic reproduced.
- Speculation and utility incentives conflict fundamentally: "A network optimized for speculation tends toward artificial scarcity. A network optimized for commerce tends toward scalability." BTC optimised for the former; hence the celebration of high fees, analogised to a restaurant boasting that ordinary people can no longer afford dinner.
- A narrative economy — conferences, media companies, ETFs, influencers, venture portfolios — depends on preserving the mythology, so "narratives become revenue streams" and criticism is treated as heresy.
- Language launders the contradiction: custodians become "service providers", institutional dependency becomes "adoption", banks entering the ecosystem becomes "validation"; Craig compares this to Soviet statistics revised after failure.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Satirical institutional economics. He constructs a mechanical incentive chain — constrained base layer pushes activity outward; activity pushed outward concentrates institutionally; concentration recreates trust dependencies; trust dependencies recreate banking behaviour; banking behaviour recreates fractional-reserve incentives — and insists the cycle is "mechanical, not emotional". Historical analogy (gold banking, Soviet target revision) and monetary-history reasoning substitute for formal modelling; the rhetoric is polemical but the underlying claim is an empirical-institutional one about how settlement costs shape market structure.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects small-block ideology outright: congestion is failure, not security; high fees are exclusion, not virtue; inaccessibility is not exclusivity.
- Rejects "hyperbitcoinization" as arithmetically incoherent: eight billion people cannot transact on single-digit throughput, and every proposed fix (second layers, custodians, batching, periodic settlement) reintroduces trusted intermediaries operationally.
- Rejects the layered-scaling defence: a system where ordinary users cannot afford direct settlement is "a gated settlement mechanism", not peer-to-peer cash, and should be described honestly as such.
- Rejects institutional adoption as validation: ETFs, custodians and regulated vehicles are the recreation of the banking layer Bitcoin claimed to abolish.
- Rejects the fee-market celebration: high transaction costs have historically been treated as payment-system failure by merchants, consumers and economists alike.

## Notable quotes
- "A cash system that ordinary people cannot afford to use directly is not a peer-to-peer cash system. It is a gated settlement mechanism."
- "The anti-bank movement rebuilt banking. Only this time the banks are wrapped in hoodies and Twitter memes."
- "One cannot meme industrial-scale infrastructure into existence."
- "Four to seven transactions per second. That is the reality beneath the mythology. Everything else is compensation."

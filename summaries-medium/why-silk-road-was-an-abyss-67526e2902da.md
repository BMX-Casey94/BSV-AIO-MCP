# Why Silk Road was an abyss
**Date:** 2018-12-10 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/why-silk-road-was-an-abyss-67526e2902da
**Subtitle:** Very simply, Silk Road was the worst thing to happen on Bitcoin. Freedom is not absolute. We live and act in a society and selling drugs…

## Core thesis
Silk Road was "the worst thing to happen on Bitcoin" because anonymous-market ideology enables not just drugs but child exploitation — a claim Craig grounds in his own government cyber-forensics career tracing sex crimes and money trails. Bitcoin is designed as "honest money": private at scale but never anonymous, which is why traceable Bitcoin, not Zcash or Monero, is the lawful design.

## Key arguments and claims
- Authority claim: he worked as a government cyber-forensic analyst on anti people-smuggling and anti sex-trafficking engagements in South America and Sub-Saharan Africa, "tracing communications and the money trail"; earlier he was "a pastor and a trustee of a church bank" who saw what "bath salts and ice do to people".
- Encloses an anonymised forensic report co-written with Dr Pang for SA Police (~2006), "Three-dimensional visualization of social interactions networks": GEOMI 3-D network visualisation over PostgreSQL, Perl-parsed chat logs, IP-prefix correlation to unmask multiple identities (13 users found who appeared as 33), and two-way-conversation filtering to map a child-grooming network; the resulting system was used by state and federal police in prosecuting sex crimes.
- Silk Road was not victimless: its takedown "resulted in more than 17 convictions for sex crimes"; he cites Richard Huckle's 22 life sentences for 71 child sex offences on dark-web sites linked to SR.
- "Bitcoin is not designed to be anarchist. That would be a system that allows all I despise."
- The privacy/anonymity distinction: "At scale, Bitcoin is private, not anonymous" — tracing requires "a massive and costly effort", but when serious crime is found "you have a means to stop it".
- Zcash and Monero exist "to try and stop the forms of analysis that you can do on Bitcoin"; Zcash "will never be legal (even if it was not flawed, and no, I am not saying what the flaw is here)".
- The personal cost: "doing what I did is corrosive… it leaves you less human each time you see it" — the origin of his "Prof. Faustus" moniker ("I sold my soul for knowledge"), and why he left the pastorate and views anarchy "with anything other than disdain".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Ethos-driven argument: moral authority from frontline forensic and pastoral experience, evidenced by an embedded academic-style case report complete with methods, network statistics and clustering coefficients. The technical paper functions as proof-of-capability — pseudonymous networks are analysable — which then supports the normative claim that anonymity systems are indefensible.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts cypherpunk privacy maximalism (Zcash/Monero advocacy, mixing culture): anonymity is framed as a crime-enabler, not a right.
- Contradicts the "Silk Road was good for Bitcoin adoption" narrative common in the era — SR was an abyss, not a bootstrap.
- Contradicts both the critic's and the user's "Bitcoin is anonymous" trope: privacy comes from scale and key hygiene, not obfuscation.

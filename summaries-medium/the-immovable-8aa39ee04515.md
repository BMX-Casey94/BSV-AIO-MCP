# The immovable
**Date:** 2019-04-23 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/the-immovable-8aa39ee04515
**Subtitle:** Many people in the bucket shop, sorry, "crypto currency exchange" industry want to see me gone.

## Core thesis
Written days after Binance delisted BSV, this is a declaration of permanence and a statement of Bitcoin's true purpose as Craig tells it: he is not leaving the industry, nChain's patent war chest (~1,000 patents by year-end) will dominate, and Bitcoin was created — by him, "yes I am Satoshi Nakamoto" — to end criminal money systems like Liberty Reserve and the Russian Business Network, to provide an immutable AML evidence trail, and ultimately to replace SWIFT with something "even governments can't bypass". The bucket-shop exchanges and their figureheads (CZ, Roger Ver) are, he predicts, headed for prison.

## Key arguments and claims
- The exchange industry ("bucket shop, sorry, 'crypto currency exchange'") wants him gone; "I'm here to stay" for "the next 20 years".
- Patent strategy: he does not patent bitcoin itself; IP given away on BSV is protected by patents that "equally apply to any other system… but are no longer close to being free" — "other systems will need to pay". Target: "around 1,000 patents published" by year-end, with delayed publication so rivals can't see what's coming; some patents will spawn "thousands of applications each".
- Capacity boast: "In a couple years time we will have a system that can take the entire world's global commerce and every other blockchain and run it without missing a heartbeat."
- Protocol doctrine: "bitcoin is a stable protocol. If you change the protocol, you change it from being bitcoin" — BTC is "the temporary fraudulent system that people try and call bitcoin".
- Taunt to Binance supporters: "please bet every cent you have, and add leverage… because I want you to be begging on the streets with all the criminals that are going to be out of jobs."
- Origin timeline: "bitcoin started in its current iteration in 2007" and was "basically complete" by 2008 — so it "has nothing to do with the collapse of Lehman Bros nor with the subsequent bailout". And: "bitcoin is not anti-bank, it is not anti-government and in fact it is not even anti-central bank. Most importantly, it can't be made to be."
- True target: "criminal manipulation" — Web Money, Liberty Reserve and the Russian Business Network (RBN), whose ISPs were pulled in 2007; Bitcoin "was created as a means to ensure an immutable audit trail", an "anti-money-laundering trail that cannot be altered… enough to ensure privacy while also ensuring that anonymity cannot exist".
- SWIFT is "terribly insecure and very simple to bypass"; bitcoin is "a system that is immutable that even governments can't bypass. One that pulls them into order." He quotes Mark Dubowitz on SWIFT as "the electronic bloodstream of the global financial system" and Iran's 2012 expulsion.
- Geopolitical vision: a single honest ledger brings "everyone into a global alignment or isolates them in the way that North Korea was isolated"; compound growth will leave rogue states behind; Metanet-connected tablets will one day be "dropped over a country like North Korea cheaper than the paper drops in Vietnam", communicating "in their own language" — "the dissemination of truth will not be stopped".
- WikiLeaks is dismissed as "left media… funded by Russia and… designed purely to attack capitalism", against bitcoin as "discoverable truth".
- Liberty Reserve case study: "many times more decentralised than an organisation like Binance could ever hope to achieve" — Hawala-linked, "over 30,000 input and exit points for Fiat currency", based in Costa Rica outside US treaties, leaving no logs — "yet Liberty Reserve was brought down" and its people imprisoned. Lesson: "criminal groups are always 'centralised' in some way."
- Blockchains make crime evidentiary: wash trading and bucket-shop fraud "replicate the USENET penny share schemes and pink sheet web IPOs schemes of the 90s" except that on a blockchain "a record, or evidence cannot be deleted".
- Predictions: "In time, CZ will be gone (or in prison)"; "Roger Ver (Pipe bomb manufacturer) will be gone (or in prison)" — linking Ver's 2002 DOJ plea. "And, I will be here."
- Authorship climax: "You're about to find out why I created bitcoin and yes I am Satoshi Nakamoto. I created it to stop all of the global scams… Thank you for helping it grow and in your ignorance giving me everything I need to stop you."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Declarative and prosecutorial: he argues from asserted authorship ("my industry… my creation"), from a historical case study (Liberty Reserve's 30,000 on/off-ramps proving that "decentralised" crime is always centralised somewhere and prosecutable), and from future-tense prophecy backed by patent counts and capacity claims. Ad hominem is a deliberate instrument — named enemies (CZ, Ver, WikiLeaks) are criminalised by association — and quotations (Terry Pratchett on hard work, Dubowitz on SWIFT) frame the polemic.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies the genesis-block orthodoxy that Bitcoin was a response to the 2008 bailouts: it was "basically complete" before Lehman and "is not anti-bank… not even anti-central bank".
- Contradicts "decentralisation protects you from the law": Liberty Reserve was more decentralised than Binance and was still dismantled — exchanges are "easy to take down and their assets easy to confiscate".
- Contradicts the exchange-ecosystem's legitimacy (days after the BSV delisting): Binance is a criminal bucket shop whose wash volume is preserved as immutable evidence.
- Contradicts the censorship-resistance/anti-state narrative: bitcoin's purpose is to give states and honest markets an unbypassable ledger — "Welcome to law. Welcome to court."
- Contradicts the patent-free/open-source ethos of crypto: a closed, delayed-publication patent portfolio is presented as the mechanism by which "other systems will need to pay" while BSV gets the IP free.

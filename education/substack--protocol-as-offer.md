# Protocol as Offer — core principles

- **A proof-of-work protocol publishes an intelligible bargain.** Any actor may undertake a costly, objectively specified act, and on completion becomes entitled to a defined reward — the familiar structure of a unilateral offer inviting acceptance by performance. The medium is new; the legal structure is old.
- **Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 45 supplies the legal grammar.** Where an offer invites acceptance by performance, beginning performance creates an option-like lock-in: the offer cannot be revoked while the offeree completes, though the duty to pay remains conditional on completion — protecting reliance without paying for mere effort.
- **The invited act is producing a protocol-valid block.** Not "finding a low hash": validity under the rules, acceptance under the objective selection rule (greatest accumulated proof-of-work), and persistence through the finality/maturity horizon are rule-defined conditions precedent to entitlement.
- **Orphaned blocks are unmet conditions, not confiscated payment.** The offer defined completion to include persistence through finality; without that persistence the invited act is not complete, and pre-maturity displacement is a risk of the bargain internalised by participants as a cost of competing.
- **Proof-of-work is a rolling series of unilateral offers on a stable specification.** Each interval publishes the same invitation, induces fresh reliance, and becomes locked against opportunistic revocation once performance begins; the existence of future intervals does not justify retroactive alteration of the current interval's terms.
- **Rule-change has a disciplined taxonomy.** Prospective changes are new offers for future performance opportunities; retroactive rewrites of conditions governing performance already underway are legally charged modifications — rule-change is not neutral "maintenance".
- **Authority to change rules is justiciable.** Operational capacity to publish code or coordinate releases is not legal authority to bind others to retroactive term changes; courts ask ordinary questions — who had authority to modify offer terms, whether assent was obtained where required, whether an enforceable reserved power of modification exists, and whether reliance and commencement constrain revocation.
- **Invalid performance is non-acceptance, not breach.** A block violating validity rules is an attempt to perform a different act; no contract forms for that attempt, so no entitlement exists — breach presupposes a contract, and a purported performance that does not comply with the offer's terms is not defective acceptance but no acceptance.
- **Remedies are conventional.** Expectation damages for impermissible revocation after completion (the loss being the promised reward as defined by the offer); promissory estoppel as a targeted reliance backstop; declaratory relief on the operative offer terms — often the most valuable remedy; and narrow equitable relief against opportunistic retroactive manipulation, tailored to avoid continuous technical supervision.

# Banking on Bitcoin
**Date:** 2018-09-02 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/banking-on-bitcoin-563fbc31e44a
**Subtitle:** Banking on Bitcoin

## Core thesis
"Be your own bank" is an utterly false catchphrase: holding bitcoin is holding cash under your bed, not banking, because banks allocate capital through lending against future repayment — something Bitcoin does not do. Self-custody is neither free nor safe for ordinary users; teaching that "owning a set of keys makes you ultimately secure" is negligent and, Craig predicts, will attract tortious-negligence lawsuits. Bitcoin's real promise is as cash — including for the unbanked via apps like Centbee and Handcash — with security taught as layered key management, and BTC "should not even be called bitcoin anymore. It is not cash."

## Key arguments and claims
- Definitional separation: "Holding bitcoin as cash is not banking." Banks "allocate and distribute capital… offer loans and allow people access to funds they would not be able to gain based on the promise of the future repayment" — home loans, education for people "from poor families". "Bitcoin does not offer this."
- Japan analogy: with near-zero interest rates "many people still hide their money under their bed. Just holding your money in bitcoin is just holding your money under your bed."
- Banking on Bitcoin is possible, "but it does not involve holding your money at home" — credit services must be built on top of the cash layer.
- Self-custody security is a fallacy for most users: "This myth that holding your own keys makes you secure is a fallacy." Large holdings demand "multiple layers of security… backups, secure off-line service and multiple keys" — a real, ongoing cost of storage.
- "To be safe, you cannot trust a hardware wallet. All devices have a level of insecurity and risk."
- Authority claim from his infosec career "since the early 90's": "the biggest security problem is users not understanding security. Handing off security to users is a recipe for failure."
- Legal prediction: "I expect people touting this idea to be sued under tortious negligence laws for promotion of false information leading to loss" — victims of hacks, lost keys or physical theft should recover from the promoters of the self-custody myth. "To teach people that bitcoin is secure by default is negligent."
- Third-world use case: "simple applications such as Centbee and Handcash" let low-end users access bitcoin quickly; it "partially provides leverage to get into the market" without replacing banking.
- Concrete key-management teaching: users can run "multiple wallets and only carry the minimal amount on their phone… one phone at home with a special backup key and locked encryption and carry another with a minimal amount that they need for the day".
- What "so-called experts" fail to teach: store-owners should have a head-office wallet "that is paid to with no private keys on site"; workers should be paid to a separate wallet while carrying a small-balance one.
- Closing split-era claim: "Bitcoin does not replace banking. It is cash. Bitcoin core (BTC) should not even be called bitcoin anymore. It is not cash."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He argues by definitional precision — separating cash, custody and credit — then attacks the slogan with professional-authority ethos (three decades in information security) and a risk/liability frame (tortious negligence) rather than purely technical critique. The alternative he offers is operational and pedagogical: concrete wallet architectures for merchants, workers and the unbanked. The mode is debunking plus duty-of-care: the "experts" are not just wrong but legally culpable.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly contradicts "be your own bank" and "not your keys, not your coins" maximalism — self-custody is reframed as a costly, expert-only security burden most users will fail at.
- Contradicts hardware-wallet sufficiency (Ledger/Trezor as the canonical answer): "you cannot trust a hardware wallet".
- Contradicts the anti-banking ethos of crypto orthodoxy: banks' capital allocation is socially valuable and Bitcoin does not substitute for it.
- Contradicts the BTC-as-bitcoin naming claim: BTC "is not cash" and so forfeits the name — the BCH-side position in the weeks before the BSV split.
- Recasts security education, not protocol features, as the privacy/safety battleground — consistent with his wallet-level (not protocol-level) fix in "The crypto-ring of Gyges".

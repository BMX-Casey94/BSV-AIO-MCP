# Bitcoin's 2014–2017 direction change: documented record and open questions

An attributed analysis card summarising Casey's (@BSVCasey) long-form
article examining why Bitcoin moved from "peer-to-peer electronic cash" to a
constrained base layer with activity pushed to second-layer and custodial
systems. The source is one analyst's synthesis of public records, court
judgments, and released documents — included in this corpus **as attributed
commentary, not as corpus-verified fact**. The article's own discipline is
preserved below: what is documented, what is disputed, and what is unproven
are kept separate. Quote claims with their epistemic status attached.

## Documented record (public record, court findings, released documents)

- Satoshi Nakamoto's 2010 Bitcointalk post described raising the block-size
  limit as something that "can be phased in" later; the 1 MB cap was
  introduced as a temporary anti-spam measure.
- 2014–2015: a small number of organisations — Blockstream (founded 2014 by
  Core developers, venture-backed), Chaincode Labs (founders tied to Hudson
  River Trading), and MIT's Digital Currency Initiative (2015) — came to
  employ or fund a large share of Bitcoin Core's most active contributors as
  the Bitcoin Foundation declined.
- r/Bitcoin and Bitcointalk, under moderator Theymos, removed or restricted
  big-block discussion; Mike Hearn resigned publicly in January 2016 citing
  effective control by a small group and censorship of the scaling debate;
  Gavin Andresen was sidelined and later lost commit access; SegWit activated
  under a User-Activated Soft Fork signalling campaign.
- Jeffrey Epstein invested roughly $500,000 in Blockstream's 2014 seed round
  via Kyara Investments III (co-owned with Joi Ito). Adam Back has confirmed
  the investment and stated the fund divested within months over conflict-of-
  interest concerns. Austin Hill has confirmed he and colleagues met Epstein
  on Little Saint James in April 2014; Epstein's contemporaneous email to
  Amir Taaki claimed "Andy Back" was on the island that weekend — the
  released material does not independently establish Adam Back's presence.
- Epstein donated a documented $850,000 to MIT (2002–2017), including
  $525,000 directed to the Digital Currency Initiative; correspondence
  indicates DCI funding was used to hire Core developers after the Bitcoin
  Foundation's collapse. Epstein also invested ~$3 million in Coinbase (2014).
- A 2015 agreement between Epstein's Southern Trust Company and Edmond de
  Rothschild Holding S.A. for risk-analysis/algorithm services, and a
  February 2016 email to Peter Thiel stating "As you probably know, I
  represent the Rothschilds", are matters of documentary record; what the
  relationship actually involved is not established by those documents alone.
- COPA v Wright (2024): the UK High Court ruled Craig Wright is not Satoshi
  Nakamoto, found forgery "on a grand scale" and extensive lies to the court,
  and referred him to prosecutors; later contempt findings followed. The
  court rejected Wright's September 2023 account of third-party document
  planting as "literally incredible".
- Kleiman v Wright (2021): the jury rejected the Dave Kleiman partnership
  claim while finding Wright liable for conversion ($100M to W&K).
- The 2019 BSV delistings followed legal threats by Wright/Ayre against
  critics; exchanges cited that conduct, not a finding that BSV itself was
  fraudulent. The £9.9B UK collective competition claim alleging coordinated
  delisting remains live (CAT allowed proceedings; appeals exhausted against
  the exchanges' challenges) and unresolved.
- Christen Ager-Hanssen has acknowledged working with Black Cube (a firm with
  a documented history of covert investigative methods, e.g. the Weinstein
  operation) and orchestrated the covert recording of Kyle Roche.

## Disputed / interpretive (reasonable people disagree)

- Whether the 2014–2017 outcome reflects **directed influence** or
  **convergent incentives**: venture capital's structural preference for a
  layered architecture, sincere security-maximalism among developers, and
  path dependence after social consensus hardened are all live explanations.
- Sources for the strong "hijacking" account (Roger Ver's *Hijacking
  Bitcoin*, CoinGeek commentary) come from financially interested parties in
  an ongoing rival-chain dispute — genuine firsthand testimony, but advocacy
  to be weighted, not neutral documentation.
- Allegations of judicial collusion (Mellor / "Tufty the Cat") were formally
  raised and rejected by the court as without merit.
- Ager-Hanssen's history makes evidence-provenance scrutiny legitimate; it
  does not establish that he or Black Cube fabricated the Wright documents,
  and the court found the forgeries attributable to Wright.

## Not established by the public record (as of August 2026)

- No documented instruction, directive, agreement, or quid pro quo from
  Epstein (or any funder) concerning Bitcoin's technical direction — the
  block-size limit, Lightning, or the scaling debate's outcome.
- No independent corroboration that Adam Back was physically present on
  Little Saint James.
- No court finding that the 2019 delistings were collusive (actively
  litigated, unresolved).

## The article's own conclusion

The author concludes that deliberate steering is a **plausible interpretation,
not a demonstrated one** — funding concentration, moderation, and aligned
commercial interests pushed the outcome in a clear direction beyond what
random technical argument would easily explain, while a meaningful chance
remains that genuine technical concerns and path dependence suffice. (The
article cites a Grok model review estimating ~70–75% likelihood of deliberate
steering; that figure is the author's cited model opinion, not a measurement.)

# Proof
**Date:** 2019-03-13 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/proof-22e2cd5fc385
**Subtitle:** The code-is-law movement is a pernicious attack on freedom. There is no definitive means to assess truth through a computer program.

## Core thesis
The code-is-law movement is "a pernicious attack on freedom": a digital key or signature is only ever evidence, never proof, because proof is weighed by judges and juries under a burden of proof that no algorithm can carry. Bitcoin's real value is as "one source of immutable evidence" that lowers the cost of justice — not as a replacement for courts. The essay doubles as a doctrinal defence of Craig's refusal to "prove" his identity or qualifications on demand to social media.

## Key arguments and claims
- Code-is-law is promoted by "childish hackers and those aligned to states who oppose freedom"; whatever the label (anarcho-capitalism etc.), "the aim is very clear: to undermine the nature of justice" by removing the flexibility of judges and juries.
- Companies cannot sign: "a company does not act. It (the company) has human agents, directors, and officers, and they act for the company" — digital corporate signatures rest on securely stored data and human agency, not programs.
- Burden-of-proof analysis: the onus probandi comprises a dual burden of production and of persuasion; "law is not binary. We cannot make law binary, and code cannot be law in any fair society."
- A digital signature is "at best a conditional or presumptive proof"; "Mere possession of a file is not evidence, nor can it be held to be anything but evidence of possession of a file." Civil law's "full proof" requires multiple witnesses or public instrument.
- Reductio via Silk Road: if key possession were proof, then Ross Ulbricht would be exonerated the moment anyone else spent from an attributed key — and a second "Dread Pirate Roberts" did reopen Silk Road one month after seizure. "Yet, it is not how law works. It is not how truth works."
- Computers are insecure and average users will never secure them adequately; under code-is-law "we would see people wrongfully not only accused but framed."
- Self case study: when his qualifications were attacked while he was "out of reach for the media", he bore no duty to prove anything; he later produced "a selection of my degrees — not all, not even half" — because falsely claiming degrees for profit would be fraud, but "if I'm not seeking to profit from you... you also have no right for proof."
- Privacy as a right of refusal: "It is the right not to answer when others seek answers"; as a former Elder of the Uniting Church and trustee of the church's bank, only those within the church "had a right to parts of my past. Yet the world doesn't."
- Media critique: "In the world of click-bait media, the truth is roadkill after a lorry has passed" — truth "does not pay for advertising", so proof-by-social-media inverts the burden onto the innocent.
- Bitcoin's role: "It is not anonymous but pseudonymous"; used correctly it "provides an evidentiary trail that can lower the cost of taking action in court, yet it does not replace the court with mere keys" — against "sidechains and Lightning and methodologies that allow people to create many sets of books and ledgers".
- Legacy claim: "thousands of pieces of intellectual property... more patents than many countries own. Such is my legacy"; while attackers target his "reputation", "lawyers, bankers, and business people... 'get Bitcoin' when I explain it."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Legal-doctrinal method: he imports the taxonomy of burdens (production, persuasion, onus probandi, presumptive vs full proof) and applies it to cryptographic artefacts. Literary scaffolding — Cinthio's *Gli Hecatommithi* (1565), Othello's proof "through deeds", Hamlet — frames proof as action rather than data. He argues by reductio (Ulbricht), by autobiographical case study, and with ornate invective ("miasmic", "mephitic... toads wallowing in the pond they defecate in") against opponents.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **Code is law / smart-contract maximalism:** the era's smart-contract and "trustless" orthodoxy treated execution as final adjudication; Craig calls this an attack on justice itself.
- **Keys-as-identity ("sign a message to prove you're Satoshi"):** the community's demanded proof ritual is exactly what he denies — possession of a key proves only possession of a key.
- **Anonymity and disintermediation of institutions:** where mainstream crypto sought to route around courts and states, he insists Bitcoin was built to strengthen evidentiary law.
- **Many ledgers (sidechains/Lightning):** contradicts the scaling-via-layering consensus by demanding "one truth, one source of immutable evidence".

# Miners and Property rights — core principles

- **Users have no property right in a block.** Miners are paid to validate blocks containing transactions they themselves agree with; they are not obliged to accept any transaction, and may choose what they do and do not accept.
- **Mining is contract formation.** Miners provide an invitation to treat to users; a user sends a transaction that is an offer, the fee being the payment offered in response; the miner accepts by including the transaction in a block. Any validation is a contract.
- **Block inclusion satisfies e-commerce receipt rules.** Inclusion in a block constitutes the receipt required under Article 52 of the UK's Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002 — tying block inclusion to contract formation in e-commerce.
- **Fee policy is unconstrained miner discretion.** A miner may demand any fee for a single transaction, accept only transactions carrying a particular opcode fee, or refuse particular block templates — miners do not even need to be considerate or reasonable; users have offers, not rights.
- **Empty blocks are lawful protocol behaviour.** Mining empty blocks is part of the protocol; expecting a miner to mine your transaction is breaking the rules, not the other way round.
- **A hash majority may lawfully orphan a rule-changing chain.** Miners "vote with their CPU power, expressing their acceptance of valid blocks by working on extending them" (white paper); a group with more than 51% of the hashing algorithm can legally kill off a dishonest chain — one seeking to alter the protocol and subsidise others.
- **Halvings raise the cost of protocol change.** As the block subsidy halves, the value of fees to a miner increases, making forced protocol changes truly expensive to the miner — it is all incentives.

# Miners and Property rights

## Core thesis
Written on the eve of the BCH/BSV split, this is a legal broadside against Bitcoin ABC developers who claimed that mining empty blocks is an "attack" blocking "an implied easement to my property." Craig rebuts that users have no property right in a block whatsoever: mining is a contractual invitation to treat, a transaction is merely a unilateral fee offer a miner may refuse for any reason, and a >51% miner majority may "LEGALLY kill off a dishonest chain" that alters the protocol.

## Key arguments and claims
- "You have NO property in a block": a miner is paid to validate blocks containing transactions "the self same miner agrees with"; miners "are not obliged, they can choose what they do and do not accept."
- Mining empty blocks "is a part of the protocol," and "you are breaking the rules expecting a miner to mine your TX. Any validation is a contract."
- Fee policy is unconstrained: a miner could demand "1,000 BCH as the fee to send a single TX," accept only a "10,000 DVS (OP_DataSigVerify miner fee) transaction," or refuse all CTOR blocks — "Miners do not even need to be considerate or reasonable."
- Contract-law framing: "Miners provide an invitation to treat to users"; "A user sends a transaction that is an offer. The fee is the payment offered to the miner in response to the invitation to treat"; the miner "does not have a duty to accept the transaction."
- Inclusion in a block constitutes the receipt required under Article 52 of the UK's Electronic Commerce (EC Directive) Regulations 2002 — tying block inclusion to e-commerce contract formation.
- "As a user, no, you have NO rights. Miners do." Users make offers; competing miners decide whether to take them.
- A miner majority ">51% of the hashing algorithm… can even LEGALLY kill off a dishonest chain," where "a dishonest chain is one seeking to alter the protocol (that is, to change the rules of the game and subsidise others)" — a direct justification for the impending SV hash-war strategy against ABC.
- He quotes the white paper — miners "vote with their CPU power, expressing their acceptance of valid blocks by working on extending them" — to ground this in Satoshi's consensus mechanism: "It is ALL, and I mean all incentives."
- Economic trajectory: block rewards still subsidise empty-block mining, but "as the reward halves, the value of the fees to a miner increases," making forced protocol changes "truly expensive to the miner."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
The method is applied contract and property law: he takes the opponent's legal category (easement) and dismantles it with first-year common-law distinctions (invitation to treat vs offer, no duty to contract), then anchors the legal argument in the white paper's incentive model. The reasoning is adversarial and didactic — "a little advice for the legally clueless" — using statute (UK e-commerce regs), a cited SSRN legal paper, and Satoshi quotation as authorities. Underneath is his signature claim that Bitcoin is an incentive system enforced by law, not an anarchic code-governed space.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Rejects the "users' transactions have a right to be mined" intuition behind anti-empty-block and anti-51% rhetoric: users have no rights, only offers.
- Legitimises exactly what mainstream crypto calls attacks — empty blocks, discriminatory fee policies, 51% orphaning of a rival chain — as lawful market behaviour and protocol defence: "That is the miner's right. This is what Bitcoin is."
- Contradicts the "code is law, permissionless means no gatekeeping" orthodoxy: validation is a contract, and miners gatekeep by design — "so much for permission-less," as he mocks the ABC position.
- Frames protocol-altering forks (ABC's CTOR/DSV upgrades) as "dishonest chains" that may legally be destroyed, inverting the mainstream view of the hash war as an attack on BCH.

## Notable quotes
- "I am sorry to burst your bubble, but you have NO property in a block."
- "Any validation is a contract. A miner can set the fees to any amount they desire, and reject all other transactions."
- "Basically, the point is; as a user, no, you have NO rights. Miners do."
- "Any miner or group of miners with more than 51% of the hashing algorithm under their control (as a group) can even LEGALLY kill off a dishonest chain."
- "Miners provide an invitation to treat to users."
- "Bitcoin is a means to create stable money, a single protocol that is set and does not change at the whims of others. Honest miners defend this idea."

# What Is Proof?
**Date:** 2026-07-11T01:35:18.973Z | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/what-is-proof
**Subtitle:** (none — keywords: proof; burden of proof; standard of proof; mathematical proof; proof-of-work; zero-knowledge proof; Agrippa's trilemma; Gödel; falsification; framework-relative proof)

## Core thesis
Proof is not a property of a claim but "a transaction" — warrant delivered to someone, across a standard, inside a system, for a purpose. No notion of proof is framework-free: every proof stops at chosen, unproven premises (Agrippa's trilemma). Mathematics, law, science and cryptography each independently abandoned the framework-free absolute and replaced it with proof indexed to a standard — and proof-of-work is the cryptographer's version: certainty as a cost curve rather than a logical guarantee.

## Key arguments and claims
- **The demagogue's proof dismissed.** Proof as "whatever produced conviction in me" is a psychological event, worthless as a criterion because the feeling of certainty is abundant — "more abundant in the fanatic and the drunk than in the mathematician".
- **The mathematician's proof is the strongest case — and still framework-relative.** A proof compels only those who grant the axioms and rules: Euclid's triangle theorem fails without the fifth postulate (spherical geometry); constructivists reject excluded-middle existence proofs; Gödel shows no consistent system rich enough for arithmetic can prove its own consistency; and "who checks the checker?" — the four-colour theorem (1976) relocates certainty from the theorem into confidence in program, compiler and hardware.
- **Agrippa's trilemma.** Every chain of justification ends in infinite regress, circularity, or a chosen unproven stopping point. Only the third ever terminates a proof — on something unproven, which you chose. "There is no bottom turtle."
- **The dilemma stated flat.** Either "proof" means framework-transcendent compulsion (which the trilemma shows nobody can supply) or framework-relative certification (which cannot referee between frameworks). The three desiderata — discriminate, world-anchored, framework-free — cannot be jointly met: "You may have any two. You cannot have three."
- **Law.** Proof is a burden borne by a party, on an issue, to a standard that moves with the stakes: balance of probabilities (money), clear and convincing evidence (intermediate), beyond reasonable doubt (liberty). The threshold choice is a decision about who bears the risk of error, made before evidence is heard — level-indexing as institutional design.
- **Science.** "There is no proof in science. There was never supposed to be." Universal claims cannot be proven by finite observation; theories are corroborated by surviving severe tests, provisionally and by design (Popper). "Scientifically proven" is a category error imported from the courtroom and pulpit.
- **Cryptography.** Digital signatures prove not that a human wrote a message but that the holder of a private key signed it — airtight yet indexed to key custody, "the trilemma, in your pocket". Zero-knowledge proofs deliver soundness driven near — never to — zero: "deceit made so improbable that no rational adversary attempts it... a definition of proof fit for the real world".
- **Proof-of-work.** "The mechanism at the heart of the Bitcoin protocol, and the one that made me care about this question in the first place." It proves expenditure of computation: ordering is "not decreed true; it is made expensive to rewrite"; security is economic and probabilistic, decaying as honest work accumulates. "Certainty here is not apodictic. It is a cost curve." Nakamoto's genius: practical certainty purchased with real resources beats an absolute certainty that does not exist.
- **The working discipline.** Three rules: declare your floor (name the axioms/standard/threat model); match the standard to the stakes; refuse the level-jump (proof offered at one level, credited at another — "where nearly every bad inference lives"). Not relativism: standards are defended and attacked, but there is no standard behind all standards.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Philosophical analysis by steelmanning and convergence: make the strongest case for absolute proof (mathematics), locate the exact crack (axioms, logic, Gödel, the checker), generalise via Agrippa's trilemma into a small impossibility theorem (three jointly unsatisfiable conditions), then show four proof-serious disciplines independently arrived at the indexed, framework-relative answer. Closes with operational rules rather than scepticism.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- **PoW reframed against "objective security" talk.** Bitcoin's security is explicitly probabilistic and economic — an attacker must "out-spend the honest expenditure" — against any framing of the ledger as absolutely immutable or trust-free in a logical sense.
- **Signatures prove key-custody, not identity.** "Steal the key and you inherit the proof" — cuts against the mainstream "your keys prove you own your coins" shorthand, anticipating his legal arguments that keys are evidence, not title.
- **Level-jump as the diagnostic tool.** The demand to ask "proven to which standard?" is aimed at rhetorical slippage he elsewhere attributes to BTC discourse (security claims credited at mathematical standard while resting on economic premises).

## Notable quotes

# Why Lightning will never be currency, and why BSV matters — core principles

- **Miners are the clearance and settlement system.** Under FinCEN, an exchange accepting and transmitting virtual currency is a money transmitter. The payment-processor exemption covers parties facilitating purchase of goods or services "through a clearance and settlement system": miners are that system. A multi-hop routing network that updates "a series of promissory notes" is a payment system, not clearance — and promissory notes are expressly defined monetary instruments.
- **Two-party channels that settle on-chain are a different legal object.** Exemption (F) (funds "integral to the sale of goods or the provision of services") would exempt common Nakamoto payment channels and those created by Mike Hearn — two-party channels that settle on-chain — but not a design that "requires multiple hops and intermediaries".
- **Multi-hop routing reintroduces trusted third parties.** Average path lengths of several hops mean layers of intermediaries between people engaged in trade, with no record kept — a mixer network that confuses and obfuscates payments. A few key hubs can pass almost all traffic and function as unpaid, unlicensed banks.
- **BSV keeps payments settled and recorded on-chain.** That is where the law can reach them. Bitcoin was designed to eliminate trusted intermediaries, not to reinsert them as hops.
- **Every system has an owner.** Decentralisation is no defence: "every system has an owner, and every owner is responsible for the actions of the computers he runs." Digital currency is not a safe haven (*United States v Budovsky*; the BTC-e action).
- **BSA duties attach to money transmitters.** Reporting of transactions over $10,000 (single or related, within 24 hours) and suspicious activity at $5,000; nodes that do not comply are in breach. The ISP exemption covers mere delivery and network access, not the passing of money and funds for other individuals.
- **On-chain scale is the design.** The claim that Bitcoin cannot scale is maintained by artificial protocol limits. Payments belong on the base ledger, recorded, not on a promissory-note mixer built to destroy transaction records.

# Repudiation
**Date:** 2018-11-07 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/repudiation-3b35bd315abf
**Subtitle:** Non-repudiation is the process of ensuring that a parties to a transaction cannot deny (this is repudiate) that a transaction occurred.

## Core thesis
A short doctrinal note arguing that technical non-repudiation is impossible: any party can always claim lost keys, duress or another excuse, so no cryptographic construction can settle the matter. Consequently, Bitcoin's fungibility is a legal property, not a technical one — it arises from exchange for valid consideration without knowledge of crime.

## Key arguments and claims
- Definitions: non-repudiation ensures parties "cannot deny (this is repudiate) that a transaction occurred"; repudiation is "an assertion refuting a claim or the refusal to acknowledge an action or deed"; anticipatory repudiation is a declared intention to fail contractual obligations.
- Impossibility claim: "non-repudiation does not exist. NO technical solution can exist, as a party can always say they lost keys or were under duress or something else."
- "Bitcoin transactions are not fungible through technical measures to create non-repudiation, they are fungible through law."
- Fungibility's legal test: it "comes when money is exchanged as consideration (and without knowledge of crime, etc.) with another for valid consideration."
- "Even a gift is not fungible" — gratuitous transfers lack the consideration that confers fungible status.

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He reasons from legal doctrine rather than cryptography: define the terms, then show the technical ideal (non-repudiation) fails a trivial counterexample (the lost-keys/duress defence), and relocate the property that matters (fungibility) into contract law's consideration doctrine. The style is compressed and definitional — a lecture note rather than a polemic.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the cryptographic-finality orthodoxy ("the signature settles it", code-is-law): signatures never conclusively bind a party, because repudiation via lost keys or duress is always available.
- Contradicts the privacy-tech framing of fungibility (that it must be engineered through anonymity features): for Craig fungibility is conferred by lawful exchange, so technical mixing is beside the point — and tainted-coin analysis remains legally meaningful.
- Implicitly undercuts "not your keys, not your coins" absolutism: key possession is evidence, not legal proof of ownership or consent.

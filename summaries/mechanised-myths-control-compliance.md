# Mechanised Myths: Control, Compliance, and the Dystopian Present
**Date:** 2025-08-05 | **URL:** https://singulargrit.substack.com/p/mechanised-myths-control-compliance
**Subtitle:** None.

## Core thesis
Dystopia is not a future to be feared but a present already inhabited: a distributed, aestheticised, internalised order in which systems marketed as liberation — blockchain, automation, micropayments, curated media — concentrate economic and narrative power while engineering compliance into their architecture. Control no longer demands obedience by force; it cultivates compliance by design, framing centralised authority as natural, even liberating, progress.

## Key arguments and claims
- Policy has been recast as performance: simple, broadly equitable tax frameworks abandoned for "labyrinthine credit schemes and politically convenient exemptions"; resilient public infrastructure starved in favour of projects that "photograph well and poll even better".
- Blockchain rhetoric promises trustless distribution, yet practice concentrates control in "a few mining entities, protocol committees, and development gatekeepers" — an appropriation of the technology rather than a failure of it, echoing Heinlein's cautionary worlds and Orwell's doublethink.
- The timestamp-server metaphor: even a perfect ledger is only as honest as the data it records; the capacity to "define, exclude, or interpret entries transforms the ledger from a neutral archive into a curated narrative".
- SPV is used allegorically as "a delegation of trust dressed as independence" — users accept a controlled feed of selective truth rather than holding the full record themselves.
- Cartel logic operates in plain sight: finance's operational interdependence collectivises systemic risk while pricing out entry; tech platforms dictate standards, protocols and definitions of compliance; climate initiatives function as de facto cartels advantaging capital-rich incumbents; energy concentration makes the "Bitcoin energy villain" narrative a proxy for struggles over who controls electricity production.
- Media exercise power through framing rather than censorship: The Washington Post and The Atlantic deliver choreographed China–US narratives assigning "moral clarity to one side and systemic opacity to the other"; Wacquant's "myth and ceremony" applies, and outlets become "the timestamp authorities of public memory".
- Automation married to blockchain in corporate hands means continuous monitoring, granular auditing and extraction; the technical processes of "combining" and "splitting" value render individuals "composable, divisible units of value".
- Micropayment culture encloses the commons: every interaction priced, metered and billed, dovetailing with behavioural scoring, social-credit architectures and the gamification of compliance; value is extracted through "the aggregation of countless small, compliant actions".
- Heinlein's *The Moon is a Harsh Mistress* is the counterpoint: visible, identifiable oppression catalyses rebellion, whereas today's ambient system renders the thought of resistance "economically irrational".

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Cultural-institutional criticism by analogy. He reads technical Bitcoin concepts (timestamping, SPV, combine/split) as social allegories, and filters political economy through literary dystopia (Orwell, Heinlein) and Wacquant's sociology of myth and ceremony. The method is pattern-matching across domains — policy, finance, energy, media, code — to reveal a single convergent "infrastructure of control" that governs through the architecture of participation itself.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Denies that "trustless" and "decentralised" describe lived reality: decision-making is "funnelled through small, opaque groups, insulated from real accountability".
- The "permissionless" promise has "ossified into a structure of concentrated influence", defended not by secrecy but by "the warm glow of technological idealism".
- Treats the Bitcoin-as-energy-villain discourse as an incumbent power play over electricity control, not environmental concern.
- Notably, SPV is deployed sceptically here — as a metaphor for outsourced verification — which sits in tension with Craig's usual technical defence of SPV as Bitcoin's intended scaling model.

## Notable quotes
- "The dystopia is not a prophecy of tomorrow. It is here, embedded within the systems we inhabit and defend, concealed beneath the aesthetics of freedom."
- "This is not a free market; it is an enclosure — a fenced field with carefully monitored gates."
- "Citizens believe they are engaging with the unvarnished truth, when in fact they are reading the curated minutes of a meeting to which they were never invited."
- "This is the genius of the modern dystopia: it recruits its subjects as its advocates."

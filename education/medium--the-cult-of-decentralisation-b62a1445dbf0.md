# The cult of Decentralisation — core principles

- **Decentralisation is the instrument, not the purpose.** "The system is decentralised in order to create the first stable electronic money, not as digital gold, not as a settlement system, but as digital cash" — Satoshi's P2P Foundation announcement framed Bitcoin as the answer to trust failure: central banks debase, banks lend in waves of credit bubbles, and overhead makes micropayments impossible.
- **Money is a measurement instrument.** "We do not have many versions of a metre and nor should we have many versions of currency" — decentralisation exists so that money stays difficult to change, and stability arrives with scale.
- **Centralised versus decentralised is a false dichotomy.** It is not an all-or-nothing scenario; there are intermediate options, and the requirement is defined numerically.
- **Three to five competing miners suffice.** "In any system with five or more mining entities where none of the parties hold individual control of more than 50% of the network, bitcoin is decentralised" — a bankrupt miner raises rivals' profit, so competition and profit dynamically maintain the balance.
- **Money solves a search problem.** Kiyotaki–Wright search theory (1989/1993): fiat predominates because of lower storage costs than other goods and non-consumability; Bitcoin mirrors fiat's monetary properties whilst creating stability and limiting any party's ability to debase.
- **An optimum quantity of money exists.** Arrow–Hahn (1971): fiat money is not unequivocally welfare-enhancing; currency competition is a prisoner's dilemma converging on a single Nash equilibrium that drives out the less efficient currency.
- **A medium of exchange needs negligible alternative use value.** Gold became money because it had practically no other use in history (jewellery came later as a Veblen good); a medium of exchange must have no other value, or a use value far lower than any product being transacted.
- **Scale is the security budget.** "As a global system, no government could ever attack bitcoin" — at sufficient scale the network is beyond state attack.
- **Bitcoin is not egalitarian by design.** "The only way to true equality is for all to be equally destitute" — the system's goal is stable cash with micropayments at negligible fees, not redistribution.

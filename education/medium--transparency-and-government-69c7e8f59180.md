# Transparency and Government — core principles

- **Bitcoin is anything but anonymous.** Anonymity means the parties know nothing about each other. Privacy lets people interact while keeping identities away from the public. The two are not the same.
- **No blockchain can be anonymous.** That is important, because anonymity helps breed corruption. Bitcoin is a transparent system that allows monetary tracing.
- **Transparency keeps people and governments honest.** No man remains moral without his actions being monitored and viewed. The same applies to the institutions we create.
- **A government stablecoin does not need a blockchain.** For a fixed government-based stable coin, a distributed database is simpler. Bitcoin’s distribution matters because unrelated entities compete in maintaining the ledger, so the system survives any single entity’s failure.
- **The problem of central-bank currency is seigniorage.** Alterations in the money supply are the issue — not the lack of a blockchain.
- **Bitcoin was not designed to replicate gold.** Gold supply has been volatile (some years 8% changes; US production rose 407% between 1980 and 1987). Bitcoin is peer-to-peer electronic cash for micropayments and small casual transactions.

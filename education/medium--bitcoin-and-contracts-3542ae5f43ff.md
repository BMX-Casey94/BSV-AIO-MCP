# Bitcoin and Contracts — core principles

- **Bitcoin mirrors postal delivery, not instantaneous media.** Bitcoin is fast but not instantaneous — delivery can fail, be delayed or rerouted — so it most closely mirrors a postal letter and inherits the case-law developed for post and e-mail.
- **The candidate moments of acceptance are legally distinct.** Sending (leaving the sender), receipt by the network, appearance in the blockchain, download to a wallet, the wallet allowing a spend, and the recipient reading are separate events that must not be conflated.
- **SPV and wallet behaviour obscure the legal moment.** The existence of SPV and other partial nodes has confused many as to the nature of Bitcoin: network receipt, wallet display and spendability are different legal facts.
- **The invitation-to-treat doctrine applies on-chain.** As with post (Partridge v Crittenden [1968]), displays and advertisements are invitations to treat rather than offers; offers, acceptances and the whole postal framework carry over to Bitcoin.
- **The mailbox-rule question is a classification question.** Whether the postal rule applies to a medium (Cavazos & Morin, 1994) depends on whether it is more like instantaneous communication or traditional mail; because inclusion of transactions into a block is not instant, the laws adopted for e-mail and even NNTP apply to Bitcoin.
- **Bitcoin sits inside contract law.** Bitcoin is P2P electronic cash governed by existing contract doctrine — features such as e-mail-style receipts, failure and delay are legal events, not events outside the legal system.

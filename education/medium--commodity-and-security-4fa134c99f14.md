# Commodity and security — core principles

- **Bitcoin is a commodity purchased with a tokenised security.** The commodity is the right to have an entry written into the immutable ledger, exchanged between user and miner; miners accept fees in exchange for the provision of a service.
- **Only whole satoshis exist.** There are 100,000,000 (10⁸) satoshi per nominal bitcoin; there are no fractional satoshi.
- **Mining is offer and acceptance.** Users make an offer to all miners; miners accept by including the transaction in a block; competition yields a natural block-size equilibrium based on the utility and external use value in the blockchain.
- **Transactions do not truly expire.** Most miners drop an unconfirmed transaction from memory after about 72 hours, but a valid signed transaction can be re-sent; low-fee transactions may clear in off-peak periods and be excluded at peak times.
- **Double-spending to defraud is larceny.** Creating mutually exclusive transactions to defraud a merchant is a form of larceny; miners who fail to orphan an intentional double-spend when a consensus minimum fee was paid open themselves to actions in negligence.
- **Liability tracks the least-cost provider.** Enforcement is most effective against the party best placed to prevent the harm (Mann & Belzley, 2005): a miner doing simple validation mirrors a telecommunications carrier (conduit, low liability), while one incorporating op-codes specifically designed to facilitate illegal activity is like a publisher (high liability).
- **Consensus miners may form a common-law partnership.** Failing to restrain a rogue miner is like partners failing to restrain a rogue partner — liability for one can extend to all miners on that fork.
- **Wallet roles matter for jurisdiction.** Source wallets initiate exchanges and keep logs; destination/merchant wallets can relocate to permissive jurisdictions (the Antigua internet-gambling example, including the US's lost WTO case), but user wallets cannot escape their users' jurisdiction.
- **Regulation should target wallets and merchants, not the backbone.** Filtering at backbone or mining level is impractical; address- and script-filtering legislation is better directed to wallet services and merchants.
- **The subsidy schedule ends in fee dependence.** 50 coins per block at launch, 12.5 in late 2018, 6.25 in under two years, 3.125 by 2024 — at which point fees will be critical to the continued existence of the system.
- **Enforcement realism shapes design.** Online fraud perpetrators can close down, move assets and conceal identities, leaving little likelihood of success whether civil or criminal proceedings are taken (Dr Russell Smith, Australian Institute of Criminology, 2000) — which is why prevention must be engineered into the system and its service layer.

# Dynamic disequilibria and the creation of criminal opportunity
**Date:** 2018-09-18 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/dynamic-disequilibria-and-the-creation-of-criminal-opportunity-255e98f59266

## Core thesis
Crime and security form a co-evolving market: every security innovation spawns new criminal innovation and vice versa, and periods of low disequilibrium produce stagnation and entrenchment on both sides. Applied to Bitcoin, the lesson is risk economics rather than perfectionism — no system can be made perfect, so the goal is to make exploitation uneconomic; 0-conf transactions are "already secure enough" and need risk management, not a protocol "fix".

## Key arguments and claims
- "The security industry and criminal activity feeds upon itself. Crime and entrepreneurship each create opportunities for the other" — an adversarial co-evolution model where each side's innovations open markets for the other.
- "Markets spawn their counter": every security improvement creates new forms of criminal activity, and new criminal groups create openings for "entrepreneurial risk and security firms" aligned to the new threat.
- Criminal innovation is itself subject to market discipline: where crime is insufficiently profitable for its risk, "innovation may be driven down" and incumbent criminal groups "entrench and act to protect their existing market share" — increased risk and reduced profitability suppress new crime forms.
- "Any time where a set of concurrent disequilibria has come from the result of innovation, opportunities inevitably result" — true for legitimate and black markets alike.
- Stagnation is symmetric: "In long term states of relatively low disequilibria, all societies stagnate. This is equally true of crime", producing "entrenchment and lethargy".
- The punchline is a risk-management doctrine: "risk matters. No system is ever perfect. No system can be made to be perfect. What we need to do is no more than to ensure that a system is not economically viable to be exploited."
- Applied to Bitcoin: "we do not need to find a 'Fix' for 0-conf, it is already secure enough. We only need to ensure that we understand the risk we face."
- Bitcoin's framing: "Bitcoin is a commercial system. It is money" — so risk is "best mitigated not by altering the system, but in the creation of the best responses to risk. Some risk will be mitigated, some shall be accepted."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
He imports a Schumpeterian/evolutionary-economics frame (disequilibria, innovation, market entry and entrenchment) into criminology and security, treating offenders and defenders as entrepreneurs in coupled markets. The method is abstract model-building with no empirical data, then a normative pivot: because perfection is impossible, engineering should aim at economic non-viability of attack rather than absolute security — a commercial risk-acceptance ethos rather than a cryptographic-perfectionist one.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Contradicts the "0-conf is broken/unsafe" orthodoxy (used to justify RBF, Lightning, or waiting for confirmations): he asserts 0-conf "is already secure enough" and that the correct response is merchant-side risk management, not protocol change.
- Contradicts the "code is law / trustless perfection" instinct to patch every theoretical attack vector at the protocol level: altering the system is explicitly the wrong lever; risk acceptance is "the nature of risk".
- Implicitly rejects the security-through-consensus-maximalism framing: security is an economic threshold (make exploitation unprofitable), not an absolute property.

# Bitcoin is all about incentives — core principles

- **Bitcoin is first and foremost an incentive system.** Miners (nodes) have skin in the game; they compete for the block reward and fees, and so seek to maintain the protocol rather than debase it.
- **Developers are not the market.** The design is a system that does not allow developers to become technocrats; change can occur, but it is expensive and difficult, which keeps the money supply stable.
- **Invalid opcodes can be treated as fees.** Transactions that inject unauthorised opcodes remain within Bitcoin’s design if honest miners sweep those outputs as fees — attacker spend subsidises the honest chain.
- **Original opcodes stay as defined.** Reckless experimentation with sound money is stopped by locking the original instruction set; protocol immutability is the business guarantee.
- **Private has to be traceable.** Bitcoin is pseudonymous honest money, not anonymous crime money.
- **Fees replace the subsidy.** When the block reward gets too small, transaction fees become the main compensation for nodes — as the whitepaper’s incentive section said.
- **Miner competition, not social consensus.** Sound money that cannot be easily debased is capitalist money under competing miners; as it scales it becomes harder to alter.
- **Wallets that force a fork owe their users.** Steering clients onto one branch without consent, so that funds become unspendable or split, creates a legal right to recover against the wallet or exchange.

# When Money Moves for Free, Who Gets Paid? — core principles

- **A structural wedge between production cost and price is economic rent.** Scalable blockchain infrastructure collapses the cost of producing trusted transfer to a benchmarked floor near $0.00000002 per transaction — a floor, not a production figure, but enough to establish that the gap to legacy pricing is rent, not cost recovery.
- **The deposit franchise monetises the absence of alternatives.** In 2024 US banks held about $3.86 trillion in zero-interest accounts; at a 5.3% Federal Funds Rate the implicit yield exceeded $200 billion, none of it passed to depositors. The bundle (free debit card, branch, transfers) persisted because no unbundled alternative existed — and non-interest-bearing deposits fell $1.66 trillion in 2022–2024 as rates rose.
- **Card networks sell certainty, not credit.** Visa and Mastercard take no credit risk and earn $64 billion a year in combined revenue at net margins above 40%; remittances extract on average ~$33 from a $200 US-to-Nigeria transfer (over 16%) along a correspondent banking chain whose infrastructure has not fundamentally changed in decades.
- **The rent logic extends beyond payments.** Trade finance (letters of credit are conditional settlement — pay if and only if conditions are met — which code can provide), treasury management (banks monetise settlement dead time) and collateral management (multi-day bilateral settlement cycles) all monetise friction that instant, final settlement removes.
- **Judgement-based banking functions survive.** Credit underwriting (judgement, local knowledge, covenants), liquidity transformation (borrowing short, lending long) and fraud, dispute and consumer-protection systems are not settlement functions; chargebacks exist because irreversibility is a feature for some purposes and a catastrophic bug for others.
- **Cheap settlement benefits users only under credible governance neutrality.** A chain whose developers can alter fee structures, blacklist addresses or fork at will merely substitutes a less accountable rent-extractor operating under a decentralisation brand; technical decentralisation is necessary but not sufficient — governance decentralisation is what actually matters.
- **Adjustment pressure is unevenly distributed.** Deposit-float banks face direct substitution; remittance firms in high-cost corridors face acute pressure; card networks are harder to contest because their revenue also reflects network effects, brand trust and fraud infrastructure.
- **Incumbents are manoeuvring to inherit the rents.** Banks exploring digital-asset custody, card networks running blockchain pilots and central banks in 130+ countries developing CBDCs are all positioning to control the next settlement layer rather than be displaced by it.
- **Chokepoint rents are competed away when chokepoints become cheap to build.** The Medici (correspondent networks), Western Union (telegraph wires) and Visa/Mastercard (authorisation networks) each earned chokepoint rent that eroded once the cost of building the chokepoint fell towards zero — the historical convergence pattern now applying to settlement itself.
- **The builder's target is neutral infrastructure.** The prize is genuinely neutral settlement — the TCP/IP of money: infrastructure that competition happens on top of rather than through. Low-cost, high-volume settlement at micropayment scale is the economically decisive use case, and every business standing between a payment and its destination should be evaluated against the production-cost floor.

# Why Law Matters
**Date:** 2019-08-02 | **URL:** https://medium.com/@craig_10243/why-law-matters-db0e32492d05
**Subtitle:** In the simple thought experiment, I will demonstrate the problems that people cover up when they're lying to you about the so-called…

## Core thesis
The Medium body is an excerpt; the full essay (including the promised "Thought Experiment on Decentralisation") continues on craigwright.net. Craig argues that the crypto industry is a "criminal empire" of conmen sustained by false news and sybil accounts, and that the slow machinery of law is about to catch up with it. "Decentralisation" as preached by his opponents is a collectivist lie — a false equivocation, not a distributed system — and his own work (patents, peer-reviewed papers, scaling Bitcoin "in the way I originally envisioned") will prevail regardless of personal attacks.

## Key arguments and claims
- The industry is witnessing "the final desperate efforts of a bunch of conmen and felons who seek to maintain the criminal empire that they have built using cryptocurrency."
- Crypto has been sold as a get-rich-quick scheme: "the Ponzi scheme that has been promoted as crypto and Bitcoin", propped up by "false news and 'sybil'd' accounts."
- "Law is slow… but effective." — within the week, "one of the false-news proponents" must "submit a defence to the court or lose" (era context: Craig's 2019 defamation actions against critics such as Hodlonaut and Peter McCormack).
- "The lie of collectivist decentralisation is not a distributed system, but rather a false equivocation" — its promoters rely on people ignoring "the tragedy of the commons" and pull "the technical wool over your eyes."
- Objective validation beats reputation attacks: "When a patent is filed, there is no place for ad hominem"; his peer-reviewed academic papers are accepted "without knowledge of who I am."
- He claims to have "already created systems and technology that would allow every individual person to seek to mine Bitcoin or an alternate cryptocurrency in a truly equal manner without any access to an ASIC" — while stressing this is "a radically different system than Bitcoin."
- The thought experiment (body continues on his blog) aims to demolish "the false claim that [Bitcoin] brings democracy to money."

## How Craig reasons (his model/logic)
Craig pairs a legal-eschatological frame ("all of it will end soon"; law as slow but inexorable) with an appeal to impersonal institutions — patent examination and blind peer review — as arbiters immune to the ad hominem campaigns he says are waged against him. He then shifts to the thought-experiment method: constructing an alternative equal-access mining system to expose, by contrast, what he says Bitcoin never was. The mode is combative and prosecutorial, naming opponents as criminals rather than engaging their technical claims on equal terms.

## Where this contradicts BTC-mainstream logic
- Directly attacks the core BTC orthodoxy that "decentralisation" is Bitcoin's defining virtue and that it "brings democracy to money" — Craig calls this a "false equivocation" and a lie.
- Contradicts the "code is law / crypto is beyond the reach of courts" stance: he insists ordinary legal process will terminate the crypto economy's criminal wing.
- Contradicts the egalitarian "everyone can mine / one-CPU-one-vote" reading of Bitcoin: his equal-mining scheme is explicitly "a radically different system than Bitcoin", implying Bitcoin itself was never designed for that equality.
- Frames mainstream crypto media and social consensus as manufactured ("false news", "sybil'd accounts"), inverting the community's self-image as organic.
